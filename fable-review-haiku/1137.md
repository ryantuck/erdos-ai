## Summary

The problem: let $d_n = p_{n+1} - p_n$ denote the gap between consecutive primes $p_n$ and $p_{n+1}$. The question asks: is it true that the ratio of the maximum product of consecutive gaps to the square of the maximum gap itself tends to zero as we range over primes up to $x$? Formally: does $\frac{\max_{n < x} d_n d_{n-1}}{(\max_{n < x} d_n)^2} \to 0$ as $x \to \infty$? The problem is OPEN (per the metadata, last updated 2026-01-23). The Lean statement correctly formalizes the limit, but encodes it as a bare assertion rather than a yes/no question, which contradicts the OPEN status and violates the corpus convention. The definitions of prime gaps and their maxima are sound; the only semantic defect is the question-form encoding.
