## Summary

Erdős Problem 1156 asks two related yes/no questions about the chromatic number χ of uniformly random graphs G(n, 1/2):

1. **Concentration (Part 1):** Is there a constant C such that χ is almost surely concentrated on at most C values?
2. **Anticoncentration (Part 2):** Can we prove χ cannot be concentrated in any interval of fixed relative width for infinitely many n?

The module docstring provides comprehensive context: Bollobás proved χ ~ n/(2 log₂ n) asymptotically, Shamir-Spencer achieved concentration within o(√n), and Heckel-Riordan showed concentration cannot be within n^c for c < 1/2. Both parts are formalized as theorems with placeholder bodies (`:= sorry`).

The formalization correctly captures both parts: Part 1 as an existential over concentration sets of bounded cardinality, and Part 2 as a universal anticoncentration statement (stronger than the original "does there exist f" — now says "for *all* f" — which is mathematically sound and aligns with Heckel-Riordan). The semantic math is sound. However, both theorems are encoded as **bare assertions** (direct existentials) rather than **yes/no question form** (`answer(sorry) ↔ …`), which violates the formalization corpus's A4 convention for open yes/no questions and the FABLE_REVIEW.md checklist. This is a formal-style defect that must be corrected.
