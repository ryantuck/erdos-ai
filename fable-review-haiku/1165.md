## Summary

The problem asks: given a simple random walk on ℤ² starting at the origin, where each step goes one unit in a cardinal direction with equal probability, let F(n) be the set of "favourite sites" (sites visited the maximum number of times up to step n). Find the probability P(|F(n)| = r infinitely often) for r ≥ 3. The problem is now solved: Tóth (2001) proved that P(|F(n)| = r i.o.) = 0 for all r ≥ 4; Hao, Li, Okada, and Zheng (2024) proved P(|F(n)| = 3 i.o.) = 1.

The Lean formalization encodes this as a conjunction of two theorems:
1. Part 1: |F(n)| = 3 infinitely often, almost surely (from the HLOZ result).
2. Part 2: For each r ≥ 4, |F(n)| = r occurs only finitely often, almost surely (from Tóth).

The definitions of the random walk model, visit counts, and favourite sites are mathematically correct and well-structured. The statement faithfully captures both proven results.
