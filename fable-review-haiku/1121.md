## Summary

Erdős problem 1121, solved by Goodman and Goodman (1945), asserts that if a finite configuration of circles in ℝ² satisfies a non-separability condition (no line disjoint from all circles divides them), then they can be covered by a single circle whose radius equals the sum of the original radii. The Lean statement formalizes this as: given n circles with centers in ℝ² and positive radii, if for every line parameterized by a unit normal v and offset d, whenever all circles are disjoint from the line all centers lie on the same side, then there exists a point p such that each circle is contained in a ball of radius Σrⱼ centered at p. Back-translation confirms the Lean and English statements are exactly equivalent; all semantic traps are handled correctly; the hypotheses and conclusion match the problem faithfully.
