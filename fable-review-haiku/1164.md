## Summary

The problem asks: let $R_n$ be the maximal integer such that almost every random walk from the origin in $\mathbb{Z}^2$ visits every lattice point $x \in \mathbb{Z}^2$ with $\|x\| \le R_n$ in at most $n$ steps. Is it true that $\log R_n \asymp \sqrt{\log n}$ (i.e., there exist constants $c_1, c_2 > 0$ such that almost surely, for all sufficiently large $n$, $c_1\sqrt{\log n} \le \log R_n \le c_2\sqrt{\log n}$)? The source page confirms this is **PROVED** (solved in the affirmative) by Révész and Kesten independently. A stronger distributional result was proved by Dembo, Peres, Rosen, and Zeitouni. The Lean formalization correctly encodes the core statement as a direct assertion: the existence of positive constants bounding $\log R_n$ between $c_1\sqrt{\log n}$ and $c_2\sqrt{\log n}$ almost surely eventually. All definitions are sound, all quantifier structure and semantics are faithful to the source, and no defects were found.
