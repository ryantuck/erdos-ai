## Summary

The page (PROVED) asks whether $f_3(n)=o(3^n)$, where $f_3(n)$ is the largest subset of $\{0,1,2\}^n$ with no three points on a
line. Moser showed $f_3(n)\gg3^n/\sqrt n$, and the answer yes is a corollary of density Hales–Jewett [FuKa91].

The Lean defines combinatorial lines and asserts: for every $\varepsilon>0$, for large $n$, every $A\subseteq\{0,1,2\}^n$ with
$|A|>\varepsilon3^n$ contains a combinatorial line.

- **Source** (verbatim): "Let $f_3(n)$ be the maximal size of a subset of $\{0,1,2\}^n$ which contains no three points on a

- **The two notions of line.** Three distinct collinear points of $\{0,1,2\}^n$ form a *Moser line*: each active coordinate runs
  $0,1,2$ or $2,1,0$. A combinatorial line is the special case where all active coordinates run $0,1,2$ together. So
  $(0,2),(1,1),(2,0)$ is a geometric line but not a combinatorial one.
  - In $\{0,1,2\}^2$ there are 8 geometric and 7 combinatorial lines; in $\{0,1,2\}^3$, 49 and 37.
  - The maxima avoiding them agree for $n\le2$ (2 and 6) and differ at $n=3$: 16 (Moser's value) against 18. These are from the
    review's exhaustive search.
- **Implication.** Every geometric-line-free set is combinatorial-line-free, so DHJ(3) implies the page's $f_3(n)=o(3^n)$.
