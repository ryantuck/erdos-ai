## Summary

The page (FALSIFIABLE) asks: "If $G$ is a graph with at most $k$ edge disjoint triangles then can $G$ be made triangle-free after
removing at most $2k$ edges?" It attributes the problem to Tuza and records the trivial $3k$ bound, the optimality examples $K_4$
and $K_5$, Haxell's $(3-\frac3{23}+o(1))k$, and Kahn and Park's proof for random graphs.

The Lean states, for every finite simple graph $G$ and every $k$ with $\nu(G)\le k$, the existence of a triangle-free $H\le G$
missing at most $2k$ edges of $G$.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: pass.**

- **Source** (verbatim): "If $G$ is a graph with at most $k$ edge disjoint triangles then can $G$ be made triangle-free after
  removing at most $2k$ edges?"
- **Back-translation** (lines 40–53): "For every finite simple graph $G$ and every $k$: if every family of triangles of $G$ that
  pairwise share at most one vertex has at most $k$ members, then some triangle-free subgraph $H\le G$ misses at most $2k$ edges
  of $G$."
- **Checks.**
  - *Edge-disjointness.* Two distinct triangles share an edge exactly when they share two vertices, so `(t₁ ∩ t₂).card ≤ 1` for
    distinct members is edge-disjointness.
  - *"At most $k$ edge disjoint triangles"* is $\nu(G)\le k$; quantifying over all $k$ with this hypothesis is the same as
    $\tau(G)\le2\nu(G)$.
  - *"Made triangle-free after removing edges"* is a triangle-free spanning subgraph $H\le G$; `G.edgeSet \ H.edgeSet` is the set of
    removed edges and is finite because `V` is.
  - *Tests.* The hypothesis is strong enough for the trivial bound: `three_k` derives $\tau\le3k$ from `hpack` alone, by deleting
    the edges of a maximum packing. The conclusion has the right strength: $K_4$ satisfies `hpack` with $k=1$ (`k4_pack`) and
    no triangle-free subgraph misses only one edge (`k4_cover`), so the statement with $2k-1$ is false (`two_k_best`).
