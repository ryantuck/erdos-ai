## Summary

$h(n)$ is the least $k$ such that every group with no $n+1$ pairwise non-commuting elements
is a union of $k$ abelian subgroups. The page asks (OPEN) to estimate $h(n)$, and records
Pyber's $c_1^n<h(n)<c_2^n$, with the lower bound due earlier to Isaacs.

The input states Pyber's bounds for all $n\ge1$, which is false at $n=1,2$. v2 restricts them
to large $n$ and adds the small values.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: DEFECT (fixed).**

- **Source** (verbatim): "Let $h(n)$ be minimal such that any group $G$ with the property that
  any subset of $>n$ elements contains some $x\neq y$ such that $xy=yx$ can be covered by at
  most $h(n)$ many Abelian subgroups. Estimate $h(n)$ as well as possible."
- **Back-translation of the input's theorem:** "there are $1<c_1<c_2$ with
  $c_1^n<h(n)<c_2^n$ for **every** $n\ge1$."
- **[defect: false-at-small-parameters]**
  - **$n=1$.**
    - The property means every 2-subset commutes, so $G$ is abelian, and `⊤` covers it:
      $h(1)\le1$.
    - $k=0$ fails, since the trivial group satisfies the property vacuously and is
      nonempty. So `erdosH 1 = 1`.
    - Then $c_1^1<1$ contradicts $c_1>1$.
  - **$n=2$.** If $x,y$ do not commute, then $x,y,xy$ are distinct and pairwise
    non-commuting. Indeed $x\cdot xy=xy\cdot x$ iff $xy=yx$, and similarly for $y$. So only
    abelian groups qualify, and `erdosH 2 = 1`.
- **v2:** `∃ N, ∀ n ≥ N, c₁ ^ n < h n ∧ h n < c₂ ^ n`. That is the standard reading of an
  asymptotic two-sided bound, and Pyber's result as the page states it.

**A2. Lean semantics traps: pass.**

- **`sInf`.** For $n\ge1$ the set is nonempty: Neumann and Pyber, for all groups. For
  $n=0$, no group has the property (a singleton has no distinct pair), so every $k$
  qualifies and `erdosH 0 = sInf ℕ = 0`. The theorems avoid $n=0$.
- **The cast `(erdosH n : ℝ)`** is fine.
- **Covering by `Fin k → Subgroup G` with repetition** means "at most $k$" for $k\ge1$.
- **Universes.** `∀ (G : Type)` restricts to universe 0. That is harmless here: coverings
  depend only on $G/Z(G)$, which is finite and of size bounded in terms of $n$.

**A3. Definitions: pass.** All three definitions match their docstrings, and finite subsets
suffice for the page's "any subset". No `sorry` in definitions.

**A4. Question form and answer polarity: no formal target for the open part.** "Estimate
$h(n)$ as well as possible" is open-ended. `erdos_problem_117` records the proved estimate.
v2's docstring labels it PROVED, and says the problem stays OPEN.
