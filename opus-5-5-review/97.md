## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: DEFECT (fixed).**

- **Back-translation:** "For every finite $P\subset\mathbb R^2$ in convex position there is
  $p\in P$ such that for every $r$, at most 3 points of $P\setminus\{p\}$ lie at distance
  $r$ from $p$."
- **[defect: false-at-small-parameters]** Take $P=\emptyset$.
  - `ConvexPosition ∅` is `∀ p ∈ ∅, …`, which is true.
  - The conclusion `∃ p ∈ ∅, …` is false.
  - So the universally quantified theorem is false, and unprovable regardless of the
    answer to Erdős's question.
  - A convex polygon has at least one vertex, at least three in fact, so the source never
    ranges over the empty set.
- **Fix:** add `P.Nonempty →`. This is the minimal repair, and it matches upstream's
  `A.Nonempty`. With it, the 1- and 2-point cases satisfy the conclusion trivially (at most
  one other vertex). So `P.Nonempty` and `3 ≤ P.card` give equivalent statements.
- **"No other 4 vertices equidistant"** is "$\forall r$, at most 3 others at distance
  $r$", which is correct.
  - Radii $r\le0$ contribute nothing, since distinct points have positive distance.
  - So the absence of upstream's `r > 0` guard is harmless.

- `variants.nonconvex` (PROVED): for every $k$, some nonempty point set has $\ge k$ points
  at distance 1 from each point.
  - Take $\{\sum_i\varepsilon_iv_i:\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^k\}$ for generic unit vectors
    $v_i$. All $2^k$ sums are distinct, and flipping one $\varepsilon_i$ gives $k$ unit
    neighbours.

- `variants.implies_linear_unit_distances` (PROVED): the page's reduction.
  - If every nonempty convex polygon has a vertex with at most $k$ others at each distance,
    then convex $n$-gons have at most $2kn$ ordered unit pairs.
  - Proof: induct by deleting that vertex. It accounts for at most $2k$ ordered pairs, and
    subsets of convex-position sets are in convex position.
