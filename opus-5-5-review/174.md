## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: pass.**

- **Source** (verbatim): "A finite set $A\subset \mathbb{R}^n$ is called Ramsey if, for any $k\geq 1$, there exists some $d=d(A,k)$
  such that in any $k$-colouring of $\mathbb{R}^d$ there exists a monochromatic copy of $A$. Characterise the Ramsey sets in
  $\mathbb{R}^n$."
- **Back-translation** (lines 25–36, 47–49): "For every $n$ and finite $A\subseteq\mathbb R^n$: [for every $k\ge1$ there is $d$
  such that every $k$-colouring of $\mathbb R^d$ makes some isometric image $\varphi(A)$, with $\varphi:\mathbb
  R^n\to\mathbb R^d$ distance-preserving, monochromatic] if and only if [$A$ lies on a sphere]."
- **Checks.**
  - *Copies.* A congruent copy of $A$ in $\mathbb R^d$ extends to a distance-preserving map of $\mathbb R^n$ once $d\ge n$.
    Ramseyness only gets easier as $d$ grows: restrict a colouring of a larger space to an embedded copy of $\mathbb R^d$. So
    requiring $\varphi$ on all of $\mathbb R^n$ is equivalent.
  - *Spherical.* `∃ center r, ∀ a ∈ A, dist a center = r`. The empty set and singletons qualify (and are Ramsey). Three collinear
    points do not (`not_isSpherical_collinear`).
  - *Colourings.* `Fin k` with `1 ≤ k`; $k=1$ is trivial and harmless.

**A2. Lean semantics traps: pass.** `EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin n)` carries the Euclidean metric, so `dist` is the right distance.
