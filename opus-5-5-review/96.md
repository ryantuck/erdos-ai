## Summary

The page asks for a proof that $n$ points in convex position determine $O(n)$ unit
distances; the status is OPEN. The Lean asserts that there is $C>0$ such that every finite
$P$ in convex position has at most $C|P|$ ordered unit-distance pairs. That is the same
proposition. Small $n$ are harmless, since a uniform $C$ covers them.

The best known bound is $n\log_2n+4n$, and the conjectured truth is about $2n$. v2 adds the
references and these four variants:

- Aggarwal's bound $n\log_2n+4n$ (proved);
- the Edelsbrunner–Hajnal construction with $2n-7$ unit pairs (proved, weakened to
  infinitely many $n$);
- the Erdős–Fishburn conjecture that $2n$ is the true bound (open);
- Erdős's conjecture $\sum_x g(x)<4n$ (open).

Three small inaccuracies:

1. **"Holds vacuously for empty sets and singletons".** It holds trivially ($0\le C\cdot|P|$),
   not vacuously; no hypothesis fails.
2. **"Bound trivially holds for any $C\ge6$ (since at most 6 ordered pairs exist among 3
