## Summary

For monic $p$ of degree $n$ with all roots in the closed unit disc, the page asks (PROVED)
whether the area of $\{|p|<1\}$ is $>n^{-O(1)}$, "or perhaps even" $>(\log n)^{-O(1)}$. The
results it records:

- Pommerenke gives $\gg n^{-4}$;
- Krishnapur–Lundberg–Ramachandran [KLR25] give $\gg(\log n)^{-1}$ and, from above,
  $\ll(\log\log n)^{-1}$, improving Wagner [Wa88];
- Pólya [Po28] gives $\le\pi$, with equality only for identical roots.

The input formalizes the main question faithfully, apart from the mislabelled measure. v2
fixes the measure, adds the parenthetical as a variant, and records the remarks.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: pass for the theorem.**

- **Source** (verbatim): "Let $p(z)=\prod_{i=1}^n (z-z_i)$ for $\lvert z_i\rvert \leq 1$. Is
  it true that $\lvert\{ z: \lvert p(z)\rvert <1\}\rvert>n^{-O(1)}$ (or perhaps even
  $>(\log n)^{-O(1)}$)?"
- **Back-translation:** "there are $\kappa,\delta>0$ such that for every $n\ge1$ and roots
  $z_1,\dots,z_n$ in the closed unit disc,
  $\mathrm{area}(\{|\prod(z-z_i)|<1\})\ge\delta n^{-\kappa}$." That is the main question,
  with the "yes" direction asserted.
- **The parenthetical** is a separate, stronger question; see B2.

**A2. Lean semantics traps: pass.**

- `(n : ℝ) ^ (-κ)` is rpow at $n\ge1$.
- `ENNReal.ofReal` of a positive real is compared against a measure.
- The guard `1 ≤ n` excludes $p=1$, whose interior is empty.
- `∏ i : Fin n, (X - C (roots i))` is monic of degree $n$, with exactly the given roots.

**A3. Definitions: DEFECT (fixed).**

- **[defect: def-docstring-mismatch]** `area S := Measure.hausdorffMeasure 2 S`, documented
  as "The 2D area of a subset of ℂ". Mathlib defines `μH[d] = mkMetric (fun r => r ^ d)`:
  covering sums of $\mathrm{diam}^d$, with no $\alpha(d)/2^d$ factor.
  - **Normalised Hausdorff measure** $\frac{\alpha(n)}{2^n}\mathcal H^n_{\mathrm{unnorm}}$
    equals Lebesgue measure on Euclidean $\mathbb R^n$. One direction is the isodiametric
    inequality, the other a Vitali covering argument.
  - **So on ℂ**, `μH[2]` $=\frac4\pi\lambda^2$. Sanity check: the unit disc has diameter 2,
    and covering it by itself gives $2^2=4=\frac4\pi\cdot\pi$.
  - **What Mathlib has.** Mathlib proves `μH[1] = volume` on ℝ and `μH[card ι] = volume` on
    `ι → ℝ` with the sup metric, but it has no isodiametric inequality and no Euclidean
    `μH[2]` lemma.
- **Consequences:**
  - `erdos_problem_116` keeps its truth value, since the factor $4/\pi$ is absorbed by
    $\delta$;
  - the theorem docstring's "2D Lebesgue measure (area)" and the `area` docstring are false;
  - Pólya's remark "area $\le\pi$", stated with `area`, would be false: the unit disc gives
    $4$.
- **The fix.** v2 sets `area S := volume S`, the Lebesgue measure from
  `Mathlib.MeasureTheory.Measure.Lebesgue.Complex`, with `Complex.volume_ball` giving
  $\pi r^2$. That matches the page's $|\cdot|$.
- `lemniscateInterior` matches its docstring. No `sorry` in definitions.

**A4. Question form and answer polarity: pass.** The problem is PROVED, and the true
statement is asserted directly.
