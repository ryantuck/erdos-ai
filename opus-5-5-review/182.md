## Summary

The page (PROVED) asks, for $k\ge3$, for the maximum number of edges of an $n$-vertex graph with no $k$-regular subgraph, and
whether it is $\ll n^{1+o(1)}$. Janzer and Sudakov [JaSu23] proved that $C(k)\,n\log\log n$ edges suffice, [CJMM24b] that
$C(k)\ll k^2$ is possible, and [PRS95] that $n\log\log n$ is best possible.

The Lean defines "contains a $k$-regular subgraph" as an injective homomorphism from a nonempty finite $k$-regular graph, and
asserts: for $k\ge3$ there are $C>0$ and $n_0$ such that every graph on $n\ge n_0$ vertices with at least $Cn\log\log n$ edges
contains a $k$-regular subgraph.

- **Checks.**

  - *Non-vacuity.* For large $n$, $Cn\log\log n<\binom n2$, so the hypothesis is satisfiable. The conclusion is not automatic:
    the edgeless graph has no $k$-regular subgraph for $k\ge1$ (`not_containsKRegularSubgraph182_bot`, proved).
  - *Quantifiers.* $C$ and $n_0$ may depend on $k$, as in [JaSu23].

**A2. Lean semantics traps: pass.** `Real.log (Real.log n)` is only used for $n\ge n_0$, where $n_0$ can be taken large; for
$n\le1$ it is $0$, and for $1<n<e$ it is negative, which only weakens the hypothesis on the small cases. `≥` against a real
threshold is fine.

**A3. Definitions: pass.**

- `containsKRegularSubgraph182 G k`: a nonempty finite `U : Type`, a graph `H` on it with every degree `k`, and an injective
  homomorphism into `G`. The image is a copy of `H` inside `G`, so this is "has a $k$-regular subgraph" (not necessarily
  induced). `U : Type` loses nothing, since the graph is on `Fin n`.
- The `Nonempty U` condition is essential: the graph on an empty type is vacuously $k$-regular for every $k$, and without the
  condition every graph would qualify.
- Sanity checks, all proved in Lean: a single vertex is a $0$-regular subgraph (`containsKRegularSubgraph182_zero`); the
  edgeless graph has no $k$-regular subgraph for $k\ge1$; $K_{k+1}$ has a $k$-regular subgraph
  (`containsKRegularSubgraph182_complete`).
