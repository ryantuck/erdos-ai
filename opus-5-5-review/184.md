## Summary

The page (OPEN at capture) conjectures that every graph on $n$ vertices decomposes into $O(n)$ edge-disjoint cycles and edges.
Erdős and Gallai proved $O(n\log n)$, Bucić and Montgomery $O(n\log^*n)$ [BM22], and Conlon, Fox and Sudakov $O_\varepsilon(n)$
under minimum degree $\varepsilon n$ [CFS14]; $K_{3,n-3}$ needs $(1+c)n$ pieces.

The Lean asserts: there is $C>0$ such that every graph on `Fin n` has `parts : Fin k → Finset (Sym2 (Fin n))` with $k\le Cn$,
pairwise disjoint, covering exactly the edge set, each part a singleton or `w.edges.toFinset` for a cycle `w` of `G`.

## A. Semantic fidelity

- **Source** (verbatim): "Any graph on $n$ vertices can be decomposed into $O(n)$ many edge-disjoint cycles and edges."
- **Back-translation** (lines 30–40): "There is $C>0$ such that for every $n$ and every graph on $n$ vertices, the edge set is a
  disjoint union of at most $Cn$ sets, each consisting of one edge or of the edges of a cycle of the graph." This is the page's
  statement.
- **Checks.**
  - *Exact partition.* Disjointness for $i\ne j$ plus `e ∈ G.edgeFinset ↔ ∃ i, e ∈ parts i` make the parts a partition of the
    edge set; the `↔` also forces every singleton part to be an edge of `G`.

  - *Satisfiability.* The single edges give a decomposition with $|E|\le\binom n2$ parts (`trivial_decomposition`, proved).
  - *Small $n$.* For $n\le1$ there are no edges and $k=0$ works.
