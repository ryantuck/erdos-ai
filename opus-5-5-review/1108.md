## Summary

The problem (OPEN) reads: "Let $A=\{\sum_{n\in S}n!: S\subset\mathbb N$ finite$\}$. If
$k\geq2$, then does $A$ contain only finitely many $k$th powers? Does it contain only
finitely many powerful numbers?" Erdős asked it at Oberwolfach in 1988. The page adds that
it is open even whether $1+n!$ is a square infinitely often (#398), describes the
motivating Mahler problem, and notes Brindza–Erdős's result that powerful sums of $r$
factorials have bounded $n_1$.

The Lean defines membership in $A$ as $\exists S$, $m=\sum_{n\in S}n!$ with $S$ a
`Finset ℕ`, and powerful as positive with $p\mid n\Rightarrow p^2\mid n$. It asserts
finiteness of $\{m\in A: m=b^k\}$ for each $k\ge2$, and of the powerful elements of $A$.
Back-translated, these are exactly the two questions, in their asked direction. That is
the corpus convention for open yes/no questions.

The only interpretive point is ℕ's zero. With $0\in S$ allowed, $A$ also contains
$4=0!+1!+2!$, $10$ and $28$. The page itself uses $0\in\mathbb N$ in the same paragraph
($1+7+7^2+7^3$ with $7^0=1$), so the Lean follows the page literally. Below $16!$, the
only square or powerful number the convention adds is $4$.
