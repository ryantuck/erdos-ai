## Summary

The page (OPEN, \$500) defines $F^{(t)}(n,\alpha)$ as the smallest $m$ for which some 2-colouring of the $t$-subsets of $[n]$
gives every $X$ with $\lvert X\rvert\ge m$ at least $\alpha\binom{\lvert X\rvert}t$ subsets of each colour. It asks whether, as
$\alpha$ moves from $0$ to $1/2$, $F^{(t)}(n,\alpha)$ changes continuously or jumps, and whether there is only one jump.
Erdős guessed one jump, at $0$. The remarks record $F^{(t)}(n,0)\asymp\log_{t-1}n$ (conjecturally), bounds of the form
$(\log n)^{1/(t-1)}$ for $\alpha>0$, the case $t=3$ by Conlon, Fox and Sudakov, and a general bound $(\log n)^{c_\alpha}$.

The Lean defines the balance condition and $F$ correctly, and states that $F^{(t)}(n,\alpha)$ is at most a power of $\log n$
for every $t\ge2$ and $0<\alpha<1/2$.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: defect (wrong target; the statement is a known result).**

- **Source** (verbatim): "Let $\alpha\in[0,1/2)$ and $n,t\geq 1$. Let $F^{(t)}(n,\alpha)$ be the smallest $m$ such that we
  can $2$-colour the edges of the complete $t$-uniform hypergraph on $n$ vertices such that if $X\subseteq [n]$ with
  $\lvert X\rvert \geq m$ then there are at least $\alpha \binom{\lvert X\rvert}{t}$ many $t$-subsets of $X$ of each colour.
  For fixed $n,t$ as we change $\alpha$ from $0$ to $1/2$ does $F^{(t)}(n,\alpha)$ increase continuously or are there jumps?
  Only one jump?"
- **Back-translation of the input's theorem** (lines 58–65): "For every $t\ge2$ and $0<\alpha<1/2$ there are $C,D>0$ and
  $N_0$ such that $F^{(t)}(n,\alpha)\le C(\log n)^D$ for $n\ge N_0$."
- **The definitions** (lines 35–44) match the page for $\alpha>0$: `c` colours every subset of `Fin n` but only the
  $t$-subsets are read; the count is of `X.powersetCard t` filtered by colour; "at least" is `≤`.
- **The upper bound is classical.** Fix $t\ge2$, $0\le\alpha<1/2$ and let $H(\alpha)<1$ be the binary entropy. For a $k$-set
  $X$ with $N=\binom kt$ subsets, the colourings of those subsets that give colour $b$ fewer than $\alpha N$ of them number
  $\sum_{j<\alpha N}\binom Nj\le x^{-\alpha N}(1+x)^N=2^{H(\alpha)N}$ for $x=\alpha/(1-\alpha)$. So in a uniformly random
  colouring, the probability that some $X$ with $\lvert X\rvert=k\ge m$ is unbalanced is at most
  $\sum_{k\ge m}2\binom nk2^{-(1-H(\alpha))\binom kt}$. As $\binom kt\ge(k/t)^t$ for $k\ge t$, each term is at most
  $2n^k2^{-k(\log_2n+2)}=2\cdot4^{-k}$ once $k^{t-1}\ge t^t(\log_2n+2)/(1-H(\alpha))$, and then the sum is below $1$. So
  $F^{(t)}(n,\alpha)\le C_{t,\alpha}(\log n)^{1/(t-1)}$ for large $n$, for **every** $\alpha<1/2$. With $D=1/(t-1)$ this is the
  input's statement (`first_pass_of_random_upper`, proved in Lean from it).
- **What Erdős guessed.** At $\alpha=0$ (read as "at least one subset of each colour") $F$ is the inverse Ramsey function,
  $\asymp\log_{t-1}n$ conjecturally. For $\alpha$ close to $1/2$ the order is $(\log n)^{1/(t-1)}$. "The jump occurs all in
  one step at $0$" therefore means $F^{(t)}(n,\alpha)\gg_{t,\alpha}(\log n)^{1/(t-1)}$ for every $\alpha>0$, a lower bound: every
  colouring must contain a set of that size in which one colour has density below $\alpha$. For $t=2$ this follows from
  Ramsey's theorem. For $t=3$ it is the theorem of [CFS11], whose title, *Large almost monochromatic subsets in hypergraphs*,
  is about exactly such sets. For $t\ge4$ only $(\log n)^{c_\alpha}$ is known (the page's last remark), which is why the
  problem is open.
- **Why the first pass went wrong.** The page prints "$F^{(t)}(n,\alpha)\gg_\alpha(\log n)^{1/(t-1)}$ for all $\alpha>0$, and
  a similar upper bound holds for $\alpha$ close to $1/2$" and "[CFS11]: $F^{(3)}(n,\alpha)\ll_\alpha\sqrt{\log n}$". With the
  page's own definition both signs are reversed: the bound for all $\alpha>0$ is the random-colouring upper bound, and
  [CFS11] gives a lower bound. (Read literally, the first bullet plus the random bound would solve the problem for every $t$,
  which contradicts its OPEN status.) The originating session followed the printed signs (A5).
- **Degenerate inputs.** For $n\le1$, $\log n\le0$; the input's $N_0$ handles this. `F_balanced` is the `sInf` of a set that
  contains $n+1$ (`mem_succ`), so it is a true minimum.

- **$\alpha=0$.** As written, "at least $0$" subsets of each colour always holds, so `F_balanced t n 0 = 0` (proved). The
