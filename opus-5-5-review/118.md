## Summary

The page asks (DISPROVED) whether $\alpha\to(\alpha,3)^2$ implies $\alpha\to(\alpha,n)^2$
for all finite $n\ge3$. Schipperus and Darby showed independently that it does not, and
Larson exhibited $\alpha=\omega^{\omega^2}$, $n=5$.

The input negates the universally quantified implication over ordinals. That is correct.
v2 adds the concrete counterexample and fixes the docstring.

- **Source** (verbatim): "Let $\alpha$ be a cardinal or ordinal number or an order type such
  that every two-colouring of $K_\alpha$ contains either a red $K_\alpha$ or a blue $K_3$. For

  blue $K_n$?"
- **Back-translation:** "it is not the case that every ordinal $\alpha$ with
  $\alpha\to(\alpha,3)^2$ satisfies $\alpha\to(\alpha,n)^2$ for all $n\ge3$." That is the
  negative answer.
- **Ordinals are the right reading.**
  - For infinite cardinals $\kappa$, Erdős–Dushnik–Miller gives $\kappa\to(\kappa,\omega)^2$,
    so the implication is trivially true there.
  - A finite $m\ge2$ is not a partition ordinal: colour a single pair blue.
  - The problem's content lies in countable ordinals, where the page and the literature
    place it ("partition ordinals").
- **Copies.** A homogeneous copy of $\beta$ is the image of an order embedding of
  `{a // a < β}`, i.e. a subset of order type $\beta$. That is the standard definition.

**A2. Lean semantics traps: pass.**

- **Colourings.** `f` takes ordered pairs, but homogeneity is checked only on `i < j`, with

  $[\alpha]^2$ arises.
- **Numerals.** `3` and `↑n` are the finite ordinals, so a blue copy of `3` is a blue
  triangle.
- **`¬ ∀ α, …`** negates the whole implication.
- **Universes.** The theorem is universe-polymorphic. $\omega^{\omega^2}$ exists in every
  universe, so the negation holds in each.
