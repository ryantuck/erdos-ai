## Summary

The page states, as a theorem proved by Altman [Al63] and verified in Lean, that $n$ points
forming a convex polygon determine at least $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ distinct distances. The
Lean theorem says exactly this. Its hypotheses are `InConvexPosition A` (no point in the
convex hull of the others) and $|A|\ge2$; its conclusion is `A.card / 2 ≤ (distinctDistances A).card`.
v2 adds the references, the PROVED (LEAN) status, the one-point strengthening (#982, open)
and Fishburn's sum conjecture (open).

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: pass.**

- **Source:** "If $n$ distinct points in $\mathbb{R}^2$ form a convex polygon then they
  determine at least $\lfloor \frac{n}{2}\rfloor$ distinct distances."
- **Back-translation:** "For every finite $A\subset\mathbb R^2$ in which no point lies in
  the convex hull of the others, if $|A|\ge2$ then $\lfloor|A|/2\rfloor\le$ the number of
  distinct values $d(p,q)$, $p\ne q\in A$."
- **"Form a convex polygon" = convex position.** For $n\ge3$, convex position means exactly
  that $A$ is the vertex set of a strictly convex $n$-gon. Three collinear points are
  excluded, because the middle one lies in the hull of the other two. For $n=2$ the
  conclusion $1\le1$ is trivial.
- **`hn : 2 ≤ A.card`** is redundant but harmless: for $n\le1$ both sides are $0$.
- **Tight:** the regular $n$-gon has exactly $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ distinct distances, so
  the statement is neither vacuous nor slack. A scratch check for $n=3..11$ gives
  $R(x)=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ at every vertex.

**A2. Lean semantics traps: pass.**

- `convexHull ℝ (↑A \ {p})` is taken over `EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 2)` with its ℝ-module
