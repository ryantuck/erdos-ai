## Summary

The page asks (OPEN) whether $h(n)/n\to\infty$. Here $h(n)$ is the least number of distinct
distances determined by $n$ points with no three on a line and no four on a circle. The
Lean asserts that for every $C>0$ there is $N$ such that every admissible set of $n\ge N$
points has at least $Cn$ distinct distances. That is the "yes" answer. v2 adds the
references, the remarks, and the Erdős–Füredi–Pach–Ruzsa bound
$h(n)<n\exp(c\sqrt{\log n})$ as a proved variant.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: pass.**

- **Source:** "Let $h(n)$ be such that any $n$ points in $\mathbb{R}^2$, with no three on a
  line and no four on a circle, determine at least $h(n)$ distinct distances. Does
  $h(n)/n\to\infty$?"
- $h(n)$ is the largest valid lower bound, which is the minimum over admissible sets.
  Admissible sets exist for every $n$, e.g. generic points, so the minimum is
  well-defined.
- So $h(n)/n\to\infty$ $\iff$ $\forall C\,\exists N\,\forall n\ge N\,\forall$ admissible
  $P$ with $|P|=n$: $\mathrm{dd}(P)\ge Cn$. That is exactly the Lean statement.
- Restricting to $C>0$ loses nothing, since the condition is monotone in $C$.
- **Not vacuous:** admissible sets exist for every $n$.

**A2. Lean semantics traps: pass.**

- `Set.ncard {d | ∃ p ∈ P, ∃ q ∈ P, p ≠ q ∧ dist p q = d}`: the set is the image of the
  finite set $P\times P$ minus the diagonal, so it is finite and `ncard` is the true count.
- No ℕ subtraction or division.
