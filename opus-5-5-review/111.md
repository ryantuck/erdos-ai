## Summary

$h_G(n)$ is the least $k$ such that every $n$-vertex subgraph of $G$ can be made bipartite
by deleting $k$ edges. The page asks (OPEN) "what is the behaviour of $h_G(n)$?" and,
explicitly, whether $h_G(n)/n\to\infty$ for every $G$ with $\chi(G)=\aleph_1$.

The remarks record three things:

- $h_G(n)\gg n$ always holds;
- [EHS82] found a $G$ with $h_G(n)\ll n^{3/2}$;
- Erdős [Er81] conjectured that $n^{1+\varepsilon}$ is achievable.

The input's only theorem is that last remark. v2 states the explicit question as
`erdos_problem_111`, with the asked direction asserted, and keeps the rest as variants.

**A1. Statement fidelity: DEFECT (fixed).**

- **Source** (verbatim): "If $G$ is a graph let $h_G(n)$ be defined such that any subgraph
  of $G$ on $n$ vertices can be made bipartite after deleting at most $h_G(n)$ edges. What is
  the behaviour of $h_G(n)$? Is it true that $h_G(n)/n\to\infty$ for every graph $G$ with

- **Back-translation of the input's theorem:** "There is a graph $G$ with $\chi(G)=\aleph_1$

- **[defect: wrong-target-statement]** This is the remark "In [Er81] Erdős conjectured that
  this can be improved to $\ll n^{1+\epsilon}$", not the problem's question.
  - The question is a universal superlinear *lower* bound. The remark is an existential
    near-linear *upper* bound.
  - Neither implies the other. A $G$ with
    $h_G(n)\asymp n\log n$ would satisfy both. A $G$ with $h_G(n)\asymp n$ would satisfy
    [Er81] and refute the question.
  - So a proof of the input's theorem would leave #111 open, and a disproof would not
    answer it either.
- **v2 main theorem:** `∀ V G, HasChromaticNumberAleph1 G → ∀ C : ℝ, ∃ N, ∀ n ≥ N,
  C·n ≤ hFun G n`, i.e. $h_G(n)/n\to\infty$.
- **`hFun` over induced subgraphs.** It maximizes over induced $n$-vertex subgraphs, which
  equals the maximum over all $n$-vertex subgraphs. Deleting edges never increases the
  number of deletions needed for bipartiteness.
