**B2. Variants and completeness: pass; enrichment added.**

- `variants.de_bruijn_erdos` (PROVED, [dBEr51]): $\chi(G)=\top$ implies that for each
  $n\ge1$ there is a finite subgraph with $\chi=n$.
  - Compactness gives finite subgraphs of arbitrarily large $\chi$.
  - Deleting vertices one at a time lowers $\chi$ by at most 1 per step, so every value
    $n$ is hit.
- `variants.aleph0_fails` (PROVED): the page's "this fails if the graph has chromatic number
  $\aleph_0$".
  - Construction: a disjoint union of finite $G_m$ with $\chi(G_m)=m$, in which every
    subgraph on at most $\max_{j\le m}F(j)$ vertices is 3-colourable (Erdős's probabilistic
    method).
  - Any $n$-chromatic subgraph, $n\ge4$, with at most $F(n)$ vertices would need a
    component of chromatic number $n$. Such a component lies in some $G_m$:
    - if $m<n$, the component has chromatic number at most $m<n$;
    - if $m\ge n$, it has at most $F(n)\le\max_{j\le m}F(j)$ vertices, so it is
      3-colourable.

    Either way the component cannot have chromatic number $n$, so no such subgraph exists.
- **Not formalized:**
  - [KoSh05]'s consistency result, which is a metatheorem and not expressible as a Lean
    theorem here;
  - Erdős's growth remark, which concerns hypothetical $F$.
