## Summary

The page (OPEN at capture, \$250) asks to determine $\lim_{k\to\infty}R(3;k)^{1/k}$, where $R(3;k)$ is the $k$-colour Ramsey
number of the triangle. It records $R(3;k)\le\lceil e\,k!\rceil$ and $R(3;k)\ge380^{k/5}-O(1)$ [ACPPRT21].

The Lean defines $R(3;k)$ as the least $n$ such that every symmetric $c:\mathrm{Fin}\,n\to\mathrm{Fin}\,n\to\mathrm{Fin}\,k$ has
a monochromatic triangle, and asserts $\exists L\in\mathbb R,\ R(3;k)^{1/k}\to L$.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: pass at capture, as an asserted direction.**

- **Source** (verbatim): "Let $R(3;k)$ be the minimal $n$ such that if the edges of $K_n$ are coloured with $k$ colours then
  there must exist a monochromatic triangle. Determine\[\lim_{k\to \infty}R(3;k)^{1/k}.\]"; remarks: "Erdős offers \$100 for
  showing that this limit is finite."
- **Back-translation** (lines 47–51): "$R(3;k)^{1/k}$ converges to a real number." This asserts finiteness of the limit (and its
  existence), the \$100 question. "Determine" has no further formal target here.
- **Checks.**
  - *Exponent.* `(1 : ℝ) / k` is $0$ at $k=0$, so the $k=0$ term is $1$; irrelevant along `atTop`.
  - *Non-degeneracy.* `sInf` would give $0$ on an empty set, and $0^{1/k}=0$ would make the statement true. The set is nonempty
    for every $k$ by the pigeonhole recursion (not formalized here), so the definition is the true $R(3;k)$ for $k\ge1$.

**A2. Lean semantics traps: pass.** The real power and the casts are correct.

**A3. Definitions: pass, with a boundary note.**

- `multicolorRamseyTriangle k`: symmetric colourings of all ordered pairs, the diagonal included, and a triangle of three
  pairwise distinct vertices with equal colours. The diagonal values are never read by the triangle condition.
- **But with no colours** the diagonal matters: for $n\ge1$ there is no function into `Fin 0` at all, so every $n\ge1$ is in the
  set and $R(3;0)=1$ (`multicolorRamseyTriangle_zero`, proved), not the conventional $2$ (only $K_0,K_1$ have no edges).
  $R(3;1)=3$ as expected (`multicolorRamseyTriangle_one`, proved). Consequently the page's recursion fails at $k=1$
  (`variants.recursion_fails_at_one`, proved: $3\not\le2+1\cdot(1-1)$); v2 states it for $k\ge2$. The limit is unaffected.

the finite-limit direction. The recorded answer is that the limit is $+\infty$, so the asserted direction is now refuted.
`not_finite_of_tendsto` (proved) shows the recorded answer implies the negation of the input's theorem.
