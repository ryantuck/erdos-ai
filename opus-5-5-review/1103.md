## Summary

The source (OPEN) asks: "Let $A$ be an infinite sequence of integers such that every
$n\in A+A$ is squarefree. How fast must $A$ grow?" Erdős notes that an exponentially
growing example exists and "does not expect such a sequence of polynomial growth". The
known bounds are:

- van Doorn–Tao: $a_j>0.24j^{4/3}$ for all $j$, and there is an example with
  $a_j<\exp(5j/\log j)$;
- Konyagin, earlier: $a_j\gg j^{15/11-o(1)}$.

The Lean file takes a strictly increasing `a : ℕ → ℕ` with every `a i + a j` squarefree
and concludes `∀ C > 0, ∃ N, ∀ j ≥ N, j ^ C < a j`, i.e. eventual super-polynomial growth.
Back-translated, "no polynomial growth" is
$\neg\exists C\,\forall^\infty j,\ a_j\le j^C$, which means
$\forall C\ \exists^\infty j,\ a_j>j^C$ (*frequently*). The Lean says *eventually*. As
properties of sequences these differ: a staircase sequence can satisfy the first and fail
the second. Nothing known makes them equivalent for sumset-squarefree sequences. So the
file formalizes a stronger, unrecorded conjecture. The hypotheses are otherwise exact, the
positivity and indexing choices are harmless, and the file's references are missing.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: DEFECT (fixed).**

- Recovered verbatim source (see the Addendum): "Let $A$ be an infinite sequence of
  integers such that every $n\in A+A$ is squarefree. How fast must $A$ grow?" The remark:
  "Erdős notes there exists such a sequence which grows exponentially, but does not expect
  such a sequence of polynomial growth."
- **Hypotheses: exact.**
  - `StrictMono a` makes $A$ an infinite increasing sequence.
  - `∀ i j, Squarefree (a i + a j)` is "every $n\in A+A$ is squarefree". It includes
    $i=j$, as the sumset must.
  - ℕ versus the page's "integers" is harmless. `Squarefree 0` is false, so $0\notin A$.
    A strictly increasing integer sequence has only finitely many negative terms.
    Deleting them preserves the (hereditary) sumset condition and shifts indices by a
    constant, which does not affect polynomial growth.
  - The hypotheses are satisfiable. Erdős's exponential example exists, and a greedy
    computation (Addendum) produces 662 terms below $3\cdot10^7$.
- **Conclusion: the defect.** The page's only stated conjecture is that there is no such
  sequence of polynomial growth. A sequence of polynomial growth satisfies
  $\exists C,\ a_j\le j^C$ for all large $j$, so the conjecture reads (R1)
  $\forall a,\ \forall C>0,\ \forall N\ \exists j\ge N,\ j^C<a_j$. The Lean asserts (R2)
  $\forall a,\ \forall C>0,\ \exists N\ \forall j\ge N,\ j^C<a_j$.
  - R2 implies R1.
  - The converse fails for sequences in general. Take $a_j=j+j_k^k$ on
    $[j_k,j_{k+1})$ with $j_{k+1}\gg j_k^k$. This strictly increasing sequence exceeds
    $j^C$ at every $j_k$ with $k>C$, yet $a_j<2j<j^2$ just before each $j_{k+1}$.
  - For the sumset-squarefree class, the only evident bridge is a finite-analogue bound
    (#1109) of $N^{o(1)}$, which would give R2 at every scale. That bound is unknown:
    Konyagin's is $N^{11/15+o(1)}$. Heredity does not help, because subsets only lose
    elements, and gluing blocks by shifts breaks squarefreeness of cross sums.
  - So a proof of R1 would not prove the Lean statement, and a counterexample to the Lean
    statement would not refute Erdős. The two are not decision-equivalent.
  - The module docstring itself states R1 ("does not expect such a sequence of polynomial
    growth to exist") while the theorem docstring and code state R2. The file is
    internally inconsistent.
  - **[defect: strengthened-statement]** Fixed: v2's main theorem is R1, and R2 is kept
    as `…variants.eventually_superpolynomial`, documented as a stronger open sharpening.
- Neither R1 nor R2 is vacuous: the hypotheses are satisfiable, and the known results
  leave both open.

**A2. Lean semantics traps: pass.**

- **Strict versus non-strict.** $<$ versus $\le$ is immaterial in both R1 and R2 as long
