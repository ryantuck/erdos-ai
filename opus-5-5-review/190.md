## Summary

The page (OPEN at capture) defines $H(k)$ as the least $N$ such that every finite colouring of $\{1,\dots,N\}$ has a
monochromatic $k$-term progression or a rainbow progression, asks to estimate it, and asks whether $H(k)^{1/k}/k\to\infty$. It
notes that $H(k)$ exists by Szemerédi's theorem and that $H(k)^{1/k}\to\infty$ is easy.

The Lean defines monochromatic $k$-term progressions, rainbow 3-term progressions, $H(k)$ by `sInf`, and asserts
$H(k)^{1/k}/k\to\infty$.

- **Source** (verbatim): "Let $H(k)$ be the smallest $N$ such that in any finite colouring of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ (into any number
  of colours) there is always either a monochromatic $k$-term arithmetic progression or a rainbow arithmetic progression (i.e.
  all elements are different colours). Estimate $H(k)$. Is it true that\[H(k)^{1/k}/k \to \infty\]as $k\to\infty$?"; remarks:

- **Which rainbow progressions?**
  - *Canonical Ramsey theory.* The page places the problem in "canonical" Ramsey theory, whose van der Waerden theorem
    (Erdős–Graham) gives a $k$-term progression that is monochromatic or rainbow.
  - *The easy remark.* $H(k)^{1/k}\to\infty$ is easy for the $k$-term version: colourings with $k-1$ colours have no rainbow
    $k$-term progression, so $H(k)$ exceeds the $(k-1)$-colour van der Waerden number, and random colourings make that at least
    about $(k-1)^{k-1}$. For 3-term rainbows, two colours already avoid rainbows, which only gives $H_3(k)\gtrsim2^k$.
  - *The mirror.* It records the solution as "every canonical $N$ exceeds $(Ck)^k$ … hence $H(k)^{1/k}/k\to\infty$".
- **The two functions differ.** A rainbow $k$-term progression contains a rainbow 3-term one, so $H_3(k)\le H(k)$. They differ
  already at $k=2$: `H_two` ($H_3(2)=3$, since $\{1,2\}$ in two colours has neither) and `canonicalH190_two` ($H(2)=2$), both
  proved in Lean.

  recorded.
