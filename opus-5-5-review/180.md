## Summary

The page (OPEN at capture) asks whether every finite family $\mathcal F$ has a member $G$ with
$\mathrm{ex}(n;G)\ll_{\mathcal F}\mathrm{ex}(n;\mathcal F)$. It notes that this is trivial without bipartite members (Erdős–Stone),
false for infinite families, and false for Hunter's star-and-matching family, and that it "may still hold for all other
$\mathcal F$".

The Lean defines subgraph containment and the two Turán numbers by `sSup`, and asserts the property for every nonempty finite
family, with a constant $C$ and all $n\ge1$.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: DEFECT (fixed).**

- **Source** (verbatim): "If $\mathcal{F}$ is a finite set of finite graphs then $\mathrm{ex}(n;\mathcal{F})$ is the maximum
  number of edges a graph on $n$ vertices can have without containing any subgraphs from $\mathcal{F}$. Note that it is trivial
  that $\mathrm{ex}(n;\mathcal{F})\leq \mathrm{ex}(n;G)$ for every $G\in\mathcal{F}$. Is it true that, for every $\mathcal{F}$,

- **[defect: wrong-polarity]** This is false, by the page's own counterexample, which the input's docstring repeats ("This has
  been disproved by a folklore counterexample"). v2 proves the negation (`erdos_problem_180`):

  - `star_avoids`, `star_card`: the star on $m+1$ vertices avoids `match2` and has $\ge m$ edges;
  - `turan_ge`: an $H$-free graph on $n$ vertices bounds $\mathrm{ex}(n;H)$ below.
