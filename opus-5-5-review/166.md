## Summary

The page (PROVED, \$250) asks: "Prove that $R(4,k)\gg\frac{k^3}{(\log k)^{O(1)}}$." It records Spencer's lower bound
$(k\log k)^{5/2}$, the Ajtai–Komlós–Szemerédi upper bound $k^3/(\log k)^2$, and Mattheus and Verstraete's proof of
$R(4,k)\gg k^3/(\log k)^4$.

The Lean defines `ramseyR4 k` as an `sInf` and states the lower bound with existential $C>0$, $\alpha\in\mathbb N$ and threshold.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: pass.**

- **Source** (verbatim): "Prove that\[R(4,k) \gg \frac{k^3}{(\log k)^{O(1)}}.\]"
- **Back-translation** (lines 44–48): "There are $C>0$, $\alpha\in\mathbb N$ and $N_0$ such that
  $C\,k^3/(\log k)^\alpha\le R(4,k)$ for every $k\ge N_0$."
- **Checks.**
  - *"$\gg$"* is "at least a positive constant times, for large $k$": $C>0$ and the threshold.
  - *"$(\log k)^{O(1)}$"* is $(\log k)^\alpha$ for some fixed $\alpha$. Restricting $\alpha$ to `ℕ` is equivalent to allowing any
    real $\alpha$: for $k\ge4$, $\log k\ge1$, so replacing $\alpha$ by $\lceil\alpha\rceil$ only weakens the bound
    (`main_iff_rpow`, with `one_le_log`). $\alpha=0$ is allowed and is harmless (it is a stronger claim).
  - *Non-triviality.* For a fixed $\alpha$ the right side is $k^{3-o(1)}$, beyond Spencer's $k^{5/2+o(1)}$; and with [AKS80], no
    $\alpha<2$ can work (`exponent_ge_two`). So the statement says something, and [MaVe23] proves it with $\alpha=4$
    (`main_of_mattheus_verstraete`).

**A2. Lean semantics traps: pass.** `x / 0 = 0` and `log 1 = 0` matter only at $k\le1$, which the threshold excludes; `^ α` is the
monoid power on `ℝ`.

**A3. Definitions: pass.** `ramseyR4 k` is the least `N` such that every graph on `Fin N` has a `4`-clique or an independent
`k`-set; that is $R(4,k)$. The set is nonempty by Ramsey's theorem (otherwise `sInf` would be `0`). `ramseyR4 0 = 0` and
`ramseyR4 1 = 1` (proved).

**A4. Question form: pass.** "Prove that …" is asserted directly; no `answer()` is needed.
