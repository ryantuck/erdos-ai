## Summary

The page asks (OPEN) whether $n$ points with all distances $\ge1$, and distinct distances
pairwise $\ge1$ apart, must have diameter $\gg n$. The Lean asserts that there is $C>0$ with
`diameter' A ≥ C·|A|` for all such $A$.

- For $|A|\ge2$ this is exactly the "yes" answer.
- For $|A|\le1$ it is false (with a total diameter) or undetermined (with the input's
  `sorry`-based one).

v2 makes `diameter'` total, adds `2 ≤ A.card`, and adds the page's remarks as variants:

- $n-1$ (open);
- Piepmeyer's 9-point example (proved);
- Kanold $\gg n^{3/4}$ (proved, constant added);
- Guth–Katz $\gg n/\log n$ (proved).

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: DEFECT (fixed).**

- **Back-translation:** "There is $C>0$ such that every finite $A\subset\mathbb R^2$ with
  all distances $\ge1$ and distinct distances $\ge1$ apart has `diameter' A` $\ge C|A|$."
- **[defect: false-at-small-parameters]** Take $A=\{a\}$. Both hypotheses hold vacuously,
  and the conclusion demands `diameter' {a} ≥ C > 0`.
  - The true diameter of a point is $0$.
  - In the input, `diameter' {a}` is `max'` of an empty set, evaluated with a `sorry` proof
    of `Nonempty`. So the conclusion is about a real number with no determined value. It is
    provable only by exploiting the inconsistent `sorryAx`, and otherwise unprovable.
  - Either way, it is not the source's proposition. The source's $n$ points have $n\ge2$
    implicitly, since "diameter $\gg n$" is meaningless for $n\le1$.
- **Fix:** add `2 ≤ A.card →`. For $2\le n\le N$ the diameter is $\ge1\ge n/N$, so any
  eventual linear bound extends to all $n\ge2$ with a smaller constant. Hence "for all
  $n\ge2$" and "for large $n$" are equivalent here.
- **The definitions are faithful.** `allPairwiseDistAtLeastOne` matches "all pairwise
  distances are at least 1". `distinctDistancesDifferByAtLeastOne` matches "if two distinct
  distances differ then they differ by at least 1".
- **Not vacuous.** Collinear points $0,1,\dots,n-1$ are admissible, with diameter $n-1$.
