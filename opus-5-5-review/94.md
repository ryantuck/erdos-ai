## Summary

For $n$ points forming a convex polygon, with $f(u)$ the number of pairs at distance $u$,
the page states (PROVED (LEAN)) that $\sum_u f(u)^2\ll n^3$. Lefmann and Thiele proved it
under the weaker hypothesis that no three points are on a line.

The Lean theorem asserts that there is $C>0$ with `distSqSum A ≤ C·|A|³` for every $A$ in
convex position. Since `distSqSum A` $=4\sum_u f(u)^2$, this is the same statement. v2
keeps it byte-identical and adds:

- references and status;
- a sanity lemma pinning down the encoding;
- the no-three-collinear theorem;
- the open Erdős–Fishburn maximality conjecture.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: pass.**

- **Source:** "Suppose $n$ points in $\mathbb{R}^2$ determine a convex polygon and the set
  of distances between them is $\{u_1,\ldots,u_t\}$. Suppose $u_i$ appears as the distance
  between $f(u_i)$ many pairs of points. Then $\sum_i f(u_i)^2 \ll n^3$."
- **Back-translation:** "There is $C>0$ such that every finite $A\subset\mathbb R^2$ in
  convex position has at most $C|A|^3$ ordered quadruples $(a,b,c,d)$ with $a\ne b$,
  $c\ne d$, $|ab|=|cd|$."
- **The encoding.** Distance $u$ is realised by exactly $2f(u)$ ordered pairs. So the
  quadruple count is $\sum_u(2f(u))^2=4\sum_uf(u)^2$, and
  $4\sum f^2\le Cn^3\iff\sum f^2\le(C/4)n^3$.
  - Scratch check on regular $n$-gons, $n=3..9$: the quadruple count equals
    $4\sum f^2$ exactly (36, 80, 200, 324, 588, 832, 1296), and $\sum f=\binom n2$.
- **"≪"** is rendered as a uniform $\exists C>0,\ \forall A$, which is standard. For
  $|A|\le1$ both sides vanish.
- **Not vacuous.** For the regular $n$-gon, $\sum f^2/n^3$ runs 0.33, 0.31, 0.40, 0.38,
  0.43, 0.41, 0.44 for $n=3..9$ (asymptotically $1/2$). So the exponent 3 is sharp, and the
  statement has content for every $C$ below the true constant.

## E. Prior-review audit (`deepmind/ai-review/94.md`)

1. **Wrong logical direction (Correctness).** The review says Lefmann and Thiele proved
   the bound "for the strictly stronger hypothesis 'no three points are collinear,' which
   implies convex position". This is backwards. No-three-collinear is the *weaker*
   hypothesis: a point inside a triangle has no three collinear points but is not in convex
   position. That is why their theorem is the *stronger* result. The same review elsewhere
   calls it the "weaker assumption", so it contradicts itself.

   collinear)" versus "vertices of the convex hull" as the readings of convex position.
   These coincide under the strict definition used, and "no three collinear" is not a
   reading of "convex" at all.
