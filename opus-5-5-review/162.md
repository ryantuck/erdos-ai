## Summary

The page (OPEN) defines $F(n,\alpha)$ through 2-colourings of $K_n$ in which every induced subgraph $H$ on at least $k$ vertices
has more than $\alpha\binom{\lvert H\rvert}2$ edges of each colour, and asks to prove $F(n,\alpha)\sim c_\alpha\log n$ for every
fixed $0\le\alpha\le1/2$. It remarks that $c_1(\alpha)\log n<F(n,\alpha)<c_2(\alpha)\log n$ is easy.

The Lean defines the balance condition and $F$ as the least admissible threshold, and states, for $0\le\alpha\le1/2$, that
$F(n,\alpha)/\log n$ tends to some $c>0$.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: defect (false at $\alpha=1/2$).**

- **Source** (verbatim): "Let $\alpha>0$ and $n\geq 1$. Let $F(n,\alpha)$ be the largest $k$ such that there exists some
  2-colouring of the edges of $K_n$ in which any induced subgraph $H$ on at least $k$ vertices contains more than
  $\alpha\binom{\lvert H\rvert}{2}$ many edges of each colour. Prove that for every fixed $0\leq \alpha \leq 1/2$, as
  $n\to\infty$, \[F(n,\alpha)\sim c_\alpha \log n\] for some constant $c_\alpha$."
- **Back-translation** (lines 53–60): "For every $0\le\alpha\le1/2$ there is $c>0$ such that for every $\varepsilon>0$, eventually
  $(c-\varepsilon)\log n\le F(n,\alpha)\le(c+\varepsilon)\log n$", where $F(n,\alpha)$ is the least $m$ for which some colouring
  makes every $X$ with $\lvert X\rvert\ge m$ balanced. The encoding of $\sim$ is right: it says $F(n,\alpha)/\log n\to c$.
- **"Largest" means "smallest".** A colouring that works for threshold $k$ works for every larger threshold, so the admissible
  thresholds form an up-set, which contains $n+1$ (no set has more than $n$ vertices; `mem_succ`). The page's "largest" can only
  mean the least threshold, which the input's `sInf` takes. (Read as "the largest $k$ such that every colouring has an
  unbalanced $H$ on $k$ vertices", it is the least threshold minus one, with the same asymptotics.)
- **The endpoint $\alpha=1/2$.** In any colouring and any $X$, the colour classes of the edges of $X$ partition its
  $\binom{\lvert X\rvert}2$ edges, so both cannot have more than half. Hence no colouring is $\tfrac12$-balanced at any
  threshold $m\le n$ (take $X$ the whole vertex set), and `F_erdos162 n (1/2) = n + 1` (`F_half`). The theorem at $\alpha=1/2$
  would need $n+1\le(c+1)\log n$ eventually, which fails because $\log n\le2(\sqrt n-1)$ (`first_pass_false`). The page includes
  the endpoint too; it is a slip there as well.
- **The endpoint $\alpha=0$.** "More than $0$" edges of each colour means no monochromatic set, so
  $F(n,0)=\min\{m:R(m)>n\}$, the inverse diagonal Ramsey function. Then $F(n,0)\sim c_0\log n$ is equivalent to the convergence
  of $R(k)^{1/k}$ (to $e^{1/c_0}$), the open Problem 77. The case is meaningful and stays in v2.
