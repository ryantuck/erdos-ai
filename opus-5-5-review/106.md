## Summary

$f(n)$ is the largest total side length of $n$ non-overlapping, possibly rotated, squares
in the unit square, and the page asks whether $f(k^2+1)=k$. The Lean definitions are
correct:

- a square is a centre, a side $>0$ and an angle;
- the closed region and the open interior come from rotated coordinates;
- the regions lie in $[0,1]^2$, with pairwise disjoint interiors;
- $f$ is the `sSup` of total side length.

The theorem `∀ k > 0, f (k ^ 2 + 1) = k` is the conjecture as asked. It is now disproved,
so v2 asserts `¬ (∀ k > 0, f (k ^ 2 + 1) = k)`, equivalently $\exists k:\ f(k^2+1)>k$.
Note that $f(k^2+1)\ge k$, and that $k=1,2$ hold (Erdős, Newman), so any counterexample
has $k\ge3$.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: pass for the proposition; see A4/A5 for its polarity.**

- **`region` and `sqInterior`.** $u,v$ are the coordinates of $p-c$ rotated by $-\theta$.
  The closed square is $|u|,|v|\le s/2$ and the open one is $<$. Every orientation is
  represented.
- **`IsValidSquareConfig`.** The closed regions lie in $[0,1]^2$, and distinct indices have
  disjoint interiors. That is the standard reading of "no common interior point" for
  non-overlapping squares.
- **`f n`.** `sSup` over exactly $n$ squares with positive sides. For $n\ge1$ the set is
  nonempty (tiny squares) and bounded by $\sqrt n$: by Cauchy–Schwarz,
  $\sum s_i\le\sqrt{n\sum s_i^2}\le\sqrt n$. So `sSup` is the true supremum.
- **Max versus sup.** Requiring sides $>0$ means the configuration space is not closed. So
  "maximum" may only be a supremum, but the value is the same quantity. Adding tiny squares
  shows $f$ is non-decreasing in $n$.
- **`0 < k`** is needed, since $f(1)=1\ne0$.
