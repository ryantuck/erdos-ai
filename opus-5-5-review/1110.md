## Summary

The source (OPEN) reads: "Let $p>q\geq2$ be two coprime integers. We call $n$
representable if it is the sum of integers of the form $p^kq^l$, none of which divide each
other. If $\{p,q\}\neq\{2,3\}$ then what can be said about the density of
non-representable numbers? Are there infinitely many coprime non-representable numbers?"

The known results are these. Erdős–Lewin showed that finitely many numbers are
non-representable if and only if $\{p,q\}=\{2,3\}$, and in fact none are, by Jansen's
induction. Yu–Chen proved density zero of the representables for $q>3$, or $q=3,p>6$, or
$q=2,p>10$. They also proved that there are infinitely many coprime non-representables for
$q>3$, or $q=3,p\ne5$, or $q=2,p\notin\{3,5,9\}$.

The Lean asserts, for every coprime $p>q\ge2$ with $(p,q)\ne(3,2)$, that
$\{n:\neg\mathrm{Rep}(n)\wedge\gcd(n,pq)=1\}$ is infinite. Back-translated, this is exactly
the second question, "yes" for all pairs. By Yu–Chen it is open only for
$(p,q)\in\{(5,3),(5,2),(9,2)\}$, and computation strongly suggests "yes" there too. The
definitions have no traps. Distinctness of summands, the role of 1, and the exclusion of 0
all come out right.
