## Summary

The problem (verbatim from the recovered page): "Let $z_1,\ldots,z_n\in \mathbb{C}$
with $\lvert z_i-z_j\rvert\leq 2$ for all $i,j$, and
$\Delta(z_1,\ldots,z_n)=\prod_{i\neq j}\lvert z_i-z_j\rvert$. What is the maximum
possible value of $\Delta$? Is it maximised by taking the $z_i$ to be the vertices of a
regular polygon?" — with **no** circle normalization. The pre-fix Lean encoded "regular
polygon" as `regularNGon n`, the $n$-gon inscribed in the **unit circle**, and its
docstrings claimed the source asks about a polygon "inscribed in a circle of diameter
2". The page's own remarks give the regular-polygon value as $n^n$ (even $n$) and
$\cos(\pi/2n)^{-n(n-1)}n^n$ (odd $n$): the intended polygon is the one of **diameter
2**, which for odd $n$ has circumradius $1/\cos(\pi/2n) > 1$ — *not* the unit-circle
polygon. Consequently the pre-fix odd variant (`answer(sorry)`, `research open`) was
trivially **false** as formalized — at $n=3$ the admissible side-2 equilateral triangle
gives $\Delta = 2^6 = 64 > 27 = 3^3 = \Delta(\texttt{regularNGon } 3)$ — while the
genuine odd-$n$ question is open. Both theorems were recast over an existential
`IsRegularNGon` predicate (any centre, scale, rotation), which is faithful to the
source's unnormalized "regular polygon".

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — DEFECT (fixed).**

- The source statement (recovered verbatim, quoted above) says "the vertices of a
  regular polygon", with no normalization. The pre-fix module docstring
  (`deepmind/1045.lean:27–28` pre-fix) and theorem docstring appended "inscribed in a
  circle of diameter $2$" — a phrase that appears nowhere on the page. This originated
  in the raw first pass (`conjectures/1045.lean:16–17`), not in restyling.
- The page's remarks pin down the intended reading: "The value of $\Delta$ when the
  $z_i$ are the vertices of a regular polygon is $n^n$ when $n$ is even and
  $\cos(\pi/2n)^{-n(n-1)}n^n \sim e^{\pi^2/8}n^n$ when $n$ is odd." Derivation: a
  regular $n$-gon of circumradius $R$ has $\Delta = R^{n(n-1)} n^n$ (ordered pairs;
  unit-circle value $n^n$ since $\prod_{j\neq i}|z_i - z_j| = |p'(z_i)| = n$ for
  $p = z^n - 1$) and maximum pairwise distance $2R$ ($n$ even, antipodal pair) or
  $2R\cos(\pi/2n)$ ($n$ odd, longest diagonal). The constraint $\le 2$ therefore allows
  $R = 1$ for even $n$ but $R = 1/\cos(\pi/2n) > 1$ for odd $n$, reproducing exactly
  the page's two values. So "the regular polygon" means the diameter-2 one, and for odd
  $n$ that is **not** `regularNGon n`.
- Concrete counterexample to the pre-fix odd variant's RHS: $n = 3$,
  $z = (2/\sqrt 3)\cdot(\text{unit triangle})$ — side 2, admissible;
  $\Delta(z) = 2^6 = 64 > 27 = 3^3 = \Delta(\texttt{regularNGon}\ 3)$. The RHS of
  `erdos_1045.variants.odd` was therefore decidably false, yet tagged
  `answer(sorry)` / `research open`: the file's "open question" was not the open
  question. **[defect]** — fixed (see Addendum).
- The main theorem's RHS was false too (as intended, `answer(False)`), but partly for
  this spurious odd-$n$ scaling reason rather than solely the Hu–Tang/Cambie
  counterexamples the docstring cites — an unfaithful encoding even where the polarity
  accidentally survives. **[defect]** — fixed.
- Post-fix back-translation: "for every $n \ge 1$ there exist vertices $w$ of a regular
  $n$-gon (some centre $c$, some $\rho \neq 0$), pairwise distances $\le 2$, such that
  every admissible configuration $z$ has $\Delta(z) \le \Delta(w)$" — i.e. the maximum
  is attained by a regular polygon. This is the question as asked. (Attainment of the
  maximum over all configurations holds by compactness, so "maximised by" = "attained
  by a regular polygon" is the right reading.)
- The value request "What is the maximum possible value of $\Delta$?" remains
  unformalized (would need `sSup` over configurations; open). Noted in the module
  docstring; omission acceptable, consistent with the prior review's judgment.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- No ℕ subtraction anywhere (the fix deliberately states values as $(n:\mathbb{R})^n$,
  avoiding $n(n-1)$ exponents on ℕ).
- Division: `(k.val : ℝ) / (n : ℝ)` in `regularNGon`/`IsRegularNGon` at $n = 0$ gives
  junk 0, but `Fin 0` is empty and every use carries $n \ge 1$ (or is inside an ∃) —
  harmless. No other division.
- No `Finset.range`; products are over `Fin n` (`univ`), matching $\prod_{i\neq j}$
  over ordered pairs. The recovered page's even-$n$ value $n^n$ (not $n^{n/2}$)
  confirms the source's $\prod_{i\neq j}$ is itself over ordered pairs, so the encoding
  matches the source exactly — no squaring discrepancy to excuse (see Part E).
- Degenerate cases: $n = 1$ — both sides are empty products $= 1$; the (fixed) main RHS
  is trivially true at $n = 1, 2$ and that matches the source (the question is
  substantive only from $n = 3$, refuted at even $n \ge 4$). `IsRegularNGon` is
  degenerate for $n \le 2$ (any point / any pair of distinct points); this only adds
  candidate witnesses that never dominate, so it cannot flip any statement — documented
  in its docstring.
- `if i ≠ j then ‖z i - z j‖ else 1` inserts multiplicative units on the diagonal —
  correct filter encoding of $\prod_{i \neq j}$.
- No `StrictMono`/`Monotone`, no liminf/limsup filters in the statements (the
  Sothanaphan variant is deliberately encoded with explicit $\exists C > 1, \exists N$
  rather than `liminf`, as a documented consequence).

**A3. Definitions — pass (post-fix).**

- `erdosDelta` matches its docstring and the source's $\Delta$ (ordered pairs;
  docstring now says "over ordered pairs" explicitly).
- `regularNGon` produces $e^{2\pi i k/n}$, circumradius 1; its docstring now states
  precisely when this is the extremal (diameter-2) polygon: even $n$ only.
- New `IsRegularNGon n w`: $\exists c, \rho \neq 0$, $w_k = c + \rho e^{2\pi i k/n}$ —
  the full family of regular $n$-gons (centre, scale, rotation; reflection is absorbed
  since $\Delta$ and admissibility are invariant and the vertex set of a reflected
  regular polygon is again of this form). No `sorry` in any definition.

**A4. Question form and answer polarity — pass (post-fix).**

- The formalized part of the source is a yes/no question ("Is it maximised by…?"), so
  `answer(…) ↔ P` with `P` the question as asked is the right shape.
- Polarity by hand: RHS true ⟺ for every $n$ some admissible regular polygon attains
  the max ⟺ answer "yes". Known: for even $n \ge 4$ every admissible regular $n$-gon
  has $\Delta = |\rho|^{n(n-1)} n^n \le n^n$ (admissibility forces $2|\rho| \le 2$),
  and Hu–Tang/Cambie found admissible configurations exceeding $n^n$ — so no regular
  polygon is a maximiser and the RHS is false: `answer(False)` = "no" is correct, and
  now false *for the right reason*.
- Odd variant: genuinely open per the page ("It remains possible that the regular
  polygon is a maximiser for odd $n$"), so `answer(sorry)` is correct post-fix.
- No binders before the colon on either `answer()` theorem (both are
  `theorem … : answer(…) ↔ ∀ …`).

**A5. Solution status — pass, with a nuance (source-verified).**

- Page banner: **FALSIFIABLE** ("Open, but could be disproved with a finite
  counterexample") — i.e. the headline problem is OPEN, because the value question and
  the odd case are open. The main theorem's `research solved` tag refers to the
  specific yes/no it encodes, which the page's own remarks settle in the negative
  (Hu–Tang, Cambie); the odd variant carries `research open`. This split is coherent;
  the module docstring now records the page-level OPEN status explicitly.
- Attributions (EHP58 origin p.143; Pommerenke upper bound; Hu–Tang $n=4,6$; Cambie
  even $n \ge 4$; Sothanaphan $C \approx 1.0378$; Cambie–Dong–Tang $\approx 1.304457$
  for $6 \mid n$, $\approx 1.26853$ for even $n$) all match the recovered remarks.
- Pre-fix docstring inaccuracy (fixed): Sothanaphan's liminf bound was stated without
  its "for even $n$" restriction, which the page includes.

## E. Prior-review audit (`ai-review/1045.md`)

Accurate on `erdosDelta` mechanics and edge cases $n \in \{0,1,2\}$; wrong at the
centre:

1. **Missed the central defect; endorsed the fabricated normalization as the source's
   own.** §5 asserts "the original problem says 'the vertices of a regular polygon
   inscribed in a circle of diameter 2' without specifying centering". The original
   says no such thing — the recovered page has only "the vertices of a regular
   polygon". The review inherited the Lean docstring's invented normalization and
   attributed it to the source, then certified fidelity against it ("Correct and
   complete… No mathematical flaws identified"). This is the recurring failure class
   *endorsing an encoding that contradicts the source* (cf. the 1005 audit).
2. **False certification of the odd variant.** §6: "Uses `answer(sorry)` reflecting
   that the truth value is unknown. ✅" — the truth value of the formalized RHS was
   *known*: false, by the $n = 3$ side-2 triangle scaling ($64 > 27$; more generally
   $\cos(\pi/2n)^{-n(n-1)} > 1$ for every odd $n \ge 3$). The evidence was on the very
   page the review cites elsewhere (the odd-$n$ value line), which the review never
   quotes — it quotes the Pommerenke/Sothanaphan remarks but skips the one revealing
   the normalization.
3. **Unnecessary excuse pattern on the pair convention.** §6 argues the ordered-pair
   product "equals $(\prod_{i<j})^2$" and is "harmless" because "the same definition is
   used on both sides". The recovered page's even-$n$ value $n^n$ shows the source's
   own $\prod_{i\neq j}$ is over ordered pairs, so there is no discrepancy to excuse.
   The both-sides argument happens to be sound for the inequality but would mask a real
   factor-of-2 exponent error in any variant stating an explicit value (as the page
   does) — same invented-then-excused pattern as the 1000/1005 audits.
4. **Minor.** Its §2 claim that [Po61]/[So25] were "not cited" predates the deepmind
   file, which added them (verified against the `/latex/1045` extraction); its
   suggestion to add Hu–Tang/Cambie citations is unactionable — the site's
   bibliography has no entries for them (confirmed from the extraction), only comment
   references. Its contributor list conflates the "Additional thanks" line with the
   forum-reaction lists. Its line references ("lines 41–44", "lines 69–72") match
   neither repo file.

What it got right: `erdosDelta` correctness bullets, the $n=0,1,2$ edge cases, the
translation-invariance observation, the judgment that the quantitative value question
is reasonable to omit, and the reuse survey.
