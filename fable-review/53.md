## Summary

The problem (verbatim from the recovered page, PROVED banner): "Let $A$ be a
finite set of integers. Is it true that, for every $k$, if $|A|$ is
sufficiently large depending on $k$, then there are [at] least $|A|^k$ many
integers which are either the sum or product of distinct elements of $A$?"
Asked by Erdős and Szemerédi [ErSz83]; solved in this form by Chang [Ch03].
The Lean (`conjectures/53.lean:40-44`) asserts: for every `k : ℕ` there is
`N : ℕ` such that every `A : Finset ℤ` with `A.card ≥ N` has
`(subsetSums A ∪ subsetProducts A).card ≥ A.card ^ k`, where the two helper
defs image the powerset of `A` under `∑`/`∏`. Read back independently, this
is exactly the affirmative direction of the question as asked — subset sums
and subset products, not pairwise ones — and since the problem was solved
affirmatively, the direct-assertion form has the correct polarity. I found
no semantic defects; the nits are scholarship gaps (citation keys used with
no bibliography, dropped page remarks, no status/provenance record) and an
undocumented empty-subset convention, all addressed in
`conjectures-v2/53.lean`.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Independent back-translation of lines 40-44: "For every natural $k$ there
  is a threshold $N$ such that every finite set $A$ of integers with
  $|A| \ge N$ yields at least $|A|^k$ distinct integers each of which is a
  sum over some subset of $A$ or a product over some subset of $A$." This
  matches the page statement: "sum or product of distinct elements of $A$"
  = subset sums/products (the elements of a subset of a set are distinct),
  counted as a union, with the threshold depending only on $k$.
- The interpretation question ("distinct elements" as subsets vs as pairwise
  $a+b$, $a\cdot b$) resolves decisively to subsets: under the pairwise
  reading the union has at most $\sim 2|A|^2$ elements, making the claim
  trivially false for $k \ge 3$ — it could not then have been "solved in
  the affirmative by Chang". This is also Erdős–Szemerédi's own $g(k)$
  formulation (simple sums $A[1]$ and simple products $A\{1\}$). The
  formalization chooses correctly.
- Quantifier structure: `∀ k, ∃ N, ∀ A` — $N$ depends on $k$ only, exactly
  "if $|A|$ is sufficiently large depending on $k$". No hidden dependence of
  $N$ on $A$.
- Types: `Finset ℤ` for "finite set of integers" — correct (the page says
  integers, not naturals); counting via `Finset.card` in ℕ;
  `A.card ^ k` is ℕ-exponentiation on the correct side of `≥`. No
  coercions needed, none present.
- Indexing conventions: none arise (no sequences).
- Not vacuous, not trivially false: the statement is the substantive
  (true, by Chang) sum-product dichotomy for subset sums/products. Spot
  checks: for $A = \{1,\dots,n\}$ subset sums number only $O(n^2)$ but
  subset products are superpolynomial; for a geometric progression
  $A = \{2, 4, \dots, 2^n\}$ products collapse to $O(n^2)$ values but the
  $2^n$ subset sums are all distinct. The quantified instances $k = 0, 1$
  are trivially true (singletons give card ≥ |A|); including them is
  harmless and matches "for every $k$".

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- No ℕ or ℤ subtraction anywhere; no division anywhere; no `Finset.range`;
  no filters/liminf/limsup (batch problem-33 class: n/a); no
  `StrictMono`/interval issues.
- No vacuous bounded quantifiers. The only degenerate regime, `A = ∅`
  (excluded anyway once `N ≥ 1`, but the statement must hold for `N = 0`
  instances the prover may choose to avoid): `subsetSums ∅ = {0}`,
  `subsetProducts ∅ = {1}`, union card 2, and `0 ^ k` is `0` for `k ≥ 1`
  and `1` for `k = 0` — the inequality holds; no junk-value hazard
  (batch problem-51 class checked).
- No default-valued binders / `optParam` (batch problem-25 class: n/a).
- ℕ-exponentiation `A.card ^ k` is total and exact; both sides of `≥` are
  ℕ. Fine.

**A3. Definitions — pass, one documentation nit (fixed in v2).**

- `subsetSums`/`subsetProducts` match their docstrings exactly
  (`powerset.image` of `∑`/`∏` is literally $\{\sum_{i\in S} i \mid
  S \subseteq A\}$, and the docstrings' $S \subseteq A$ includes $\emptyset$
  just as `powerset` does). No `sorry` in any def. No drift between defs
  and theorem usage.
- Degenerate-input behavior: `∅ ∈ powerset A` contributes the empty sum `0`
  and empty product `1`; singletons contribute every element of `A` to both
  images. Under the strictest reading of "sum or product of distinct
  elements" (at least one, or even two, elements), the formal union is a
  *superset* of the intended set, enlarged by at most `|A| + 2` elements.
  For a lower-bound assertion `card ≥ |A|^k` an enlargement is
  direction-safe: intended-true ⇒ formal-true, and the discrepancy is
  absorbed by increasing $N$. Not a defect; the convention (which also
  matches upstream `subsetSums` over `Set M`, whose `∃ B : Finset M,
  ↑B ⊆ A ∧ …` admits `B = ∅`) is now documented in the v2 def docstrings.

## E. Prior-review audit (`deepmind/ai-review/53.md`)

(`deepmind/deepmind/53.lean`); its mathematics is largely sound. Findings:

1. **Reuse claim verified true.** Its quoted upstream `subsetSums (A : Set

   (top level, as claimed) in the upstream clone at HEAD dd1c2beb, and its

   namespace-collision remark concerns the styled file's `namespace
   Erdos53`; the raw input has no namespace, but also does not import that
   library, so no collision exists either way.
2. **Citation list verified.** Its seven page keys ([Er77c], [ErGr80],
   [ErSz83], [Er91], [Er97], [Er97e], [Ch03]) match the recovered page
   exactly. However, "the full citation details included in the docstring
   are accurate and complete" is true only of the *styled* artifact — the
   raw input under review here carries the keys with **no** bibliography, a
   gap the prior review's framing masks and which is fixed in v2.
3. **Overstated asymptotic ("precise growth rate").** It states Chang
   showed $\ln g(k) \sim (\ln k)^2/\ln\ln k$. This "~" (asymptotic
   equality) does not follow from the results recorded on the page: the
   Erdős–Szemerédi upper bound has $\log\log$ *multiplying*
   ($\exp(c(\log k)^2\log\log k)$) while a Chang-type lower bound has it
   *dividing* — a $(\log\log k)^2$ gap in the exponent. The "~" would
   require Chang to have also sharpened the upper bound to
   $\exp((1+o(1))(\log k)^2/\log\log k)$; whether the Annals paper does
   this cannot be checked offline. The claim is at best unsupported by the
   cited page content and is flagged as an overstatement of the same class
   as the fc research subagent's summary it appears to derive from
   (recorded in `claude-session-logs-formal-conjectures/712659ee-….jsonl`
   line 21). It does not affect the verdict on the formalization, which
   captures only the qualitative statement — correctly, as the prior
   review itself notes. **DEFERRED** for verification against the paper.
4. **Interpretation argument re-derived, correct.** Its Interpretation-B
   refutation is right: pairwise sums number at most
   $\binom{|A|}{2} \le |A|^2$ and likewise products, so the union is
   $< 2|A|^2 < |A|^3$ for $|A| > 2$ — trivially false for $k \ge 3$,
   forcing the subset reading. Its appeal to the Erdős–Szemerédi $A[1]$,
   $A\{1\}$ definitions is consistent with the fc log's research summary.
5. **Empty-subset observation correct, sharpened here.** It says the empty
   subset "adds at most 2 extra elements… does not affect the truth
   value". Correct, and one can say more: the enlargement is
   *direction-safe* for a `≥` lower bound (formal set ⊇ intended set), so
   this convention could not even in principle falsify the assertion —
   the relevant check given this batch's junk-value warnings.
6. **Polarity endorsement re-verified.** Its claim that `answer(True)` +
   `research solved` correctly reflect Chang's affirmative solution checks
   out against the recovered PROVED banner and the mirror (per the
   problem-34-class check, nothing needed negating).
7. **Missed by the prior review:** the raw input's absent bibliography
   (point 2 above); the page's "See also [52]"/OEIS "Possible"/accessed
   date, none recorded in either artifact; and the possibility of
   documenting the upper-bound remark in the file. All addressed in v2.
