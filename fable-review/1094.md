## Summary

The problem (OPEN, [ELS88][ELS93]): for all $n \ge 2k$ the least prime factor of
$\binom{n}{k}$ is $\le \max(n/k, k)$, with only finitely many exceptions; [ELS88] lists
the 14 known exceptions ($\binom{7}{3}$ through $\binom{284}{28}$). The Lean statement
asserts `Set.Finite {(n,k) : ℕ × ℕ | 1 ≤ k ∧ 2*k ≤ n ∧ ¬ (choose n k).minFac ≤ max (n/k) k}`
— finiteness of the exception set — which is exactly the source's "with only finitely
many exceptions" assertion. Read back independently, the Lean and the (recovered)
English agree term-for-term; the ℕ floor division is provably harmless because the
compared quantity is an integer. I found no semantic defects; the findings are
enrichment-level only (missing remarks/citation keys from the page, and three
page-confirmed variants worth stating), all applied — see Addendum.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement: "For all $n\geq 2k$ the least prime factor of
  $\binom{n}{k}$ is $\leq \max(n/k,k)$, with only finitely many exceptions."
- Independent back-translation of the theorem (pre-fix lines 19–25): "the set of pairs
  $(n,k)$ with $k \ge 1$, $n \ge 2k$, whose binomial coefficient's least prime factor
  exceeds $\max(\lfloor n/k\rfloor, k)$, is finite." Identical content: "conjecture
  holds with finitely many exceptions" ⟺ "the exception set is finite".
- The added hypothesis `1 ≤ k` is a *correct necessary reading*, not drift: for $k = 0$,
  $\binom{n}{0} = 1$ has no prime factor (Lean junk value `minFac 1 = 1`), $n/k$ is
  undefined in the source (Lean junk `n/0 = 0`), and every $(n,0)$ would be a spurious
  "exception" (`1 ≤ max 0 0` fails), making the set infinite and the statement trivially
  false. With `1 ≤ k` the statement is the intended nontrivial conjecture.
- Not vacuous / not trivially false: infinitely many pairs satisfy the hypotheses; the
  exception predicate is genuinely satisfiable (all 14 listed exceptions satisfy it —
  verified numerically, see below) and conjecturally finite.
- Numerical audit: all 14 exceptions listed on the page (and in the docstring) satisfy
  the Lean exception predicate, and an exhaustive scan of $n \le 600$, $1 \le k \le n/2$
  finds **exactly** the 14 listed pairs and no others. The Lean predicate and the
  real-division predicate classify identically on the whole range.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- **ℕ division**: `n / k` floors, but the comparison `minFac ≤ max (n/k) k` has an
  integer LHS, and for $m \in \mathbb{N}$: $m \le \lfloor n/k \rfloor \iff m \le n/k$
  (real). So `max (n/k) k` under floor division decides the source inequality *exactly*
  — no defect, confirmed by the exhaustive numerical scan above.
- **Division by zero**: `n / 0 = 0` in ℕ, but `1 ≤ k` excludes $k = 0$ from the set, so
  the junk value is unreachable within the predicate's guarded region (and even where
  the let-bound expression is evaluated, the conjunction fails first on `1 ≤ k`).
- **`minFac` junk values**: `minFac 1 = 1`, `minFac 0 = 2`. Under the hypotheses
  $k \ge 1$, $n \ge 2k$ we get $\binom{n}{k} \ge \binom{2k}{k} \ge 2$, so `minFac` is
  the genuine least prime factor. No junk reachable.
- No ℕ subtraction, no `Finset.range`, no bounded quantifiers, no filters, no
  `StrictMono`/interval issues in the statement.
- Set-builder over `ℕ × ℕ` with `let` bindings: semantically transparent (the raw
  session's `lake build` compiled it).
