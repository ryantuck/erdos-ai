## Summary

The problem: for $0 < \alpha < 1$ let
$f(\alpha,n) = \frac{1}{\log n}\sum_{1\le k\le n}(\tfrac12 - \{\alpha k\})$; does
$f(\alpha,n)$ have an asymptotic distribution function, i.e. a non-decreasing $g$ with
$g(-\infty)=0$, $g(\infty)=1$ and
$\lim_{n\to\infty}|\{\alpha\in(0,1): f(\alpha,n)\le c\}| = g(c)$? The problem is OPEN
on erdosproblems.com. The Lean (`deepmind/1002.lean`) defines `erdosF` as the exact
sum-over-`Finset.Icc 1 n` divided by `Real.log n`, and states
`answer(sorry) ↔ ∃ g, Monotone g ∧ g → 0 at −∞ ∧ g → 1 at +∞ ∧ ∀ c, volume((0,1) ∩
{α | erdosF α n ≤ c}).toReal → g c`. Read back independently, the Lean asserts exactly
the site's "In other words" formulation, with correct question-form encoding for an
open yes/no problem. No semantic defects were found; the nits are scholarship
(missing bibliography entries, now stubbed) and textual consistency (redundant
`Filter.` qualification, now fixed).

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- The recovered page's statement matches the module docstring essentially verbatim
  (including the "In other words" definition of asymptotic distribution function).
- Independent back-translation of the theorem (lines 71–79 post-fix): "there is a
  monotone $g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ tending to 0 at $-\infty$ and 1 at $+\infty$
  such that for every real $c$, the Lebesgue measure of
  $\{\alpha\in(0,1): f(\alpha,n)\le c\}$ tends to $g(c)$ as $n\to\infty$." This is
  exactly the question as asked.
- Quantifier structure: `∃ g … ∀ c …` matches "is there a non-decreasing $g$ …
  [for all $c$] $\lim = g(c)$". The universal over $c$ is implicit in the source but
  is the only sensible reading of "…$= g(c)$" as defining a function.
- Types: $\alpha, c, g$ real; $n : \mathbb{N}$; the sum is computed in ℝ
  (`Int.fract (α * ↑k)` with the cast inside), and the measure is converted by
  `.toReal` — since the set is contained in $(0,1)$ its measure is $\le 1$, so
  `toReal` is faithful (no `⊤` collapse).
- Indexing: `Finset.Icc 1 n` $= \{1,\dots,n\}$ matches $\sum_{1\le k\le n}$
  term-by-term. No off-by-one.
- Not vacuous/trivial: for each $n$, $c \mapsto |\{\alpha : f(\alpha,n)\le c\}|$ is
  the CDF of $f(\cdot,n)$ under uniform measure on $(0,1)$; the statement is a
  genuine weak-convergence-type question, true for the shifted analogue (Kesten) and
  open here. Neither side of the iff is degenerate.
- One deliberate point of literalism: the formalization requires convergence at
  **every** $c$, not merely at continuity points of $g$ (the standard
  weak-convergence notion). This matches the source's own explicit definition, so it
  is fidelity, not a modeling liberty — see E for discussion. Note also that `g` is
  not required right-continuous, so the `∃ g` form is equivalent to "the pointwise
  limit exists at every $c$ and has the right boundary behavior"; the `Monotone g`
  conjunct is then automatic (a pointwise limit of monotone-in-$c$ functions is
  monotone), harmless to include.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- ℕ subtraction: none — the only subtraction is `(1:ℝ)/2 - Int.fract …` in ℝ.
- Division: `/ Real.log ↑n` is field division. `n = 0`: `Icc 1 0 = ∅`, sum `0`,
  `Real.log 0 = 0`, `0/0 = 0`. `n = 1`: `Real.log 1 = 0`, so `erdosF α 1 = 0` by the
  division-by-zero convention. Both are junk values at finitely many indices, harmless
  under `Tendsto … atTop`.
- `Finset.Icc 1 n` (not `range`) — correct for the 1-to-$n$ sum; no off-by-one.
- No vacuous bounded quantifiers.
- Filter encodings: `Tendsto g atBot (nhds 0)` / `atTop (nhds 1)` are the correct
  renderings of $g(-\infty)=0$, $g(\infty)=1$ (the source's notation means limits, as
  $g$ need not be defined at $\pm\infty$); `Tendsto (fun n : ℕ => …) atTop (nhds (g c))`
  is the correct rendering of $\lim_{n\to\infty}$.
- `Monotone` (non-strict) correctly captures "non-decreasing"; `≤ c` (closed sublevel
  set) matches the source's $f(\alpha,n)\le c$.
- Measurability: for fixed $n$, $\alpha \mapsto$ `erdosF α n` is a finite sum of
  measurable functions of $\alpha$ (`Int.fract` is measurable, $\alpha \mapsto \alpha k$
  continuous) divided by a constant, so the sublevel set is Borel and `volume` gives
  the intended Lebesgue measure. (Even for non-measurable sets Mathlib's `Measure`
  returns the outer measure — monotone, hence $\le 1$ here — not `⊤`; see E.)

**A3. Definitions — pass.**

## E. Prior-review audit (`ai-review/1002.md`)

Largely sound; three corrections and two omissions:

1. **False factual claim (minor).** The citation table says `[Er64b]` appears "in
   `@[category]` and docstring". `[Er64b]` does not appear in any attribute — the
   attribute line is `@[category research open, AMS 11 28]`. It appears only in the
   theorem docstring.
2. **Wrong Lean semantics claim, right conclusion.** §6 asserts "Lean's `volume`
   applied to non-measurable sets would give `⊤`, but `.toReal` of `⊤` is `0`, which
   would make the limit statement trivially satisfiable with `g = 0`." This is wrong
   twice over: (a) a Mathlib `Measure` evaluated on an arbitrary set returns its
   induced **outer measure**, which is monotone — for subsets of $(0,1)$ it is
   $\le 1$, never `⊤`; (b) even granting the false premise, `g = 0` fails the
   required `Tendsto g atTop (nhds 1)`, so the statement would become *false*, not
   trivially true. The conclusion (measurability holds and nothing degenerates)
   stands, but the argument is the same "invent a failure mode, then excuse it"
   pattern flagged in the 1000 audit.
3. **Framing correction on `∀ c` vs continuity points.** §§5–6 present the
   quantification over all $c$ (vs only continuity points of $g$) as a "substantive
   modeling decision" resolving an "ambiguity in the original". The mathematical
   claim — that pointwise-everywhere convergence is strictly stronger than weak
   convergence (convergence at continuity points) — is correct; e.g. CDFs of point
   masses at $(-1)^n/n$ converge at every $c \ne 0$ but oscillate at $c = 0$. But the
   fidelity framing is off: the source *itself* defines the notion via "In other
   words, is there a non-decreasing $g$ … $\lim … = g(c)$", with no continuity-point
   restriction, and the Lean matches that definition exactly. No modeling discretion
   was exercised; weakening to continuity points (as the ai-review muses) would
   *deviate* from the source. Worth recording: because `g` is not required
   right-continuous, the formal statement is equivalent to "$\lim_n |\{f \le c\}|$
   exists for every $c$, with boundary limits 0 and 1", which is the honest literal
   reading of the page.
4. **Missed:** the module docstring defined no bibliography entries for `[Er64b]` or
   `[Ke60]` (B1 fail — now fixed with stubs); the redundant `Filter.` qualification
   under `open Filter` (now fixed).
5. **Unverifiable-then, verified-now:** its "matches website" citation claims are now
   source-confirmed via the recovered page (keys and Kesten remark match).
