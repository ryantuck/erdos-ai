## Summary

The problem (verbatim from the recovered page): "Let $f(n)\to \infty$ (possibly very
slowly). Is there a graph of infinite chromatic number such that every finite subgraph
on $n$ vertices can be made bipartite by deleting at most $f(n)$ edges?" Status:
**OPEN**, $500 (conjectured affirmatively by Erdős–Hajnal–Szemerédi [EHS82]; Erdős
offered \$500 for a proof but only \$250 for a counterexample). The Lean statement
(`conjectures/74.lean:25-33`) asserts: for every f : ℕ → ℕ tending to infinity there
is a graph G (on some type V with decidable equality and adjacency) that is
k-colorable for no k, such that every finite vertex subset S admits a 2-coloring
c : V → Bool with at most 2·f(|S|) monochromatic *ordered* pairs inside S — i.e. at
most f(|S|) monochromatic unordered edges, i.e. the induced subgraph on S is within
f(|S|) edge deletions of bipartite. Read back independently, this is exactly the
affirmative (conjectured) direction of the question as asked. I found **no semantic
defects** — only scholarship gaps (no bibliography, missing page remarks: prize
asymmetry, [Ro82] attribution, ℵ₁ failure with the [111] cross-reference) and
polish-level nits, all addressed in `conjectures-v2/74.lean`.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Independent back-translation of lines 26–32: "∀ f : ℕ → ℕ with f → ∞, ∃ a type V,
  decidable equality on V, a simple graph G on V with decidable adjacency, such that
  (i) G is not k-colorable for any finite k, and (ii) for every finite S ⊆ V there is
  c : V → Bool with #{(u,v) ∈ S×S : u ∼ v and c(u) = c(v)} ≤ 2·f(|S|)." This matches
  the source question's affirmative direction, with the graph chosen *after* f
  (∀f ∃G) — the correct reading of "Let f(n) → ∞ … Is there a graph …", and the same
  quantifier order as upstream formal-conjectures.
- **Deletion-distance encoding.** "Can be made bipartite by deleting at most k edges"
  ⟺ "some 2-coloring has at most k monochromatic edges": deleting the monochromatic
  edges of c makes c a proper 2-coloring; conversely a proper 2-coloring of the
  deleted graph leaves only deleted edges monochromatic. Exact, both directions.
- **Ordered-pair factor of 2.** `(S ×ˢ S).filter (fun p => G.Adj p.1 p.2 ∧ c p.1 = c p.2)`
  counts ordered pairs; `SimpleGraph` adjacency is symmetric and irreflexive, so each
  monochromatic unordered edge {u,v} ⊆ S contributes exactly the two pairs (u,v),
  (v,u) and the diagonal contributes nothing. The count is exactly 2m, and
  2m ≤ 2·f(|S|) ⟺ m ≤ f(|S|) — no slack, no parity artifact. The docstring says
  precisely this (problem-70-class docstring/code cross-check: consistent).
- **"Every finite subgraph on n vertices" vs finite vertex subsets.** The Lean
  quantifies over S : Finset V and counts edges of the *induced* subgraph G[S] with
  n = |S|. Equivalent to "every finite subgraph": any subgraph H with V(H) = S has
  E(H) ⊆ E(G[S]), so a deletion set for G[S] restricts to one for H of at most the
  same size. The induced subgraphs are the binding instances.
- **"Infinite chromatic number"** ↔ `∀ k : ℕ, ¬G.Colorable k`, i.e. `chromaticNumber
  = ⊤` in ℕ∞ — exactly "no finite chromatic number". The page's remark that the
  statement *fails* for graphs of chromatic number ℵ₁ does not affect faithfulness:
  the question asks only for infinite chromatic number (any witness would necessarily
  have χ = ℵ₀, but the statement need not and does not impose it).
- **Universe.** `V : Type` (Type 0) where upstream uses `Type u`: harmless for
  truth-value. Any graph of infinite chromatic number has a countable subgraph of
  infinite chromatic number (for each k pick a finite non-k-colorable subgraph — De
  Bruijn–Erdős — and take the union), and the deletion property is inherited by
  subgraphs, so a witness in any universe yields one on a countable vertex type in
  Type 0; conversely ULift transports upward.
- Not vacuous/trivial: `Tendsto f atTop atTop` excludes eventually-bounded f; f may
  still vanish on an initial segment, which forces the witness graph (which may
  depend on f) to have no short odd cycles — the genuine content of the conjecture,
  not a degenerate escape. No degenerate witness satisfies the statement (infinite
  chromatic number rules out trivial graphs); S = ∅ gives the true-but-inert
  0 ≤ 2·f(0).

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**
