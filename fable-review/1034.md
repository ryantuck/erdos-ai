## Summary

The problem (verbatim from the recovered page): "Let $G$ be a graph on $n$ vertices with
$>n^2/4$ many edges. Must there be a triangle $T$ in $G$ and vertices
$y_1,\ldots,y_t$, where $t>(\frac{1}{2}-o(1))n$, such that every vertex is joined to at
least two vertices of $T$?" — a conjecture of Erdős and Faudree, a stronger version of
#905, **disproved** by Ma and Tang (site status: DISPROVED (LEAN)). The Lean statement
(`deepmind/1034.lean`) encodes the yes/no question as `answer(False) ↔ (∀ ε > 0, ∃ N₀,
∀ n ≥ N₀, ∀ G` with `> n²/4` edges, `∃` triangle `u v w` with more than `(1/2 - ε)n`
vertices adjacent to at least two of them`)`, with the count as a separate def. Read
back independently, the Lean and the English agree, including the ε–N₀ rendering of
$o(1)$ and the answer polarity; I found no semantic defects, only enrichment-level nits
(missing page remarks, Lean-verification status, citation volume), all applied in place.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Independent back-translation of the theorem (lines 74–81 post-fix): "the answer is
  'no' to: for every real ε > 0 there is N₀ such that for all n ≥ N₀ and every graph G
  on n vertices with more than ⌊n²/4⌋ edges, there exist u, v, w forming a triangle such
  that the number of vertices adjacent to at least two of u, v, w exceeds (1/2 − ε)n."
  Writing $g(n)$ for the min over admissible $G$ of the max over triangles of the count,
  the RHS says exactly $\liminf g(n)/n \ge 1/2$, which is precisely the source's
  "$t > (\frac12 - o(1))n$" claim (uniform in $G$, as the source intends — the $o(1)$
  is a function of $n$ only). Exact match.
- "vertices $y_1, \ldots, y_t$ … each joined to at least two vertices of $T$": $t$
  distinct such vertices exist iff the filter-count is ≥ t, so "count > (1/2−ε)n" is the
  correct rendering of "$t > (1/2-ε)n$ such vertices exist".
- Triangle-vertex inclusion: the filter ranges over all of `Fin n`, and each triangle
  vertex is itself counted (e.g. `x = u` satisfies `Adj u v ∧ Adj u w`). The page's
  $h(n)$ paragraph counts "*other* vertices", a difference of exactly 3. The two
  readings give **logically equivalent** quantified statements: count′ = count − 3, and
  for any ε, instantiating the other form at ε/2 and taking n > 6/ε absorbs the 3 into
  εn/2; the converse is monotone. So the choice is genuinely immaterial *for this
  asymptotic statement* (this is a sound instance of the asymptotic argument, unlike the
  fabricated ones in the 1000/1005 audits — the source statement itself carries the
  $o(1)$).
- Not vacuous / not trivially false: by Mantel's theorem every admissible graph has a
  triangle, so the inner ∃ is never vacuously blocked; and the RHS is genuinely false
  (Ma–Tang), never trivially so — for small n it even holds (e.g. n = 3: the only
  admissible graph is $K_3$, count = 3 > (1/2−ε)·3), so `answer(False)` encodes real
  mathematical content about large n.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- **ℕ division** in `G.edgeFinset.card > n ^ 2 / 4` (line 78): `n^2/4` floors, and for
  an integer card, `card > ⌊n²/4⌋` ⟺ `card ≥ ⌊n²/4⌋ + 1` ⟺ real `card > n²/4` — checked
  separately for n even (n²/4 exact) and n odd (n² ≡ 1 mod 4, so real-> means
  ≥ ⌊n²/4⌋+1). Exact, no off-by-one at any n.
- **No ℕ subtraction** anywhere; `1 / 2 - ε` is in ℝ (forced by `ε : ℝ`), so `1 / 2` is
  genuine real 0.5, not integer division 0.
- No `Finset.range`; the only filter is over `univ : Finset (Fin n)`, correct.
- Degenerate n: for n ≤ 2 the edge hypothesis is unsatisfiable (max edges ≤ 1 = ⌊4/4⌋),
  so those cases are vacuous — consistent with the asymptotic statement and harmless
  under `∃ N₀`.
- "For all sufficiently large n" is encoded as `∃ N₀, ∀ n ≥ N₀` — standard and correct;
  the `∀ ε > 0` outermost correctly renders the $o(1)$.
- Strict `>` in both the edge condition and the count condition matches the source's
  "$>n^2/4$" and "$t > (1/2-o(1))n$".

**A3. Definitions — pass.**

- `adjToAtLeastTwoOfTriangle` (lines 57–61): counts x with
  `(Adj x u ∧ Adj x v) ∨ (Adj x v ∧ Adj x w) ∨ (Adj x u ∧ Adj x w)` — exactly "adjacent
  to at least two of u, v, w" (the three pairs exhaust the ways; "all three" is
  subsumed). Matches its docstring; behavior on degenerate inputs (u = v etc.) is
  well-defined and never reached under the triangle hypothesis. No `sorry` in any def.
- `noncomputable` + `open Classical` is required (`SimpleGraph.Adj` has no decidability
  instance here) — the archived upstream build log (`ba25ea22…jsonl`) shows exactly this
  fix making the file compile. Do not remove.
- No drift between what the def computes and what the theorem needs.

**A4. Question form and answer polarity — pass (source-verified).**

- The source is a **yes/no question** ("Must there be…?"), so the `answer(…) ↔ P` shape
  is correct, with P the question as asked (the positive conjecture), so `True` would
  mean "yes".
- Polarity by hand: Ma–Tang construct, for each small ε and large n, admissible graphs
  in which *every* triangle has at most $(2-\sqrt{5/2}+o(1))n \approx 0.4189n <
  (1/2-ε)n$ qualifying vertices; hence the RHS fails at e.g. ε = 1/20, i.e. the RHS is
  false and the question's answer is "no". `answer(False)` is correct.
- No binders before the colon (line 74: `theorem erdos_1034 : answer(False) ↔ …`).

**A5. Solution status — pass, one enrichment applied.**

- Module docstring, theorem docstring, and `@[category research solved]` agree:
  disproved by Ma and Tang. Source-verified against the recovered banner.
- The recovered banner is actually **DISPROVED (LEAN)** — "solved in the negative and
  the proof verified in Lean." As in the 1000 precedent, the category tag understates
  this (a `formally solved` tag would need the verification link, which is not in the
  archive), so the fact is now recorded in the module docstring instead (Addendum
  fix 1).

**Restyling drift check — pass; drift was *corrective*.** The raw
`conjectures/1034.lean` states the conjecture as a **bare positive assertion**
(`theorem erdos_problem_1034 : ∀ ε…` `:= sorry`) — the *refuted* direction — while its
own module docstring says "Disproved by Ma and Tang", an internal inconsistency. The
restyling (`e135509c…jsonl`) recast it as `answer(False) ↔ …`, which is the correct

docstring; the theorem docstring retains the full question form, so no meaning was lost.
