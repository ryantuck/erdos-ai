## Summary

The problem (verbatim from the recovered page): "Does every graph with infinite
chromatic number contain a cycle of length $2^n$ for infinitely many $n$?" —
conjectured by Mihók and Erdős, status **PROVED** ("solved in the affirmative"), the
resolution following from Liu–Montgomery [LiMo20] as observed by Zach Hunter. The Lean
(`conjectures/63.lean:14-18`) asserts: for every simple graph $G$ (on any vertex type)
with `G.chromaticNumber = ⊤`, for every $N$ there is $n \ge N$ and a cycle in $G$ of
length $2^n$. Read back independently, this is exactly the affirmative answer to the
question as asked — a direct assertion in the *true* direction of a solved problem,
which is the acceptable form for this pipeline. I found no semantic defects; the
findings are scholarship enrichment (the raw file uses citation keys but carries no
bibliography, and omits the page's remarks, cross-reference to #64, and two
formalizable page-confirmed variants).

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Independent back-translation of lines 14–17: "for every type $V$ and simple graph
  $G$ on $V$ with infinite chromatic number, and every $N$, there exist $n \ge N$, a
  vertex $v$, and a closed walk at $v$ that is a cycle of length $2^n$." This is
  precisely "every graph with infinite chromatic number contains a cycle of length
  $2^n$ for infinitely many $n$" — the question's affirmative direction, which is the
  proved direction.
- Quantifiers: `{V : Type*}` and `(G : SimpleGraph V)` as theorem binders universally
  quantify the assertion — correct for a direct assertion (no `answer()` iff to
  contaminate; cf. A4). Hypothesis `hχ` before the ∀N: correct order (the cycle
  lengths may depend on $G$, and $N$-cofinality is per-graph).
- Types: vertex type unconstrained (no `[Fintype V]`) — correct: a graph with infinite
  chromatic number is necessarily infinite, so restricting to finite graphs would make
  the statement vacuous. `SimpleGraph` is the standard reading of "graph" here.
  Lengths and exponents in ℕ; no coercions needed.
- Indexing: no sequences to reconcile; `2 ^ n` over all $n \ge N$ is literal.
- Not vacuous / not trivial: the hypothesis is satisfiable (e.g. the complete graph on
  ℕ has no finite coloring, so `chromaticNumber = ⊤`), and the conclusion is a genuine
  theorem-level fact (resolved only in 2020 via [LiMo20]). Vacuous instances (empty
  $V$, finitely-colorable $G$) fail the hypothesis, not the intent.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- No ℕ subtraction, no division, no `Finset.range` anywhere in the file.
- `G.chromaticNumber = ⊤` verified against Mathlib source
  (`Mathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Coloring/Vertex.lean:216`, clone at 355bc1e):
  `chromaticNumber : ℕ∞ := ⨅ n ∈ Set.ofPred G.Colorable, (n : ℕ∞)`. When $G$ is
  $n$-colorable for no finite $n$, the infimum is over the empty set and equals `⊤`;
  conversely any finite colorability forces `chromaticNumber < ⊤`. So `= ⊤` is exactly
  "infinite chromatic number". (Uncountable-χ graphs also satisfy it — a fortiori
  fine.)
- Degenerate exponents: `IsCycle` (a circuit — nonempty trail — with `support.tail`
  nodup) forces length ≥ 3 in a simple graph (length 1 needs a loop; a length-2 closed
  walk repeats its edge, violating `IsTrail`). So cycles of length $2^0 = 1$ and
  $2^1 = 2$ cannot exist. **Harmless**: the ∀N∃n≥N form only has content cofinally,
  and for $N \ge 2$ nothing degenerate is reachable; the informal statement's content
  (lengths 4, 8, 16, …) is exactly preserved. Re-derived, not assumed (see E).
- "Infinitely many $n$" as `∀ N, ∃ n, N ≤ n ∧ P n`: correct — in ℕ, cofinal ↔
  infinite. No filter machinery needed; nothing junk-valued (no `sSup`/`liminf`, no
  default-valued binders — the batch checklist items are all inapplicable here, checked
  explicitly).
- No `StrictMono`/`Monotone`, no interval endpoints.
