## Summary

The problem: let $p(n)$ be the partition function and let $F(n)$ count the distinct
prime factors of $\prod_{1\le k\le n} p(k)$. Two questions: does $F(n)\to\infty$ with
$n$ (settled affirmatively — Schinzel, via the asymptotic formula for $p(n)$ and a
result of Tijdeman [Ti73], details in Erdős–Ivić [ErIv90]), and is $F(n)>n$ for all
sufficiently large $n$ (OPEN; best known is $F(n)\gg\log n$, Schinzel–Wirsing
[ScWi87]). The Lean file defines $p(n)$ as `Nat.card (Nat.Partition n)` and $F(n)$ as
`(∏ k ∈ Finset.Icc 1 n, p k).primeFactors.card`, then states part 1 as
`Tendsto F atTop atTop` and part 2 as `∀ᶠ n in atTop, F n > n`. Read back
independently, both statements match the source questions term for term, with the
correct polarity for the solved part and the conjectured affirmative direction for the
open part (this corpus's convention). I found no semantic defects; the findings are
scholarship-level nits (citation keys used but never defined, page remarks and OEIS
references omitted) plus one page-confirmed enrichment variant, all applied in
`conjectures-v2/1106.lean`.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "Let $p(n)$ denote the partition
  function of $n$ and let $F(n)$ count the number of distinct prime factors of
  $\prod_{1\leq k\leq n}p(k)$. Does $F(n)\to \infty$ with $n$? Is $F(n)>n$ for all
  sufficiently large $n$?" The module docstring (lines 13–16) reproduces this
  essentially verbatim.
- Independent back-translation of `erdos_problem_1106_part1` (lines 41–43): "the
  function $n \mapsto$ (number of distinct prime factors of $\prod_{k=1}^{n} p(k)$)
  tends to infinity along $n \to \infty$." Exactly question 1's affirmative content.
- Independent back-translation of `erdos_problem_1106_part2` (lines 49–51): "for all
  sufficiently large $n$, $F(n) > n$." Exactly question 2's affirmative content, with
  strict `>` matching the source's $F(n)>n$.
- `partitionCount1106` (lines 27–28): `Nat.card (Nat.Partition n)`. Mathlib's
  `Nat.Partition n` is the type of partitions of $n$ (multiset of positive parts
  summing to $n$), a finite type, so `Nat.card` is its cardinality $p(n)$, with
  $p(0)=1$ (empty partition) — the standard convention.
- `F1106` (lines 31–32): the product ranges over `Finset.Icc 1 n` $= \{1,\dots,n\}$,
  exactly the source's $1 \le k \le n$ (not `range`, so no 0-index shift and no
  off-by-one at either end). `Nat.primeFactors` is the finset of distinct primes
  dividing the product; `.card` is $\omega(\cdot)$. Term-by-term correct.
- Not vacuous, and the small-$n$ sanity check confirms the encoding is the intended
  one: $p(1),\dots,p(6) = 1,2,3,5,7,11$ give $F(n) = n-1$ for $1 \le n \le 6$ under
  this definition (e.g. $F(6) = \omega(2310) = \omega(2\cdot3\cdot5\cdot7\cdot11) = 5$),
  so $F(n) > n$ genuinely fails for small $n$ and the "sufficiently large" qualifier
  is doing real work — the `∀ᶠ` encoding is essential, not decorative.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass on all checklist items.**

- Division: none. The only arithmetic is a product, and since $p(k) \ge 1$ for all

  correct eventuality encoding.
- Strict vs non-strict: `F1106 n > n` matches the source's strict $F(n) > n$. No
  `StrictMono`/`Monotone` or interval-endpoint issues arise.
