## Summary

The problem (verbatim from the recovered page, **OPEN** banner): "If
$G_1,G_2$ are two graphs with chromatic number $\aleph_1$ then must there
exist a graph $G$ whose chromatic number is $4$ (or even $\aleph_0$) which is
a subgraph of both $G_1$ and $G_2$?" The Lean input formalizes both readings
as direct assertions of the questioned (affirmative) direction: a weak
version (`conjectures/62.lean:30-38`) demanding a common subgraph H with
`¬ H.Colorable 3` (χ ≥ 4), and a strong version (lines 50-58) demanding
`H.chromaticNumber = ⊤` (χ ≥ ℵ₀), both under the hypothesis that G₁, G₂ have
chromatic number exactly ℵ₁ via the two-clause predicate
`HasChromaticNumberAleph1`. Both statements are faithful: the hypothesis
predicate correctly pins χ = ℵ₁, the containment predicate is the standard
non-induced subgraph notion, and the "≥ 4"/"≥ ℵ₀" relaxations of the page's
"is 4"/"is ℵ₀" are equivalent under the existential over H (argued in A1).
The only gaps are scholarship-level: no bibliography block for the cited
keys, three page keys uncited, and the page's finite-collection question
noted in a docstring but not formalized. Enrichments applied in
`conjectures-v2/62.lean`; the input file is untouched.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Independent back-translation of lines 30-38 (weak): "For all graphs G₁, G₂
  (on arbitrary vertex types) whose chromatic number is exactly ℵ₁, there
  exist a type U and a graph H on U such that H is not 3-colorable and H
  embeds into both G₁ and G₂ by injective adjacency-preserving maps." Lines
  50-58 (strong) are identical with "not 3-colorable" replaced by
  "chromatic number ⊤ in ℕ∞". This matches the page's question, split into
  its "4" and "(or even ℵ₀)" readings — the split itself is faithful to the
  parenthetical structure of the source.
- **"chromatic number is 4" vs `¬ Colorable 3` (χ ≥ 4):** the page asks for χ
  exactly 4; the Lean asks χ ≥ 4. Under the existential over H these are
  equivalent (ZFC): if χ(H) ≥ 4, de Bruijn–Erdős (choice is available in
  Lean's classical logic) gives a finite subgraph F of H that is not
  3-colorable; deleting edges of F one at a time lowers χ by at most 1 per
  step (χ(F) ≤ χ(F−e) + 1) and ends at χ = 1, so some intermediate subgraph
  F' has χ(F') = 4 exactly; F' is still a common subgraph since
  `containsSubgraph` composes. Same argument for the strong version: χ(H) ≥
  ℵ₀ yields a countable subgraph (union of finite subgraphs of unbounded
  chromatic number) with χ exactly ℵ₀. So neither encoding silently
  strengthens or weakens the question. Documented in the v2 docstrings.
- Quantifier structure: ∀ G₁ G₂ (hypotheses) → ∃ H (conclusion) — matches
  "if …, must there exist …". All binders sit after the colon, so the
  assertion has the right shape.
- **Universe parameters (checked explicitly, since every quantifier here is
  `Type*`):** the def's two color quantifiers, the theorems' vertex types,
  and the conclusion's `∃ U : Type*` each get auto-bound universe parameters,
  universally quantified at the theorem level. Every instantiation is
  equivalent to the intended universe-free statement: (i) non-colorability by
  countable types is universe-invariant (a countable coloring anywhere
  composes with an injection into ℕ, and `ULift ℕ` lives in every universe);
  (ii) colorability by an ℵ₁-sized type is universe-invariant
  (`Cardinal.lift_aleph`/`lift_mk_eq` give an equivalence between ℵ₁-sized
  types in different universes); (iii) a witness H in one universe transports
  to any other via `ULift` (adjacency and colorings transfer along the
  equivalence). So the ∀-over-universes reading neither strengthens nor
  weakens the statement. Not a defect; noted in the v2 def docstring.
- Not vacuous: graphs with χ = ℵ₁ exist (the complete graph on a type of
  cardinality ℵ₁ satisfies both clauses — no countable coloring since
  colorings of complete graphs are injective; the identity colors it by
  itself), and they exist in every universe, so the hypotheses are
  satisfiable at every instantiation. The conclusions cannot be satisfied
  degenerately: an empty or small H is 3-colorable, so `¬ H.Colorable 3`
  (a fortiori `chromaticNumber = ⊤`) forces genuine content, and containment
  in both G₁, G₂ is required.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- No ℕ subtraction, no division, no `Finset.range`, no liminf/limsup or
  filter encodings, no `StrictMono`/`Monotone`, no intervals anywhere.
- `¬ H.Colorable 3`: `Colorable n = Nonempty (G.Coloring (Fin n))`, so this
  is exactly "not 3-colorable", i.e. χ ≥ 4 — verified against the Mathlib
  definition in the scratch clone (Coloring/Vertex.lean:167).
- `H.chromaticNumber = ⊤`: `chromaticNumber = ⨅ n ∈ {n | Colorable n}, (n : ℕ∞)`

  exactly "infinite chromatic number". Note ℕ∞ cannot distinguish ℵ₀ from
  larger infinite values, but by the A1 extraction argument the existential
  statement is unaffected.

  optParam, problem-25 class); no `sSup`/`sInf`/`liminf` junk values

**A3. Definitions — pass.**

- `HasChromaticNumberAleph1` (lines 10-12) matches its docstring: clause 1
  (no countable color type works) says χ > ℵ₀, i.e. χ ≥ ℵ₁; clause 2
  (some type of size exactly ℵ₁ works) says χ ≤ ℵ₁; the conjunction pins
  χ = ℵ₁. Degenerate check: for V finite or countable, clause 1 fails
  (color by V itself), so the predicate is false — correct. Requiring
  `#α = aleph 1` *exactly* in clause 2 is not a restriction: a coloring by
  fewer colors composes with an injection into any ℵ₁-sized type.
- `containsSubgraph` (lines 15-16) matches its docstring: injective
  adjacency-preserving map = non-induced subgraph containment, the standard
  meaning of "subgraph" here, identical in content to Mathlib's

  `containsSubgraph G H` = "G contains H"; used as
  `containsSubgraph G₁ H ∧ containsSubgraph G₂ H` = "H is a subgraph of
  both" ✓.
