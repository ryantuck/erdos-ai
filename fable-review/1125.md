## Summary

The problem (Kemperman's, posed via Erdős [Er81b, p.31]): if $f : \mathbb{R} \to
\mathbb{R}$ satisfies $2f(x) \le f(x+h) + f(x+2h)$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$ and
$h > 0$, must $f$ be monotonic? Kemperman [Ke69] proved yes when $f$ is measurable;
Laczkovich [La84] solved it in the affirmative in full generality (page banner PROVED,
"solved in the affirmative"). The Lean theorem (`conjectures/1125.lean:13`) asserts:
every $f$ satisfying the inequality for all $x$ and all $h > 0$ is `Monotone` or
`Antitone`. Read back independently, this is exactly the affirmative answer to the
question as asked, with "monotonic" given its standard two-sided reading. I found **no
semantic defects**; the findings are scholarship gaps (citation keys used but never
defined with bibliographic data, missing [Er81b] page number) and one page-confirmed
enrichment (Kemperman's measurable case), all applied in `conjectures-v2/1125.lean`.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "Let $f:\mathbb{R}\to \mathbb{R}$
  be such that \[2f(x) \leq f(x+h)+f(x+2h)\] for every $x\in \mathbb{R}$ and $h>0$.
  Must $f$ be monotonic?"
- Independent back-translation of lines 13–17: "for every $f : \mathbb{R} \to
  \mathbb{R}$, if $2 f(x) \le f(x+h) + f(x+2h)$ for all real $x$ and all real $h > 0$,
  then $f$ is non-decreasing or non-increasing." Hypothesis matches term-by-term:
  `2 * f x ≤ f (x + h) + f (x + 2 * h)` under `h > 0` is exactly the source inequality,
  with all arithmetic in ℝ.
- Quantifier structure matches: $f$ universally quantified (the question is about *all*
  such $f$), $x$ and $h$ universally quantified inside the hypothesis with the
  positivity of $h$ explicit as `h > 0`. Nothing that should be existential is
  universal or vice versa.
- Types: everything is `ℝ → ℝ`; no coercions occur or are needed. No indexing
  conventions arise (no sequences).
- "Monotonic" reading: `Monotone f ∨ Antitone f` is the standard two-sided reading
  (non-decreasing or non-increasing). A one-sided reading (`Monotone f` only) would
  also be defensible — and is in fact what the hypothesis forces (see the note below) —
  but the disjunctive form is the faithful literal rendering and is implied by
  Laczkovich's theorem, so it is a true statement in the correct direction.
- Not vacuous / not trivially false: the hypothesis is satisfiable (any non-decreasing
  $f$, e.g. any constant), and the conclusion is exactly Laczkovich's non-trivial
  theorem. Degeneracy note (verified by hand): under the hypothesis the `Antitone`
  disjunct collapses to constants — if $f$ is antitone, then for $h > 0$ both
  $f(x+h) \le f(x)$ and $f(x+2h) \le f(x)$, so
  $2f(x) \le f(x+h) + f(x+2h) \le 2f(x)$ forces $f(x+h) = f(x)$ for all $x, h > 0$,
  i.e. $f$ constant. Hence under the hypothesis the disjunction is *equivalent* to
  `Monotone f`, the sharp form; the disjunct does not create a spurious escape hatch.
  Now documented in the v2 module docstring.
