## Summary

The problem (a conjecture of Erdős reported in [GoGo45], PROVED): if $C_1,\ldots,C_n$
are circles in $\mathbb{R}^2$ with radii $r_1,\ldots,r_n$ such that no line disjoint
from all the circles divides them into two non-empty sets, then the circles can be
covered by a circle of radius $r=\sum r_i$. Goodman and Goodman proved this in the same
1945 paper, and their proof generalises to higher dimensions. The Lean statement
(`conjectures/1121.lean:23`) takes centers and positive radii, encodes lines as
$\{x:\langle x,v\rangle=d\}$ for unit $v$, states non-separability as "whenever a line
is disjoint from every disk ($\lvert\langle c_i,v\rangle-d\rvert>r_i$ for all $i$), all
centers lie on the same side", and concludes $\exists p,\forall i,
\operatorname{dist}(c_i,p)+r_i\le\sum_j r_j$. Read back independently, this is exactly
the source statement in the true (proved) direction, in the corpus's standard
direct-assertion form for solved assertions. I found no semantic defects; the nits are
scholarship-level (the `[GoGo45]` key is used but never defined — the raw file has no
module docstring or bibliography) and one page-confirmed optional variant.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "If $C_1,\ldots,C_n$ are circles
  in $\mathbb{R}^2$ with radii $r_1,\ldots,r_n$ such that no line disjoint from all the
  circles divides them into two non-empty sets then the circles can be covered by a
  circle of radius $r=\sum r_i$." The module-less raw docstring paraphrases this
  essentially verbatim.
- Independent back-translation of lines 23–34: "for all $n$, centers $c_i\in\mathbb{R}^2$
  and radii $r_i>0$: if for every unit $v$ and offset $d$ with
  $\lvert\langle c_i,v\rangle-d\rvert>r_i$ for all $i$, the predicates
  $\langle c_i,v\rangle>d$ agree for all $i,j$ — then some point $p$ satisfies
  $\operatorname{dist}(c_i,p)+r_i\le\sum_j r_j$ for all $i$."
- **Line encoding is complete and faithful.** Every line in the plane is
  $\{x:\langle x,v\rangle=d\}$ for some unit $v$ and real $d$ (the two representations
  $(v,d)$ and $(-v,-d)$ per line are both quantified over — harmless). For unit $v$ the
  distance from $c$ to the line is $\lvert\langle c,v\rangle-d\rvert$.
- **Disjointness, circles-as-curves vs closed disks.** The source says "circles"; the
  Lean condition $\lvert\langle c_i,v\rangle-d\rvert>r_i$ is disjointness of the line
  from the *closed disk*. These coincide: a line disjoint from the bounding circle
  cannot meet the open disk either, since a line is connected and unbounded, so if it
  had a point at distance $<r_i$ from $c_i$ and (necessarily, by unboundedness) a point
  at distance $>r_i$, the intermediate value theorem on $t\mapsto
  \operatorname{dist}(\ell(t),c_i)$ would give a point on the circle. Conversely
  disjointness from the closed disk trivially gives disjointness from the circle. And
  for the compact disk, disjoint-from-line $\iff$ distance $>r_i$, which is the strict
  inequality used. So the encoding is exact for either reading of "circle".
- **Non-separability.** Under the disjointness hypothesis,
  $\lvert\langle c_i,v\rangle-d\rvert>r_i>0$ forces $\langle c_i,v\rangle\ne d$, and the
  whole disk lies strictly on its center's side; so the side of the *center* is the side
  of the *circle*, and the biconditional `∀ i j, ⟨cᵢ,v⟩ > d ↔ ⟨cⱼ,v⟩ > d` says exactly
  "all circles on one side", i.e. the line does not divide them into two non-empty sets.
  This is precisely the contrapositive-free reading of "no line disjoint from all the
  circles divides them into two non-empty sets". Term-by-term faithful.
- **Covering.** The farthest point of circle/disk $i$ from $p$ lies at distance
  $\operatorname{dist}(c_i,p)+r_i$ (attained along the ray from $p$ through $c_i$; also
  correct when $p=c_i$), so `dist (center i) p + radius i ≤ ∑ j, radius j` is exactly
  $C_i\subseteq\bar B(p,\sum_j r_j)$ — "covered by a circle of radius $r=\sum r_i$",
  again for either the curve or disk reading.
- **Not vacuous/trivial.** $n=0$: hypotheses vacuous, conclusion holds with any $p$ (the
  plane is nonempty) — matches the empty informal reading, harmless. $n=1$: `hns` is
  trivially satisfied (its inner biconditional is reflexive) and the conclusion forces
  $p=c_0$ ($\operatorname{dist}(c_0,p)\le 0$) — true and faithful. For $n\ge 2$ the
  hypothesis is substantive (it fails for, e.g., two far-apart unit circles) and the
  conclusion is the genuinely nontrivial Goodman–Goodman theorem. No degenerate witness
  trivializes the statement.
- Positivity `hr : ∀ i, 0 < radius i` is the natural reading of "circles with radii
  $r_i$" (a radius-0 "circle" is a point) and is what makes the strict side-assignment
  argument above clean. The theorem is *weaker* with the extra hypothesis, and the
  hypothesis is faithful, so this is correct.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- No ℕ subtraction, no division, no `Finset.range` anywhere; all arithmetic is in ℝ
  (`radius : Fin n → ℝ`, inner products, `dist`, real `|·|`).
- Strict `>` in `|⟨cᵢ,v⟩ − d| > rᵢ` is exactly disjointness of a line from a *closed*
  disk (compact vs closed: disjoint iff positive distance); non-strict `≤` in the
  conclusion matches covering by a closed disk of radius exactly $\sum r_i$. Both
  strictness choices are the right ones.
- Strict `>` vs `≥` in the side predicate `⟨cᵢ,v⟩ > d` is immaterial under the
  disjointness hypothesis (equality is impossible, as shown in A1), so the biconditional
  is unambiguous.
- No filter/liminf/"infinitely many" encodings, no `StrictMono`/`Monotone`, no
  vacuous bounded quantifiers beyond the intended $n=0$/$n=1$ degeneracies analyzed
  above.
- `@inner ℝ _ _ x y` is Mathlib's real inner product; over ℝ it is symmetric, so the
  argument order `⟪center i, v⟫` vs `⟪v, center i⟫` is immaterial.

## E. Prior-review audit (`deepmind/ai-review/1121.md`)

The mathematical core of the prior review is sound and was re-derived; three findings:

1. **False general lemma, right conclusion (§6, disjointness).** It justifies the
   strict inequality with: "a closed set and a closed set are disjoint iff they have
   positive distance". This is **false in general** — two disjoint closed sets can have
   distance 0 (e.g. a hyperbola and its asymptote). The equivalence holds *here*
   because the disk is **compact** (compact-vs-closed disjointness does give positive
   distance). The conclusion (strict `>` is correct) survives, but the stated
   justification is the recurring prior-review class of unsound arguments that would
   mask a real bug where compactness fails (cf. the unsound `sSup` argument in the 1005
   audit).
2. **Bibliographic verification overclaimed (§2).** It presents the full AMM reference
   and concludes "Citation is accurate and complete … consistent with the website's
   reference tag [GoGo45]". The website material recoverable for this problem carries
   only the *key* [GoGo45]; no `/latex/1121` fetch exists, so journal/volume/pages were
   never site-verified — the data it "verified" is the styling model's own output fed
   back to it. The data happens to match reviewer knowledge (no factual error
   established), but the verification claim is unsupported — the same
   endorse-unverified-bibliography pattern found in the 1120 audit. Moreover, against
   the artifact under review *here*, the claim is doubly wrong: `conjectures/1121.lean`
   contains no bibliography at all (the prior review was written against the styled
   copy).
3. **Minor imprecision (§6, edge cases).** For $n=1$ it says "Hypothesis is vacuous."
   The `hns` hypothesis is not vacuous (its quantifier domains are inhabited); it is
   *trivially satisfied* because the inner biconditional is reflexive. Harmless here.

**Correct and re-verified claims:** the contrapositive reading of `hns` and the
sufficiency of checking centers (§6); the triangle-inequality covering argument
(§6, re-derived: $\operatorname{dist}(x,p)\le r_i+\operatorname{dist}(c_i,p)\le\sum r_j$
