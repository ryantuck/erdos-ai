## Summary

The problem (PROVED, by Bálint [Ba60b]): let $f(x) \in \mathbb{R}[x]$ have all real
roots $a_0 < \cdots < a_n$ in arithmetic progression; then the differences between
consecutive zeros of $f'(x)$, beginning from the midpoint of $(a_0, a_n)$ towards the
endpoints, are monotonically increasing. (The site's "degree $n$" with $n+1$ listed
roots and its "$a_m$" are typos; the formalization correctly follows the root count.)
The Lean (`conjectures/1114.lean:29-37`) takes the monic normalization
$f = \prod_{i=0}^{n}(X - (a + i d))$, $d > 0$, $n \ge 2$, and asserts the existence of
a strictly increasing enumeration $b_0 < \cdots < b_{n-1}$ of the zeros of $f'$ whose
gaps $g_i = b_{i+1} - b_i$ satisfy $g_i \le g_j$ for
$\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor \le i < j \le n-2$. Everything it says is correct — the
threshold arithmetic is exactly right, and clauses 1–3 pin $b$ down uniquely on
$[0, n)$ — but read literally it asserts only the **right** monotone run; the source's
"towards the endpoints" claims both runs, and the left one is recovered only via a
symmetry fact that is provable about this configuration yet appears nowhere in the
formal statement. That is a genuine (if mild) statement-weakening defect for a
fidelity-first corpus; v2 states both runs.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — one [defect]: one-sided statement; everything stated is
correct (source-verified).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "Let $f(x)\in \mathbb{R}[x]$ be
  a polynomial of degree $n$ whose roots $\{a_0<\cdots<a_n\}$ are all real and form an
  arithmetic progression. The differences between consecutive zeros of $f'(x)$,
  beginning from the midpoint of $(a_0,a_m)$ towards the endpoints, are monotonically
  increasing."
- Source typos, resolved by the formalization the right way: (i) a degree-$n$
  polynomial cannot have the $n+1$ listed roots — the Lean builds a degree-$(n+1)$
  product of $n+1$ linear factors, so its parameter `n` is the degree of $f'$ and the
  number of its zeros; (ii) "$a_m$" is undefined — read $a_n$ (the raw archived HTML
  has $a_m$ twice; both log-preserved live-fetch extractions render it $a_n$).
- Monic normalization is WLOG: any $f$ with these roots is $c \cdot \prod (X - a_i)$,
  $c \ne 0$, and scaling changes neither the zero set of $f'$ nor the gaps. AP
  parametrization `a + ↑i * d` with `hd : 0 < d` exactly captures increasing distinct
  AP roots.
- Back-translation of `conjectures/1114.lean:29-37`: for all $n \ge 2$, $a$, $d > 0$,
  there exists $b : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{R}$ with (1) $b$ strictly increasing on
  indices $< n$; (2) each $b_j$ ($j < n$) a root of $f'$; (3) every root of $f'$ equal
  to some $b_j$, $j < n$; (4) $g_i \le g_j$ whenever
  $\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor \le i < j$ and $j + 1 < n$. Clauses 1–3 are satisfiable and
  pin $b$ uniquely on $[0, n)$: $f$ has $n+1$ distinct real roots, so Rolle gives $n$
  roots of $f'$ interlacing them, and $\deg f' = n$ caps the count at exactly those
  $n$. All indices used in clause 4 are $< n$, so the unconstrained values of $b$ at
  indices $\ge n$ are harmless.
- Threshold arithmetic verified by hand and by script: the $n - 1$ gaps are indexed
  $0..n-2$; the first gap index at or right of center is $\lceil(n-2)/2\rceil$, and
  the identity $\lceil(n-2)/2\rceil = \lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor$ holds for every
  $n \ge 2$ (checked $n = 2..11$; both parities). `j + 1 < n` is exactly
  $j \le n - 2$. So clause 4 is precisely "the right run of gaps, central gap
  included, is non-decreasing outward". Term-by-term exact.
- **[defect] The left run is missing.** The source claims monotone growth from the
  midpoint towards *the endpoints* — both directions. The formal statement asserts
  only the right run. The two are equivalent in truth value, but only via the symmetry
  fact $g_i = g_{n-2-i}$ (from $f(2c - x) = \pm f(x)$ at $c = a + nd/2$, hence
  $f'$'s zeros symmetric about $c$), which is a *theorem about the configuration* that
  appears nowhere in the formal statement. Read literally — the A1 standard — the Lean
  asserts a strictly weaker proposition than the source; a prover discharging
  `erdos_problem_1114` would have proved less than Bálint did. The docstring is honest
  about the reduction (it invokes symmetry explicitly), so this is a statement-fidelity
  defect, not a docstring defect. Fixed in v2 by adding the left run
  ($g_j \le g_i$ for $i < j \le \lfloor(n-2)/2\rfloor$); the mirror map
  $i \mapsto n-2-i$ carries the added clause exactly onto the existing right-run
  clause, confirming the two runs are stated consistently.
- Not vacuous: for $n \ge 4$ the right run compares at least one genuine pair
  ($n = 4$: $g_1 \le g_2$). For $n \in \{2, 3\}$ both runs are vacuous, correctly so
  (one gap, resp. two symmetric central gaps — nothing to compare). Numerical
  spot-check ($a = 0$, $d = 1$, $n = 2..8$, exact-coefficient bisection): gaps are
  symmetric, minimal at the center, and monotonically increasing outward in both
  directions — the statement (including the v2 left run) holds in every checked case.
- `hn : 2 ≤ n` restricts to $f$ of degree $\ge 3$; the excluded cases $n \in \{0,1\}$
  are vacuous/trivial (no gaps), so no content is lost. Harmless.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- ℕ subtraction: `n - 1` under `hn : 2 ≤ n` never truncates ($n - 1 \ge 1$). (In the
  v2 left-run clause, `n - 2 ≥ 0` is safe for the same reason; at $n = 2$ it is $0$
  and the clause is correctly vacuous.)
- ℕ division: `(n - 1) / 2` floors — and the floor is *intended*
  ($\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor$, per the docstring); verified above that this floor is the
  correct right-run start for both parities. No division by zero anywhere.
- `Finset.range (n + 1)` = $\{0, \dots, n\}$ — exactly the $n + 1$ roots
  $a, a+d, \dots, a+nd$. No off-by-one.
- Vacuous bounded quantifiers: clause 4 is vacuous for $n \le 3$, matching the source
  (nothing to compare there); clauses 1–3 are never vacuous for $n \ge 2$.
- No filters/limits/density encodings arise. Strict `<` in clause 1 correctly renders
  $b_0 < b_1 < \cdots$; non-strict `≤` in clause 4 is the literal reading of
  "monotonically increasing" (numerically the growth is strict away from the center

  choice).
- Casts: `↑i * d` and (in v2's variant) `↑n * d` multiply in ℝ after coercion.
  Correct.
