## Summary

The problem asks: is there a constant $c > 0$ such that every graph on $2^n$ vertices
with minimum degree $> (1-c)2^n$ contains the $n$-dimensional hypercube $Q_n$ as a
subgraph? The status on the recovered source page is **OPEN** ([Er93, p.345], tag:
graph theory). The Lean file (`deepmind/1035.lean`) defines $Q_n$ on vertex type
`Fin n → Bool` (adjacency = Hamming distance exactly 1), subgraph containment as an
injective adjacency-preserving map, and states
`answer(sorry) ↔ ∃ c : ℝ, c > 0 ∧ ∀ n, ∀ G on Fin (2^n), (∀ v, (G.degree v : ℝ) >
(1-c) * 2^n) → ContainsSubgraph G (hypercubeGraph n)`. Read back independently, the
Lean and the English agree; the question form, polarity, and OPEN status are all
correctly encoded. No semantic defects found — only polish-level items, mostly already
addressed by earlier pipeline sessions (this review verified those fixes rather than
needing new ones).

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "Is there a constant $c>0$ such
  that every graph on $2^n$ vertices with minimum degree $>(1-c)2^n$ contains the
  $n$-dimensional hypercube $Q_n$?" The module and theorem docstrings reproduce it
  faithfully (the added "as a subgraph" merely makes the containment notion explicit).
- Independent back-translation of lines 75–82 (post-fix numbering): "there is a real
  $c > 0$ such that for every $n$ and every graph $G$ on $2^n$ vertices, if every
  vertex of $G$ has degree $> (1-c) \cdot 2^n$, then there is an injective
  adjacency-preserving map from $Q_n$ into $G$." This is exactly the question asked.
- "Minimum degree $> t$" is encoded as `∀ v, degree v > t`, which is equivalent to
  `minDegree > t` on the nonempty vertex type `Fin (2^n)` — faithful.
- Quantifier structure: $c$ existential, $n$ and $G$ universal inside the iff, in the
  right order. The universal over *all* $n$ (rather than "sufficiently large $n$") is
  the natural reading and is moreover **equivalent** to the eventual form here: if $c$
  works for all $n \ge N$, then $c' = \min(c, 2^{-N})$ works for all $n$, since for
  $n < N$ the hypothesis `degree > (1-c')2^n` forces an integer degree
  $> 2^n - c'2^n > 2^n - 1$, i.e. $\ge 2^n$, which is impossible on $2^n$ vertices
  (max degree $2^n - 1$) — the implication is vacuously true; for $n \ge N$,
  $c' \le c$ makes the hypothesis stronger. So the encoding cannot diverge from the
  intended asymptotic question.
- Not vacuous / not trivially false: `Fin n → Bool` has cardinality $2^n$ = the host's
  vertex count, so containment is never impossible for cardinality reasons (an
  injection exists; the complete graph $K_{2^n}$ contains $Q_n$ under any bijection).
  Small-$n$ sanity: $n = 0$ — $Q_0$ is one isolated vertex, contained in any graph on
  `Fin 1`; $n = 1$ — $Q_1$ is one edge, and any graph on 2 vertices with a vertex of
  positive degree contains it. No degenerate case makes the RHS trivially true or
  false; the statement is the substantive open conjecture.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- No ℕ subtraction: `(1 - c)` is ℝ subtraction (`c : ℝ`). The multiplication and
  comparison happen in ℝ after the cast `(G.degree v : ℝ)`. Correct.
- No division anywhere.
- `(2 ^ n : ℝ)` elaborates as `(2 : ℝ) ^ n` (Monoid power by a natural), which equals
  the cast of the ℕ power used in `Fin (2 ^ n)` — the two occurrences of $2^n$ agree
  in value. No off-by-one, no `Finset.range` use in the statement.
- Vacuous-quantifier check: for fixed $c$, small $n$ can make the degree hypothesis
  unsatisfiable (implication vacuously true), exactly as in the informal problem —
  see the equivalence argument in A1. This matches, not alters, the source.
- Strictness: the source's "$>(1-c)2^n$" is encoded as strict `>` — exact match.
- Instance binder `[DecidableRel G.Adj]` inside the ∀ quantifies over the instance;
  harmless, since `degree` (a `Finset.card` of a filtered `univ`) is
  instance-independent up to propositional equality (`Decidable` is a subsingleton).

**A3. Definitions — pass.**

- `hypercubeGraph` (lines 47–58): adjacency `u ≠ v ∧ card {i | u i ≠ v i} = 1` is the
  standard $Q_n$; the `u ≠ v` conjunct is technically redundant (Hamming distance 1
  already forces $u \ne v$) but harmless and arguably clarifying. `symm` proof (swap
  the filter predicate via `ne_comm`) and `loopless` (`h.1 rfl`) are correct.
  Degenerate input: `hypercubeGraph 0` is a single isolated vertex (empty `univ`
  makes the card-1 condition unsatisfiable) — the correct $Q_0$.
- `ContainsSubgraph` (lines 60–62): injective map preserving adjacency = "contains a
  copy as a (not necessarily induced) subgraph" — the correct notion for this problem.
- No `sorry` in any `def`; definitions used consistently by the theorem.

**A4. Question form and answer polarity — pass.**

- The source is a yes/no question ("Is there a constant $c > 0$…?"), so
  `answer(sorry) ↔ P` with `P` the existence statement as asked is the right shape;
  `True` would mean "yes, such a $c$ exists". Polarity verified by hand.
- The problem is OPEN (recovered banner), so `answer(sorry)` — not `True`/`False` —
  is correct.
- No binders before the colon (line 75: `theorem erdos_1035 : answer(sorry) ↔ …`),
  so the iff is not spuriously universally quantified.

**A5. Solution status — pass (source-verified).**

- Recovered page banner: **OPEN**, tooltip "This is open, and cannot be resolved with
  a finite computation," plus the owner's standard disclaimer. `@[category research
  open]` and both docstrings agree. The page's "Formalised statement? No" reflects
  its 2026-02-22 access date, before this pipeline's contribution.

## E. Prior-review audit (`ai-review/1035.md`)

The prior review examined an intermediate snapshot (post-`answer()`-fix, pre-citation
and pre-`G.degree` fixes). Findings:

1. **Citation discrepancy misdiagnosed and excused (the recurring failure pattern).**
   The prior review saw the file citing [Er93] = "On some of my favourite theorems",
   Bolyai Soc. Math. Stud. 2, pp. 97–132, noticed "p.345" is incompatible, and
   concluded "this discrepancy originates from the source… It is possible the page
   reference pertains to a different edition… The formalization faithfully reproduces
   the website's citation, which is acceptable." **Wrong on both counts.** The
   recovered `/latex/1035` bibliography gives [Er93] = "Some of my favorite solved
   and unsolved problems in graph theory", Quaestiones Mathematicae (1993), 333–350 —
   p.345 fits perfectly. The discrepancy originated in the *restyling step* (which
   pasted a different Erdős-1993 paper under the same key from a template/sibling),
   not in the source, and the file was *not* faithfully reproducing the website. A
   later pipeline session (log a0715b9d) independently caught and fixed this. Same
   class as the 1000 audit finding: inventing an external explanation for a
   discrepancy instead of resolving it — here it would have left a wrong bibliography
   in place.
2. **Unsound edge case in the "bound on $c$" argument (conclusion survives).** The
   prior review argues $c \ge 1$ cannot witness the existential because then "the
   degree condition becomes trivially satisfied by all graphs" while e.g. the empty
   graph lacks $Q_n$. At $c > 1$ this is right (threshold negative), but at $c = 1$
   the threshold is $0$ and the empty graph does **not** satisfy `degree > 0` — the
   proposed counterexample fails the hypothesis, so the argument has a hole exactly
   at the boundary. The conclusion survives via a different witness: for $n = 2$,
   a perfect matching on 4 vertices has minimum degree $1 > 0$ but does not contain
   $Q_2 = C_4$. Re-derived, not just nodded along.
3. **Accurate elsewhere.** Its reuse survey (verified by grep here), its variants
   summary (verified against the recovered page), its `answer(sorry) ↔` endorsement,
   and its remaining correctness bullets (vertex type, $Q_n$ construction, degree
   cast, monomorphism notion, $n \in \{0,1\}$ edge cases, `DecidableRel`) all check
   out.
4. **Flagged-but-then-fixed items.** The `G.degree` suggestion was applied upstream;
   the two page variants it noted remain unformalized by deliberate choice (B2).
   Nothing it flagged remains both valid and unaddressed.
