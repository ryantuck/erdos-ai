## Summary

The problem (conjectured by Erdős, Ko, and Rado 1961; proved by Ahlswede and
Khachatrian 1997 via their complete intersection theorem; $500 reward): if
$\mathcal{F}$ is a family of $2n$-element subsets of $[4n]$ with
$|A \cap B| \ge 2$ for every $A, B \in \mathcal{F}$, then
$|\mathcal{F}| \le \frac{1}{2}\big(\binom{4n}{2n} - \binom{2n}{n}^2\big)$. The
Lean theorem (`conjectures/83.lean:16-22`) states exactly this: for all
$n > 0$ and every `F : Finset (Finset (Fin (4 * n)))` that is $2n$-uniform and
pairwise $2$-intersecting, `F.card ≤ (choose (4n) (2n) - choose (2n) n ^ 2) / 2`
in ℕ. The ℕ subtraction never truncates (Vandermonde) and the ℕ division is
exact (the difference is even for all $n \ge 1$), so the ℕ arithmetic coincides
with the intended rational bound. Direct-assertion form with the proved (true)
polarity is correct for this solved problem. I verified the statement by
exhaustive brute force at $n = 1$ and verified the extremal construction at
$n = 2$; no semantic defects were found — only enrichment-level nits
(no bibliography, tightness remark not formalized).

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (LaTeX, from the archived page — see
  Addendum): "Suppose that we have a family $\mathcal{F}$ of subsets of $[4n]$
  such that $\lvert A\rvert=2n$ for all $A\in\mathcal{F}$ and for every
  $A,B\in \mathcal{F}$ we have $\lvert A\cap B\rvert \geq 2$. Then
  $\lvert \mathcal{F}\rvert \leq \frac{1}{2}\left(\binom{4n}{2n}-\binom{2n}{n}^2\right)$."
- Independent back-translation of lines 16–21: for every positive natural $n$
  and every finite family $F$ of subsets of a $4n$-element type, if each member
  has exactly $2n$ elements and every ordered pair of members (including
  $A = B$) meets in at least $2$ elements, then
  $|F| \le \big(\binom{4n}{2n} - \binom{2n}{n}^2\big)/2$ (ℕ arithmetic).
  This matches the source proposition exactly.
- Quantifiers: everything universally quantified in the right order; the
  implicit "$n \ge 1$" of the informal statement is made explicit as `0 < n`.
  Including the diagonal pair $A = B$ in the intersection hypothesis is
  harmless: $|A \cap A| = 2n \ge 2$ holds automatically for $n \ge 1$, so the
  hypothesis is equivalent to its distinct-pairs form.
- Types: `Finset (Finset (Fin (4 * n)))` is the idiomatic encoding of a family
  of subsets of $[4n]$ (finite, no multiplicity — matching a *family of sets*);
  all arithmetic in ℕ, exact (see A2). `Fin (4 * n)` has the right cardinality;
  no indexing subtleties arise since the ground set is only used as an
  unstructured $4n$-element set.
- Not vacuous / not trivial: verified computationally. At $n = 1$ an exhaustive
  search over all $2^6$ families of $2$-subsets of $[4]$ gives maximum family
  size $1 = (\binom{4}{2} - \binom{2}{1}^2)/2$ — true and tight. At $n = 2$ the
  extremal family (all $4$-subsets of $[8]$ with $\ge 3$ elements from $[4]$)
  has size $17 = (\binom{8}{4} - \binom{4}{2}^2)/2$, is $2$-intersecting, and is
  maximal (cannot be extended). The bound is far below the trivial
  $\binom{4n}{2n}$, so the statement has real content.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass (each checked explicitly).**

- **ℕ subtraction** `choose (4n) (2n) - choose (2n) n ^ 2`: never truncates.
  By Vandermonde, $\binom{4n}{2n} = \sum_{k=0}^{2n} \binom{2n}{k}^2 \ge
  \binom{2n}{n}^2$ (verified numerically for $n \le 14$ as well). Even at
  $n = 0$: $1 - 1 = 0$, no truncation.
- **ℕ division** `/ 2`: exact for all $n \ge 1$. $\binom{4n}{2n}$ is even
  (fixed-point-free complementation involution $A \mapsto [4n] \setminus A$ on
  $2n$-subsets; no fixed points since $A \cap A^c = \emptyset$ but $|A| = 2n > 0$),
  and $\binom{2n}{n}^2$ is even (the same involution restricted to the
  complement-closed class of $2n$-subsets with exactly $n$ elements in each half
  of $[4n]$, a class of size $\binom{2n}{n}^2$). Verified numerically for
  $n = 1, \ldots, 10$. Hence the difference is even and floor division equals
  exact division; moreover even if it were odd, flooring would only strengthen
  the (integer-valued) bound, never weaken it.
- **`Finset.range`** — not used; no off-by-one surface.
- **Vacuous quantifiers / degenerate inputs** — the smallest quantified
  parameter is $n = 1$ (checked by hand and by brute force above; the
  problem-20/78/80 failure class does not occur). $n = 0$ is excluded by
  `0 < n`; the statement would in fact still be true there
  ($F \subseteq \{\emptyset\}$; $F = \{\emptyset\}$ violates the intersection
  hypothesis since $|\emptyset \cap \emptyset| = 0$, and $F = \emptyset$
  satisfies $0 \le 0$) — see Part E on the prior review's "necessary" claim.
- **Filter/liminf/limsup encodings** — none used (no problem-33 class risk).
- **Strict vs non-strict** — `≥ 2` and `≤` match the source exactly.
- **Default-valued binders** (problem-25 class) — none.
- **Constant quantifier order** (problem-55 class) — no existential constants.
