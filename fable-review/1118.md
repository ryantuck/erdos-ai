## Summary

The problem (Problem 2.40 in [Ha74], attributed to Erdős): let $f(z)$ be a
non-constant entire function such that for some $c$ the set
$E(c)=\{z : |f(z)|>c\}$ has finite measure. What is the minimum growth rate of
$f$? And if $E(c)$ has finite measure, must there exist $c'<c$ with $E(c')$ of
finite measure? The page's status is SOLVED: Hayman's conjecture — that
$\int_0^\infty r/\log\log M(r)\,\mathrm{d}r<\infty$, with
$M(r)=\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|$, and that this is best possible — was proved
independently by Camera [Ca77] and Gol'dberg [Go79b]; the second question was
answered in the negative by Gol'dberg, who proved that the threshold set
$T=\{c>0:|E(c)|<\infty\}$ can equal $[m,\infty)$ or $(m,\infty)$ for any $m>0$.
The Lean file (`conjectures/1118.lean`) states both parts as direct assertions:
`erdos_problem_1118_growth_rate` (lines 31–37) and
`erdos_problem_1118_negative_answer` (lines 49–54). Part 2 is faithful and
correctly polarized. Part 1 follows the page's printed $\int_0^\infty$
*literally* — `IntegrableOn … (Ioi 0)` — and is thereby **false** for members of
the hypothesis class whose maximum modulus crosses $e$ at a positive radius: the
integrand has a genuine non-integrable singularity there that Lean's junk-value
conventions do not repair. The intended content is convergence at infinity;
this is a defect (same family as 1004's literally-false page bound).

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — Part 2 pass (source-verified); Part 1 defect (see A2).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (all four captures agree): "Let $f(z)$ be
  a non-constant entire function such that, for some $c$, the set
  $E(c)=\{ z: \lvert f(z)\rvert >c\}$ has finite measure. / What is the minimum
  growth rate of $f(z)$? / If $E(c)$ has finite measure then must there exist
  $c'<c$ such that $E(c')$ has finite measure?"
- `exceedanceSet` (line 19–20) is exactly $E(c)$; `maxModulus` (line 15–16) is
  exactly $M(r)$ for $r\ge 0$ (attained on the compact circle for entire $f$;
  at $r=0$ it is $\|f(0)\|$; for $r<0$ the set is empty and `sSup` returns the
  junk value 0, irrelevant on `Ioi 0`).
- Non-constancy `∃ z, f z ≠ f 0` (line 32) is a correct encoding: any function
  (entire or not) is constant iff it everywhere equals its value at 0.
- Hypothesis `hc : 0 < c` (line 33): the source says only "for some $c$", but
  for non-constant entire $f$, finiteness of $|E(c)|$ forces $c>0$ (for $c<0$,
  $E(c)=\mathbb{C}$; for $c=0$, $E(0)$ is the complement of the countable,
  discrete zero set, of infinite measure), and the page's own $T$ is defined
  with $c>0$. Harmless, arguably clarifying; not a defect.
- Part 2 (lines 49–54) back-translates to: "there exist a non-constant entire
  $f$ and $c>0$ with $|E(c)|<\infty$ and $|E(c')|=\infty$ for all
  $c'\in(0,c)$" — exactly the negative answer Gol'dberg proved (witnessed by
  his $T=[m,\infty)$ functions with $c=m$). The restriction to $c'>0$ loses
  nothing (see above: $c'\le 0$ gives infinite measure automatically). Not
  vacuous, quantifiers in the right order, existential inside the statement.
- Part 1 is where fidelity fails — not against the page's *printed* formula
  (which it renders faithfully) but against the theorem Camera and Gol'dberg
  actually proved; the printed $\int_0^\infty$ is informal shorthand for
  convergence at infinity and is false when read with literal Lean semantics
  on all of $(0,\infty)$. Details in A2.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — one [defect], remainder pass.**

- **[defect] Non-integrable interior singularity in Part 1's conclusion
  (lines 35–36).** The conclusion `IntegrableOn (fun r => r / Real.log
  (Real.log (maxModulus f r))) (Ioi 0) volume` demands Lebesgue integrability
  of $|r/\log\log M(r)|$ over the whole ray. Consider any admissible $f$ with
  $M(r)$ crossing $e$ at some $r_0>0$ — such $f$ exist inside the hypothesis
  class: take Gol'dberg's $g$ with $T_g=[m,\infty)$ (page-confirmed) and
  rescale to $f=g/A$, which is still non-constant entire with
  $E_f(m/A)=E_g(m)$ of finite measure, and has $\|f(0)\|<e$ for large $A$,
  hence (with $M_f$ continuous, strictly increasing, unbounded) a crossing
  $M_f(r_0)=e$. Just above $r_0$: $\log M_f$ is locally Lipschitz (convexity
  of $\log M$ in $\log r$ by Hadamard three-circles ⇒ locally Lipschitz), and
  $\log$ is Lipschitz near $\log M_f(r_0)=1$, so
  $0<\log\log M_f(r)\le K(r-r_0)$, and the integrand dominates
  $r_0/(K(r-r_0))$, whose integral over $(r_0, r_0+\delta)$ diverges. Lean's
  totality conventions (`Real.log x = 0` for $x\le 0$, `y/0 = 0`) zero out
  only isolated bad *points*, not the neighborhood where
  $\log\log M_f\in(0,\varepsilon)$; a.e.-equality cannot rescue a genuinely
  non-integrable function. So the theorem as stated is **false**. The true
  content — and the standard reading of the printed $\int_0^\infty$ — is tail
  convergence: fixed in v2 to
  `∃ r₀ : ℝ, IntegrableOn … (Ioi r₀) volume` (not compile-verified).
  (Also worth noting: the other potentially worrying crossing, $M(r)=1$, is
  *harmless* — approaching it, $\log\log M \to -\infty$ or hits junk 0, and
  the integrand stays bounded near it.)
- Field division by zero returns 0 at the isolated points where
  $\log\log M(r)=0$ exactly — harmless per the above, and correctly understood
  as junk.
- No ℕ subtraction, no `Finset.range`, no indexing to reconcile.
- No vacuous bounded quantifiers: Part 2's `∀ c', 0 < c' → c' < c → …` has a
  nonempty domain $(0,c)$ since $c>0$.
- `volume … < ⊤` / `= ⊤` on `ℝ≥0∞` are the correct encodings of finite /
  infinite measure (`< ⊤ ↔ ≠ ⊤`); `volume` on ℂ is planar Lebesgue measure
  and `volume` on ℝ the linear one — both appropriate. Measure of the
  (open, hence measurable, for continuous $f$) set $E(c)$ is well-defined.
- No `StrictMono`/`Monotone` or interval-endpoint issues beyond those covered.

## E. Prior-review audit (`deepmind/ai-review/1118.md`)

Written against the styled copy (`deepmind/deepmind/1118.lean`), whose Lean
statements are mathematically identical to the raw file's; its mathematics is
auditable. Three substantive findings:

1. **Unsound integrability argument, wrong conclusion (§6).** The prior review
   notices the small-$r$ subtlety and then dismisses it: "Lean's `Real.log` is
   total … and division by zero yields 0, so the integrand is always
   well-defined. The `IntegrableOn` condition … correctly handles these edge
   cases — the integrand is eventually positive and **the finite-interval
   portion is bounded**." The bolded claim is false: near a radius with
   $M(r_0)=e$ the integrand behaves like $r_0/(K(r-r_0))$ and is unbounded and
   non-integrable, and such radii occur for genuine members of the hypothesis

   precisely the recurring "wrong claims about Lean semantics" class. Its
