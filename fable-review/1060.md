## Summary

The problem (erdosproblems.com #1060, OPEN, from problem B11 of Guy's collection
[Gu04]): let $f(n)$ count the solutions $k$ to $k\sigma(k) = n$, where $\sigma$ is the
sum-of-divisors function. Is it true that $f(n) \le n^{o(1/\log\log n)}$? Perhaps even
$f(n) \le (\log n)^{O(1)}$? The Lean file defines `countSolns n` as the number of
$k \in [1, n]$ with $k \cdot \sigma_1(k) = n$ (Mathlib's `σ 1`), then states both
conjectured bounds as direct assertions with `sorry`: the weak form as
$\forall \varepsilon > 0$, eventually $f(n) \le n^{\varepsilon/\log\log n}$, and the
strong form as $\exists C > 0$, $\forall n \ge 2$, $f(n) \le (\log n)^C$. Read back
independently, both Lean statements faithfully render the two questions in the
conjectured ("yes") direction, which is the acceptable raw-file form; I found no
semantic defects, only enrichment-level nits (missing bibliography/status metadata, now
added).

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "Let $f(n)$ count the number of
  solutions to $k\sigma(k)=n$, where $\sigma(k)$ is the sum of divisors of $k$. Is it
  true that $f(n)\leq n^{o(\frac{1}{\log\log n})}$? Perhaps even
  $\leq (\log n)^{O(1)}$?"
- `countSolns` (`conjectures/1060.lean`): the count of $k \in$ `Finset.Icc 1 n` with
  $k \cdot \sigma_1(k) = n$. Completeness of the range: for $k \ge 1$,
  $\sigma(k) \ge k \ge 1$, so any solution has $k \le k\sigma(k) = n$ (indeed
  $k \le \sqrt n$); no solution is lost by the `Icc 1 n` truncation. The excluded
  $k = 0$ (Mathlib: $\sigma(0) = 0$) solves $k\sigma(k) = n$ only for $n = 0$, where
  the intended count over positive integers is 0 = `countSolns 0` (`Icc 1 0 = ∅`).
  Exact for every $n$, not just asymptotically.
- Weak form back-translation: "for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is $N$ such that for
  all $n \ge N$, $f(n) \le n^{\varepsilon/\log\log n}$." This is precisely the
  unfolding of $f(n) \le n^{o(1/\log\log n)}$: $g(n) = o(1/\log\log n)$ iff
  $g(n)\log\log n \to 0$ iff $\forall \varepsilon > 0$ eventually
  $g(n) \le \varepsilon/\log\log n$; conversely any $f$ satisfying the eventual bounds
  has $\log_n f(n) = o(1/\log\log n)$ on the indices where $f(n) \ge 1$ and the bound
  is trivial where $f(n) = 0$. Equivalence exact.
- Strong form back-translation: "there is $C > 0$ with $f(n) \le (\log n)^C$ for all
  $n \ge 2$." The usual reading of $(\log n)^{O(1)}$ is the *eventual* bound
  $\exists C, f(n) \ll (\log n)^C$; the Lean's global-from-2 form is **equivalent**:
  the smallest $n \ge 2$ with $f(n) \ge 1$ is $n = 6 = 2\cdot\sigma(2)$ (checked:
  $f(2)=f(3)=f(4)=f(5)=0$, since $k\sigma(k) \in \{1, 6, 12, 28, 30, \dots\}$), and
  $\log 6 > 1$, so the finitely many pre-threshold indices with $f(n) \ge 1$ are
  absorbed by enlarging $C$ (each needs $C \ge \log f(n)/\log\log n$ with
  $\log\log n > 0$). No small-$n$ falsification exists.
- Not vacuous/trivial: since $k\sigma(k) = n$ forces $k \mid n$, trivially
  $f(n) \le d(n) \le n^{O(1/\log\log n)}$ (classical divisor bound), and also
  $f(n) \le \sqrt n$. The weak question asks precisely to improve the divisor-bound
  exponent from $O(1/\log\log n)$ to $o(1/\log\log n)$ — substantive, one step beyond
  the trivial bound, and the formalization captures exactly that gap. The strong form
  is stronger still (implies the weak: $C(\log\log n)^2 \le \varepsilon\log n$
  eventually).

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- No ℕ subtraction anywhere.
- ℕ division: none. Field division `ε / Real.log (Real.log n)`: for $n \ge 3$,
  $\log n > 1$ so $\log\log n > 0$ and the division is genuine. Junk indices:
  $n = 1$ gives $\log\log 1 = \log 0 = 0$ (Lean junk), so the exponent is
  $\varepsilon/0 = 0$ and the RHS is $1^0 = 1 \ge 1 =$ `countSolns 1`; $n = 2$ gives a
  negative exponent, RHS $\in (0,1)$, and `countSolns 2 = 0`; $n = 0$ gives RHS
  $0^0 = 1 \ge 0$. So the statement is not even falsified at junk indices, and in any
  case they are absorbed by the `∃ N` quantifier. Now documented in the theorem
  docstring (Addendum fix 3).
- `Real.rpow` with base `(n : ℝ) ≥ 0` and `(Real.log n) ^ C` with $C$ real: standard;
  base $\log n \ge \log 2 > 0$ for all $n \ge 2$ in the strong form.
- No `Finset.range` off-by-ones: `Icc 1 n` is the inclusive range $1 \le k \le n$
  matching the completeness argument above.
- No vacuous bounded quantifiers; "for all sufficiently large" is encoded by explicit
  `∃ N, ∀ n ≥ N` (equivalent to the `atTop` eventual filter; a raw file need not use
  filter vocabulary).
- No `StrictMono`/`Monotone` or interval-endpoint issues.
