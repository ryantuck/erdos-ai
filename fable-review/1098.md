## Summary

The problem: let $G$ be a group and $\Gamma = \Gamma(G)$ its non-commuting graph (vertices
the elements of $G$, an edge between $g$ and $h$ iff $gh \neq hg$). **If $\Gamma$ contains
no infinite complete subgraph, then is there a finite bound on the size of complete
subgraphs of $\Gamma$?** The page status is PROVED ("solved in the affirmative"): Neumann
[Ne76] proved that $\Gamma$ has no infinite complete subgraph iff the centre of $G$ has
finite index, and that centre-index $n$ forces cliques of size $\le n$. The Lean file
defines `nonCommutingGraph` correctly and its implication (no infinite clique → uniform
finite clique bound) is exactly the mathematical content asked about. The one semantic
defect is form: the source poses a **yes/no question**, but `erdos_1098` was encoded as a
**direct assertion** with the group as theorem binders — contrary to checklist A4 and this
corpus's uniform convention for solved yes/no questions (`answer(True) ↔ ∀ …`, cf.
`deepmind/308.lean`, `deepmind/1090.lean`, `deepmind/1006.lean`, `deepmind/110.lean`).
The question was already flattened to an assertion at the *raw* stage (the restyling
preserved it), and neither prior review caught it — even though the ai-review session's own
WebFetch extraction labeled the sentence "**Question:**".

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass on content (source-verified); form defect deferred to A4.**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "Let $G$ be a group and
  $\Gamma=\Gamma(G)$ be the non-commuting graph, with vertices the elements of $G$ and an
  edge between $g$ and $h$ if and only if $g$ and $h$ do not commute, $gh\neq hg$. …
  If $\Gamma$ contains no infinite complete subgraph, then is there a finite bound on the
  size of complete subgraphs of $\Gamma$?"
- Independent back-translation of the pre-fix theorem: "for every group $G$, if there is
  no infinite set that is a clique of the non-commuting graph, then there is an $n$ such
  that every finite clique has at most $n$ elements." Content-wise this is precisely the
  proposition the question asks about, in the affirmative direction Neumann proved.
- Quantifiers: the group is (implicitly) universally quantified, as in the source's "Let
  $G$ be a group"; the bound $n$ is existential *after* the hypothesis, i.e. a uniform
  bound per group — correct (a per-clique bound would be trivially true and vacuous;
  this encoding avoids that trap).
- "Infinite complete subgraph" ↔ `∃ S : Set G, S.Infinite ∧ IsClique S`; "finite bound on
  the size of complete subgraphs" ↔ `∃ n, ∀ S : Finset G, IsClique ↑S → S.card ≤ n`. Both
  are the standard, faithful encodings. Note the conclusion quantifies over `Finset`
  cliques only, but under the hypothesis that no infinite clique exists this loses
  nothing, and a uniform bound on finite cliques in fact implies no infinite clique — so
  the pair (hypothesis, conclusion) reproduces the question exactly.
- Not vacuous: the hypothesis is satisfiable (any abelian or centre-finite-index group)
  and the conclusion does not follow trivially (compactness does *not* give it: "no
  infinite clique" a priori allows unbounded finite cliques — e.g. graphs in general can
  have no infinite clique yet unbounded clique number; ruling that out for non-commuting
  graphs is the substance of Neumann's theorem). Degenerate cliques (∅, singletons) are
  harmlessly admitted with card 0/1.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- No ℕ subtraction, no division, no `Finset.range`, no indexing anywhere: the statement
  is purely set/graph-theoretic. None of the arithmetic traps can arise.
- `Set.Infinite` is `¬ S.Finite` — the correct notion; no cardinality subtleties (the
  group may be of arbitrary cardinality; `Type*` keeps it universe-polymorphic).
- `SimpleGraph.IsClique` is `S.Pairwise G.Adj`, i.e. pairwise adjacency of *distinct*
  elements — exactly "complete subgraph", and the distinctness proviso is what makes ∅
  and singletons cliques, matching graph-theoretic convention.
- No `StrictMono`/`Monotone`, filters, or interval-endpoint issues arise.
- Vacuous-quantifier check: `∀ S : Finset G, IsClique ↑S → …` has no degenerate
  first-term issue; the empty clique gives `0 ≤ n`, harmless.

**A3. Definitions — pass.**

- `nonCommutingGraph` (`deepmind/1098.lean:38-41`): `Adj g h := g * h ≠ h * g` matches
  the source. Symmetry: from `gh ≠ hg`, `Ne.symm` gives `hg ≠ gh` — correct.
  Looplessness: `g * g = g * g` refutes `Adj g g` via `hab rfl` — correct. Central
  elements are correctly isolated vertices. No `sorry` in any definition; the def is used
  consistently by the theorem.
- Degenerate inputs: the trivial group gives the empty-edged one-vertex graph; abelian
  groups give edgeless graphs — both make the hypothesis true and the conclusion true
  with $n = 1$ (cliques are subsingletons). Consistent.

**A4. Question form and answer polarity — DEFECT (fixed).**

- The source problem is a **yes/no question** ("…then is there a finite bound…?"). Per
  checklist A4 this requires the shape `answer(…) ↔ P`; the corpus convention for solved
  yes/no questions answered affirmatively is `answer(True) ↔ P` (cf. `deepmind/308.lean`,
  `deepmind/1090.lean`, `deepmind/437.lean`; with type binders inside the iff as in
  `deepmind/1006.lean`, `deepmind/110.lean`). `erdos_1098` was instead a bare implication
  — the *true direction*, so not a false statement, but the wrong encoding of a question
  per the checklist, and it erases the question/answer structure (`True` = "yes" is no
  longer recorded anywhere machine-readable). **[defect]** — fixed to
  `answer(True) ↔ ∀ (G : Type*) [Group G], (¬ ∃ …) → ∃ n, …` (see Addendum; not
  compile-verified).
- Binders before the colon: pre-fix, `(G : Type*) [Group G]` sat before the colon. For a
  bare assertion that is acceptable, but under the `answer()` recast they must move
  inside the iff (else the iff is universally quantified per-group, which is the wrong
  proposition: for a single abelian $G$ the RHS is trivially true, and
  `∀ G, (answer(True) ↔ P G)` would then wrongly force every instance to carry the
  answer). The fix moves them inside. Post-fix, `erdos_1098` is binder-free before the
  colon.
- Polarity verified by hand: RHS true ⟺ every group with no infinite non-commuting
  clique has a uniform finite clique bound ⟺ the question's answer is "yes". Status
  PROVED-affirmative ⇒ `answer(True)`. Correct.
- Where the drift entered: the *raw* file (`conjectures/1098.lean`) already stated the
  assertion ("then there is a finite bound"), i.e. the question was flattened by the
  original formalizer, not by the restyler. Direct assertion is tolerable raw style for a
  solved problem in the true direction, but the styled artifact must follow A4.

  ($g = hz$, $z$ central ⇒ $gh = hzh = hhz = hg$), so a clique meets each of the $n$
  cosets at most once — cliques have $\le n$ vertices. True as stated; no correction
  needed. Finiteness of the index is required (encoded via `index = n`, `n ≠ 0`,
  following Mathlib's convention that `index = 0` means infinite index).
