## Summary

The problem ([Va99, 1.3], status OPEN): let $x/2 < y < x$ and $C > 1$; if
$d = \max_{p_n < x}(p_{n+1} - p_n)$ is the maximal prime gap below $x$, is it true that
$\pi(y + Cd) - \pi(y) \sim Cd/\log y$? The Lean file defines `maxPrimeGap` (the max of
`Nat.nth Nat.Prime (k+1) - Nat.nth Nat.Prime k` over the indices of primes below $x$)
and asserts, for every $C > 1$ and $\varepsilon > 0$, an $\varepsilon$–$N$ statement:
for all $x \ge N$ and all natural $y$ with $x < 2y$ and $y < x$, the ratio
$(\pi(y + \lfloor Cd\rfloor) - \pi(y))/(Cd/\log y)$ lies within $\varepsilon$ of 1.
Read back independently, this is exactly the uniform asymptotic asked about, stated in
the conjectured ("yes") direction — the raw-corpus convention for open yes/no
questions. The floor is lossless at integer $y$, no ℕ-subtraction/division trap fires
on any admissible pair, and the integer-$y$ restriction is equivalent to the
real-variable reading (via Westzynthius's theorem — see A1). I found **no Part A
defects**. The one checklist failure is B1: the docstring cites `[Va99, 1.3]` but the
file defines no bibliography — fixed in `conjectures-v2/1138.lean` with the canonical
log-recovered stub. The prior ai-review is audited in Part E: its verdict stands, but
it hallucinates a "Vardi, I. / *Computational Recreations in Mathematica* (1991)"
identity for `[Va99]` and justifies the ℕ-vs-ℝ design choice with a false
supremum-equality claim.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "Let $x/2<y<x$ and $C>1$. If
  $d=\max_{p_n<x} (p_{n+1}-p_n)$, where $p_n$ denotes the $n$th prime, then is it true
  that \[\pi(y+Cd)-\pi(y)\sim\frac{Cd}{\log y}?\]" The theorem docstring
  (`conjectures/1138.lean:20-34`) reproduces it faithfully, plus an accurate paraphrase
  of the page's remarks (it drops only the RH parenthetical $h = y^{1/2+o(1)}$ —
  restored in v2).
- Independent back-translation of the theorem (lines 35–41): "for every real $C > 1$
  and $\varepsilon > 0$ there is $N$ such that for all naturals $x \ge N$ and all
  naturals $y$ with $x < 2y$ and $y < x$,
  $\bigl|\frac{\pi(y + \lfloor C d(x)\rfloor) - \pi(y)}{C d(x)/\log y} - 1\bigr| <
  \varepsilon$." This is the $\varepsilon$–$N$ unfolding of "$\pi(y+Cd) - \pi(y) \sim
  Cd/\log y$ as $x \to \infty$, uniformly over $x/2 < y < x$, for each fixed $C$" —
  the natural (and the prior review's) reading of the source, in the "yes" direction.
- `maxPrimeGap` (lines 16–18): `Finset.range (Nat.primeCounting' x)` ranges over
  exactly the indices $k$ with $p_k < x$ (Mathlib's `primeCounting'` counts primes
  *strictly below* its argument, and $p_k < x \iff k < \pi'(x)$ for the 0-indexed
  enumeration `Nat.nth Nat.Prime`, $p_0 = 2$). The summand is $p_{k+1} - p_k$, so the
  sup is precisely $d = \max_{p_n<x}(p_{n+1}-p_n)$ — including the boundary case where
  the *next* prime $p_{k+1} \ge x$, which the source's condition (on $p_n$ only)
  indeed allows.
- Indexing, verified at the first term (not just "in the limit"): source 1-indexed
  $p_1 = 2$; Lean 0-indexed `Nat.nth Nat.Prime 0 = 2`; the bijection $n = k+1$ maps
  gap-sets onto each other exactly. For $x = 3$: primes below 3 = {2}, range =
  $\{0\}$, $d = p_1 - p_0 = 3 - 2 = 1$ — correct.
- **Floor is lossless.** The numerator's argument is $y + \lfloor Cd\rfloor_{\!+}$
  (`Nat.floor`). Since $y$ is an integer and primes are integers, a prime $p$
  satisfies $p \le y + Cd$ iff $p \le \lfloor y + Cd\rfloor = y + \lfloor Cd\rfloor$
  — so `Nat.primeCounting (y + ⌊C·d⌋₊)` equals $\pi(y + Cd)$ *exactly*, not
  approximately. The denominator keeps the un-floored real $Cd/\log y$, matching the
  source. (`Nat.primeCounting n` counts primes $\le n$, matching $\pi$.)
- **Integer $y$ vs real $y$.** The source's $y$ is (in the analytic-number-theory
  idiom) a real variable; the Lean quantifies $y : \mathbb{N}$. The two readings are
  equivalent, but not for the reason the prior review gives (see E2): for real
  $y \in (n, n+1)$ the count $\#\{p : y < p \le y + Cd\}$ differs from the count at a
  neighbouring admissible integer by a bounded amount (the symmetric-difference
  intervals have length $< 1$ and contain at most one integer each), and
  $\log y/\log n \to 1$ uniformly; since $d/\log x \to \infty$ as $x \to \infty$
  (Westzynthius 1931 — unconditional), the expected count $Cd/\log y \to \infty$, so
  the bounded perturbation vanishes in the ratio. Hence uniform convergence over
  natural $y$ holds iff it holds over real $y$. Faithful; now documented in the v2
  docstring.
- Not vacuous or trivially false: for every $x \ge 3$ admissible $y$ exist (e.g.
  $x = 3, y = 2$), and the assertion is the substantive open conjecture (its truth
  would already imply strong results on primes in short intervals near maximal-gap
  scale).

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- **ℕ subtraction** (line 18): `Nat.nth Nat.Prime (k+1) - Nat.nth Nat.Prime k` never
  truncates — `Nat.nth Nat.Prime` is strictly monotone (the primes are infinite).
  The two prime counts are subtracted *after* casting to ℝ (lines 38–39), so even
  that (nonnegative anyway, $\pi$ monotone) subtraction is truncation-safe.
- **Division**: the only divisions are in ℝ. For every admissible pair the
  denominator is strictly positive: $x < 2y \wedge y < x$ forces $y \ge 2$ (both
  $y = 0$ and $y = 1$ make the constraints unsatisfiable) hence
  $\log y \ge \log 2 > 0$, and $y < x$ then forces $x \ge 3$ hence
  $\pi'(x) \ge 1$ and `maxPrimeGap x` $\ge 1$. So the division-by-zero junk value is
  *never consulted* — for $x \le 2$ the inner quantifier over $y$ is vacuous, and for
  $x \ge 3$ the denominator is positive. (Sharper than the prior review's "choose
  $N \ge 3$" — see E3.)
- **`Finset.range` off-by-one**: `range (π'(x))` = indices $0,\dots,\pi'(x)-1$ =
  exactly the primes below $x$, verified term-by-term in A1. No off-by-one.
- **Vacuous bounded quantifiers**: the inner `∀ y` is vacuous precisely for
  $x \le 2$, which is harmless under the `∃ N, ∀ x ≥ N` asymptotic form and matches
  the source's asymptotic intent.
- **"Sufficiently large" encoding**: `∃ N, ∀ x, N ≤ x → …` with the $y$-quantifier
  inside is the standard uniform $\varepsilon$–$N$ encoding of $\sim$ (uniform in
  $y$; $N$ depends only on $C, \varepsilon$). Correct.
- **Strictness**: `x < 2 * y` ⟺ $x/2 < y$ (exact over ℤ/ℝ, no ℕ-division used —
  the file correctly multiplies instead of dividing); `y < x` strict as in the
  source. `Nat.floor` on $C \cdot d \ge 0$ has no negative-input subtlety.

## E. Prior-review audit (`deepmind/ai-review/1138.md`)

The prior review's overall verdict ("mathematically correct and faithful") is right,
and most of its §6 correctness ledger checks out on re-derivation. Three substantive
findings:

1. **Hallucinated attribution presented as verification (§2).** It reports the
   citation check as "**Correct**" and asserts the website lists the reference as
   "specifically Vardi, I., Section 1.3" — false: the recovered page carries only the
   bare key `[Va99,1.3]`, with no author name anywhere. It then guesses "The full
   reference appears to be: Vardi, Ilan. *Computational Recreations in Mathematica*
   (1991)" — a fabricated identification (and self-underminingly flagged by its own
   next clause, "though on the website it is listed as `[Va99]`": a 1991 book cannot
   be a `99` key). The canonical `[Va99]` on erdosproblems.com is the **1999 Budapest
   booklet "Some of Paul's favorite problems" by "Various"** (upstream contributing
   guide, recovered from log `e5e19a56…`; consistent across sibling problems
   1068/1137/1139). The review thereby endorsed the styled file's hallucinated
   "[Va99] Vardi, I., 1.3." line (`deepmind/deepmind/1138.lean:29`), which remains
   uncorrected in that archive. Classes: hallucinated-attribution, plus verifying a
   claim against a source that does not contain it (cf. the 1136 audit's finding 4).
2. **False supremum-equality argument, right conclusion (§6, "Subtle point").** To
   justify `∀ y : ℕ` it claims "π is a step function, so the supremum of the
   deviation over real $y$ in $(x/2, x)$ equals the supremum over natural $y$." This
   is **false as stated**: the deviation depends on $y$ through the *continuous*
   factor $1/\log y$ as well, and for real $y \in (n, n+1)$ the window
   $(y, y + Cd]$ can capture one more prime than $(n, n + Cd]$ does — so the real
   supremum can strictly exceed the supremum over naturals. The *conclusion* (the
   ℕ-encoding is faithful) survives, but by a genuinely different argument: the
   perturbation is a bounded count against an expected count $Cd/\log y \to \infty$,
   which needs the nontrivial (if classical) input $d/\log x \to \infty$
   (Westzynthius 1931) — the average-gap bound alone gives only $Cd/\log y \gtrsim C$.
   This is the recurring invent-a-lemma-that-happens-to-be-unneeded pattern flagged
   in the 1000/1005/1136 audits: reasoning that would mask a real defect on a
   problem where the expected count stays bounded.
3. **Edge-case ledger incomplete, conclusions harmless (§6.5).** Its
   division-by-zero analysis ("maxPrimeGap x = 0 for x ≤ 2 … the existential ∃ N
   allows choosing N ≥ 3") is weaker than what is true — for $x \le 2$ the inner
   quantifier is *vacuous* (no admissible $y$ exists), so the junk value is never
   consulted for **any** $x$ and no choice of $N$ is needed — and it never examines
   the other zero-denominator path, $\log y = 0$ at $y = 1$ (also unreachable:
   $y \ge 2$ is forced by the constraints). Right verdict, incompletely audited.
4. **What it got right (re-derived):** the range-of-the-max analysis including the
   $p_{k+1} \ge x$ boundary case; no ℕ-subtraction underflow (strict monotonicity);
   the floor conversion (though it never notices the *exactness* at integer $y$, the
   strongest fact available); the uniform $\varepsilon$–$N$ reading; the
   $x < 2y$ encoding of $x/2 < y$; and the single-statement/no-variants call (§3),
   confirmed by the recovered page. Its §1 reuse observation is correct for the
   upstream repo and out of scope here.
5. Nothing it flagged went unfixed in `conjectures/1138.lean` (its only actionable
   suggestion, the upstream `primeGap` reuse, does not apply to this repository).
