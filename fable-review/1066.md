## Summary

The problem: $n$ points in $\mathbb{R}^2$ with all pairwise distances $\geq 1$; join two
points when their distance is exactly $1$ (a "penny/coin/minimum-distance graph", always
planar). Let $g(n)$ be maximal such that every such graph has an independent set of at
least $g(n)$ vertices. Estimate $g(n)$, or perhaps $\lim g(n)/n$ — status OPEN, with
record bounds $\frac{8}{31}n \leq g(n) \leq \frac{5}{16}n$ (Swanepoel [Sw02]; Pach–Tóth
[PaTo96]). The Lean file has no `answer()` statement: it decomposes the open estimation
problem into the two record bounds as direct assertions — `erdos_1066` (lower bound,
$\forall n \geq 1$) and `erdos_1066.variants.upper_bound` (upper bound, for all
sufficiently large $n$), both `research solved`. Read back independently, both Lean
statements encode exactly $g(n) \geq \frac{8}{31}n$ and (an eventual form of)
$g(n) \leq \frac{5}{16}n$; the encodings' directions and quantifier structures are
correct. No semantic defects found; the notable fidelity nuance is the deliberate
lower/upper asymmetry discussed in A1.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified), with one documented asymmetry.**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "Let $G$ be a graph given by $n$
  points in $\mathbb{R}^2$, where any two distinct points are at least distance $1$
  apart, and we draw an edge between two points if they are distance $1$ apart. Let
  $g(n)$ be maximal such that any such graph always has an independent set on at least
  $g(n)$ vertices. Estimate $g(n)$, or perhaps $\lim \frac{g(n)}{n}$." The module
  docstring reproduces this essentially verbatim.
- Independent back-translation of `erdos_1066` (deepmind/1066.lean): "for every
  $n \geq 1$ and every injective $f : \{0,\dots,n-1\} \to \mathbb{R}^2$ with pairwise
  distances $\geq 1$ between distinct indices, some $S \subseteq \{0,\dots,n-1\}$ has
  $|S| \geq \frac{8}{31}n$ (as reals) and no two $S$-points at distance exactly 1."
  Since $g(n) = \min_{\text{configs}} \alpha(\text{config})$, this is precisely
  $g(n) \geq \frac{8}{31}n$. Direction correct: a *lower* bound on $g$ must quantify
  universally over configurations and existentially over independent sets — it does.
- Back-translation of `variants.upper_bound`: "for all sufficiently large $n$ there is a
  valid configuration in which *every* independent set has $\leq \frac{5}{16}n$ points,"
  i.e. $\alpha(\text{config}) \leq \frac{5}{16}n$, hence $g(n) \leq \frac{5}{16}n$ for
  those $n$. Direction correct: an *upper* bound on $g$ needs one witness configuration
  and a universal claim over its independent sets — it has both, and correctly restates
  the min-distance and injectivity constraints on the witness (essential: without the
  min-distance constraint this would be problem 1070, not 1066).
- **Asymmetry note.** The page states both record bounds unqualified
  ($\frac{8}{31}n \leq g(n) \leq \frac{5}{16}n$). The Lean formalizes the lower bound
  literally for all $n \geq 1$ but hedges the upper bound to "sufficiently large $n$"
  (`∃ N₀`). The hedged form is strictly *weaker* than the page's claim, hence implied by
  it — a safe, conservative reading (padded block constructions naturally give
  $g(n) \le \frac{5}{16}n + O(1)$ rather than a clean bound at every $n$; whether
  Pach–Tóth's published theorem is per-$n$ exact is unverifiable offline). Conversely
  the $\forall n \geq 1$ lower bound takes the page at its word; I spot-checked small
  cases for literal falsity (cf. the 1004/EPS87 precedent) and found none: $n \leq 3$
  need $|S| = 1$ (trivial); $n = 4$ needs $|S| = 2$, i.e. the config is not $K_4$, and
  four points pairwise at distance exactly 1 do not exist in $\mathbb{R}^2$. No
  counterexample; whether Swanepoel's paper proves the clean $\forall n$ form or an
  asymptotic one is DEFERRED (literature unreachable). Both choices are defensible;
  neither is a defect against the recovered source.
- Indexing: `Fin n` indices vs. the source's unindexed point sets — injectivity
  (`hf_inj`) makes indices faithful proxies for points; `hf_min` alone already forces
  injectivity (distance $\geq 1 > 0$), so `hf_inj` is redundant but harmless and
  clarifying.
- Not vacuous/trivial: both statements are substantive quantitative claims; the lower
  bound is not satisfiable by degenerate witnesses ($\emptyset$ fails the cardinality
  bound for $n \geq 4$), and the upper bound's witness must be a genuinely extremal
  construction.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- No ℕ subtraction, no ℕ division anywhere. `8 / 31 * (n : ℝ)` and
  `5 / 16 * (n : ℝ)` are real-field divisions of nonzero literals — exact.
- Cardinality comparisons are made after casting to ℝ (`(S.card : ℝ) ≥ …`), so
  $|S| \geq \frac{8}{31}n$ means $|S| \geq \lceil \frac{8n}{31} \rceil$ — the intended
  reading.
- No `Finset.range`, no off-by-one exposure.
- Vacuous quantifiers: for $S = \emptyset$ or singleton the independence condition
  `∀ i ∈ S, ∀ j ∈ S, i ≠ j → …` is vacuously true — correct (small sets are
  independent). `hn : n ≥ 1` excludes $n = 0$; the statement would anyway be trivially
  true there ($S = \emptyset$, $0 \geq 0$), so the guard is harmless conservatism.
- No filters/limits, no `StrictMono`/`Monotone`, no interval-endpoint issues. Strictness
  audit: hypothesis distances $\geq 1$ (non-strict, per source "at least distance 1");
  adjacency `= 1`; independence `≠ 1` — since all pairwise distances are $\geq 1$,
  `≠ 1` coincides with `> 1`, matching both the graph-theoretic notion and the
  "minimum distance $> 1$" phrasing of the higher-dimensional letter version.

**A3. Definitions — pass (trivially).**

- No custom `def`/`abbrev`/`structure`; both statements inline the configuration and
  independence conditions. No `sorry` outside proof bodies.

**A4. Question form and answer polarity — pass.**

- The source problem is an open-ended *estimation* request ("Estimate $g(n)$, or
  perhaps $\lim g(n)/n$") — neither a yes/no question nor a value request with a known
  value; there is no specific "in particular, is it true that…" question in the problem
  box (unlike 1005 or 1070). Decomposing into direct assertions of the two record
  bounds (Case 3, no `answer()`) is the same modeling choice this corpus made for 1070
  and is appropriate; the un-encoded open core is noted below (B2).
- No binders before the colon interact with any `answer()` (the pre-fix file has no
  `answer()` at all; the newly added open variant is binder-free — see Addendum).

**A5. Solution status — pass (source-verified).**

- Recovered page banner: **OPEN** (tooltip: "This is open, and cannot be resolved with
  a finite computation."), with the owner's standard disclaimer. The *problem* is open,
  but both formalized statements are proved results, so `@[category research solved]`
  on each is correct — the same reasoning as `reviews/1066.md` §6/§7 and the 1070
  precedent. The docstrings attribute the bounds correctly (Swanepoel lower, Pach–Tóth

## E. Prior-review audit (`ai-review/1066.md`)

Accurate on the Lean semantics; the scholarship section had one hit, one miss, and
several small inaccuracies:

1. **Half-caught citation corruption.** The pre-fix file (recovered from logs) cited
   [Sw02] as *"Unit distances and diameters in Euclidean spaces"*, DCG 28 (2002),
   351–370 and [PaTo96] as *"How many unit equilateral triangles can be generated by n
   points in convex position?"*. Both entries pointed at **wrong papers** (real titles,
   wrong works). The prior review flagged only the PaTo96 title as "may be imprecise"
   and did **not** flag Sw02 at all — despite reproducing the suspicious entry
   verbatim in its own §2. The authoritative `/latex/1066` data (recovered from the
   pipeline logs) shows both were wrong: Sw02 is *Independence numbers of planar
   contact graphs*, 649–670; PaTo96 is *On the independence number of coin graphs*,
   Geombinatorics 6, 30–33. An upstream fix session subsequently corrected **both**
   (its diff and commit message are preserved in the logs), which is why
   `deepmind/1066.lean` is clean today — but the review itself missed half the defect
   it was best positioned to catch.
2. **Attribution imprecision.** It repeatedly credits the $\frac{6}{19}n$ construction
   (and the disproof of $g(n) = n/3$) to "Chung, Graham, and Pach" as a trio; the page
   says "Chung and Graham, and *independently* Pach". Likewise its parenthetical
   "(Pach's observation)" on the $n/4$ bound garbles the page: Pollack noted the
   four-colour-theorem implication; Pach's observation was that four-colourability of
   these graphs has a simple inductive proof.
3. **Correct claims, verified.** Its Lean-semantics claims all check out on
   re-derivation: `hf_inj` redundant given `hf_min` (distance $\geq 1 > 0$ forces
   distinct images); the $n = 0$ case vacuously true (empty finset, $0 \geq 0$), so
   `hn` is mere conservatism; the upper bound correctly re-imposes injectivity and
   min-distance on the witness; the 1066-vs-1070 distinction (min-distance packing vs.
   arbitrary placements) correctly identified; `UnitDistancePlaneGraph`/`indepNum`
   reuse claims verified from logs (§C2). No invented-then-excused discrepancies of
   the 1000/1005 pattern were found in its correctness section.
4. **Unaddressed recommendations.** Its recommendation to add historical context and
   the 1070 connection to the module docstring was never applied to
   `deepmind/1066.lean` (only the citation fixes were) — now done (Addendum). Its
   variant suggestions (n/4 bound, ℝ^d generalisation) were likewise unapplied — now
   done; its "limit exists in $[\frac{8}{31}, \frac{5}{16}]$" suggestion is sound but
   requires defining $g$ (see B2) and is left as future work. One nuance it missed:
   the limit's *existence* is provable outright (Fekete subadditivity), so only the
   value is open — a variant asserting existence should be `research solved`-style,
   not open.
