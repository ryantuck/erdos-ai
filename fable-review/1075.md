## Summary

The problem (erdosproblems.com/1075, OPEN, tag: hypergraphs, source [Er74c, p.80]): let
$r \geq 3$; there exists $c_r > r^{-r}$ such that for any $\varepsilon > 0$, if $n$ is
sufficiently large, any $r$-uniform hypergraph on $n$ vertices with at least
$(1+\varepsilon)(n/r)^r$ edges contains a subgraph on $m$ vertices with at least $c_r m^r$
edges, where $m = m(n) \to \infty$ as $n \to \infty$. The Lean (`deepmind/1075.lean:62`)
states: $\forall r \geq 3$, $\exists c_r > r^{-r}$, $\forall \varepsilon > 0$,
$\forall M$, $\exists N_0$, $\forall n \geq N_0$, every $r$-uniform hypergraph on `Fin n`
with $\geq (1+\varepsilon)(n/r)^r$ edges (real arithmetic) has a vertex set $S$ with
$|S| \geq M$ and $\geq c_r |S|^r$ induced edges. Read back independently, this matches the
source: the $\forall M\,\exists N_0$ encoding of "$m(n) \to \infty$" is the standard and
correct one, and the induced-subgraph reading of "subgraph on $m$ vertices" is equivalent to
the general-subgraph reading for this existence claim. A solved variant capturing Erdős's
1964 theorem ($c_r = r^{-r}$ under an $\varepsilon n^r$ edge hypothesis) is present; its
Lean statement is faithful to the page's remark, but its docstring (inherited from the prior
review) mischaracterized the $\varepsilon n^r$ threshold as "strengthened" when for small
$\varepsilon$ it is *weaker* than $(1+\varepsilon)(n/r)^r$ — fixed.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "Let $r\geq 3$. There exists
  $c_r>r^{-r}$ such that, for any $\epsilon>0$, if $n$ is sufficiently large, the following
  holds. Any $r$-uniform hypergraph on $n$ vertices with at least $(1+\epsilon)(n/r)^r$ many
  edges contains a subgraph on $m$ vertices with at least $c_rm^r$ edges, where
  $m=m(n)\to \infty$ as $n\to \infty$." The module docstring reproduces this faithfully.
- Independent back-translation of lines 62–71 agrees with the source. Quantifier order is
  right: $c_r$ depends only on $r$ (chosen before $\varepsilon$ and $M$); $N_0$ depends on
  $\varepsilon$ and $M$.
- "$m = m(n) \to \infty$" encoding: `∀ M, ∃ N₀, ∀ n ≥ N₀, … S.card ≥ M` says that for each
  target $M$ the guarantee eventually produces a subgraph on $\geq M$ vertices. This is
  equivalent to the existence of a function $m_\varepsilon(n) \to \infty$ (take, for each
  $n$, the largest $M$ with $N_0(M) \le n$, with $N_0$ monotone WLOG) such that eventually
  every qualifying hypergraph has a subgraph on $\geq m_\varepsilon(n)$ vertices of the
  required density. The source leaves open whether $m(n)$ may depend on $\varepsilon$; the
  encoding takes the natural per-$\varepsilon$ reading. Faithful.
- "Subgraph on $m$ vertices" is encoded as the induced subhypergraph on a chosen $S$. For a
  lower bound on edge count these are **equivalent** (not merely "standard"): any subgraph
  on vertex set $S$ has at most as many edges as the induced one, and the induced one is
  itself a subgraph — so a dense subgraph on $S$ exists iff the induced subgraph on $S$ is
  dense. No fidelity loss either way. (Cf. Part E on the prior review's garbled version of
  this argument.)
- Not vacuous, not trivial: for a fixed admissible $c_r = r^{-r} + \delta$, the full-vertex
  witness $S = \mathrm{univ}$ settles only $\varepsilon \geq \delta r^r$ (then
  $(1+\varepsilon)r^{-r} \geq c_r$); for small $\varepsilon$ the statement has real
  content, exactly as in the source. The hypothesis is satisfiable for large $n$ since
  $\binom{n}{r} \sim n^r/r! > (1+\varepsilon) n^r/r^r$ for small $\varepsilon$
  ($r! < r^r$).
- Variant `erdos_1075.variants.erdos_1964` (lines 80+, renamed — see Addendum): faithful to
  the page remark "Erdős [Er64f] proved that this is true with $c_r=r^{-r}$ whenever the
  graph has at least $\epsilon n^r$ many edges." It follows from Erdős's 1964 theorem
  (positive-density $r$-uniform hypergraphs contain complete $r$-partite subhypergraphs
  $K^{(r)}_r(t,\dots,t)$ for any prescribed $t$): the $rt$-vertex part-union $S$ carries
  $\geq t^r = r^{-r}(rt)^r$ induced edges, and $rt \geq M$ for $t$ large. The
  `research solved` tag is justified.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- **ℕ subtraction:** none anywhere.
- **Division:** all division is in ℝ. `(n : ℝ) / r` and `1 / (r : ℝ) ^ r` have positive
  denominators under `hr : r ≥ 3`. No ℕ flooring.
- **`Finset.range`:** not used; no off-by-one surface.
- **Vacuous/degenerate cases:** at `n = 0` the edge hypothesis `0 ≥ (1+ε)·0^r` holds (with
  `r ≥ 3`, so `((0:ℝ)/r)^r = 0`) while no `S : Finset (Fin 0)` has `S.card ≥ M` for
  `M ≥ 1`; this is harmless because `N₀` is existentially chosen (any prover takes
  `N₀ ≥ 1`). At `M = 0`, `S = ∅` works (`0 ≥ c_r · 0^r = 0` since `r ≥ 3 > 0`), matching
  the encoding's intent that only large `M` matter. `uniform : ∀ e ∈ edges, e.card = r` is
  vacuous for `edges = ∅` — a legitimate hypergraph.
- **Filter/limit encodings:** the `∀ M, ∃ N₀` idiom correctly captures both "sufficiently
  large $n$" and "$m(n) \to \infty$" (analysis in A1); no `Tendsto` needed.
- **Strictness:** `c_r > 1 / (r:ℝ)^r` is strict, as the source's $c_r > r^{-r}$ requires —
  this strictness is the entire content of the problem (non-strict is [Er64f]). `≥` on the
  edge bounds matches "at least". `S.card ≥ M` non-strict, correct.

**A3. Definitions — pass.**

## E. Prior-review audit (`ai-review/1075.md`)

Its quantifier-by-quantifier table (§6) is correct, and its two actionable flags (missing
citations; missing [Er64f] variant) were both subsequently applied to `deepmind/1075.lean`.
Three substantive findings:

1. **False threshold comparison, propagated into the artifact.** §3 asserts the [Er64f]
   variant has a "stronger edge hypothesis $\varepsilon n^r \gg (1+\varepsilon)(n/r)^r$".
   This is false in general: $\varepsilon n^r > (1+\varepsilon)(n/r)^r$ iff
   $\varepsilon > 1/(r^r-1)$, and the interesting regime of [Er64f] is precisely small
   $\varepsilon$, where its hypothesis is *weaker* (it covers hypergraphs far sparser
   than the conjecture's threshold — that breadth is the strength of the 1964 theorem;
   its weakness is the conclusion constant). The mistaken direction was inherited by the
   deepmind variant docstring as "threshold is strengthened to $\varepsilon n^r$" — the
   one defect this review found and fixed. Same failure class as 1000's
   invented-then-excused discrepancy: an unexamined asymptotic comparison stated with
   confidence.
2. **Backwards implication in the induced-subgraph justification.** §5 argues the induced
   interpretation is "mathematically stronger (finding $c_r m^r$ edges in the induced
   subgraph implies finding them in any sub-hypergraph)". The parenthetical implication is
   backwards — edges found in the induced subgraph need not lie in an arbitrary
   sub-hypergraph; the true relationship is that the induced subgraph *maximizes* edge
   count over all subgraphs on the same vertex set, making the two readings of the
   existence claim **equivalent** (neither strictly stronger). Right conclusion (induced
   encoding is correct), unsound argument.
3. **Minor.** Its line references (structure "at line 35", `inducedSubgraph` "line 40")
   match the upstream `FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/1075.lean` copy (recovered in the
   formal-conjectures session log), not this repo's files — consistent, once the target is
   identified. Its proposed variant name `erdos_1075.variants.erdos_1964` was applied
   upstream as `erdos_1075_erdos_1964` — the convention deviation is repaired by this
   review's rename.

What it got right: the reuse survey (corroborated by the log-captured repo survey), the
[Er64f] bibliographic data, the correctness table, and the observation that real division
handles $r \nmid n$.
