## Summary

The problem: let $1 = d_1 < \cdots < d_{\tau(n)} = n$ be the divisors of $n$ and, for
$\alpha > 1$, $h_\alpha(n) = \sum_i (d_{i+1}/d_i - 1)^\alpha$. Is it true that
$\liminf_{n\to\infty} h_\alpha(n) \ll_\alpha 1$? The recovered page's banner is
**PROVED** ("This has been solved in the affirmative") — by Vose [Vo84] via an explicit
sequence — while it remains open whether the candidates $n!$ and
$\mathrm{lcm}\{1,\ldots,n\}$ themselves have bounded $h_\alpha$. The Lean file defines
the sorted divisor list and $h_\alpha$ (real `rpow` over consecutive-pair ratios), states
the main question as `answer(True) ↔ ∀ α > 1, ∃ C, ∀ N, ∃ n ≥ N, hAlpha α n ≤ C`, and
adds two open `answer(sorry)` variants for the $n!$ and lcm candidates. Read back
independently, the Lean and the English agree at every point I checked; the only errors
found were scholarly (the [Er81h] bibliography entry carried the wrong paper title) plus
enrichment gaps (the page's third remark — the $\sum d_{i+1}/d_i$ inequality and Erdős's
follow-up liminf question — was absent). Notably, the page's "easy to see" inequality
$\sum_i d_{i+1}/d_i > \tau(n) + \log n$ is **literally false** for
$n \in \{2,3,4,6,8,12,24\}$ (verified by exhaustive computation to $3\times 10^5$); per
the 1004/EPS87 precedent the corrected bound ($\tau(n)-1$ in place of $\tau(n)$) was
formalized with the counterexamples documented.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "Let $1=d_1<\cdots<d_{\tau(n)}=n$
  be the divisors of $n$, and for $\alpha>1$ let
  $h_\alpha(n) = \sum_i \left( \frac{d_{i+1}}{d_i}-1\right)^\alpha.$ Is it true that
  $\liminf_{n\to \infty}h_\alpha(n) \ll_\alpha 1?$"
- Independent back-translation of `deepmind/1099.lean:78-79`: "True iff for every real
  $\alpha > 1$ there is a real $C$ such that every $N$ admits $n \ge N$ with
  $h_\alpha(n) \le C$." That is exactly "for every $\alpha>1$,
  $\liminf_{n\to\infty} h_\alpha(n) < \infty$", which is the Vinogradov
  "$\liminf \ll_\alpha 1$" with the constant allowed to depend on $\alpha$ — the ∃C
  correctly sits inside the ∀α. Equivalence of "∃C bounded infinitely often" with
  "liminf < ∞" holds since $h_\alpha \ge 0$: liminf ≤ C gives infinitely many
  $n$ with $h_\alpha(n) \le C+1$, and conversely.
- Indexing: `ds.zip ds.tail` pairs $(d_1,d_2),\ldots,(d_{\tau-1},d_\tau)$ — exactly the
  source's $i = 1,\ldots,\tau(n)-1$ terms, term-by-term. The page's remark "the liminf
  is trivially $\ge 1$, considering the term $i=1$" checks out against the encoding:
  the first pair is $(1, p)$ with $p$ the least prime factor, contributing
  $(p-1)^\alpha \ge 1$.
- Not vacuous: only $n \in \{0,1\}$ give degenerate $h_\alpha(n)=0$ (empty/singleton
  divisor list), and the `∀ N, ∃ n ≥ N` shape prevents those finitely many degenerate
  values from witnessing the statement; primes give $h_\alpha(p) = (p-1)^\alpha \to
  \infty$, so the claim is substantive.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- No ℕ subtraction anywhere: `(p.2 : ℝ) / (p.1 : ℝ) - 1` subtracts in ℝ after casting.
- Division: divisors of $n \ge 1$ are $\ge 1$ (Mathlib's `Nat.divisors` excludes 0), so
  `p.1 ≠ 0` in every generated pair; $n = 0$ yields the empty list. No flooring, no
  division-by-zero.
- Real exponent `^ α` is `Real.rpow`; the base $d_{i+1}/d_i - 1$ is strictly positive
  because `Finset.sort (· ≤ ·)` of the (distinct-element) divisors Finset is strictly
  increasing — so no negative-base `rpow` junk arises.
- `Finset.Icc 1 n` in the lcm variant is $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ inclusive — correct; at
  $n = 0$ it is empty and `Finset.lcm ∅ id = 1`, giving $h_\alpha(1) = 0 \le C$,
  harmless. `n.factorial` at $n \in \{0,1\}$ gives $h_\alpha(1) = 0$, harmless.
- "Infinitely many" is encoded elementarily (`∀ N, ∃ n ≥ N ∧ …`), standard and correct;
  no filter machinery needed.
- No `range` off-by-ones, no `StrictMono`/`Monotone` or interval-endpoint issues arise.

**B2. Variants and completeness — pass; enrichment applied.**

- The problem is single-question; the main statement plus the two page-named candidate
  sequences ($n!$, lcm) were already captured — nothing from the problem box is
  missing.
- The page's remarks contain two further formalizable statements, both previously
  absent (the prior review dismissed them as "supplementary context"):
  1. the "easy to see" bound $\sum_i d_{i+1}/d_i > \tau(n) + \log n$ — **literally
     false at small $n$**: fails exactly for $n \in \{2,3,4,6,8,12,24\}$ up to
     $3\times10^5$ (e.g. $n=2$: sum $=2$ vs $2+\log 2 \approx 2.69$). The provably
     correct easy bound is $\sum_i d_{i+1}/d_i > \tau(n) - 1 + \log n$ for $n \ge 2$
     (from $r-1 > \log r$ termwise and $\sum \log r_i = \log n$ telescoping over
     $\tau(n)-1$ terms). Per the 1004/EPS87 precedent, the corrected version was
     formalized (`erdos_1099.variants.ratio_sum_lower`) with the counterexamples
     documented in its docstring.
  2. Erdős's follow-up question whether
     $\liminf (\sum_i d_{i+1}/d_i - \tau(n) - \log n) < \infty$, which the page says
     "would follow from an affirmative answer to the main question" — an answer Vose
     supplied, so this is *solved affirmatively*. Derivation re-checked by hand:
     $\sum_i r_i - \tau(n) - \log n = \sum_i (r_i - 1 - \log r_i) - 1 \le h_2(n) - 1$
     using $r - 1 - \log r \le (r-1)^2$ for $r \ge 1$ (monotone: its derivative is
     $(2r-1)(r-1)/r \ge 0$), so the $\alpha = 2$ case of Vose's theorem bounds it
     infinitely often. Added as `erdos_1099.variants.ratio_sum_liminf`,
     `answer(True)`, research solved.
- The docstring's cross-reference to problem #673 is accurate: `deepmind/673.lean:26`
  confirms $G(n) = \sum_{1\le i<\tau(n)} d_i/d_{i+1}$, as the 1099 docstring states.
- No OEIS references appear on the recovered page (none to add).
