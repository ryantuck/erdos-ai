## Summary

The problem (source page recovered from session logs, accessed 2026-02-22): let
$f(t)$ ($= f(t,n)$) be minimal such that any 2-colouring of the edges of $K_n$ admits
vertex-disjoint monochromatic copies of $K_t$ (colours may differ) covering all but at
most $f$ vertices; estimate $f(t)$ — in particular, does $f(t)^{1/t} \to 1$, and is
$f(t) \ll t$? Status: SOLVED ("resolved in some other way than a proof or disproof").
The Lean file defines `minLeftover t n` (an `sInf` encoding of $f(t,n)$, correct) and a
bespoke Ramsey number, then asserts the page's Burr–Erdős–Spencer formula
$f(t,n) = R(t,t-1) + x$, $x \equiv n+1-R \pmod t$, plus a Moon variant
$f(3,n) = 4$ for all $n \ge 8$. **Both statements are provably false**: every family of
disjoint monochromatic $K_t$'s covers a multiple of $t$ vertices, so
`minLeftover t n ≡ n (mod t)` unconditionally, while the asserted RHS is
$\equiv n+1 \pmod t$ — the two sides are never equal for $t \ge 2$; and
`minLeftover 3 n = 4` forces $n \equiv 1 \pmod 3$, so it fails at e.g. $n = 9$. The
page's printed formula is itself internally inconsistent (off by one); the corrected
form $f(t,n) = R(t,t-1) - 1 + x$ passes every available cross-check and has been applied.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — DEFECT (both theorems), otherwise faithful.**

- Recovered verbatim source statement: "Let $f(t)$ be minimal such that, in any
  two-colouring of the edges of $K_n$, the edges can be partitioned into vertex
  disjoint monochromatic copies of $K_t$ (not necessarily the same colour) with at
  most $f(t)$ vertices remaining. Estimate $f(t)$. In particular, is it true that
  $f(t)^{1/t}\to 1$? Is it true that $f(t)\ll t$?" The site's "the edges can be
  partitioned … with vertices remaining" does not literally parse; the file's
  vertex-cover reading (following Moon's "Disjoint triangles in chromatic graphs") is
  the standard interpretation and is correct. Now noted explicitly in the docstring.
- Back-translation of the (pre-fix) `erdos_1015` (`deepmind/1015.lean:93-96`): "for
  $t \ge 3$ and all sufficiently large $n$, the minimum leftover equals
  $R(t,t-1) + ((n+1-R) \bmod t)$." This matches the page's *printed* remark verbatim —
  and is exactly why it is false. **Unconditional refutation:** for any symmetric
  colouring $c$, any admissible family covers $t \cdot |{\rm family}|$ vertices (the
  cliques have card $t$ and are pairwise disjoint), so every achievable leftover is
  $n - t k$; hence $\min_{\rm family}$ leftover $\equiv n \pmod t$ for each $c$, and
  `minLeftover t n` — the max of these over $c$ (the `sInf` set is nonempty and
  upward-closed, see A3) — is $\equiv n \pmod t$. The RHS is
  $\equiv R + (n+1-R) = n+1 \pmod t$ for all $n \ge R - 1$. Since $t \ge 3 \nmid 1$,
  LHS ≠ RHS for **every** $n \ge R-1$, so no $N_0$ exists. **[defect] — fixed** to the
  $R(t,t-1) - 1 + x$ form (see Addendum for the justification of this particular
  correction; not compile-verified).
- `erdos_1015_moon` (pre-fix line 105): `∀ n ≥ 8, minLeftover 3 n = 4`. By the same
  congruence, `minLeftover 3 9 ∈ {0, 3, 6, 9}`, never 4. **[defect] — fixed** to the
  faithful reading of the page's "$f(3)=4$, at least for $n\geq 8$": 4 is the *least
  uniform bound* over $n \ge 8$ (an unfolded `IsLeast`, using only basic quantifiers).
- Quantifier structure otherwise fine: $t$ as a binder with `∃ N₀, ∀ n ≥ N₀` correctly
  renders "for $n$ sufficiently large depending on $t$"; direct-assertion form (no
  `answer()`) is right for the solved BES75 formula.
- Not vacuous post-fix: the corrected equality pins `minLeftover` to a specific
  residue-class representative; the Moon statement asserts a nontrivial least bound.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass post-fix (the pre-fix failure was mathematical, not
a Lean trap).**

- ℕ subtraction `n + 1 - ramseyNumber₂ t (t-1)`: truncates for small $n$, but any
  witnessing $N_0$ must exceed $R$ for the formula to hold on a full residue system,
  and `∃ N₀` quantifies away the truncation region. Harmless (both prior reviews got
  this right). New subtraction `ramseyNumber₂ t (t-1) - 1` introduced by the fix:
  exact, since for $t \ge 3$ the set defining $R(t,t-1)$ excludes $N = 0$ (`Fin 0` has
  no subset of card $t \ge 3$ or $t-1 \ge 2$), so $R \ge 1$; documented in the
  docstring.
- `%` precedence: `(n + 1 - R) % t` is explicitly parenthesized; `R - 1 + (…) % t`
  parses as `(R-1) + ((…) % t)` since `%` binds tighter than `+`. Correct.
- No `range` off-by-ones, no vacuous bounded quantifiers (the empty `cliques` family
  makes the `sInf` set nonempty at $r = n$, which is intended, not degenerate).
- `sInf` on ℕ: both defining sets are upward-closed (larger $r$ trivially; larger $N$
  by restricting a colouring of `Fin (N+1)` to the first $N$ vertices and re-embedding
  the monochromatic witness), so `sInf` is the intended minimum. `ramseyNumber₂`'s set
  is nonempty by Ramsey's theorem (statement-level fact; fine).
- Degenerate cases: `ramseyNumber₂ 2 1 = 1` and `ramseyNumber₂ 3 2 = 3` under the
  definition (checked by hand), matching the standard conventions the formula needs.
- Strict/non-strict and `Bool` colour symmetry (`∀ i j, c i j = c j i`) all correct;
  `c i i` is unconstrained but harmless behind the `i ≠ j` guard.

**A3. Definitions — pass.**

- `IsMonoClique` matches its docstring; the `i ≠ j` guard correctly excludes the
  diagonal. With the symmetry hypothesis at every use site, "clique of card $t$, all
  edges colour $b$" is exactly a monochromatic $K_t$.
- `minLeftover` matches its docstring ("smallest $r$ such that …"): the set is
  nonempty ($r = n$ via the empty family) and upward-closed, so `sInf` is the true
  minimum $\max_c \min_{\rm family}({\rm leftover})$. The congruence
  `minLeftover t n ≡ n (mod t)` — the crux of this review — is now recorded in its
  docstring. No `sorry` in any definition.
- `ramseyNumber₂` matches its docstring; colour-asymmetric ($s$↔`false`, $t$↔`true`)
  but equivalent to the symmetric convention by complementation.

**A4. Question form and answer polarity — acceptable, one enrichment applied.**

- The page couples a soft value request ("Estimate $f(t)$") with two yes/no
  sub-questions, and is SOLVED via the BES75 determination. Formalizing the BES75
  formula as a direct assertion (`research solved`, no `answer()`) is a legitimate
  encoding of the resolution. However the two sub-questions *as asked* were entirely
  absent — and the styled docstring had dropped them (see drift check). The
  $f(t) \ll t$ question is now formalized as
  `erdos_1015.variants.linear_bound : answer(False) ↔ ∃ C, ∀ t ≥ 3, ∃ N₀, ∀ n ≥ N₀,
  minLeftover t n ≤ C * t` — polarity checked by hand: RHS true would mean "yes,
  $f(t) \ll t$"; the answer is no (eventually $f(t,n) \ge R(t,t-1) - 1$, exponential
  in $t$), so `answer(False)` with a false RHS is correct. No binders before the
  colon. The $f(t)^{1/t} \to 1$ question needs real limits/roots not present in the
  file and is left as a noted omission (deferred).

**Justification of the applied formula correction** (this is the load-bearing
judgment): the page's printed $f = R(t,t-1) + x$, $x \equiv n+1-R \pmod t$ gives
$f \equiv n+1 \pmod t$, contradicting the forced $f(t,n) \equiv n \pmod t$. Candidate
minimal corrections: (i) drop the "+1" in the congruence ($f = R + x'$,
$x' \equiv n - R$): restores the congruence but gives $\max_n f(3,n) = R(3,2)+2 = 5$,
contradicting Moon's $f(3) = 4$; (ii) keep the page's congruence datum and subtract
one from the constant ($f = R - 1 + x$): restores the congruence, gives
$\max_n f(3,n) = 4$ (= Moon), and at $t = 2$ yields $f(2,n) = R(2,1) - 1 + (n \bmod 2)
= n \bmod 2$, which is *exactly* the directly computable truth (every edge of $K_n$
is monochromatic; greedily match). Correction (ii) is the unique candidate passing
all three independent checks and is what was applied — with the caveat, stated in the
docstring and here, that [BES75] itself could not be consulted; if the paper's exact
statement differs in presentation, the formalized value $f(t,n) \equiv n \pmod t$ in
$[R(t,t-1)-1,\, R(t,t-1)+t-2]$ is the only candidate consistent with the page's data.
