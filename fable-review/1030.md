## Summary

The problem (Erdős and Sós, [Er93, p. 339]): if $R(k,l)$ is the Ramsey number, prove the
existence of some $c > 0$ such that $\lim_k R(k+1,k)/R(k,k) > 1 + c$. Status on the
recovered page: **OPEN**. The Lean file defines the classical two-colour Ramsey number
`ramseyR k l` via `sInf` over $\{N \mid$ every simple graph on `Fin N` has a $k$-clique
or an independent $l$-set$\}$, and states the conjecture as
$\exists c > 0, \exists K_0, \forall k \ge K_0,\ R(k+1,k)/R(k,k) \ge 1+c$ — exactly
$\liminf_k R(k+1,k)/R(k,k) > 1$, the standard reading of the informal "lim". A second
theorem formalizes the page's explicitly-stated weaker open question
($\exists c > 1$ with $R(k+1,k)-R(k,k) > k^c$ eventually). Read back independently, the
Lean and the English agree; the only defect found is bibliographic: the module
docstring's full entry for [Er93] named the wrong paper (a 1996 volume spanning pages
97–132, irreconcilable with the cited p. 339).

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "If $R(k,l)$ is the Ramsey number
  then prove the existence of some $c>0$ such that $\lim_k \frac{R(k+1,k)}{R(k,k)} >
  1+c$." The module docstring reproduces it faithfully.
- Independent back-translation of `erdos_1030` (lines 69–73 pre-fix): "there exist
  $c > 0$ and $K_0$ such that for every $k \ge K_0$, $R(k+1,k)/R(k,k) \ge 1+c$ (real
  division of casts)." With the existential over $c$, this is *exactly equivalent* to
  $\liminf_k R(k+1,k)/R(k,k) > 1$: any such $c$ forces $\liminf \ge 1+c > 1$;
  conversely $\liminf = 1 + 2c > 1$ gives eventually $\ge 1+c$. The informal "lim" is
  Erdős's usual shorthand; if it were read as demanding the limit exist, the
  formalization is the standard weaker (liminf) reading, and the docstring is
  transparent about the reformulation. Not a defect; docstring note added (Addendum).
- `ramseyR k l` = least $N$ such that every `G : SimpleGraph (Fin N)` has
  `¬G.CliqueFree k` (a $k$-clique exists) or `¬Gᶜ.CliqueFree l` (an independent
  $l$-set exists). This is the classical $R(k,l)$: verified at the boundary,
  `ramseyR 1 l = 1` for $l \ge 1$ (the one-vertex graph has a 1-clique, the
  zero-vertex graph has neither), `ramseyR 2 2 = 2`, `ramseyR 3 2 = 3`. `sInf` of the
  set is the least witness, which is the definition of $R$; no upward-closure argument
  is even needed for that.
- The page's $R(k+1,k)$ vs the file's `ramseyR (k+1) k` (clique $k{+}1$ / independent
  $k$): the two-colour Ramsey number is symmetric, and the file's convention is applied
  consistently across both theorems and the def docstring. No mismatch.
- Not vacuous: $R(k+1,k) \ge R(k,k)$ always, so the content is precisely
  "bounded away from ratio 1", a substantive open strengthening; the $\forall k \ge K_0$
  form cannot be satisfied by junk values (see A2).

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- No ℕ subtraction: the weak variant subtracts *after* casting to ℝ
  (`(ramseyR (k+1) k : ℝ) - (ramseyR k k : ℝ)`), so no truncation.
- Division: real division of casts. `ramseyR k k = 0` only at $k = 0$
  (`ramseyR 1 1 = 1`), where `x / 0 = 0` yields `0 ≥ 1 + c` — *false*, so the junk
  value cannot fabricate a witness; $K_0 \ge 1$ is forced, matching the intended
  asymptotic claim. Harmless in the correct direction.
- Degenerate `ramseyR`: for $k = 0$ or $l = 0$, `CliqueFree 0` is false in Mathlib
  (the empty set is a 0-clique), so the defining set is all of ℕ and `ramseyR` returns
  0. Only $k = 0$ is affected in the theorems, and it is excluded as above.
- `(k : ℝ) ^ c` in the weak variant is real `rpow`; at $k = 0$, $0^c = 0$ for $c > 1$
  — harmless, and again behind $K_0$.
- No `Finset.range`, no bounded quantifiers with degenerate first terms, no
  `StrictMono`/`Monotone` issues. The eventually-quantifier ($\exists K_0 \forall k \ge
  K_0$) is the correct "for all sufficiently large" encoding.

**A3. Definitions — pass.**

- `ramseyR` matches its docstring and the source's $R(k,l)$, including degenerate
  inputs (analyzed above). No `sorry` in any `def`. The def is byte-identical between
  `conjectures/1030.lean` and `deepmind/1030.lean` and is used consistently by both
  theorems.

**A4. Question form and answer polarity — pass.**

- The source is a **direct assertion** ("…then prove the existence of some $c>0$…"),
  so Case 3 applies: bare proposition, no `answer()` wrapper. That is what the file
  does (contrast with the 1005 defect, where a genuine question was wrongly asserted —
  here the checklist call in `reviews/1030.md` is correct against the recovered
  source text).
- The weak variant is likewise stated as the bare existence assertion; the page frames
  it as the statement Erdős–Sós "could not even prove", i.e. the open assertion in the
  positive direction. "For any $c>1$" is the idiomatic "for at least one $c>1$"
  (an "∀c>1" reading would make "even weaker" false framing; and the main conjecture
  implies the ∃-form via $R(k,k) \ge 2^{k/2}$, confirming "weaker"). Encoding correct.
- No binders before the colon on the main theorem; `answer()` does not occur in the
  file (correctly).

**A5. Solution status — pass (source-verified).**

## E. Prior-review audit (`ai-review/1030.md`)

Mostly accurate on the Lean semantics; findings:

1. **Endorsed an unverifiable citation that was in fact wrong.** §2 states the [Er93]
   reference is "correct" and "the page reference (p. 339) matches". The recovered page
   only shows the key `[Er93,p.339]` — it carries no title — so nothing on the page
   corroborated the docstring's title "_On some of my favourite theorems_"; and that
   title is provably the wrong paper for a p. 339 pin (its page range is 97–132). The
   prior review certified as verified something it could not verify (the 1001/1002
   failure class: confident claims about source data), and the subsequent fix session
   compounded the error into full wrong bibliographic data. This review's only defect
   is exactly the item the prior review signed off on.
2. **Overclaimed strength ("stronger than just liminf > 1").** §5 says the
   formalization is "**Stronger than** just liminf > 1 (it gives a uniform lower
   bound)". False for the statement as a whole: with $c$ existentially quantified,
   "$\exists c>0$ eventually $\ge 1+c$" is *equivalent* to $\liminf > 1$ (proof in A1).
   Only for a *fixed* $c$ is "eventually $\ge 1+c$" stronger than "$\liminf > 1$" — a
   conflation of the inner formula with the closed statement. Same family as the 1005
   audit's unsound boundedness argument: the conclusion (encoding acceptable) survives,
   the justification does not.
3. **Correct and verified claims:** `CliqueFree 0` is false in Mathlib, giving
   `ramseyR 0 l = ramseyR k 0 = 0` (re-derived: `IsNClique 0 ∅` holds) — correct;
   division-by-zero only at $k = 0$ since `ramseyR 1 1 = 1` — correct (re-derived);
   `ramseyR` duplication across 1014/986 — confirmed; its proposed weak-variant Lean
   text was adopted verbatim and is sound (subtraction in ℝ, `rpow`, eventual form).
4. **Flagged-but-unfixed:** nothing — everything ai-review flagged (BEFS89 credit,
   cross-refs 544/1014, OEIS, weak variant) was applied by the fix session. The BEFS89
   *provable-lemma* suggestion was left on the table; now added as a variant.
