## Summary

The problem (verbatim from the recovered page, **DISPROVED** banner): "Is it true that
the number of graphs on $n$ vertices which do not contain $G$ is
$\leq 2^{(1+o(1))\mathrm{ex}(n;G)}$?" The answer is no: Morris and Saxton [MoSa16]
showed there are at least $2^{(1+c)\mathrm{ex}(n;C_6)}$ $C_6$-free graphs for
infinitely many $n$; Erdős–Frankl–Rödl [EFR86] had proved the answer is yes for
non-bipartite $G$. The Lean input (`conjectures/59.lean:40-45`) asserts, as a **bare
direct assertion**, the universal statement itself — for every finite $H$ and every
$\varepsilon > 0$, eventually $\#\{H\text{-free graphs on }[n]\}
\leq 2^{(1+\varepsilon)\cdot\mathrm{ex}(n;H)}$ — i.e. exactly the proposition that
Morris–Saxton refuted. The docstring (lines 26-39) correctly records the disproof, so
docstring and theorem contradict each other: this is the problem-34/56-class
wrong-polarity defect flagged in this batch's checklist. Everything else (the
$o(1)$-as-$\forall\varepsilon$ encoding, the three helper definitions, coercions) is
sound. Fixed in `conjectures-v2/59.lean` by negating the statement and adding the four
page-confirmed variants; the input file is untouched.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — FAIL (wrong polarity; source-verified).**

- Independent back-translation of lines 40-45: "For every type $U$, every finite graph
  $H$ on $U$ (with decidable adjacency), and every $\varepsilon > 0$, there is $N$ such
  that for all $n \geq N$, the number of labeled $H$-free graphs on $[n]$ is at most
  $2^{(1+\varepsilon)\cdot\mathrm{ex}(n;H)}$." This is the *affirmative* answer to the
  question — the direction refuted by [MoSa16] for $H = C_6$. Read literally, the
  theorem asserts a **false** proposition (false at any $\varepsilon < c$ along
  Morris–Saxton's sequence of $n$, since $\mathrm{ex}(n;C_6) \geq n - 1 > 0$).
- Apart from polarity, the translation is faithful term-by-term: labeled graphs on
  $[n]$ = `SimpleGraph (Fin n)`; "$\leq 2^{(1+o(1))\mathrm{ex}(n;G)}$" as
  $\forall \varepsilon > 0$, eventually $\leq 2^{(1+\varepsilon)\mathrm{ex}}$ is the
  standard correct unpacking of an upper bound with a $(1+o(1))$ exponent (only
  positive $\varepsilon$ is needed for an upper bound).
- `[Fintype U]` restricts "every graph $G$" to finite graphs — the intended setting
  (Turán numbers of infinite forbidden graphs on $n$ vertices degenerate); fine.
- The two instance binders `[Fintype U] [DecidableRel H.Adj]` are not used by either
  definition in the statement body; they only narrow the quantification domain. This
  is harmless here (classically every finite graph carries both instances), and in the
  negated fix they make the negation *stronger*-typed but still true via a concrete
  decidable $C_6$.
- Not vacuous: the inner statement is substantive (and indeed substantive enough to be
  false). Degenerate $H$ (edgeless, or empty vertex type) satisfies the inner bound
  via count $= 0$ or $\mathrm{ex} = \binom{n}{2}$, so no degenerate witness either
  proves or refutes the statement spuriously — the negation in v2 is true only via
  genuine mathematics ($C_6$). Checked explicitly (batch degenerate-input item).

**A3. Definitions — pass, with one flagged compile risk.**

- `containsSubgraph59` (lines 12-13) matches its docstring: injective homomorphism =
  non-induced subgraph containment (adjacency required in the forward direction only),
  the standard notion for $\mathrm{ex}(n;G)$ and identical in content to Mathlib's
  `SimpleGraph.IsContained`. Note it requires isolated vertices of $H$ to embed too

  asymptotically irrelevant.
- `countGFreeGraphs59` (lines 23-24): `Nat.card` on the subtype of
  `SimpleGraph (Fin n)`; the ambient type is finite, so the subtype is finite and
  `Nat.card` is the true count with no instance requirements. Degenerate check:
  $n = 0$ gives the one empty graph, counted correctly.

  edgeless-$H$ case documented in A2. Small-case check (batch problem-56 item, elements
  not just cardinality): $H = K_3$, $n = 3$ — triangle-free graphs on 3 labeled
  vertices have at most 2 edges, achieved by a path; the candidate set is
  $\{0, 1, 2\}$ and `sSup` $= 2 = \mathrm{ex}(3; K_3)$ ✓. For $n < |U|$ every graph is
  $H$-free and the sup is $\binom{n}{2}$ ✓ (conventional).
