## Summary

The problem (OPEN, [Er67, p.72]): for every $C > 0$ there should exist
$\varepsilon > 0$ such that for all sufficiently large $n$, for any nodes
$x_1, \ldots, x_n \in [-1,1]$ there exist targets $y_1, \ldots, y_n \in [-1,1]$ such
that every polynomial $P$ of degree $< (1+\varepsilon)n$ with $P(x_i) = y_i$ for at
least $(1-\varepsilon)n$ indices satisfies
$\max_{x \in [-1,1]} |P(x)| > C$ — i.e. approximate interpolation of some adversarial
target data forces large sup-norm, even with a proportion of degrees of freedom to
spare. The Lean statement (`conjectures/1133.lean:36-45`) renders this as
`∀ C > 0, ∃ ε > 0, ∃ N, ∀ n ≥ N, ∀ x, … → ∃ y, … ∧ ∀ P, natDegree < (1+ε)n →
card{i | P(xᵢ) = yᵢ} ≥ (1-ε)n → ∃ t ∈ [-1,1], |P(t)| > C`. Read back independently,
the Lean and the recovered source agree quantifier-for-quantifier: the same
$\varepsilon$ serves both the degree slack and the agreement fraction, $y$ is chosen
after $x$ but uniformly against all $P$ (the whole point of the conjecture), the
degree bound is strict, and the existential-witness conclusion is exactly
"sup-norm $> C$". I found no semantic defects; the only findings are scholarship
enrichment (missing [Er67] citation, recoverable from logs) and an optional
page-confirmed variant, both applied in `conjectures-v2/1133.lean`.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "Let $C>0$. There exists
  $\epsilon>0$ such that if $n$ is sufficiently large the following holds. For any
  $x_1,\ldots,x_n\in [-1,1]$ there exist $y_1,\ldots,y_n\in [-1,1]$ such that, if $P$
  is a polynomial of degree $m<(1+\epsilon)n$ with $P(x_i)=y_i$ for at least
  $(1-\epsilon)n$ many $1\leq i\leq n$, then
  $\max_{x\in [-1,1]}\lvert P(x)\rvert > C$." The module docstring (lines 12–26)
  reproduces this and the page's remark essentially verbatim.
- Independent back-translation of lines 37–44: "for all $C > 0$ there is
  $\varepsilon > 0$ and a threshold $N$ such that for all $n \ge N$ and all node
  families $x \colon \mathrm{Fin}\, n \to [-1,1]$ there is a target family
  $y \colon \mathrm{Fin}\, n \to [-1,1]$ such that every real polynomial $P$ with
  $\deg P < (1+\varepsilon)n$ agreeing with $y$ at $\ge (1-\varepsilon)n$ indices has
  a point of $[-1,1]$ where $|P| > C$." This is the source proposition exactly.
- Quantifier structure: $\forall C\, \exists \varepsilon\, \exists N\, \forall n\,
  \forall x\, \exists y\, \forall P$ matches the source; crucially `∃ y` precedes
  `∀ P`, so one target family must defeat *all* qualifying polynomials
  simultaneously — the intended (and only nontrivial) reading. $\varepsilon$ is
  chosen before "$n$ sufficiently large", matching "There exists $\epsilon>0$ such
  that if $n$ is sufficiently large…".
- Types: real polynomials (`Polynomial ℝ`), real nodes/targets via `Fin n → ℝ` with
  membership in `Set.Icc (-1) 1`, and both count comparisons performed in ℝ after
  casting (`(card : ℝ) ≥ (1-ε)*n`, `(natDegree : ℝ) < (1+ε)*n`) — no ℕ floor/
  truncation distortion of the thresholds. "At least $(1-\epsilon)n$ many" is exactly
  `card ≥ (1-ε)n` over ℝ.
- Indexing: `Fin n` (0-indexed) vs the source's $1 \le i \le n$ is a pure relabeling;
  nothing in the statement depends on index values, only on counts. Exact.
- Not vacuous / not trivially false. Two degeneracy probes:
  (i) *Repeated nodes.* The source's "any $x_1, \ldots, x_n$" does not require
  distinctness and neither does the Lean. If nodes repeat, the existential $y$ can
  assign distinct targets within a repeated block, so a single polynomial can agree
  on at most one index per block; when the number of distinct nodes falls below
  $(1-\varepsilon)n$ the agreement hypothesis becomes unsatisfiable and the inner
  implication holds vacuously — the same is true of the informal statement, so the
  encoding is faithful, not an artifact.
  (ii) *Large $\varepsilon$.* If $\varepsilon \ge 1$ the agreement threshold
  $(1-\varepsilon)n \le 0$ is met by every $P$ (card $\ge 0$), and the statement
  would demand that every low-degree polynomial — including $P = 0$ — exceed $C$
  somewhere, which is false; so the formal statement cannot be discharged by a
  degenerate choice of $\varepsilon$, and its content lives exactly in the intended
  small-$\varepsilon$ regime.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- No ℕ subtraction anywhere: `1 - ε` and `1 + ε` are real arithmetic on `ε : ℝ`; `n`
  enters only via coercion to ℝ.
- No division anywhere in the statement.
- No `Finset.range`; the only Finset is `univ : Finset (Fin n)`, and
  `univ.filter (fun i => P.eval (x i) = y i)` is the agreement set — its cardinality
  compared in ℝ, as analyzed above. (Elaboration of `filter` on a real-equality
  predicate uses `Real.decidableEq`, a classical noncomputable instance; the file's
  `noncomputable section` (line 8) accommodates this. Compile status is out of
  scope.)
- Boundary $n = 0$: `Fin 0` families are trivial, `(1+ε)*0 = 0`, and
  `(natDegree : ℝ) < 0` is unsatisfiable, so the inner implication is vacuous; the
  `∃ N` makes this irrelevant regardless. No trap.
- Zero polynomial: `natDegree 0 = 0`, so $P = 0$ *is* quantified over (its informal
  degree $-\infty$ also satisfies any bound) — faithful, and handled by the
  existential $y$: any witness must (and may) place fewer than $(1-\varepsilon)n$
  zeros among the $y_i$, which is a special case of the conjecture's content (no
  polynomial of sup-norm $\le C$ may agree often), not an encoding artifact.
- "Sufficiently large" via `∃ N, ∀ n, N ≤ n` is the standard correct encoding; no
  filter machinery needed.
- Strict vs non-strict: degree bound strict (`<`), agreement bound non-strict (`≥`),
  conclusion strict (`>`) — each matches the source ("degree $m < (1+\epsilon)n$",
  "at least $(1-\epsilon)n$", "$> C$"). Intervals `Icc` (closed) match $[-1,1]$.
- Conclusion `∃ t ∈ Icc (-1) 1, |P.eval t| > C` versus the source's
  $\max_{[-1,1]} |P| > C$: for the (nonempty, bounded, attained by
  compactness/continuity) value set, the max exceeds $C$ iff some point does; the
  existential form is exact and avoids `iSup` junk-value questions entirely.

## E. Prior-review audit (`deepmind/ai-review/1133.md`)

The prior review is substantively accurate — its point-by-point correctness table
(§6) checks out row by row against my independent back-translation, and its
website claims ([Er67, p.72], OPEN, tags analysis/polynomials) match the recovered
page. Findings:

1. **Muddled zero-polynomial argument (right conclusion).** §6's "subtle point"
   says the bad case is avoided "only if the $y$ values are chosen to be nonzero
   (which they can be since $y$ is existentially quantified) … the existential
   choice of $y$ can simply avoid the zero polynomial. No issue here." Two
   imprecisions: (a) what a witness needs is that *fewer than $(1-\varepsilon)n$*
   of the $y_i$ are zero, not that all are nonzero; (b) more importantly, framing
   $P = 0$ as a case the witness "simply avoids" misreads the logic — defeating
   $P = 0$ (like defeating every polynomial of sup-norm $\le C$) is part of what a
   witness $y$ must accomplish, i.e. part of the conjecture's content, not a
   consistency check on the encoding. The conclusion (no encoding issue) is
   correct: including $P = 0$ under `natDegree` is faithful, since its informal
   degree $-\infty$ also satisfies any degree bound. Also minor: "natDegree
   $0 < (1+\varepsilon)n$ would hold for large $n$" — it holds for all $n \ge 1$.
   This is the same genre as the 1000/1005 audit findings (a justification that
   does not actually prove what it claims), though here harmlessly.
2. **Sup-vs-existential equivalence claim — verified correct.** §6(\*)'s assertion
   that "the sup exceeds $C$ iff there exists a point exceeding $C$" is right (it
   even holds without attainment, for the nonempty bounded value set).
3. **Ambiguity claim — verified correct.** §5's claim that "degree
   $m < (1+\epsilon)n$" readings ($\deg P < (1+\varepsilon)n$ vs
