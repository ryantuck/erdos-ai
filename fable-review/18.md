## Summary

The problem (OPEN, $250): call m *practical* if every integer 1 ≤ n < m is
a sum of distinct divisors of m, and for practical m let h(m) be the least
k such that at most k distinct divisors always suffice. Erdős asks three
questions of increasing strength on the factorial side: (1) are there
infinitely many practical m with h(m) < (log log m)^{O(1)}? (2) is
h(n!) < n^{o(1)}? (3) or perhaps even h(n!) < (log n)^{O(1)}? (the $250 is
offered in [Er81h] for a proof *or disproof* of (3), "although this is not
entirely clear" per the page). The Lean file defines `IsPractical`
(exactly the page's definition) and `practicalH` (the least-k `sInf`
encoding of h), then states the three conjectures as direct assertions of
the conjectured direction: `erdos_problem_18a` (∃ C > 0 with practical
m ≥ N and h(m) < (log log m)^C for every N), `erdos_problem_18b`
(∀ ε > 0 eventually h(n!) < n^ε), `erdos_problem_18c` (∃ C > 0 eventually
h(n!) < (log n)^C). Read back independently, all three agree with the
source questions; I found no semantic defects — only scholarship and
enrichment nits.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "We call $m$
  practical if every integer $1\leq n<m$ is the sum of distinct divisors of
  $m$. If $m$ is practical then let $h(m)$ be such that $h(m)$ many
  divisors always suffice. Are there infinitely many practical $m$ such
  that $h(m) < (\log\log m)^{O(1)}$? Is it true that $h(n!)<n^{o(1)}$? Or
  perhaps even $h(n!)<(\log n)^{O(1)}$?"
- `IsPractical` (`conjectures/18.lean:30-32`), back-translated: every n with
  1 ≤ n < m is a sum of a finset of divisors of m. A `Finset` sum is
  automatically a sum of *distinct* divisors; `Nat.divisors m` is the
  positive divisors of m (including m itself, which is harmless since every
  target n is < m and any finset containing m already oversums unless the
  rest is empty). Exactly the page's definition, including the half-open
  range 1 ≤ n < m.
- `practicalH` (`:37-39`), back-translated: the least k such that every
  1 ≤ n < m is a sum of at most k distinct divisors of m. The defining set
  is upward-closed in k and, for practical m, nonempty (k = m.divisors.card
  works), so `sInf` is the genuine minimum — exactly the docstring's and
  the source's "h(m) many divisors always suffice" gloss made precise as
  the least such k. For non-practical m the set is empty and
  `Nat.sInf ∅ = 0` (see A3).
- `erdos_problem_18a` (`:48-52`): "∃ C > 0 such that ∀ N ∃ m ≥ N practical
  with h(m) < (log log m)^C." The ∀N∃m≥N idiom is the standard, correct
  "infinitely many" encoding, and the constant C is quantified *outside*
  the infinitude — the correct reading of "infinitely many m with
  h(m) < (log log m)^{O(1)}" (a single exponent working along the whole
  subsequence). The independently produced upstream formal-conjectures file
  makes the same quantifier choice.
- `erdos_problem_18b` (`:60-63`): "∀ ε > 0, eventually h(n!) < n^ε" — the
  standard n^{o(1)} encoding. `erdos_problem_18c` (`:73-76`): "∃ C > 0,
  eventually h(n!) < (log n)^C" — the standard (log n)^{O(1)} encoding.
  Both match the questions as asked; (3) ⟹ (2) since (log n)^C < n^ε
  eventually, matching the page's "Or perhaps even" escalation.
- Not vacuous/trivial: 18a cannot be witnessed degenerately — m must exceed
  every N, and for m ≥ 2 practical, h(m) ≥ 1 (n = 1 needs one divisor), so
  the inequality genuinely constrains; the easy constructions (powers of 2
  give h(2^k) ≈ log m) do *not* satisfy the (log log m)^C bound, so the
  statement is a real open conjecture, not a triviality. 18b/18c are
  eventual statements about a specific sequence; nothing degenerate
  satisfies them.
