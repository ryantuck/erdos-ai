# Fable Review: Erdős Problem 1078

**Verdict:** NEEDS REVISION (one Part A defect: the theorem as written was false — wrong
quantifier polarity on the $-o(1)$ slack; fixed in place, see Addendum)
**Confidence:** high on the defect itself (the pre-fix statement is refuted by an explicit
finite counterexample); medium overall — no compiler is available, full bibliographic data
for [BES75b]/[Ha01]/[HaSz06] was not recoverable, and `FormalConjecturesForMathlib/` is not

## Summary

The problem (source page recovered from session logs, edition 06 October 2025, accessed
2026-02-22, status **PROVED**): "Let $G$ be an $r$-partite graph with $n$ vertices in each
part. If $G$ has minimum degree $\geq (r-\frac{3}{2}-o(1))n$ then $G$ must contain a
$K_r$." A conjecture of Bollobás, Erdős, and Szemerédi [BES75b], proved by Haxell [Ha01];
Haxell and Szabó [HaSz06] proved the sharp threshold $(r-1)n-\lceil sn/(2s-1)\rceil$,
$s=\lfloor r/2\rfloor$. The Lean file (`deepmind/1078.lean`) modelled the $r$-partite
structure, the minimum-degree hypothesis, and the transversal-$K_r$ conclusion correctly,
but rendered the $-o(1)$ as **"for every $\varepsilon>0$, eventually minimum degree
$\geq (r-3/2-\varepsilon)n$ suffices"**. That statement is *false*: for $\varepsilon \geq r$
the degree bound is negative, so the empty $r$-partite graph satisfies the hypothesis
vacuously and has no transversal $K_r$ (concretely: $r=2$, $\varepsilon=2$, $G=\bot$,
any $n\geq 1$ refutes the pre-fix `erdos_1078` outright). The correct rendering of a
$-o(1)$ slack *below* a threshold is existential: some fixed $\varepsilon>0$ works —
true by [HaSz06], whose sharp threshold lies below $(r-3/2)n$ by
$\approx n/(2(2s-1))$. Fixed in place (not compile-verified).

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — one [defect] (fixed); everything else exact.**

- Recovered verbatim source statement: "Let $G$ be an $r$-partite graph with $n$ vertices
  in each part. If $G$ has minimum degree $\geq (r-\frac{3}{2}-o(1))n$ then $G$ must
  contain a $K_r$." Direct assertion (a conjecture, now a theorem), not a question.
- Vertex set `Fin r × Fin n` with `IsMultipartite` (no intra-part edges) is a faithful
  model of "$r$-partite with $n$ vertices in each part": the parts are the fibers
  $\{i\} \times \mathrm{Fin}\ n$, each of size exactly $n$.
- "Contains a $K_r$" vs `HasTransversalClique`: equivalent. Any $K_r$ in an $r$-partite
  graph has $r$ pairwise-adjacent vertices, no two of which can share a part (intra-part
  pairs are non-adjacent), hence exactly one per part — i.e. a transversal clique.
  Conversely a transversal clique is a $K_r$ (the $r$ chosen vertices are distinct since
  their part indices differ, and pairwise adjacent). The `∃ f : Fin r → Fin n` encoding
  needs no injectivity hypothesis for exactly this reason.
- Minimum degree as `∀ v, (G.degree v : ℝ) ≥ ((r:ℝ) - 3/2 - ε) * n` is the correct
  pointwise rendering of $\delta(G) \geq (r-3/2-\varepsilon)n$, with all arithmetic in ℝ.
- **[defect] — quantifier polarity of the $-o(1)$.** The pre-fix theorem read
  `∀ ε : ℝ, ε > 0 → ∃ n₀, ∀ n ≥ n₀, …`. Back-translated: *every* fixed slack
  $\varepsilon$ below $r-3/2$ eventually suffices. False: larger $\varepsilon$ *weakens*
  the hypothesis, so the claim strengthens without bound as $\varepsilon$ grows.
  Counterexample to the pre-fix statement: take $\varepsilon = r$ (so
  $r - 3/2 - \varepsilon < 0$), any $n \geq \max(n_0, 1)$, and $G = \bot$ (the empty
  graph, which is multipartite); every degree is $0 \geq$ a negative bound, yet no
  transversal clique exists. The "$\forall \varepsilon$" convention is correct for
  $+o(1)$ slack *above* a threshold (weaker conclusion as $\varepsilon\to 0^+$ is
  approached from a true region), not for $-o(1)$ *below* it. The faithful and true
  rendering is existential: $\exists \varepsilon > 0$ such that eventually minimum degree
  $\geq (r-3/2-\varepsilon)n$ forces $K_r$. By [HaSz06] the exact threshold is
  $(r-1)n - \lceil sn/(2s-1)\rceil \leq (r-3/2)n - n/(2(2s-1))$, so any
  $\varepsilon < 1/(2(2s-1))$ works — the statement is substantive (genuinely
  sub-$(r-3/2)n$ degrees are admitted) and true. Note also that by monotonicity in
  $\varepsilon$, any "vanishing-sequence" reading of $-o(1)$ that allows the zero
  sequence collapses to the plain $(r-3/2)n$ threshold (Haxell's theorem); the fixed
  existential form is the strongest of the natural readings and is confirmed by the
  page's sharp-threshold remark. **Fixed** (see Addendum; not compile-verified).
- Not vacuous post-fix: the prover must exhibit real slack; $n_0$ can be chosen
  $\geq 1$, avoiding the degenerate $n = 0$ (where `HasTransversalClique` is false for
  $r \geq 1$ since `Fin 0` is empty).
- `hr : r ≥ 2` is a harmless domain restriction (the source's "$r$-partite … $K_r$"
  presupposes $r \geq 2$ to be non-degenerate; for $r \leq 1$ the statement is trivially
  true anyway).

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass (post-fix; one new construct audited).**

- No ℕ subtraction in the main theorem: $(r:ℝ) - 3/2 - \varepsilon$ is computed in ℝ
  after the cast. Correct.
- No division in the main theorem beyond the literal `3 / 2` in ℝ (fine).
- The new variant (added per Step 4) uses ℕ arithmetic deliberately, audited term by
  term: $s = r/2$ is exactly $\lfloor r/2\rfloor$ in ℕ-division; $2s - 1 \geq 1$ for
  $r \geq 2$ (no truncation, no division by zero); $\lceil sn/(2s-1)\rceil =
  (sn + (2s-1) - 1)/(2s-1) = (sn + 2s - 2)/(2s-1)$ in ℕ-division (numerator subtraction
  safe since $2s \geq 2$); the outer $(r-1)n - \lceil\cdot\rceil$ does not truncate since
  $\lceil sn/(2s-1)\rceil \leq sn \leq (r-1)n$ (as $s \leq r-1$ for $r \geq 2$).
- No `Finset.range`, no filters, no `StrictMono`/`Monotone` in this file. The
  eventuality is encoded by explicit `∃ n₀, ∀ n ≥ n₀` — fine.
- Degenerate cases: $n = 0$ (see A1) excluded by the prover's choice of $n_0$ in the
  main theorem and by `hn : n ≥ 1` in the variant, where it is necessary (at $n = 0$
  the degree hypothesis is vacuous over the empty vertex type but the conclusion is
  false).

**A3. Definitions — pass.**

- `IsMultipartite` matches its docstring and the standard definition ("no edges within

- No `sorry` in any `def`; no drift between definitions and theorem usage.

**A4. Question form and answer polarity — pass.**

## E. Prior-review audit (`ai-review/1078.md`)

The prior review's headline conclusion — "**mathematically correct**… No mathematical
flaws identified" — is wrong. Findings:

1. **Endorsed a false theorem (major).** Its §6 asserts "Quantifier structure:
   `∀ r ≥ 2, ∀ ε > 0, ∃ n₀, ∀ n ≥ n₀` is correct." The pre-fix statement is refuted by
   $(r,\varepsilon,G) = (2, 2, \bot)$ — a two-line counterexample well within the
   review's own edge-case analysis style (it *did* analyze $n = 0$ but never tried a
   large $\varepsilon$). Same failure class as the 1005 audit's endorsement of an
   encoding contradicting the source: nodding along with the raw file's stated
   convention instead of re-deriving it.
2. **Sign-blind o(1) argument.** Its §5 claims "for every ε > 0 … is the standard and
   unambiguous way to formalize o(1) conditions." That convention applies to $+o(1)$
   slack *above* a threshold (e.g. "$(1/2+o(1))n$ suffices" → $\forall\varepsilon>0$,
   $(1/2+\varepsilon)n$ suffices). For $-o(1)$ slack *below* a threshold the universal
   quantifier flips the strength of the claim and is false in general; the correct form
   is existential. The review never checked the direction.
3. **Degree-bound remark stops one step short.** Its §6 notes "the maximum possible
   degree is $(r-1)n$, so the bound $(r-3/2-\varepsilon)n$ is meaningful for $r \geq 2$"
   — without observing that for $\varepsilon \geq r - 3/2$ the bound is $\leq 0$ and the
   hypothesis vacuous, which is precisely the lever that falsifies the statement.
4. **Citations claim incomplete.** Its §2 says the website "confirms" the three keys
   used, with "No issues found." The recovered page shows the *statement's* source keys
   are [BES75] and [Er75], neither of which appeared in the styled file — a genuine
   citation omission it missed. Its further claim that bare stubs are "consistent with
   the style used across the codebase" is also false: sibling files (1015, 1079) carry
   full bibliographic entries.
5. **What it got right:** the $n = 0$ edge-case analysis is correct; the
   `HasTransversalClique` and `IsMultipartite` correctness bullets check out; the
   reuse survey (550's sigma-type `completeMultipartiteGraph` inapplicable; degree from
   Mathlib) is accurate, up to a wrong path ("ErdosProblems/550.lean" — it is
   `deepmind/550.lean` in this repo); its variant suggestion (sharp threshold) was good
   and is now implemented.
6. Nothing it flagged went unfixed prior to this review — but only because it flagged
   nothing binding; the one real defect went to this review to find.
