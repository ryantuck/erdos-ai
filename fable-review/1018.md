## Summary

The problem (verbatim from the recovered page): "Let $\epsilon>0$. Is there a constant
$C_\epsilon$ such that, for all large $n$, every graph on $n$ vertices with at least
$n^{1+\epsilon}$ edges must contain a subgraph on at most $C_\epsilon$ vertices which is
non-planar?" Status **SOLVED**: Kostochka and Pyber [KoPy88] answered yes, producing a
subdivision of $K_5$ on $O_\epsilon(1)$ vertices; Erdős [Er71] notes
$C_\epsilon \to \infty$ as $\epsilon \to 0$. The Lean (`deepmind/1018.lean`) quantifies
$\forall \varepsilon > 0, \exists C\, N_0, \forall n \ge N_0$, every `SimpleGraph (Fin n)`
with `edgeFinset.card ≥ ⌈(n:ℝ)^(1+ε)⌉₊` has an induced subgraph on a `Finset` of at most
$C$ vertices that is not `IsPlanar` (an opaque predicate, planarity being absent from
Mathlib), plus a second theorem formalizing the $C_\epsilon \to \infty$ observation. The
mathematical content matches the source exactly; the one defect is **form**: the source
is a yes/no question, but `erdos_1018` was a bare assertion instead of
`answer(True) ↔ …`, contrary to checklist A4, this repo's own restyling guide
(`ADHERE_TO_DEEPMIND_STYLE.md` §2c: known-yes → `answer(True)`), and the corpus
convention — including the closest sibling `deepmind/1019.lean` (same SOLVED status, same
opaque `IsPlanar`), which uses `answer(True) ↔`. Fixed in place.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified), form defect deferred to A4.**

- Independent back-translation of the pre-fix theorem: "for every real $\varepsilon > 0$
  there are naturals $C, N_0$ such that every simple graph on $n \ge N_0$ labeled
  vertices with at least $\lceil n^{1+\varepsilon}\rceil$ edges has a vertex subset $S$,
  $|S| \le C$, whose induced subgraph is non-planar." This is exactly the (affirmed)
  content of the question asked.
- Quantifiers: $C$ depends only on $\varepsilon$ (bound after `∀ ε`, before `∀ n`) —
  matches "$C_\epsilon$". "For all large $n$" ↔ `∃ N₀, ∀ n ≥ N₀` — standard and correct.
  The threshold is genuinely needed: for small $n$ and small $\varepsilon$ a graph with
  $\ge n^{1+\varepsilon}$ edges can be outright planar (e.g. $n = 5$, tiny $\varepsilon$:
  5 edges), so dropping $N_0$ would make the statement false; the encoding keeps it.
- "Subgraph" vs `G.induce`: equivalent under the intended interpretation of planarity —
  a non-planar subgraph on vertex set $S$ makes $G[S]$ (an edge-supergraph on the same
  vertices) non-planar, and conversely $G[S]$ is itself a subgraph on $\le C$ vertices.
  Caveat: with `IsPlanar` *opaque* (no monotonicity axiom) this equivalence is not
  derivable **in Lean**, so the choice of the induced form is a semantic commitment; it
  is the faithful one, and is now documented in the theorem docstring (Addendum fix 2).
- Types: `ε : ℝ`, `C N₀ : ℕ`, vertices `Fin n`, `Finset (Fin n)` for the witness set —
  all appropriate. `(n : ℝ) ^ (1 + ε)` is real rpow on a cast base, so the arithmetic
  happens in ℝ as intended.
- Indexing: no sequences; the only index-like object is `Fin n` as a canonical n-vertex
  set, which loses no generality (planarity and edge counts are isomorphism-invariant
  under the intended interpretation).
- Not vacuous/trivial: for fixed $\varepsilon < 1$ and large $n$, graphs with
  $\ge n^{1+\varepsilon}$ edges exist (need $n^{1+\varepsilon} \le \binom{n}{2}$), so the
  hypothesis is realizable and the claim substantive. For $\varepsilon \ge 1$ the edge
  hypothesis is eventually unsatisfiable ($n^{1+\varepsilon} \ge n^2 > \binom{n}{2}$) and
  the statement is vacuously true for those $\varepsilon$ — but that matches the informal
  problem's own semantics (the content lives at small $\varepsilon$); not a defect.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- No ℕ subtraction anywhere in this file's statements.
- Division: none. `⌈(n : ℝ) ^ (1 + ε)⌉₊` (`Nat.ceil`) on a nonnegative real; "at least
  $n^{1+\varepsilon}$ edges" for an ℕ-valued edge count is exactly
  `card ≥ ⌈n^{1+ε}⌉₊` (including when $n^{1+\varepsilon}$ is an integer, since
  `⌈x⌉₊ = x` there). At $n = 0$: `(0:ℝ)^(1+ε) = 0` (rpow, positive exponent), ceiling 0,
  hypothesis trivially satisfiable — harmless since `∃ N₀` may skip it, and the
  conclusion is anyway irrelevant below the threshold.
- No `Finset.range`; `n ≥ N₀` is used directly, no off-by-one surface.
- Vacuous quantifiers: only the benign $\varepsilon \ge 1$ vacuity discussed in A1.
- "For all large $n$": `∃ N₀, ∀ n ≥ N₀` in the main theorem and `∀ᶠ n in atTop` in the
  variant are the standard equivalent encodings; consistent with each other.
- `Tendsto f (nhdsWithin 0 (Set.Ioi 0)) atTop` in the variant is the correct encoding of
  $f(\varepsilon) \to \infty$ as $\varepsilon \to 0^+$ (right-hand limit, ℕ-valued
  `atTop`).
- No `StrictMono`/`Monotone` or interval-endpoint issues. Strictness of inequalities
  (`≥` on edges and `≤ C` on the witness) matches the source's "at least"/"at most".

**A3. Definitions — pass.**

- `opaque IsPlanar {V : Type*} [Fintype V] (_ : SimpleGraph V) : Prop`
  (`deepmind/1018.lean:48`): planarity is absent from Mathlib; axiomatizing it as an
  `opaque` `Prop` is the established corpus approach and matches the signatures in
  `deepmind/630.lean:56`, `deepmind/631.lean:68`, `deepmind/1019.lean:45` (modulo the
  anonymous binder `_` vs `G`, which is immaterial). `opaque` correctly prevents the
  kernel from unfolding into a `sorry` (the raw file's `def … := sorry` weakness was
  fixed during the pipeline).
- No `sorry` inside any `def`/`abbrev`/`structure`/`opaque` (Part D).
- Usage is consistent: `IsPlanar (G.induce (↑S : Set (Fin n)))` needs
  `Fintype ↥(↑S)`, supplied by Mathlib's `FinsetCoe.fintype`; the `haveI := dG` pattern
  supplies `Fintype G.edgeSet` for `edgeFinset`. (Both confirmed by the recovered
  pipeline session's successful `lake build` of the pre-fix file.)

**A4. Question form and answer polarity — DEFECT (fixed).**

- The source is a **yes/no question** ("Is there a constant $C_\epsilon$ …?"). Checklist
  A4 requires the shape `answer(…) ↔ P`; the repo's own restyling guide
  (`ADHERE_TO_DEEPMIND_STYLE.md` §2c) mandates `answer(True)` when the answer is known to
  be yes; and the corpus applies this uniformly to solved yes/no questions — most
  pointedly `deepmind/1019.lean` (`answer(True) ↔ ∀ n …`), the sibling problem from the
  same paper [Er71] with the same opaque `IsPlanar`. `erdos_1018` was instead a bare
  assertion. **[defect]** — fixed to `answer(True) ↔ ∀ ε : ℝ, ε > 0 → …`
  (`deepmind/1018.lean:65-73`); not compile-verified (see Addendum).
- Polarity verified by hand: RHS true ⟺ for every $\varepsilon$ such a $C_\varepsilon$
  exists ⟺ the question's answer is "yes". Kostochka–Pyber proved yes (page-verified),
  so `answer(True)` is correct. The pre-fix bare assertion did at least assert the *true*
  direction — this was a form defect, not a polarity defect.
- No binders before the colon (`theorem erdos_1018 :` is binder-free), so the iff is not
  spuriously universally quantified; likewise the variant, whose `∀ f` correctly sits
  inside the statement (it is a direct assertion, so no `answer()` wrapper is wanted
  there).
- Restyling drift (same pattern as the 1005 defect): the raw
  `conjectures/1018.lean` module docstring asked the question verbatim ("Is there a
  constant C_ε …?") and carried the Kostochka–Pyber $K_5$-subdivision remark and tags;
  the styled module docstring silently converted the question into an assertion, dropped
  "for all large n" (making its prose claim ill-formed — $n$ unbound, no threshold),
  and dropped the remark and tags. The raw *code* was already assertion-form (acceptable
  raw style for a solved problem with true polarity), but the restyle step, whose own
  guide mandates `answer(True)`, failed to convert it. All restored/fixed (Addendum).

**A5. Solution status — pass (source-verified).**

**B2. Variants and completeness — pass.**

- The problem is single-part and fully captured. The page's two remarks are both
  covered: the $C_\epsilon \to \infty$ observation is formalized
  (`erdos_1018.variants.C_tends_to_infinity`, renamed per corpus convention — see C1),
  and the $K_5$-subdivision strengthening is recorded in the module docstring. A formal
  variant of the $K_5$-subdivision result would require a subdivision-containment
  notion not present in the file or Mathlib — noted as optional enrichment, **skipped**
  per the constructs-already-present rule.
- Sanity check of the variant's mathematics (it is a *derived* claim, so worth
  re-deriving): true. For any $C$, standard girth constructions give, for all small
  $\varepsilon$ (roughly $\varepsilon < c/C$) and large $n$, graphs with
  $\ge n^{1+\varepsilon}$ edges and girth $> C$; every induced subgraph on
  $\le C$ vertices of such a graph is acyclic, hence planar, so any witnessing $f$ has
  $f(\varepsilon) > C$ there. Hence $f \to \infty$ along $0^+$: the
  `Tendsto … (nhdsWithin 0 (Set.Ioi 0)) atTop` conclusion is the right statement.
