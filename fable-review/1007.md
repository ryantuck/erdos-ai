## Summary

The problem: the dimension of a graph $G$ is the minimal $n$ such that $G$ can be
embedded in $\mathbb{R}^n$ with every edge a unit line segment (a notion of Erdős,
Harary, and Tutte); Erdős asked Soifer in January 1992: **what is the smallest number of
edges in a graph with dimension 4?** The answer is 9, achieved solely by $K_{3,3}$
(House [Ho13]; alternative proof Chaffee–Noble [ChNo16], who also showed the dimension-5
minimum is 15, achieved by $K_6$ and $K_{1,3,3}$). The Lean file defines
`IsUnitDistRep`/`HasUnitDistRep` and states the minimum-9 result, a $K_{3,3}$-uniqueness
variant, and the dimension-5 analogue. The skeleton is right, but the pre-fix
`IsUnitDistRep` demanded `G.Adj u v ↔ dist (f u) (f v) = 1` — a *faithful* representation
— whereas the recovered source (and the EHT definition it attributes) requires only that
edges have unit length, leaving non-adjacent pairs unconstrained. That changes the class
of "dimension exactly 4" graphs being quantified over, so the pre-fix theorems asserted
statements that House's and Chaffee–Noble's proofs do not establish. Additionally the
source is a value request, which per the checklist needs the `answer()` value shape.
Both defects fixed in place (not compile-verified).

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — DEFECT (fixed).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "The dimension of a graph $G$ is
  the minimal $n$ such that $G$ can be embedded in $\mathbb{R}^n$ such that every edge
  of $G$ is a unit line segment. / What is the smallest number of edges in a graph with
  dimension $4$?" Remarks: notion defined by Erdős, Harary, and Tutte; posed by Erdős to
  Soifer in January 1992; answer 9, "achieved solely by $K_{3,3}$", proved by House
  [Ho13]; alternative proof and the dimension-5 value 15 ($K_6$, $K_{1,3,3}$) by
  Chaffee–Noble [ChNo16].
- **[defect] Wrong definition of dimension.** The source constrains *edges only* ("every
  edge of $G$ is a unit line segment"); an embedding sends distinct vertices to distinct
  points, and non-adjacent vertices may or may not lie at unit distance. This is exactly
  the Erdős–Harary–Tutte definition (Mathematika 12 (1965)), under which, e.g., placing
  the vertices of *any* $(n+1)$-vertex graph at the vertices of a regular unit
  $n$-simplex is a legal representation (all pairs at distance 1) — the trick behind
  $\dim G \le |V|-1$ and $\dim K_n = n - 1$. The pre-fix Lean instead required
  `∀ u v, G.Adj u v ↔ dist (f u) (f v) = 1`, i.e. the *faithful* ("strict")
  unit-distance representation, a genuinely different graph parameter (faithful
  dimension ≥ EHT dimension). Consequences: the universal half of `erdos_1007`
  quantified over "graphs of *faithful* dimension exactly 4" — a class that a priori
  contains graphs of EHT dimension ≤ 3 (whose every faithful $\mathbb{R}^3$
  representation is obstructed only by coincidental unit distances) and excludes any
  graph of EHT dimension 4 whose faithful dimension exceeds 4. House's theorem says
  nothing about that class, so the pre-fix statement (and a fortiori the uniqueness and
  dimension-5 variants) was not the proved result; whether it is even true would need
  new arguments (e.g. perturbation/degeneracy analyses for graphs with ≤ 8 edges) that
  appear in none of the cited works. The parenthetical in both module and theorem-level
  docstrings, "(adjacent vertices are at Euclidean distance exactly 1, and non-adjacent
  vertices are not)", likewise misstates the source. This defect entered at the raw pass
  (`conjectures/1007.lean` has the same biconditional and parenthetical) — it is not
  restyling drift. **Fixed:** `IsUnitDistRep` now requires `Function.Injective f ∧
  ∀ u v, G.Adj u v → dist (f u) (f v) = 1`, with docstrings rewritten to the EHT
  definition (not compile-verified).
- Injectivity is correctly retained: it is part of "embedded" (EHT: distinct vertices to
  distinct points), and without it each side of $K_{3,3}$ — non-adjacent twins — could
  collapse to a single point, making $K_{3,3}$ 1-dimensional. (Under the pre-fix
  biconditional the same collapse was available, contra the prior review's opening
  claim; see Part E.)
- "Dimension exactly 4" as `HasUnitDistRep G 4 ∧ ¬HasUnitDistRep G 3` is correct, given
  the padding monotonicity `HasUnitDistRep G n → HasUnitDistRep G (n+1)` (append a zero
  coordinate; distance-preserving, so valid for both the weak and the strict reading).
  Now documented in `HasUnitDistRep`'s docstring.
- Restriction to `Fintype V` is harmless: an infinite graph of dimension exactly 4 has
  infinitely many edges, so the source's minimum ranges over finite graphs.
- Not vacuous/trivial: the witness class is nonempty ($K_{3,3}$ in $\mathbb{R}^4$ via
  the Lenz two-orthogonal-circles construction; $\lnot$-embeddable in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is
  EHT's $\dim K_{3,3} = 4$), so both halves of the `IsLeast` statement have content.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- No ℕ subtraction, no division, no `Finset.range`, no filters/limits anywhere.
- `dist` on `EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin n)` is the Euclidean (ℓ²) metric — the intended one.
- Degenerate cases: the empty graph and edgeless graphs satisfy `IsUnitDistRep`
  hypotheses vacuously in the edge clause but are excluded from the dimension-4 class by
  `¬HasUnitDistRep G 3` (they embed in $\mathbb{R}^1$ with pairwise distances ≠ 1 — and
  under the post-fix weak definition, in $\mathbb{R}^1$ trivially at spacing 2), so no
  degenerate witness perturbs the statement.
- `G.edgeFinset.card` under `[Fintype V] [DecidableRel G.Adj]` is the standard finite
  edge count; $K_{3,3}$ has 9 edges, $K_6$ has 15, $K_{1,3,3}$ has $3+3+9=15$ —
  consistent with the page.
- Post-fix `IsLeast S 9` on `S : Set ℕ` unfolds to `9 ∈ S ∧ ∀ m ∈ S, 9 ≤ m` — exactly
  the pre-fix existence + lower-bound conjunction, with no `sInf`-junk-value subtleties.

**A4. Question form and answer polarity — DEFECT (fixed).**

- The source is a **value request** ("What is the smallest number of edges…?"). Per the
  checklist this takes the `… = answer(…)`/value shape; the pre-fix `erdos_1007` was a
  bare two-conjunct assertion with 9 hard-coded and no `answer()`. The style review
  (`reviews/1007.md` §6) had raised exactly this conditionally ("if the original problem
  is phrased as a question … Case 2 with `= answer(9)` would be more appropriate") but
  the condition was never checked against the source, which *is* phrased as a question.
  **Fixed:** `erdos_1007` is now `IsLeast {m : ℕ | ∃ …, HasUnitDistRep G 4 ∧
  ¬HasUnitDistRep G 3 ∧ G.edgeFinset.card = m} answer((9 : ℕ))`, the upstream
  formal-conjectures idiom for solved least-value questions (the pattern
  `IsLeast {…} answer(sorry)` appears verbatim in the recovered upstream session logs).
  Not compile-verified.
- Polarity by hand: `answer((9 : ℕ))` is the concrete, non-tautological solved value;
  `IsLeast` renders "the smallest … is 9" including attainment. Correct.
- No binders before the colon on the `answer()` theorem (`theorem erdos_1007 :` is
  binder-free). The uniqueness variant takes binders, which is fine — it is a direct
  assertion without `answer()`.
- The variants are direct assertions of proved results (uniqueness [Ho13]; dimension-5
  value [ChNo16] as `IsLeast … (15 : ℕ)`), correctly without `answer()` — they are
  remark content, not the posed question.

## E. Prior-review audit (`ai-review/1007.md`)

Substantive findings:

1. **The A1 defect was seen and waved through with a false claim.** §5 explicitly
   identifies the two candidate readings ("adjacent vertices have distance 1" vs the
   biconditional, "faithful dimension vs unit-distance dimension") and then endorses the
   biconditional as "the standard definition in the literature". That is wrong: the
   definition the page states and attributes to Erdős–Harary–Tutte constrains edges
   only, and EHT's own results (e.g. $\dim \le |V|-1$ via the regular unit simplex,
   under which *all* pairs are at distance 1) are only valid under the weak reading. The
   faithful variant is a different parameter. This is the same failure class as the 1005
   audit: the review correctly reads the source, then endorses an encoding that
   contradicts its own reading.
2. **Self-contradictory injectivity paragraph (false sub-claim, right conclusion).** §4
   opens: "injectivity is already implied by the adjacency condition for connected
   graphs" — false. $K_{3,3}$ is connected, and same-side vertices are non-adjacent
   twins: mapping each side to a single point satisfies the biconditional (the two
   points at distance 1), so injectivity is *not* implied. The paragraph's own
   subsequent analysis derives this and lands on the correct conclusion, but the opening
   claim would have licensed dropping the injectivity conjunct on connected graphs —
   which would have made the formalization outright false.
3. **Citation guesses superseded by recovered data.** §2 guessed [Ho13]'s title as
   "Graphs requiring a minimum number of edges to achieve a given dimension (or
   similar)" — the actual title, in the log-recovered `/latex/1007` extraction, is "A
   4-dimensional graph has at least 9 edges". It also carried the raw file's "Noble,
   S."; the extraction gives "Noble, Matt". Both were corrected in the styled file by
   the earlier fix session.
4. **Minor:** §4 calls `(V : Type)` "universe-polymorphic" — `Type` is the fixed
   universe `Type 0` (harmless here, since every finite graph is isomorphic to one on a
   `Type 0` carrier).
5. **What it got right:** the padding-monotonicity argument for the exactly-4 encoding
   (correct, for both readings); flagging the missing uniqueness and dimension-5
   variants (both subsequently added); the $K_6$/$K_{1,3,3}$ dimension-5 facts (match
   the recovered page); the reuse survey conclusions.
6. `reviews/1007.md` (style, reference only): its conditional flag on `answer()` usage
   was the right instinct — the condition (source phrased as a question) is confirmed
   true by the recovery, so Case 2 applies and the flag should have been acted on; its
   "Case 3 applies" checkbox is factually wrong against the source text.
