## Summary

The problem: given any $n$ points in $\mathbb{R}^2$, the number of $k$-rich lines (lines
containing $\ge k$ of the points) is $\ll n^2/k^3$, provided $k \le n^{1/2}$ — a
conjecture of Erdős, Croft, and Purdy [Er87b, p.169], proved true by Szemerédi and
Trotter [SzTr83] (the k-rich-lines corollary of the Szemerédi–Trotter incidence theorem).
The Lean file (`deepmind/1069.lean`) defines lines parametrically (`IsLine`), counts
$k$-rich lines with `Set.ncard` (`numKRichLines`), and states
`∃ C : ℝ, C > 0 ∧ ∀ S k, 2 ≤ k → (k:ℝ)^2 ≤ S.card → numKRichLines S k ≤ C·|S|²/k³`
as a direct assertion (no `answer()`), tagged `research solved`. Read back
independently, the Lean and the recovered source statement agree: the `∃ C` correctly
renders the Vinogradov $\ll$ with a constant uniform in both $S$ and $k$; `k² ≤ n`
renders `k ≤ √n` exactly; the extra hypothesis `2 ≤ k` is a necessary-for-meaning
restriction (for $k \le 1$ the count is infinite), not a fidelity loss. No semantic
defects found; only polish-level enrichment applied.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "Given any $n$ points in
  $\mathbb{R}^2$, the number of $k$-rich lines (lines which contain $\geq k$ of the
  points) is, provided $k\leq n^{1/2}$, $\ll \frac{n^2}{k^3}$."
- Independent back-translation of the theorem (lines 77–81): "there is a real $C > 0$
  such that for every finite $S \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ and every natural $k$ with
  $k \ge 2$ and $k^2 \le |S|$, the number of lines containing at least $k$ points of $S$
  is at most $C|S|^2/k^3$." This matches the source: $\ll$ means "at most an absolute
  constant times", and putting `∃ C` outermost makes the constant uniform in both $S$
  and $k$, which is the intended reading.
- `k² ≤ n` vs `k ≤ √n`: for naturals $k, n$, $(k:\mathbb{R})^2 \le n \iff k \le
  \sqrt{n}$ — the encoding is exact, no floor/rounding issue (and equivalent to the ℕ
  statement `k^2 ≤ S.card` by `Nat.cast_le`-style monotonicity of the cast).
- The hypothesis `2 ≤ k` is absent from the source's wording but necessary for the
  statement to be about a genuine (finite) count: for $k \le 1$ and $S$ nonempty, every
  line through a point of $S$ is $k$-rich, the set is infinite, and the informal claim
  is meaningless. The standard statement of the rich-lines theorem is for
  $2 \le k \le \sqrt{n}$. Not a defect; now documented in the docstrings (Addendum
  fix 2/3).
- Not vacuous/trivial: for $2 \le k$, $k^2 \le n$, the count is a genuine finite number
  (at most $\binom{n}{2}$, each rich line being determined by two of its $\ge 2 $ points
  in $S$) and the bound $Cn^2/k^3 \ge Cn^2/n^{3/2} = C\sqrt{n}$ is a substantive
  restriction; the hypothesis $k^2 \le n$ is exactly what makes the $n^2/k^3$ term
  dominate the $n/k$ term of the full Szemerédi–Trotter bound, so the formalized
  statement is the true theorem, neither weakened nor accidentally false.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- No ℕ subtraction anywhere.
- Division: only real division `C * n^2 / k^3`, with $k \ge 2$ so $k^3 > 0$; never the
  Lean $x/0 = 0$ convention on the path actually used. (Had `2 ≤ k` been dropped, the
  statement would *remain literally true* at $k = 0,1$ — at $k=0$ both sides collapse to
  $0$ by the division-by-zero and `ncard`-junk conventions; at $k=1$ the LHS is junk 0 —
  i.e. true only via junk values. The hypothesis keeps the statement honest. See E for
  the prior review's imprecision here.)
- `Finset.range`: not used; no off-by-one surface.
- Vacuous quantifiers: none (the two hypotheses are genuine restrictions, satisfiable
  for all $n \ge 4$).
- `Set.ncard` junk value on infinite sets: for $k \ge 2$ the set of $k$-rich lines is
  finite (each is determined by 2 of its points of $S$), so `ncard` is the true count —
  the encoding of "the number of $k$-rich lines" is correct on the whole domain of the
  theorem. Now documented in `numKRichLines`'s docstring (Addendum fix 2).
- `S.filter (· ∈ L)` requires decidability of `· ∈ L`; supplied by
  `open scoped Classical` (line 36). `k ≤ (S.filter (· ∈ L)).card` is exactly "L
  contains at least k points of S".
- No `StrictMono`/`Monotone`, interval-endpoint, or filter-encoding issues arise (no
  asymptotics in the main statement — the $\ll$ is correctly made an explicit constant,
  not an `IsBigO`, which is the right call since the bound is claimed for *all* $n, k$
  in range, not eventually).

**A3. Definitions — pass.**

   absent and called the omission "acceptable". Under this corpus's own precedent
