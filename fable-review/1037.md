## Summary

The problem (recovered verbatim from the archived page): "Let $G$ be a graph on $n$
vertices in which every degree occurs at most twice, and the number of distinct degree
[sic] is $>(\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon)n$. Must $G$ contain a trivial (empty or complete)
subgraph of size 'much larger' than $\log n$?" — a question of Chen and Erdős
[Er93, p.347]. The page's status banner is **DISPROVED (LEAN)** ("solved in the negative
and the proof verified in Lean"): Cambie, Chan, and Hunter gave, in the page's comment
section, a construction with at least $\frac{3}{4}n$ distinct degrees, every degree
appearing at most twice, and largest trivial subgraph of size $O(\log n)$. The Lean
statement (`deepmind/1037.lean:60`) encodes the question as
`answer(False) ↔ (∀ ε > 0, ∀ C > 0, ∃ N₀, ∀ n ≥ N₀, ∀ G, [degree multiplicity ≤ 2] →
[distinct degrees > (1/2+ε)n] → ∃ clique or independent set of size > C·log n)`,
resolving "much larger than $\log n$" as superlogarithmic (beyond $C \log n$ for every
fixed $C$). Read back independently, the Lean RHS is exactly the question as asked, and
`answer(False)` correctly records the negative answer. No semantic defects; the one
defect found is bibliographic (the `[Er93]` entry named a paper that cannot contain the
cited p.347).

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Module docstring reproduces the page statement verbatim (modulo fixing the page's
  typo "distinct degree" → "distinct degrees" and $\epsilon \to \varepsilon$).
- Independent back-translation of lines 61–76: "for every $\varepsilon > 0$ and every
  $C > 0$ there is $N_0$ such that every graph on $n \ge N_0$ vertices in which each
  degree value occurs at most twice and which has more than $(1/2+\varepsilon)n$
  distinct degrees contains a clique or an independent set of more than $C \log n$
  vertices." That is the question as asked, with "much larger than $\log n$" read as
  $\omega(\log n)$ uniformly over the family — the standard superlogarithmic reading
  (equivalently: $\min_G \max$ trivial-subgraph size is $\omega(\log n)$).
- Quantifiers: $\varepsilon$ is the problem's implicit "for every fixed
  $\varepsilon > 0$" parameter and is correctly outermost; $N_0$ may depend on
  $\varepsilon$ and $C$ but not on $G$ — the correct uniformity. The negation
  ($\exists \varepsilon, C$ with hypothesis-satisfying graphs of bounded trivial
  subgraphs for arbitrarily large $n$) is exactly what the CCH construction supplies
  ($3/4 = 1/2 + 1/4$, so any $\varepsilon < 1/4$ works).
- "Trivial (empty or complete) subgraph" = independent set or clique: encoded as
  `G.IsClique ↑S ∨ Gᶜ.IsClique ↑S` (disjunction of two existentials) — correct;
  `Gᶜ.IsClique` is the standard independent-set idiom (cf. `deepmind/1036.lean`).
- Not vacuous / not trivially decided: the hypotheses are satisfiable (the construction
  itself), and refuting the RHS genuinely requires a nontrivial graph — random graphs
  have $\Theta(\log n)$ trivial subgraphs but only $O(\sqrt{n \log n})$ distinct
  degrees, so they fail the hypothesis; conversely graphs with many distinct degrees
  (e.g. threshold graphs) have huge cliques. Note also that under "every degree at most
  twice" one automatically has $\ge n/2$ distinct degrees, so the hypothesis threshold
  $(1/2+\varepsilon)n$ sits exactly at the meaningful margin — the encoding preserves
  this.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- No ℕ subtraction anywhere.
- Division: `1 / 2 + ε` elaborates in ℝ (forced by `ε : ℝ`), so `1 / 2` is real
  $0.5$, not ℕ floor division. `Real.log n` at $n \in \{0, 1\}$ gives $0$; then any
  singleton clique ($n \ge 1$) already exceeds $C \cdot 0 = 0$, and at $n = 0$ the
  distinct-degree hypothesis `0 > 0` is false — both edge cases harmless, and the
  `∃ N₀` slack makes them irrelevant anyway.
- No `Finset.range`; `Finset.univ` over `Fin n` is the correct vertex set.
- Vacuous quantifier check: for $\varepsilon \ge 1/2$ the hypothesis
  "distinct degrees $> (1/2+\varepsilon)n \ge n$" is unsatisfiable and the inner
  implication vacuously true; this matches the informal problem (only small
  $\varepsilon$ carry content) and cannot flip the RHS's truth value, which is decided
  by $\varepsilon < 1/4$.
- `∀ _ : DecidableRel G.Adj` supplies the instance `G.degree` needs; universally
  quantifying it inside the conjecture is the right polarity (the statement must hold
  for every graph under any instance, and `Finset.filter`/`degree` values are
  instance-independent propositionally). Same idiom as the corpus.
- Strict inequalities: hypothesis `>` matches the source's `>`; conclusion `>` matches
  the "exceeds $C \log n$" reading. Log base is immaterial under `∀ C` (constants
  absorb); `Real.log` matches sibling 1036.
- `(Finset.univ.filter (fun v => G.degree v = d)).card ≤ 2` for all `d : ℕ` is exactly
  "every degree occurs at most twice"; `(Finset.univ.image (fun v => G.degree v)).card`
  is exactly the number of distinct degrees.

**A3. Definitions — pass.** No custom `def`/`abbrev`/`structure`; nothing to check, no
`sorry` outside proof bodies.

**A4. Question form and answer polarity — pass (source-verified).**

- The source is a yes/no question ("Must $G$ contain…?"), so the `answer(…) ↔ P` shape

- Polarity by hand: RHS true would mean "yes, such graphs must contain superlogarithmic

- No binders before the colon (line 60: `theorem erdos_1037 :` is binder-free), so the

## E. Prior-review audit (`ai-review/1037.md`)

Accurate in its final conclusions, but four findings:

1. **False intermediate claim in §5, correct conclusion (the recurring
   invent-then-excuse class).** §5 writes: "The `answer(False)` direction then states
   that **for every** ε > 0 and C > 0, there exists a graph meeting the degree
   constraints whose largest trivial subgraph has size ≤ C·log n." That reading is
   mathematically **impossible**: by Ramsey's theorem every $n$-vertex graph contains a
   homogeneous set of size $\ge \tfrac{1}{2}\log_2 n$, so for
   $C < 1/(2\ln 2)$ *no* graph has all trivial subgraphs $\le C\ln n$. The correct
   negation swaps the quantifiers to $\exists \varepsilon, \exists C$ — which the very
   next sentences of §5 derive correctly. The paragraph contradicts itself and would
   have endorsed a false statement had the encoding actually matched its first reading.
2. **Endorsed an internally contradictory citation.** §2 states the `[Er93]` entry
   "matches standard references: Erdős, P., *On some of my favourite theorems*, …
   97–132" without noticing that the problem's own citation is **p.347**, outside
   97–132. The correct key resolution (Quaestiones Mathematicae 16, 333–350) was
   recoverable from the very sources available to that review. This is the B1 defect.
3. **Stale factual claims, subsequently mooted.** Its Code Reuse/Readability sections
   describe inline degree filters (`(Finset.univ.filter (fun w => G.Adj v w)).card`)
   "appearing three times" — true of the version it reviewed, but commit `2d5425c0`
   later adopted its own `G.degree` suggestion, so those observations no longer
   describe the artifact.
4. **Its Sawhney flag was mis-implemented.** §2 correctly noted Sawhney's unclear role
   and asked for clarification "if he co-authored the construction"; the follow-up
   edit instead promoted him into the construction credit (module docstring only),
   which the page does not support and which contradicted the theorem docstring. Fixed
   per the page.

What it got right: the `answer(False) ↔` shape and polarity call; the quantifier-order
analysis ($N_0$ independent of $G$); the degree-multiplicity and distinct-degree
encodings; the `IsClique`/`Gᶜ.IsClique` reading (including the empty-set/singleton
remark); the natural-log convention being harmless; the edge-case analysis at
$n \in \{0,1\}$.
