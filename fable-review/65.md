## Summary

The problem (verbatim from the recovered page, **OPEN** banner): "Let $G$ be a graph
with $n$ vertices and $kn$ edges, and $a_1<a_2<\cdots$ be the lengths of cycles in $G$.
Is it true that $\sum\frac{1}{a_i}\gg \log k$? Is the sum $\sum\frac{1}{a_i}$ minimised
when $G$ is a complete bipartite graph?" — a problem of Erdős and Hajnal; question 1 is
solved ([GKS84], sharp constant by [LiMo20]), so only question 2 remains. The Lean
(`conjectures/65.lean:31-44`) formalizes question 2 in the direction asked: for every
graph $G$ on `Fin n` with exactly $a\cdot b$ edges where $a+b=n$, the reciprocal sum
over the distinct cycle lengths of $K_{a,b}$ (a correct hand-rolled
`completeBipartiteGraph65`) is at most that of $G$. Read back independently, the Lean
and the English question agree, with the comparison graph pinned down uniquely (given
$n$ and $e=ab$ with $a+b=n$, $\{a,b\}$ is determined). I found no Part A defect; the
findings are scholarship-level — no bibliography for the two keys the docstring cites,
omission of the page's crucial remark that forthcoming work of Montgomery, Milojević,
Pokrovskiy, and Sudakov proves the sum is **maximised** (not minimised) by complete
bipartite graphs for large $k$, and a statically-verifiable missing import
(`Mathlib.Combinatorics.SimpleGraph.Finite` for `edgeFinset`). Enrichments and fixes
applied in `conjectures-v2/65.lean`; the input file is untouched.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Independent back-translation of lines 31-43: "for all $n$, all $G$ on $n$ labeled
  vertices, all $a,b$ with $a+b=n$ and $|E(G)|=ab$, and $T_G$ = the set of cycle
  lengths of $G$, there is a set $T_K$ = the set of cycle lengths of
  $K_{a,b}$ with $\sum_{m\in T_K} 1/m \le \sum_{m\in T_G} 1/m$." That is exactly "the
  sum is minimised when $G$ is a complete bipartite graph", restricted to the
  comparison class in which $K_{a,b}$ lives (same vertex count $n$, same edge count).
  The $\le$ direction is the correct rendering of "minimised".
- The enumeration $a_1<a_2<\cdots$ on the page is of *distinct* cycle lengths; the
  `Finset ℕ` of lengths with the two-sided characterization (`hT_sub`/`hT_sup`,
  lines 36-37) counts each length once. Term-by-term faithful; no indexing issue
  arises (the sum is over a set, not a sequence).
- Comparison-class scope: only edge counts of the form $ab$ with $a+b=n$ are covered.
  This is the natural reading — for other edge counts no complete bipartite graph with
  the same parameters exists, so the question compares nothing. Moreover, for fixed
  $(n, e)$ with $e = ab$, $a+b=n$, the pair $\{a,b\}$ is unique ($a,b$ are the roots
  of $x^2-nx+e$), so the formalization's parameterization over $(a,b)$ introduces no
  ambiguity and no hidden strengthening (the prior review worried about "multiple
  partitions with $ab$ close to $e$" — for exact equality there are none; see Part E).
- Not vacuous / not trivially false at small parameters: hypotheses are satisfiable
  (e.g. $G \cong K_{a,b}$ itself, with $T_K = T_G$, gives equality), and the
  conclusion's $T_K$ exists (cycle lengths of a finite graph form a finite set,
  classically a `Finset`). Spot-checks of the *assertion* at small sizes (1004-class
  check for a literally-false page statement): $K_{2,2}=C_4$ (sum $1/4$) vs all
  4-vertex/4-edge graphs (unicyclic; $C_3$-based give $1/3$) ✓; $K_{2,3}$ (sum $1/4$)
  vs all 5-vertex/6-edge graphs (e.g. bowtie: $\{3\}$, $1/3$; only-$\{5\}$ impossible
  since two distinct 5-cycles on 5 vertices force shorter cycles) ✓; $K_{2,4}$ (sum
  $1/4$) vs 6-vertex/8-edge graphs (girth $\ge 5$ impossible at that density;
  only-$\{5\}$ impossible) ✓. Indeed $K_{2,b}$ is exactly the generalized-theta
  extremal configuration at its density (the unique way to have $e = 2v-4$ with a
  single cycle length is $b$ internally disjoint paths of length 2 between two hubs,
  which *is* $K_{2,b}$). No small counterexample; the universally quantified assertion
  is a genuine open conjecture at small/medium $k$ — but see A5/B2 for the
  page-reported forthcoming maximisation result, which would falsify it for large $k$.
- The theorem's own docstring (lines 18-29) matches the code and flags question 1 as
  solved and omitted — accurate, but it omits the maximisation remark (fixed in v2).

## E. Prior-review audit (`deepmind/ai-review/65.md`)

The prior review (written against the styled `deepmind/65.lean`) is substantively
good — its two headline scholarship flags are real — but has four errors/omissions:

1. **False claim about the code.** §6: "The `answer(sorry)` wrapper provides some
   protection (it could evaluate to `False`)." There is **no** `answer()` wrapper in
   either artifact — the raw file and the styled copy (recovered verbatim from the
   logged `Write` in session ba81b00a…, and its later `Edit`) both state a bare
   `theorem … := by sorry` / `:= sorry`. The claimed safety mechanism does not exist;
   the mitigation actually available is documentation plus the reversed-inequality
   variant, both applied in v2.
2. **"Wrong direction" framing conflates question with answer.** §6 calls the $\le$
   inequality "the wrong direction" given MMPS. The $\le$ direction is *exactly the
   question as asked* ("minimised?"); flipping it to $\ge$ in the main statement
   would formalize MMPS's theorem instead of Erdős's question, which would itself be
   an A1 infidelity. The correct response to MMPS (given the page keeps OPEN) is the
   caveat + variant, not a sign flip — the prior review's own option (c) ("at
   minimum, document … in the docstring") is the sound one.
3. **Overstated citation verification.** §2 asserts [LiMo20]'s JAMS 36 (2023),
   1191–1234 data "matches the website". The recovered `/latex/65` extraction carries
   only "arXiv:2010.15802 (2020)" for this key, and the page proper shows no
   bibliographic detail (it loads `/bibs/` on demand). The JAMS data is plausible
   reviewer knowledge, not website-verified — the same claiming-verification-
   without-source pattern flagged in earlier audits (1001-class, inverted).
4. **Missed uniqueness observation.** §5 worries "there may be multiple partitions
   $a+b=n$ with $ab$ close to $e$", treating the choice of comparison graph as an
   ambiguity the formalization "sidesteps". For the exact-equality encoding actually
   used there is no ambiguity at all: $(n, e)$ determines $\{a, b\}$ as the roots of
   $x^2 - nx + e$. The formalization is canonical, not merely "a reasonable choice".
