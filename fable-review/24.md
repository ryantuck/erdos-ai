## Summary

The problem (PROVED): does every triangle-free graph on $5n$ vertices contain
at most $n^5$ copies of $C_5$? Győri proved the bound $1.03n^5$, improved by
Füredi; the exact bound was proved independently by Grzesik [Gr12] and by
Hatami–Hladký–Král–Norine–Razborov [HHKNR13]. The Lean theorem
(`conjectures/24.lean:15-23`) asserts: for every $n$ and every triangle-free
`G : SimpleGraph (Fin (5 * n))` (with a decidability instance quantified in),
the number of injective `f : Fin 5 → Fin (5 * n)` with `G.Adj (f i) (f (i+1))`
for all `i : Fin 5` is at most `10 * n ^ 5`. Read back independently: it counts
*labeled* 5-cycles, of which each unordered $C_5$ copy contributes exactly
$|D_5| = 10$, so the bound is exactly equivalent to "at most $n^5$ copies" —
the affirmative (true) direction of the question, which is the correct polarity
for this pipeline's direct-assertion convention on solved problems. I found no
semantic defects; the nits are scholarship-level (citation keys used but never
defined, the page's four source keys absent, no record of the page's
generalization question).

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "Does every triangle-free
  graph on $5n$ vertices contain at most $n^5$ copies of $C_5$?"
- Independent back-translation of lines 15–23: "for all $n$, every triangle-free
  simple graph on the vertex set $\{0,\dots,5n-1\}$ admits at most $10n^5$
  injective maps $f$ from the 5-element cyclic index set such that consecutive
  images (cyclically) are adjacent."
- **Labeled-vs-unordered accounting, verified by hand:** an injective `f` with
  all five cyclic adjacencies traces a cycle subgraph on its 5-vertex image
  (5 distinct vertices, edges $\{f(i), f(i{+}1)\}$); conversely each unordered
  $C_5$ subgraph admits exactly the maps $f\circ\sigma$ for $\sigma$ in the
  dihedral group of the index cycle, $|D_5| = 5\cdot 2 = 10$, no more (any
  adjacency-preserving indexing of a 5-cycle is one of the 10). So
  `card = 10 · (#unordered C₅ subgraphs)` holds *exactly, for every graph* (no
  triangle-freeness needed for the accounting), and `card ≤ 10·n⁵` ⟺
  `#copies ≤ n⁵`. The encoding is faithful, not merely asymptotically so.
- "Copies of $C_5$" means subgraphs isomorphic to $C_5$; in a triangle-free
  graph every 5-cycle is automatically induced (any chord of a $C_5$ joins two
  vertices at cycle-distance 2 and closes a triangle), so the encoding's
  chord-agnostic adjacency test cannot over- or under-count against any
  reasonable reading of "copies".
- Types: vertex set `Fin (5 * n)` is exactly "a graph on $5n$ vertices" (the
  source restricts to multiples of 5; so does the Lean); counting in ℕ with the
  integer bound `10 * n ^ 5` — no casts needed, no coercion issues.
- Independent confirmation: the upstream formal-conjectures file at HEAD
  (2026-08-16) states the same problem as `answer(True) ↔ ∀ n G, G.CliqueFree 3
  → G.copyCount (cycleGraph 5) ≤ n ^ 5`, and Mathlib's `SimpleGraph.copyCount
  G H` is "the number of subgraphs of `G` isomorphic to `H`"
  (`Mathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Copy.lean:528-530`) — the unordered count
  with bound $n^5$, i.e. exactly the input's statement divided by the factor 10.
- Not vacuous or trivially true: the bound is sharp (balanced blow-up of $C_5$:
  triangle-free, exactly $n^5$ copies — every closed 5-walk in $C_5$ must use
  steps of a single direction, so the blow-up's 5-cycles pick one vertex per
  part), so nothing weaker than the true theorem satisfies the statement.
  Degenerate case $n = 0$: `Fin 0` is empty, no functions `Fin 5 → Fin 0`
  exist, both sides are 0 — consistent.
