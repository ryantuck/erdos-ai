## Summary

The problem (Erdős–Turán, source [Va99, 5.74]): "Describe (by statistical means) the
arithmetic structure of the orders of subgroups of $S_n$." The page owner reproduces it
verbatim and explicitly states he is "not entirely sure what it is asking for"; the page
adds "The original source is ambiguous as to what the problem is". Status: OPEN. The
Lean file defines `IsSubgroupOrderOfSn n m` (∃ subgroup of `Perm (Fin n)` of
cardinality m — correct) and asserts one concrete interpretation: the proportion of
divisors of $n!$ that occur as subgroup orders of $S_n$ tends to 0, encoded in ε–N form
as `count < ε * total`. Read back independently, the Lean says exactly what the
docstring's interpretation says, and the encoding is trap-free. Since the original is
not a proposition at all, no formalization can be literally faithful; the file's
transparent "concrete interpretation" framing is the right treatment, and the
interpretation itself is mathematically reasonable (Lagrange gives the divisor ambient
set; structural obstructions make most exponent patterns implausible as subgroup
orders). The nits: the cited key `[Va99]` is defined nowhere in the file, the OPEN
status and the page's double ambiguity caveat are unrecorded, and the docstring states
the conjectured conclusion ("becomes a vanishingly small fraction") in the indicative,
as if established.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass, with an inherent interpretation caveat
(source-verified).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "Describe (by statistical means)
  the arithmetic structure of the orders of subgroups of $S_n$." This is a descriptive
  request, not a proposition; there is no English proposition to diff against. The
  docstring reproduces the statement verbatim and flags the ambiguity, matching the
  page's own caveats.
- Independent back-translation of lines 30–35: "for every real ε > 0 there is N such
  that for all n ≥ N, the number of divisors m of n! for which $S_n$ has a subgroup of
  order m is less than ε times the number of divisors of n!." That is precisely "the
  proportion of divisors of n! that are subgroup orders of $S_n$ tends to 0": the
  divisor count τ(n!) ≥ 1 always (n! ≥ 1, so `Nat.divisors` is nonempty — note
  `Nat.divisors 0 = ∅` is never hit), hence `count < ε·total ⟺ count/total < ε`, and
  since the ratio lies in [0,1] the ε–N form is exactly `Tendsto (ratio) atTop (nhds 0)`.
  Lean and docstring agree term-for-term.
- Completeness of the ambient set: by Lagrange every subgroup order divides
  |S_n| = n!, so filtering `n.factorial.divisors` misses no subgroup order. Correct.
- Not vacuous and not trivially false: the claim is genuinely asymptotic and
  substantive. Hand-computed small cases: for n ≤ 4 *every* divisor of n! is a subgroup
  order (e.g. S₄ realizes all 8 divisors of 24), and S₅ realizes 13 of the 16 divisors
  of 120 — the missing ones are 15 (C₁₅ needs degree 3+5=8 > 5), 30 (every group of
  order 30 contains an element of order 15; max element order in S₅ is 6), and 40 (a
  Sylow-5 count forces a normal C₅, so H ≤ N(C₅) = F₂₀ of order 20 < 40). So the
  proportion starts at 1 and the statement's content is entirely in the limit — the
  `∃ N` handles the small cases correctly.
- **Interpretation caveat (confidence-limiting, not a defect):** the formalized
  conjecture appears nowhere on the page — it is the upstream model's invention. It is
  plausible: a "generic" divisor of n! has independent random exponents
  a_p ≈ ν_p(n!)/2, and realizing such patterns is structurally obstructed — e.g. for
  primes p < q in (n/3, n/2], no subgroup of S_n has order exactly p²q² (both Sylows
  are forced normal since q ≢ ±1 mod p and p² ≢ 1 mod q for primes in this range, so
  the group would be C_p² × C_q², of minimal degree 2p + 2q > n). But no proof and no
  literature are known to this reviewer, and the page offers none. The file must (and
  in v2 does) present this as the formalizer's conjecture, not as the problem's or as
  known fact. This differs from sibling 1162, whose direct assertion was an established
  theorem [RoTr25]; here the asserted statement is itself open.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- No ℕ subtraction anywhere.
- No division anywhere: the multiplied form `count < ε * total` deliberately avoids
  ratio division and is equivalent to it because total ≥ 1 (above). No
  division-by-zero or ℕ-flooring surface at any n, including n = 0
  (0! = 1, `divisors 1 = {1}`, and `IsSubgroupOrderOfSn 0 m ⟺ m = 1` via the trivial
  group — true values, not junk).
- No `Finset.range`; `∀ n ≥ N` has no off-by-one against the source (there is no
  source indexing to match).
- No vacuous bounded quantifiers; `∀ ε > 0 → ∃ N → ∀ n ≥ N` is the standard and
  correct ε–N encoding of a limit equal to 0 for a nonnegative sequence; strict `<` is
  the right inequality for it.
- `Nat.card ↥H` for `H : Subgroup (Perm (Fin n))`: the ambient group is finite, so
  every subgroup is finite and `Nat.card` is the honest cardinality (no junk 0).
- `Finset.filter` over the undecidable predicate is licensed by the local
  `Classical.propDecidable` instance (line 10) inside `noncomputable section` — the
  standard pattern; nothing silently changes the predicate's meaning.
- No `StrictMono`/`Monotone` or interval-endpoint issues arise.

## E. Prior-review audit (`deepmind/ai-review/1163.md`)

   pre-correction draft and rationalized its error instead of catching it. Same
   recurring failure class as the 1131/1132/1138/1162 audits (hallucinated [Va99]
   expansions: Vardi/Vaughan/Vershik/Montgomery across the corpus).
2. **Fabricated evidential support for the interpretation.** Its §6.5 claims "It is
   widely believed (and supported by computational evidence) that the number of
   divisors of n! grows much faster than the number of distinct subgroup orders." No
   source is given; the page has no remarks; this reviewer knows no literature on this
   exact statement; and the checkable computations point the *other* way at small
   sizes (proportion 1 for n ≤ 4, 13/16 for S₅) — genuine computational evidence for

   heuristics (support/normalizer obstructions, cf. A1), not on a "wide belief" or

   total ≥ 1 (1 ∣ n! and n! ≠ 0); the ℝ-casts are correctly placed. Its §3
   observation that exactly one interpretation of a vague problem is captured, and
   transparently so, matches this review.
4. **Scope drift, harmless.** Its §4 describes `open Equiv Classical` and its §1/§4
