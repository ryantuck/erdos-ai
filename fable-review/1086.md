## Summary

The problem (source-verified, verbatim): "Let $g(n)$ be minimal such that any set of $n$
points in $\mathbb{R}^2$ contains the vertices of at most $g(n)$ many triangles with the
same area. Estimate $g(n)$." Status OPEN. Erdős–Purdy [ErPu71] proved
$n^2\log\log n \ll g(n) \ll n^{5/2}$ and believed the lower bound closer to the truth;
the best known upper bound is $g(n) \ll n^{20/9}$ [RaSh17]. The Lean file formalizes the
Erdős–Purdy belief as: for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is $C > 0$ such that every
finite $S \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ and every $A$ determine at most
$C|S|^{2+\varepsilon}$ ordered triangles of area $A$. The shape, quantifier order, and
open-status encoding are right, but the pre-fix counting definition admitted collinear
triples as "triangles of area 0", making the theorem **refutably false**: $n$ collinear
points give $n(n-1)(n-2) \sim n^3$ ordered zero-area triples, and $n^3 \not\le
Cn^{2+\varepsilon}$ for $\varepsilon < 1$. The prior ai-review saw this case and excused
it with an unsound argument. Fixed by excluding zero-area triples in the definition.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — one defect (fixed); otherwise pass (source-verified).**

- Independent back-translation of `erdos_1086` (`deepmind/1086.lean`): "for every
  $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a $C > 0$ such that for every finite point set $S$ in the
  plane and every real $A$, the number of ordered triples of distinct points of $S$
  whose triangle has unsigned area $A$ is at most $C\,|S|^{2+\varepsilon}$."
- The source's literal ask is the open-ended "Estimate $g(n)$"; the file formalizes the
  page's recorded belief of Erdős–Purdy ("believed the lower bound to be closer to the
  truth") in its standard reading $g(n) = n^{2+o(1)}$, i.e.
  $\forall \varepsilon > 0,\ g(n) \ll_\varepsilon n^{2+\varepsilon}$. This
  interpretation step is documented in the theorem docstring and matches the raw first
  pass ("The conjecture is that $g(n) = O(n^{2+\varepsilon})$ for every
  $\varepsilon > 0$") and corpus practice for "Estimate…" problems. Reasonable scope,
  not a defect.
- Quantifier order `∀ ε, ∃ C, ∀ S A` is correct: $C$ depends on $\varepsilon$ only,
  uniformly in $S$ and $A$ — exactly $\ll_\varepsilon$. Ordered-triple counting
  (factor 6, absorbed by $C$) is explicitly documented and harmless.
- **[defect] Not "not trivially false": pre-fix, the statement was literally false.**
  `countTrianglesWithArea S 0` counted all ordered triples of pairwise-distinct
  *collinear* points (unsigned area $= 0$). Taking $S$ = $n$ collinear points and
  $A = 0$: the count is $n(n-1)(n-2) \sim n^3$, while the claimed bound is
  $Cn^{2+\varepsilon}$ — false for every fixed $C$ once $\varepsilon < 1$ (e.g.
  $\varepsilon = 1/10$, $n \to \infty$). The informal problem counts *triangles*, which
  are nondegenerate by definition (the source's equivalent form, "triangles of area
  $1$", makes this explicit — a positive area presupposes nondegeneracy). **Fixed** by
  adding `triangle_area p q r ≠ 0` to the filter, so collinear triples are never
  counted and `countTrianglesWithArea S 0 = 0`; for $A > 0$ the count is unchanged
  (area $= A > 0$ already implies nondegeneracy). Not compile-verified (no `lake build`
  in this container).
- Types: `Finset ℝ²` with `ℝ² = EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 2)` (from the shared library) is
  an appropriate model of finite planar point sets; the count is cast to ℝ before
  comparison, and `(S.card : ℝ) ^ (2 + ε)` is a real-exponent power of a nonnegative
  base. No indexing conventions arise (no sequences).

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- No ℕ subtraction, no ℕ division. The only division is inside the library's
  `triangle_area` ( `/ 2` in ℝ) — exact and safe.
- `(S.card : ℝ) ^ (2 + ε)` at `S.card = 0`: `Real.rpow 0 (2+ε) = 0` since
  $2 + \varepsilon > 0$; the count is also 0 for $|S| < 3$ (a pairwise-distinct triple
  needs 3 elements), so boundary cases hold and are not vacuously wrong.
- For $A < 0$ the filter is unsatisfiable (`|·| = A < 0`), count 0 — matches the
  informal problem, where negative areas index nothing.
- No `range`, no bounded quantifiers, no filter/limit encodings (`∀ S` rather than an
  asymptotic filter is the *stronger* uniform-constant form of $\ll$, standard for this
  corpus and equivalent here since the count is trivially finite for each $n$).
- No `StrictMono`/`Monotone` or interval-endpoint issues.

**A3. Definitions — one defect (the A1 item, located in the def), otherwise pass.**

- `countTrianglesWithArea` now matches its docstring and the source's notion of
  "triangles with area $A$", including degenerate inputs: empty/small $S$ give 0;
  $A \le 0$ gives 0; each unordered triangle is counted exactly 6 times (its three
  vertices are distinct, so all $3! = 6$ orderings appear). Pre-fix it disagreed with
  its own docstring, which said "form a triangle".
- The library `triangle_area` (recovered from logs:
  `positiveOrientation.areaForm (a -ᵥ c) (b -ᵥ c) / 2`, in namespace
  `EuclideanGeometry`, with lemma `triangle_area_eq_det`) is a **signed** area, so the
  file's `|triangle_area p q r|` is the correct unsigned area, and
  `triangle_area p q r ≠ 0` is exactly affine independence (nondegeneracy) — which also
  subsumes the three pairwise-distinctness conjuncts (kept for clarity; documented).
- No `sorry` in any def.

**A4. Question form and answer polarity — pass.**

- The source is an open-ended estimation request ("Estimate $g(n)$"), not a yes/no
  question and not a solved value request, so there is no `answer()` wrapper to check;
  the direct-assertion encoding of the specific conjectured bound, tagged
  `research open` with `by sorry`, is the corpus's standard treatment (cf. 1005's
  handling and its `variants.limit_eq_one_quarter`). The theorem is binder-free before
  the colon.

**A5. Solution status — pass (source-verified).**

- Recovered page banner: **OPEN** ("This is open, and cannot be resolved with a finite
  computation."), with the owner's standard disclaimer. `@[category research open]` and
  both docstrings agree. Attributions match the page: question attributed (by Erdős and
  Purdy) to Oppenheim; bounds by Erdős–Purdy, improvements by Pach–Sharir,
  Dumitrescu–Sharir–Tóth, Apfelbaum–Sharir, Apfelbaum, best bound Raz–Sharir. The page
  says "Formalised statement? No" as of access (2026-02-22).

## E. Prior-review audit (`ai-review/1086.md`)

Three substantive findings, one of them the defect this review fixes:

1. **Unsound "not a bug" argument on degenerate triangles (the recurring
   invent-then-excuse pattern).** §6 correctly observes that for $A = 0$ the count of
   collinear triples "can be … $O(n^3)$ in the worst case (all points collinear)", then
   concludes "this is not a bug" via two unsound steps: (a) "even $n^3$ is bounded by
   $Cn^{2+\varepsilon}$ for large enough $C$ for any fixed finite $n$" — irrelevant,
   since one $C$ must work for **all** $n$ simultaneously (by this logic every
   statement with finite per-$n$ counts would be true); (b) "the asymptotic bound
   $g(n) \ll n^{2+\varepsilon}$ still holds for the $A = 0$ case by Szemerédi-Trotter
   type bounds" — false: Szemerédi–Trotter bounds incidences, not collinear triples,
   and $n$ points on a single line give $\binom{n}{3}$ zero-area triples, so **no**
   $n^{2+\varepsilon}$ bound holds at $A = 0$. The review thus endorsed an encoding
   that its own reading refutes (the 1005-audit pattern), missing the file's one real
   [defect].
2. **Stale factual claims about the artifact.** §1 says the file "defines its own
   `triangleArea` in namespace `Erdos1086`" and §2's table marks [Er75f], [PaSh92],
   [DST09], [ApSh10], [Ap13] as **missing** — none of this is true of
   `deepmind/1086.lean`, which imports and uses the shared `triangle_area` and defines
   all five keys. The review evidently examined an earlier revision; its
   recommendations were applied afterwards without the review being re-run. (The style
   review `reviews/1086.md` is stale the same way: it asserts "exactly one import" and
   "no `open` statements; none present", while the final file has two imports and
   `open EuclideanGeometry`, and it checkmarks a `triangleArea` that no longer exists.)
3. **Missed citation error.** The [Er75f] entry it audited (and implicitly endorsed as
   "should add" with the file's eventual wording) carries the wrong title — see B1.

What it got right: the ordered-triples/factor-6 analysis; the `∀ε ∃C` encoding of
$\ll_\varepsilon$; the uniform-over-$S$-and-$A$ reading of $g(n)$; the variants survey
(higher-dimensional results and known bounds), which matches the recovered page,
including [Pu74]'s role.
