## Summary

The problem (verbatim from the recovered page): "Let $k\geq 0$. Let $f(k)$ be such that
every graph on $n\geq f(k)$ vertices with at least $\binom{n-k-1}{2}+\binom{k+2}{2}+1$
edges contains a cycle on $n-k$ vertices. Determine or estimate $f(k)$." The page marks
it **SOLVED** ("resolved in some other way than a proof or disproof"): Woodall [Wo72]
proved that every graph on $n \ge 2k+3$ vertices with that many edges contains a cycle
on $\ell$ vertices for *all* $3 \le \ell \le n-k$, which "settles this question
completely". The Lean theorem `erdos_1012` (`deepmind/1012.lean`) states exactly
Woodall's theorem — the complete solution, which subsumes the original question at
$\ell = n-k$ — and, read back independently, matches the source term-by-term (edge
threshold, vertex bound, cycle-length range, `Walk.IsCycle` encoding). The one defect
found is in the *docstrings*, which claimed Woodall's result gives "$f(k) = 2k+3$": that
is false ($f(0) = f(1) = 1$ by Ore and Bondy, stated in the same docstring three lines
earlier), is not claimed by the source page, and survived three prior reviews. Woodall's
theorem yields $f(k) \le 2k+3$. Fixed in place.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Independent back-translation of `erdos_1012` (lines 75–81): "for all $k, n \in \mathbb{N}$
  with $n \ge 2k+3$, every simple graph $G$ on vertex set `Fin n` with
  $|E(G)| \ge \binom{n-k-1}{2} + \binom{k+2}{2} + 1$ contains, for every
  $3 \le \ell \le n-k$, a cycle of length $\ell$." This is verbatim Woodall's theorem as
  stated in the page's remarks. Formalizing the complete solution rather than the
  open-ended "determine or estimate" prompt is the right scope (see A4).
- "Cycle on $\ell$ vertices" (source) = cycle of length $\ell$ (Lean): for
  `p : G.Walk v v` with `p.IsCycle`, `p.length` equals the number of distinct vertices,
  and Mathlib's `IsCycle` forces length ≥ 3 — consistent with `hl₁ : 3 ≤ l`.
- Interpretive addition, acceptable: the source says "Let $f(k)$ be such that…"; the
  module docstring reads $f(k)$ as the *least* such integer. That is the standard
  reading, the one under which Ore's $f(0)=1$ and Bondy's $f(1)=1$ are exact values, and
  the one that makes "determine $f(k)$" meaningful. Noted, not a defect.
- Not vacuous: the edge hypothesis is satisfiable for every $n \ge 2k+3$ —
  $\binom{n}{2} - \left[\binom{n-k-1}{2} + \binom{k+2}{2} + 1\right] \ge (k+1)^2 - 1 \ge 0$
  at $n = 2k+3$ (equality iff $k=0$, where the threshold is exactly $\binom{3}{2}=3$ and
  the unique witness $K_3$ has its 3-cycle). Sharpness sanity check: two cliques
  $K_{n-k-1}$ and $K_{k+2}$ glued at one vertex have $n$ vertices,
  $\binom{n-k-1}{2}+\binom{k+2}{2}$ edges, and circumference $n-k-1 < n-k$ — so the
  "+1" in the threshold is exactly right, confirming the formula was transcribed
  correctly.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- ℕ subtraction: `n - k - 1` under `hn : n ≥ 2*k + 3` satisfies
  $n-k-1 \ge k+2 \ge 2$ — no truncation; `l ≤ n - k` has $n - k \ge k+3 \ge 3$ — no
  truncation, and the range $3 \le \ell \le n-k$ is nonempty. (The new `variants.bondy`
  does hit ℕ truncation at $n \le 1$; its docstring documents why it is harmless —
  the edge hypothesis is unsatisfiable for $n \le 3$.)
- No division anywhere; `Nat.choose` is exact.
- No `Finset.range`; the only cardinality is `G.edgeFinset.card`, well-defined via
  `[DecidableRel G.Adj]` + `Fintype (Fin n)`.
- No vacuous bounded quantifiers in the main theorem (per the satisfiability computation
  above the hypotheses are jointly satisfiable for every $n \ge 2k+3$).
- No filters/densities; no `StrictMono`/`Monotone`; inequalities `3 ≤ l ≤ n - k` are
  non-strict on both ends, matching the source's $3 \le \ell \le n-k$.

**A3. Definitions — pass.**

- The styled file has no helper defs: the raw pass's bespoke
  `ContainsCycleOfLength1012` (injective `Fin m → V` with modular adjacency) was
  replaced, following the prior review, by the inline Mathlib-idiomatic
  `∃ v (p : G.Walk v v), p.IsCycle ∧ p.length = l`. The two encodings are equivalent
  for $m \ge 3$ in a simple graph, so the replacement preserved semantics. No `sorry`
  in any def (there are none).

**A4. Question form and answer polarity — pass (judgment call, endorsed).**

- The source is a **value request** ("Determine or estimate $f(k)$"), which per the
  checklist would suggest `f(k) = answer(…)`. The formalization instead asserts
  Woodall's theorem directly, with no `answer()`. I endorse this, more strongly than
  the prior style review's "borderline": an `answer()` encoding would require a concrete
  value, and the *only* candidate the old docstring offered — $f(k) = 2k+3$ — is
  **false** ($f(0) = f(1) = 1$). The page gives no closed form for $f(k)$; what is known
  is $f(k) \le 2k+3$ (Woodall) plus the exact small cases. Encoding the complete
  solution as a direct assertion is the faithful choice; an `answer(2*k+3)` variant
  would have introduced a genuine mathematical error.
- Direct assertion in the *true* direction (the page's SOLVED banner refers to exactly
  this resolution); binders before the colon are correct universal quantification for
  an assertion (no `answer()`/iff in scope, so the A4 binder trap does not apply).

**A5. Solution status — pass (source-verified).**

- Recovered banner: **SOLVED**, tooltip "This has been resolved in some other way than a
  proof or disproof" — i.e. resolved neither by proving nor refuting a yes/no statement,
  matching a determine-type problem settled by Woodall's theorem. `@[category research
  solved]` and both docstrings agree. Attributions (Erdős existence; Ore $f(0)=1$;
  Bondy $f(1)=1$; Woodall complete solution) all match the page's remarks.
- **[defect] (docstring, fixed):** both the module docstring ("This settles the question
  completely with $f(k) = 2k + 3$", pre-fix line 35) and the theorem docstring ("this
  answers Erdős' original question with $f(k) = 2k + 3$", pre-fix lines 64–65)
  overstated the solution. The page says only "This settles this question completely."
  Woodall's theorem shows every $n \ge 2k+3$ has the defining property, i.e.
  $f(k) \le 2k+3$; the claim $f(k) = 2k+3$ is contradicted by $f(0) = f(1) = 1$ stated
  in the *same docstring*. A wrong docstring over a right theorem is still a defect
  (checklist C1); since it misstates the problem's solution I weight it seriously, but
  the Lean proposition itself is unaffected, so the verdict is not capped at NEEDS
  REVISION by Part A. Fixed in both places (see Addendum).
