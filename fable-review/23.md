## Summary

The problem (OPEN, falsifiable): can every triangle-free graph on $5n$
vertices be made bipartite by deleting at most $n^2$ edges? The blow-up of
$C_5$ shows $n^2$ would be best possible; the best known bound is
$1.064n^2$ (Balogh–Clemen–Lidický [BCL21]). The Lean
(`conjectures/23.lean:21-28`) asserts: for every $n$ and every triangle-free
`G : SimpleGraph (Fin (5 * n))` there is a 2-colouring
`f : Fin (5 * n) → Fin 2` such that the number of monochromatic edges
(ordered pairs `p.1 < p.2` that are adjacent and equi-coloured) is at most
`n ^ 2`. Read back independently, this is exactly equivalent to the
deletion formulation of the question (equivalence re-derived in A1), and
the direct assertion of the conjectured "yes" direction is this raw
corpus's convention for open yes/no questions. No semantic defects found;
the nits are scholarship enrichment (status, bibliography, the [Er92b]
generalization remark) and minor style.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "Can every
  triangle-free graph on $5n$ vertices be made bipartite by deleting at most
  $n^2$ edges?" The module docstring reproduces it faithfully.
- Independent back-translation of lines 21–28: "for all $n$, every
  triangle-free graph $G$ on vertex set $\mathrm{Fin}(5n)$ admits a
  2-colouring $f$ with at most $n^2$ monochromatic edges."
- **The colouring encoding is exactly equivalent to the deletion
  formulation**, not merely analogous. (⇐) Given $f$ with $\le n^2$
  monochromatic edges, delete exactly those; in the remaining graph every
  edge is bichromatic under $f$, so the colour classes are independent sets
  and the graph is bipartite. (⇒) If deleting an edge set $E'$ with
  $|E'| \le n^2$ leaves a bipartite graph, take $f$ = its bipartition
  colouring; every monochromatic edge of $G$ under $f$ is absent from the
  bipartite remainder, hence lies in $E'$, so the count is $\le n^2$.
  (Empty colour classes are fine: "bipartite" does not require both parts
  nonempty, and the constant colouring corresponds to deleting all edges.)
  The file's own docstring states this formalization choice explicitly.
- **Edge counting is term-by-term exact**: the filter over
  `univ ×ˢ univ` with `p.1 < p.2 ∧ G.Adj p.1 p.2 ∧ f p.1 = f p.2` counts
  each unordered monochromatic edge exactly once — adjacency is irreflexive
  so `p.1 ≠ p.2`, and `Fin (5 * n)` is linearly ordered so exactly one of
  `(u, v)`, `(v, u)` passes the `<` test. No double-counting, no missed
  loops.
- Vertex set: `Fin (5 * n)` for a "graph on $5n$ vertices" is faithful —
  the asserted property is isomorphism-invariant, and every graph on $5n$
  vertices is isomorphic to one on `Fin (5 * n)`. (The upstream
  formal-conjectures rendering quantifies over arbitrary `V` with
  `Fintype.card V = 5 * n`; semantically identical scope.)
- `G.CliqueFree 3` ⟺ no 3-clique ⟺ triangle-free. Correct.
- Not vacuous/trivial: $n = 0$ gives the empty graph and the empty
  colouring with $0 \le 0$ — true and consistent with the source read at
  $n = 0$; for $n \ge 1$ the statement is a substantive open conjecture
  (the $C_5$ blow-up shows the bound would be tight, so no slack
  trivializes it).

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- No `Finset.range`; the pair enumeration is `univ ×ˢ univ`, complete by
  construction. No off-by-one.
- No vacuous bounded quantifiers: the only degenerate instance is $n = 0$,

- No filters/limits to encode; the problem is a for-all-finite-graphs

- Strictness: `p.1 < p.2` (strict) is exactly what unordered-edge
