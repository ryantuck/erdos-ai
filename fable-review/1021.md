## Summary

The problem asks: is it true that for every $k \ge 3$ there is a constant $c_k > 0$ such
that $\mathrm{ex}(n, G_k) \ll n^{3/2 - c_k}$, where $G_k$ is the 1-subdivision of $K_k$
(the bipartite graph between $y_1,\dots,y_k$ and $z_1,\dots,z_{\binom{k}{2}}$, each
$z_j$ joined to a unique pair of $y_i$)? An Erdős–Simonovits conjecture, PROVED in the
affirmative by Conlon–Lee ($c_k = 6^{-k}$), improved by Janzer ($c_k = 1/(4k-6)$). The
Lean (`deepmind/1021.lean`) builds $G_k$ on the vertex type
`Fin k ⊕ {p : Fin k × Fin k // p.1 < p.2}`, defines non-induced subgraph containment via
injective homomorphism, and states `answer(True) ↔ ∀ k ≥ 3, ∃ c > 0, ∃ C > 0, ∀ n, ∀ G
on Fin n, G G_k-free → |E(G)| ≤ C·n^{3/2-c}`. Read back independently, the Lean and the
English agree exactly; the only defects found are bibliographic (Part B), both now
fixed. Notably, the *problem page itself* contains a mathematical error (it states
$\mathrm{ex}(n, C_6) \ll n^{7/6}$, which is below the known $\gg n^{4/3}$ lower bound);
the file's $C_6$ variant already carried the correct exponent $4/3$, and the
discrepancy is now documented in the docstrings.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "Is it true that, for every
  $k\geq 3$, there is a constant $c_k>0$ such that $\mathrm{ex}(n,G_k) \ll
  n^{3/2-c_k}$, where $G_k$ is the bipartite graph between $\{y_1,\ldots,y_k\}$ and
  $\{z_1,\ldots,z_{\binom{k}{2}}\}$, with each $z_j$ joined to a unique pair of $y_i$?"
  The page's remarks confirm $G_k$ "can also be described as the 1-subdivision of
  $K_k$", as the docstring says.
- Independent back-translation of `erdos_1021` (line 113): "for every $k \ge 3$ there
  exist $c > 0$ and $C > 0$ such that every $n$-vertex graph with no (not necessarily
  induced) copy of the 1-subdivision of $K_k$ has at most $C n^{3/2-c}$ edges." This is
  exactly $\mathrm{ex}(n, G_k) \ll n^{3/2-c_k}$ with the Vinogradov $\ll$ unfolded.
- $\ll$ / all-$n$ vs eventually: the `∀ n` form is equivalent to the eventual form
  here — for the finitely many small $n \ge 1$, $n^{3/2-c} \ge 1$ (any admissible $c$
  can be shrunk below $3/2$), so $C$ absorbs them; at $n = 0$ both sides are $0$
  (`Real.rpow` gives $0^{y} = 0$ for $y \ne 0$), or trivially $0 \le C$ if the exponent
  were $0$. No degenerate failure.
- Graph encoding verified vertex-by-vertex: edge-vertices are indexed by the subtype of
  ordered pairs $i < j$, so there are exactly $\binom{k}{2}$ of them, each adjacent to
  exactly its two endpoints (`subdivKAdj`, lines 60–65); $y$–$y$ and $z$–$z$ pairs are
  non-adjacent (`| _, _ => False`), so the graph is bipartite as in the source.
  `symm`/`loopless` proofs are correct by case analysis (the two mixed cases carry
  syntactically identical predicates; all diagonal cases are `False`).
- `containsSubgraph` (injective vertex map sending edges to edges) is the correct
  **non-induced** containment for extremal numbers. This choice is load-bearing: with
  induced containment (e.g. `Nonempty (G_k ↪g G)`) the statement would be **false** —
  $K_N$ contains no induced copy of the non-complete graph $G_k$ yet has
  $\sim n^2/2$ edges. (See Part E: the prior style review's reuse suggestion would have
  introduced exactly this defect.)
- Not vacuous: the hypothesis class is nonempty (sparse graphs are $G_k$-free) and the
  bound is the substantive Conlon–Lee theorem; no degenerate witness trivializes it.
- Indexing: no sequence indexing in this problem; `k ≥ 3` matches the source range
  literally.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- **ℕ subtraction:** none. The exponent arithmetic `(3 : ℝ) / 2 - c` is entirely in ℝ
  (and in the new Janzer variant, `4 * (k : ℝ) - 6` casts *before* subtracting; for
  $k \ge 3$ the denominator is $\ge 6$, so the field division is safe and nonzero).
- **Division:** `(3 : ℝ) / 2` is exact field division; no ℕ division anywhere.
- **`Finset.range`:** not used; no off-by-one surface.
- **Vacuous quantifiers:** `∀ n` includes $n = 0, 1, 2$, where every graph on `Fin n`
  is $G_k$-free ($G_k$ has $k + \binom{k}{2} \ge 6$ vertices); the bound holds there
  trivially ($0 \le C \cdot 0$ resp. $|E| = 0, \le 1 \cdot C$), matching the informal
  statement's indifference to small $n$. No silent alteration.
- **Filter encodings:** none needed; $\ll$ is correctly unfolded to `∃ C > 0, ∀ n`.
- **StrictMono/intervals:** not applicable; `k ≥ 3`, `c > 0`, `C > 0` are all
  non-strict/strict exactly as the source demands.

## E. Prior-review audit (`ai-review/1021.md`)

Accurate on the definitions and the main statement, but three substantive findings:

1. **Double arithmetic error + uncritical propagation of the page's false bound.**
   Its §3 states: "Bondy–Simonovits [BoSi74] proved ex(n, C₆) ≪ n^{1+1/3} = n^{7/6}.
   This gives c₃ = 1/3." Both equalities are wrong: $n^{1+1/3} = n^{4/3} \ne n^{7/6}$,
   and the correct saving is $c_3 = 3/2 - 4/3 = 1/6$ (it wrote the value $3/2 - 7/6 =
   1/3$ that would follow from the page's erroneous $7/6$). Its §2 likewise repeats
   "proved ex(n, C₆) ≪ n^{7/6}" without noticing that $7/6$ contradicts the
   generalized-quadrangle lower bound $\gg n^{4/3}$ — i.e. it endorsed a source claim
   that is *refutable from standard knowledge*, the same failure class as 1000's
   invented-then-excused discrepancy. The `deepmind` file's $C_6$ variant (added after
   that review) uses the correct $4/3$ and $c_3 = 1/6$.
2. **Reversed inequality.** Its §3 claims "The relationship ex(n, H_k) ≤ ex(n, G_k)
   holds since H_k-freeness is weaker than G_k-freeness." The premise is right
   ($G_k \subseteq H_k$, so $G_k$-free $\Rightarrow$ $H_k$-free) but the conclusion is
   backwards: the weaker restriction admits *more* graphs, hence a *larger* extremal
   number — $\mathrm{ex}(n, G_k) \le \mathrm{ex}(n, H_k)$. The review derived the
   opposite of what its own premise implies (cf. the 1005 audit's
   "endorsing an encoding that contradicts its own reading" pattern).
3. **Misses.** It did not notice (a) that [Er74c] had no bibliography entry (it marked
   Er74c "✅ Referenced in formalization" without checking for a definition); (b) the
   question-form/binder issue that `reviews/1021.md` caught (later fixed); (c) any of
   the [Er64c] bibliographic facts (it flagged the key as merely "missing", and the
   subsequent enrichment attached the wrong paper unchecked). Its line references
   (e.g. "`containsSubgraph` (line 63)") match neither the raw nor the styled file.

What it got right: the graph-construction and `containsSubgraph` correctness analyses
are sound; the universal-vs-eventually-$n$ argument is correct; `answer(True)` polarity
correct; the missing Er64c/BoSi74 citations and missing $C_6$ variant it flagged were
subsequently added to `deepmind/1021.lean`.

Also audited `reviews/1021.md` (style review, reference only): its two blockers
(answer-wrapper, binders) were both fixed in the styled file. But its non-blocking
suggestion that `containsSubgraph` "duplicates the concept of `Nonempty (H ↪g G)`" is
mathematically wrong: `SimpleGraph.Embedding` (`↪g`) is an *induced* embedding
(`Adj ↔ Adj`), and using it would make `erdos_1021` false ($K_N$ has no induced $G_k$).
Its checkbox "Bibliography entries for related papers: [Er71], [Er74c], [CoLe21],
[Ja19]" was inaccurate — the version it reviewed had no formatted bibliography at all,
and [Er74c] never received an entry until this review.
