## Summary

The problem (verbatim from the recovered page): "Let $G$ be a graph with girth $>4$ (that
is, it contains no cycles of length $3$ or $4$). Can the edges of $G$ always be directed
such that there is no directed cycle, and reversing the direction of any edge also creates
no directed cycle?" Status banner: **DISPROVED** ("This has been solved in the negative")
— Nešetřil and Rödl [NeRo78b] proved that for every integer $g$ there is a graph of girth
$g$ every orientation of which contains a directed cycle or a cycle obtained from one by
reversing a single directed edge. The Lean main theorem (`deepmind/1006.lean:82`) encodes
the yes/no question as `answer(False) ↔ ∀ (V) (G), 5 ≤ G.egirth → ∃ o : Orientation G,
o.IsRobustlyAcyclic`, over hand-rolled but correct definitions of orientation, acyclicity
(`Relation.TransGen` irreflexivity), and single-edge reversal. The main theorem is a
faithful, correctly-polarized translation. The pre-existing variant
`erdos_1006_arbitrary_girth`, however, was defective: adopted verbatim from the prior
ai-review's suggestion, its formal statement said something different from (and far weaker
and more trivial than) the Nešetřil–Rödl theorem its docstring described. Fixed in place.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — main theorem pass (source-verified); variant DEFECT (fixed).**

- Independent back-translation of `erdos_1006` (lines 82–85): "the following is false:
  every simple graph (any vertex type) whose extended girth is at least 5 admits an
  orientation that is acyclic and stays acyclic after reversing any single directed
  edge." This is exactly the source question with answer "no". Girth $> 4$ ⟺ no cycles of
  length 3 or 4 ⟺ `5 ≤ G.egirth` (Mathlib `egirth : ℕ∞` is the infimum of cycle lengths,
  `⊤` for acyclic graphs; cycles in a simple graph have length ≥ 3). Exact.
- `5 ≤ G.egirth` admits acyclic graphs (`egirth = ⊤`, and `5 ≤ ⊤` holds in `ℕ∞`). Correct:
  forests have girth $> 4$ vacuously and trivially admit robustly acyclic orientations (a
  directed cycle's support contains an undirected cycle), so they don't distort the
  statement.
- Not vacuous/trivial: graphs of girth ≥ 5 exclude the cheap counterexamples ($K_3$;
  the girth-4 Grötzsch graph), so the RHS's falsity genuinely requires the Nešetřil–Rödl
  construction. Substantive.
- **[defect] `erdos_1006_arbitrary_girth` (pre-fix lines 95–98).** Docstring: "For every
  integer $g$, there **exists** a graph $G$ with girth $\ge g$ such that **no** orientation
  of $G$ is robustly acyclic." Lean RHS: `∀ (g : ℕ) (V) (G), g ≤ G.egirth → ∃ o,
  o.IsRobustlyAcyclic` under `answer(False) ↔`. Three failures:
  1. **∀/∃ mismatch**: the docstring's proposition is $\forall g\,\exists G\,\forall o\,
     \neg\mathrm{RA}(o)$; the Lean RHS (whose falsity the theorem asserts) negates to
     $\exists g\,\exists G\,\forall o\,\neg\mathrm{RA}(o)$ — a single counterexample for a
     single $g$, not one for every $g$. The full Nešetřil–Rödl strength is simply not
     expressible as `answer(False) ↔ (∀ g, Q g)`; falsity of a universal is witnessed by
     one $g$.
  2. **Trivialization at `g = 0`**: since `(↑(0:ℕ) : ℕ∞) ≤ egirth` always holds, the RHS
     is equivalent to "every simple graph admits a robustly acyclic orientation", whose
     falsity is witnessed by $K_3$ (any acyclic orientation of a triangle is a transitive
     tournament; reversing the transitive shortcut edge creates a directed 3-cycle). As
     encoded, the "variant" was strictly more trivial than the main theorem and carried
     zero Nešetřil–Rödl content.
  3. Even read charitably as "for each $g$ the girth-$g$ question is answered no", the
     single outer `answer(False)` cannot say that (see 1.).
  **Fixed** (Addendum): restated as a direct assertion
  `∀ g : ℕ, ∃ (V : Type) (G : SimpleGraph V), g ≤ G.egirth ∧ ∀ o : Orientation G,
  ¬o.IsRobustlyAcyclic` — the checklist's Case 3 form is right because this is a *proved
  theorem* quoted by the page, not a question posed. Not compile-verified.
- Quantifier structure of the main theorem is otherwise right: graph universally
  quantified, orientation existentially, all inside the iff.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- No ℕ subtraction, no division, no `Finset.range` anywhere. n/a.
- `ℕ∞` comparisons: `5 ≤ G.egirth` elaborates with the numeral in `ℕ∞`; in the fixed
  variant `g ≤ G.egirth` coerces `g : ℕ` to `ℕ∞` — both standard and correct (and the
  `g = 0` degeneracy is now on the harmless side of the implication: it only makes the
  existential's girth constraint weaker for small $g$, where the claim is still true).
- Vacuous cases: an edgeless graph satisfies the RHS via `dir := fun _ _ => False`
  (`IsRobustlyAcyclic` holds vacuously) — consistent with the problem's intent, and the
  ∀-side of a `False`-polarity iff, so harmless anyway.
- "No directed cycle" via `∀ v, ¬Relation.TransGen o.dir v v`: a directed closed walk
  through $v$ yields `TransGen o.dir v v`, and conversely; irreflexivity of the transitive
  closure ⟺ no directed cycle, for finite and infinite graphs alike (`TransGen` chains are
  finite). Correct encoding.
- No `StrictMono`/`Monotone`, interval, or filter subtleties arise.

**A3. Definitions — pass.**

- `Orientation` (lines 47–51): `adj_of_dir` (only edges are directed), `dir_of_adj`
  (every edge directed at least one way), `antisymm` (never both ways). Together: exactly
  one direction per edge, nothing on non-edges — matches its docstring ("exactly one")
  and the standard notion. `SimpleGraph`'s irreflexivity rules out loops via
  `adj_of_dir`. No degenerate-input drift.
- `Orientation.IsAcyclic` (55–56): as analyzed in A2, correct.
- `flipDir` (60–61): `fun u v => (u = b ∧ v = a ∧ dir a b) ∨ (dir u v ∧ ¬(u = a ∧ v = b))`
  — adds $b \to a$ (guarded by `dir a b`, redundant at its use site where `o.dir a b` is
  hypothesized, but harmless), removes $a \to b$, keeps everything else. Correct; note the
  flipped relation is again an orientation of the same graph (the reversed pair is still
  an edge), so checking raw-relation acyclicity is exactly the problem's notion.
- `Orientation.IsRobustlyAcyclic` (65–68): original acyclicity ∧ acyclicity of every
  single-edge flip. Quantifying over directed pairs `∀ a b, o.dir a b → …` visits each
  undirected edge exactly once (by `antisymm`) — matches "reversing the direction of any
  edge". No `sorry` in any `def`/`structure`; no def/theorem drift.

**A4. Question form and answer polarity — pass (main theorem).**

- The source is a yes/no question ("Can the edges of $G$ always be directed…?"), so
  `answer(…) ↔ P` with `P` the question as asked is the right shape; RHS true would mean
  "yes". Disproved ⇒ `answer(False)`. Polarity verified by hand against the recovered
  DISPROVED banner ("solved in the negative").
- No binders before the colon (`theorem erdos_1006 : answer(False) ↔ …`, line 82); the
  graph quantifiers correctly sit inside the iff.
- The fixed variant is deliberately *not* in `answer()` form (direct assertion of a proved
  theorem — checklist Case 3), with the rationale recorded in its docstring.

**A5. Solution status — pass (source-verified).**

- Recovered banner: **DISPROVED**, tooltip "This has been solved in the negative." Module
  docstring, theorem docstring, and `@[category research solved]` all agree; the disproof
  is attributed to Nešetřil–Rödl [NeRo78b] in prose and bibliography, and the problem's
  origin to Ore via [Er71] — all matching the page's remarks verbatim. The page also
  records "Formalised statement? No" (as of edition 02 December 2025), so no upstream
  Lean formalization supersedes this file.

## E. Prior-review audit (`ai-review/1006.md`)

Accurate on the main theorem's semantics, but three substantive findings:

1. **It authored the defect.** §3 proposes, as Lean code, exactly the
   `answer(False) ↔ ∀ (g : ℕ) (V) (G), g ≤ G.egirth → ∃ o, o.IsRobustlyAcyclic` variant
   while describing it in English as "for *every* integer g, there *exists* a graph with
   girth g admitting no robustly acyclic orientation" and claiming it "would capture the
   full strength of the Nešetřil–Rödl result". As shown in A1, the proposed Lean is
   ∀/∃-inverted relative to its own English, trivializes at `g = 0` to a statement
   refuted by $K_3$, and captures none of the Nešetřil–Rödl strength. The pipeline
   adopted the snippet verbatim into `deepmind/1006.lean`, converting a review suggestion
   into a shipped defect. This is the recurring failure pattern already catalogued in the
   1005 audit — *endorsing an encoding that contradicts its own reading of the source* —
   now in generative form.
2. **Stale/incorrect citation claims.** §2 lists `[NeRo78b]` as "*A short proof of the
   existence of highly chromatic hypergraphs without short cycles*" and asserts "The
   three references listed do match those on the website." The keys matched, but that
   title did not: the pipeline's own `/latex/1006` fetch (recovered from the same session
   log) gives "On a probabilistic graph-theoretical method", Proc. Amer. Math. Soc.,
   pp. 417–421, 1978 — and a later step of that very session corrected the file
   accordingly. (Both are real 1978 Nešetřil–Rödl PAMS papers; the review conflated
   them.) Its `[Er76b]` title ("*Problems and results in graph theory*") likewise matches
   neither the file nor the sibling-file stub ("Problems in combinatorial and graph
   theory"). The review document was never updated, so its §2 is doubly stale against the
   artifact it reviews.
3. **What it got right** (re-derived, not nodded along): the `egirth = ⊤`/forest analysis
   is correct; the `answer(False)` polarity argument is correct; the flipDir and
   TransGen analyses are correct; the directed-vs-undirected edge quantification
   equivalence in §5 is correct; the Ore/Gallai attribution note it requested was indeed
   applied to the file. Its line references (e.g. "`Orientation` structure (lines
   45–49)") are off by ~2 against the current file — the file grew after review; minor.

Also noted: the style review (`reviews/1006.md`) §10 says "N/A: No variants present" —
true when written, but the variant added afterwards was never style-reviewed, which is
how a non-convention name (`erdos_1006_arbitrary_girth`) and the defective encoding
slipped through.
