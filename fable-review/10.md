## Summary

The problem (OPEN): *"Is there some $k$ such that every large integer is the sum of a
prime and at most $k$ powers of 2?"* Erdős described it as 'probably unattackable', and
in [ErGr80] Erdős and Graham suggest that no such $k$ exists; Granville and
Soundararajan [GrSo98] conjecture the opposite (3 powers suffice for odd integers,
hence 4 for even). The Lean file (`conjectures/10.lean`) defines
`IsSumPrimeAndPowersOf2 n k` — `∃ p S, p.Prime ∧ S.card ≤ k ∧ n = p + Σ_{e∈S} 2^e`
over a `Finset` of exponents — and asserts
`∃ k N, ∀ n ≥ N, IsSumPrimeAndPowersOf2 n k`. The definition is a faithful (and
provably equivalent-to-the-multiset-reading) encoding of "sum of a prime and at most
$k$ powers of 2", and the quantifier structure correctly renders "every sufficiently
large integer". The one semantic defect is the direction: this raw corpus's uniform
convention for open yes/no questions is direct assertion of the *believed/conjectured*
direction with the belief documented (cf. `fable-review/1101.md`, `1109.md`), and the
file documents the Erdős–Graham "no" suggestion in the very docstring above a theorem
asserting "yes" — an internal contradiction and a wrong-polarity encoding. Fixed in v2
by negating to the Erdős–Graham direction, with the split of expert opinion documented
and the Granville–Soundararajan "yes" side captured as a variant.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass on content, one direction defect (deferred to A4).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (session-log capture of `tidy/10.html`): "Is
  there some $k$ such that every large integer is the sum of a prime and at most $k$
  powers of 2?" The file's docstring paraphrase ("every sufficiently large integer")
  is the standard reading of "every large integer".
- Independent back-translation of `conjectures/10.lean:25-27`: "there exist $k$ and
  $N$ such that every $n \ge N$ can be written as $p + \sum_{e \in S} 2^e$ with $p$
  prime and $|S| \le k$." Modulo direction (A4), this is exactly the question's
  proposition: `∃ N, ∀ n ≥ N` is the correct "for all sufficiently large" encoding,
  and the inner predicate is the representation property.
- **Distinct exponents are without loss of generality.** The source says "at most $k$
  powers of 2" (repetition not addressed); the `Finset` encoding forces distinct
  exponents. The two readings define the same set: if
  $n = p + 2^{e_1} + \cdots + 2^{e_m}$ with $m \le k$ and repeats, then, since binary
  addition only merges digits ($s_2(a+b) \le s_2(a) + s_2(b)$), the total
  $t = \sum 2^{e_i}$ has binary weight $s_2(t) \le m \le k$ and hence is a sum of
  $\le k$ *distinct* powers; the converse inclusion is trivial. The original
  formalization session's closing note makes the same observation, and the upstream
  formal-conjectures encoding (a `Multiset` with `card ≤ k`, recovered from logs) is
  extensionally equal. Not a defect; now documented in the v2 def docstring.
- Degenerate cases: `S = ∅` is allowed, so a prime itself counts (zero powers used) —
  consistent with "at most $k$" and with the upstream `Multiset` encoding;
  `IsSumPrimeAndPowersOf2 n 0 ↔ n.Prime`. $2^0 = 1$ is a permitted power, as in the
  source's neighbor problem #9 ($p + 2^k + 2^l$, $k, l \ge 0$). $n = 0, 1$ are never
  representable ($p \ge 2$) and are irrelevant under "sufficiently large".
- Not vacuous/trivial in either direction: the "yes" side asserted by the input is
  the genuine open question; the "no" side (v2) has provable instances for small $k$
  (e.g. $k = 0$: infinitely many composites) with the full $\forall k$ the open
  content. No degenerate witness satisfies either.
