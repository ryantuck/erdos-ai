## Summary

The problem (OPEN on the source page): let $p_1 < \cdots < p_u$ be primes
and $k \geq 1$; let $F_k(p_1,\ldots,p_u)$ be such that every interval of $k$
positive integers contains at least $F_k(p_1,\ldots,p_u)$ multiples of at
least one $p_i$; estimate $F_k$, particularly for $k = \alpha p_u$ with
constant $\alpha > 2$ — [Va99, 1.8], with the page remarking that [Va99]
*reports* Erdős and Selfridge found "the exact bound" for $2<\alpha<3$ (no
reference locatable) and that for $\alpha > 3$ very little is known; see
also #970 (Jacobsthal's function). The Lean
(`conjectures/1143.lean:37-42`) defines `countDivisible S k n` — the number
of $m \in \{n+1,\ldots,n+k\}$ divisible by some element of $S$ — and asserts
for every finite nonempty set $S$ of primes, $k \geq 1$, and every $n$, that
$\mathrm{countDivisible}(S,k,n) \geq k(1-\prod_{p\in S}(1-1/p)) - 2^{|S|}$
(in ℝ). Since "estimate $F_k$" is not formalizable as a single proposition,
the file states a concrete inclusion–exclusion lower bound as a proxy and
says so honestly in its docstring. I verified the bound by hand (truncated
inclusion–exclusion, error $< 2^{|S|}-1$) and by brute force; it is true,
with `hS` (primality) essential. The Lean matches its own docstring; the
defects are scholarly, not semantic.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified), with an explicit scope
caveat.**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "Let $p_1<\cdots<p_u$
  be primes and let $k\geq 1$. Let $F_k(p_1,\ldots,p_u)$ be such that every
  interval of $k$ positive integers contains at least $F_k(p_1,\ldots,p_u)$
  multiples of at least one of the $p_i$. Estimate $F_k(p_1,\ldots,p_u)$,
  particularly in the range $k=\alpha p_u$ for constant $\alpha>2$." Status
  banner **OPEN**, tooltip "This is open, and cannot be resolved with a
  finite computation."
- The docstring's reading of $F_k$ as "the minimum, over all starting points
  $n \geq 0$, of the count of integers in $\{n+1,\ldots,n+k\}$ divisible by
  at least one $p_i$" is the correct precise rendering of the source's
  "largest guaranteed count" phrasing: intervals of $k$ positive integers
  are exactly $\{n+1,\ldots,n+k\}$ for $n \geq 0$, and the maximal $F$ with
  "every interval contains at least $F$" is the minimum over intervals.
  `∀ n : ℕ` ranges over exactly these intervals — term-by-term exact, no
  off-by-one at $n = 0$ (the interval $\{1,\ldots,k\}$).
- **Scope caveat (not a defect, made explicit):** the source problem is an
  open-ended estimation request; the file formalizes a *specific, true,
  provable* sieve lower bound as a proxy, and the docstring says so ("We
  formalize a sieve lower bound… The open problem is to determine sharper
  estimates"). This is the established pragmatic resolution for "estimate"
  problems (the prior review reached the same judgment); the docstring's
  honesty about it is what keeps A1 a pass. The formal statement is a
  routine sieve exercise, not the open problem — a reader must not mistake
  the `sorry` for open-problem difficulty. Recorded prominently in the v2
  module docstring.
- **The proxy bound is true** — verified independently: for nonempty $T
  \subseteq S$ the number of multiples of $P_T = \prod_{p \in T} p$ in
  $(n, n+k]$ is $\lfloor (n+k)/P_T\rfloor - \lfloor n/P_T\rfloor$, which
  differs from $k/P_T$ by strictly less than $1$; primality (distinctness)
  gives $\mathrm{lcm}(T) = P_T$, so inclusion–exclusion over the
  $2^{|S|}-1$ nonempty subsets yields
  $|\mathrm{count} - k(1-\prod(1-1/p))| < 2^{|S|}-1$, hence the stated
  bound with $2^{|S|}$ holds with room to spare. Brute-force corroboration
  (this review): for $S \in \{\{2\},\{3\},\{2,3\},\{2,3,5\},\{3,5,7\},
  \{2,3,5,7\},\{5,7,11\}\}$, all $k \leq 60$ ($\leq 40$ for the larger
  sets), and all $n$ over a full period $\prod_{p\in S} p$ (the count is
  periodic in $n$), the bound holds; minimum observed slack ranged from
  $1.33$ ($S=\{3\}$) to $13.46$ ($S=\{2,3,5,7\}$), and even the sharper
  constant $2^{|S|}-1$ never failed.
- Not vacuous: for fixed $S$ and large $k$ the right side is positive and
  growing, so the inequality has content; for small $k$ relative to
  $2^{|S|}$ it is trivially true (RHS negative), which is inherent to the
  additive error term and correctly handled by the ℝ-valued statement.

## E. Prior-review audit (`deepmind/ai-review/1143.md`)

Written against a styled draft (its "69 lines" and `§1.8` remarks match the
early styled file captured in the logs, not the 45-line raw input). Its
correctness verdict is right, but it contains two substantive errors, one
false mathematical claim, and one unsound derivation:

1. **Endorsed a hallucinated reference (§2).** It quotes the draft's
   "[Va99] Vaughan, R.C., *On a problem of Erdős, Straus, and Schinzel*,
   Combinatorica 19 (1999), 111–115" and asserts "[1.8] … is the Vaughan
   1999 paper" and "The formalization's Vaughan citation matches the
   website's primary source." Both claims are false: the recovered
   `/latex/1143` extraction identifies [Va99] as "Various, *Some of Paul's
   favorite problems*", the Budapest July 1999 conference booklet, and
   "1.8" is the problem's number *within that booklet*, not a paper
   reference. Vaughan's genuine paper of that title concerns unit-fraction
   representations (and, to reviewer knowledge, appeared in Mathematika
   around 1970, not Combinatorica 1999 — medium confidence on the venue,
   high confidence it is not this problem's source). This is the
   claiming-to-verify-against-the-site-without-doing-so failure mode: the
   review "verified" a fabricated entry against a page that contradicts
   it. The final upstream file corrected the key; the raw file under
   review here never carried the fabrication (it cites only the bare key).
2. **False mathematical claim about the primality hypothesis (§6).** "The
   bound actually holds for any set of positive integers (not just
   primes), since the inclusion-exclusion argument doesn't use primality."
   **Wrong.** Inclusion–exclusion counts multiples of
   $\mathrm{lcm}(T)$, and the density identity
   $\sum_{\emptyset \neq T}(-1)^{|T|+1}/\prod_{p\in T}p = 1 - \prod(1-1/p)$
   requires $\mathrm{lcm}(T) = \prod T$, i.e. pairwise coprimality.
   Concrete counterexample (verified computationally this review):
   $S = \{2,4\}$, $k = 40$, $n = 0$ — the interval $[1,40]$ contains $20$
   multiples of $2$ or $4$, but the formula demands
   $40(1 - \tfrac12\cdot\tfrac34) - 4 = 21$. The hypothesis `hS` is
   essential, not decorative. (Its adjacent conclusion — that restricting
   to primes "is not incorrect" — survives, but for the opposite reason it
   gave: the restriction is *load-bearing*.) Same failure family as the
   1005 audit's unsound-boundedness argument: a fabricated justification
   that happens not to change the verdict here, but would bless a broken
   generalization if anyone acted on it.
3. **Unsound inclusion–exclusion derivation, right conclusion (§6).** It
   writes the interval count as
   $\sum_{\emptyset\neq T}(-1)^{|T|+1}\lfloor k/\prod_{p\in T}p\rfloor$ —
   valid only for $n = 0$. The general interval needs the difference of
   floors $\lfloor (n+k)/P_T\rfloor - \lfloor n/P_T\rfloor$ (which is
   $\lfloor k/P_T\rfloor$ or $\lceil k/P_T\rceil$ depending on $n$). Its
   error bound "each floor introduces an error of at most 1 … total error
   at most $2^{|S|}-1$" survives because the difference-of-floors also
   deviates from $k/P_T$ by less than 1, so the conclusion (bound valid,
   $2^{|S|}$ conservative vs. $2^{|S|}-1$) is correct — but the derivation
   as written proves it only for $n = 0$, while the theorem quantifies
   over all $n$.
4. **What it got right, re-derived:** the `hne` degenerate analysis (empty
   $S$: LHS $0 \geq$ RHS $-1$) checks out; the ℝ-cast rationale (RHS can
   be negative) is correct; the ambiguity assessment ("MODERATE — the
   formalization captures a consequence, not the open problem") is fair
   and matches this review's scope caveat; its recommendations (named
   `F_k` definition; `∃`-form filter; clarify `[Va99, 1.8]`; #970
   cross-reference) were all sound and are implemented in v2 (the first
   two had already been adopted by the upstream styled file). Its
   flagged-but-unfixable item (an Erdős–Selfridge variant) is correctly
   impossible without the unstated bound — though it did not notice *why*
   (the page never states the formula), nor did it notice the page's "I
   cannot find a relevant paper" hedge that its own §2 partially
