## Summary

The problem (OPEN on the source page): "Are there infinitely many $n$ (or any
$n>105$) such that $n-2^k$ is prime for all $1<2^k<n$?" — [Va99, 1.7], with
the page remarking that the only known such $n$ are $4, 7, 15, 21, 45, 75,
105$ (OEIS A039669), that Mientka and Weitzenkamp [MiWe69] verified there are
no others up to $2^{44}$, that Vaughan [Va73] proved the qualifying $n \le N$
number fewer than $\exp(-c\frac{\log\log\log N}{\log\log N}\log N)N$, that Guy
discusses it as A19 in [Gu04], and that Erdős's stronger conjecture (problem
#236) — the number of $1<2^k<n$ with $n-2^k$ prime is $o(\log n)$ — would
force the answer "no". The Lean (`conjectures/1142.lean:24-26`) defines
`AllPowerOfTwoComplementsPrime n := ∀ k, 1 ≤ k → 2 ^ k < n → (n - 2 ^ k).Prime`
and asserts `∀ N, ∃ n > N, AllPowerOfTwoComplementsPrime n`. Read back
independently, this is exactly the asked ("yes") direction of the open
infinitude question, stated as a direct assertion — this raw corpus's
convention for open yes/no questions (cf. siblings 1141, 1146). The
mathematics is sound; the guarded ℕ subtraction and the $k$-range translation
are exact, and the vacuous members $n \in \{0,1,2\}$ are finitely many, hence
invisible to the infinitude statement. The defects are scholarly: the sole
citation key is never defined, and the parenthetical weaker sub-question "(or
any $n>105$)" is dropped without note.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified + computationally
corroborated); one omission deferred to B2.**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "Are there infinitely
  many $n$ (or any $n>105$) such that $n-2^k$ is prime for all $1<2^k<n$?"
  Status banner **OPEN**, tooltip "This is open, and cannot be resolved with a
  finite computation."
- Independent back-translation of lines 24–26: "for every $N$ there is an
  $n > N$ such that $n - 2^k$ is prime for every $k \geq 1$ with $2^k < n$" —
  i.e. there are infinitely many such $n$. `∀ N, ∃ n > N, P n` is a correct
  standard encoding of "infinitely many" over ℕ (the set $\{n \mid P\,n\}$ is
  unbounded iff infinite).
- The source's condition on $k$ is $1 < 2^k < n$, i.e. $2^k > 1$ **and**
  $2^k < n$. Over ℕ, $2^k > 1 \iff k \geq 1$, so the Lean hypotheses
  `1 ≤ k → 2 ^ k < n →` translate the chained inequality **term-by-term
  exactly**; in particular $k = 0$ (which would probe $n - 1$) is correctly
  excluded, matching $2^0 = 1 \not> 1$.
- The docstring (line 15) quotes the question as "Are there infinitely many
  n such that n - 2^k is prime for all 1 < 2^k < n?" — dropping the source's
  parenthetical "(or any $n>105$)". The main (infinitude) question is
  faithfully captured; the parenthetical weaker sub-question is a genuine part
  of the problem-text and its silent omission is a completeness defect handled
  in B2.
- Not vacuous/trivial: whether the set is infinite is genuinely open (the
  page: "cannot be resolved with a finite computation"); Vaughan's bound
  shows the set is extremely sparse but does not settle finiteness. The only
  degenerate satisfiers of the predicate are $n \in \{0,1,2\}$ (see A2),
  finitely many, incapable of witnessing `∃ n > N` for $N \geq 2$ — they do
  not trivialize the statement.
- Membership verified against the page's own data, not just read off: brute
  force to $2\times 10^6$ under the Lean encoding yields exactly
  $\{0, 1, 2\} \cup \{4, 7, 15, 21, 45, 75, 105\}$ — the page's known list
  plus the three vacuous members — and $106$ fails via $k=1$
  ($104 = 8 \cdot 13$). Per-value witness primes: $4$: $2$; $7$: $5, 3$;
  $15$: $13, 11, 7$; $21$: $19, 17, 13, 5$; $45$: $43, 41, 37, 29, 13$;
  $75$: $73, 71, 67, 59, 43, 11$; $105$: $103, 101, 97, 89, 73, 41$ — all
  prime, and in each case the $k$-range terminates exactly where $2^k \geq n$.
- Indexing: no sequences, no index shifts to reconcile.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- **ℕ subtraction** `n - 2 ^ k` (line 10) is guarded by the hypothesis
  `2 ^ k < n`, so it never truncates and the argument of `.Prime` is
  positive. Correct.
- No division anywhere.
- No `Finset.range`, no filters, no `StrictMono`/`Monotone`, no intervals.
- **Vacuous quantifier at small $n$:** for $n \leq 2$ no $k$ satisfies
  `1 ≤ k ∧ 2 ^ k < n` ($2^1 = 2 \not< 2$), so $0, 1, 2$ satisfy
  `AllPowerOfTwoComplementsPrime` vacuously. ($n = 3$ genuinely fails:
  $k = 1$ gives $3 - 2 = 1$, not prime.) These three junk members are
  immaterial to the infinitude statement, matching the source's intent (OEIS
  A039669 is explicitly "Numbers $n > 2$ such that …", confirming the
  convention that the trivial cases are excluded by fiat, not by content).
  **However**, any finite-enumeration variant (e.g. the Mientka–Weitzenkamp
  search) *must* guard them: `∀ n ≤ 2^44, P n → n ∈ {4,…,105}` is **false**
  at $n = 0$ without a `2 < n` hypothesis. The v2 variant carries the guard,
  and the def's docstring now documents the trap.
- Strict inequalities `1 ≤ k` (non-strict on $k$, equivalent to strict
  $2^k > 1$) and `2 ^ k < n` (strict) both match the source's
  $1 < 2^k < n$ verbatim; nothing rides on boundary cases ($2^k = n$ would
  give $n - 2^k = 0$, and the source excludes it).
- `∀ N, ∃ n > N` for "infinitely many": standard and correct over ℕ; no
  filter/density subtleties arise.

**A3. Definitions — pass.**

- The sole def `AllPowerOfTwoComplementsPrime` (lines 9–10) matches its
  docstring ("$n - 2^k$ is prime for every $k \geq 1$ with $2^k < n$") and
  the source's property exactly, including the degenerate inputs analyzed in
  A2. No `sorry` in any def. The theorem uses the def consistently — no
  drift.

**A4. Question form and answer polarity — pass.**

- The source is a yes/no question ("Are there infinitely many…?"), **open**.
  The file states the asked ("yes") direction as a direct assertion with a
  `sorry` proof. This raw corpus has no `answer()` elaborator (Mathlib-only
  imports), and its uniform convention for open yes/no questions is precisely
  this direct assertion of the asked direction (verified against sibling 1141,
  identical situation and [Va99] cluster). Polarity is correct-by-convention:
  proving the statement would answer the question "yes", and nothing is
  asserted as *known* beyond the `sorry`. In styled question form it would be
  `answer(sorry) ↔ Set.Infinite {n : ℕ | …}` — noted in the v2 docstring,
  and exactly what the upstream styled file (recovered from logs) does.
- Worth recording: the page's evidence (Vaughan's sparsity bound; Erdős's
  stronger conjecture in #236, which — since there are $\sim \log_2 n$
  admissible $k$ — would force a negative answer for all large $n$) points
  toward "no". That does not change the encoding: the question is open, and
  the convention states the asked direction. The v2 docstring records the
  heuristic.
- No binders before the colon (line 24, `theorem erdos_problem_1142 :` is
  binder-free); no `answer()` in the file, so the binder rule is moot.
