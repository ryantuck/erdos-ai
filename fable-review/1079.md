## Summary

The problem (Erdős [Er75]): let $r \ge 4$; if $G$ is a graph on $n$ vertices with at least
$\mathrm{ex}(n; K_r)$ edges, must $G$ contain a vertex with degree $d \gg_r n$ whose
neighbourhood contains at least $\mathrm{ex}(d; K_{r-1})$ edges? The page status is
**SOLVED**: true (unless $G$ is itself the Turán graph), by Bollobás–Thomason [BoTh81];
Bondy [Bo83b] showed that with $> \mathrm{ex}(n; K_r)$ edges the vertex can be chosen of
maximum degree. The Lean (`deepmind/1079.lean:71`) encodes the yes/no question as
`answer(True) ↔ ∀ r ≥ 4, ∃ c > 0, ∃ n₀, ∀ n ≥ n₀, ∀ G, e(G) ≥ ex(n;K_r) → ∃ v, deg v ≥ cn ∧
e(N(v)) ≥ ex(deg v; K_{r-1})`, using Mathlib's `SimpleGraph.extremalNumber` and a local
induced-edge counter. Read back independently, the main theorem matches the question with the
standard reading of $\gg_r$; the `∃ n₀` is not mere safety but load-bearing (the inner
statement is false at $n \in \{0, 1\}$). The one defect found is in the companion Bondy
variant, which quantified *universally* over max-degree vertices where the source only
warrants an existential.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "Let $r\geq 4$. If $G$ is a graph on
  $n$ vertices with at least $\mathrm{ex}(n;K_r)$ edges then must $G$ contain a vertex with
  degree $d\gg_r n$ whose neighbourhood contains at least $\mathrm{ex}(d;K_{r-1})$ edges?"
  The module docstring reproduces it faithfully.
- Independent back-translation of `erdos_1079` (lines 71–81): "for every $r \ge 4$ there are
  $c > 0$ and $n_0$ such that every graph $G$ on $n \ge n_0$ vertices with
  $e(G) \ge \mathrm{ex}(n; K_r)$ has a vertex $v$ of degree $\ge cn$ with at least
  $\mathrm{ex}(\deg v; K_{r-1})$ edges inside $N(v)$." This is the question as asked, with
  $d \gg_r n$ given its standard meaning ($d \ge c(r)\,n$); $c$ and $n_0$ correctly depend on
  $r$ only and are fixed *before* $G$, so the bound is uniform over graphs, as $\gg_r$
  requires. Both conclusion conjuncts hold for the *same* $v$. ✓
- `extremalNumber n (⊤ : SimpleGraph (Fin r))`: Mathlib's definition (recovered from logs:
  `noncomputable def extremalNumber (n : ℕ) (H : SimpleGraph W) : ℕ := sup { G : SimpleGraph
  (Fin n) | H.Free G } (#·.edgeFinset)`, with `A.Free B := ¬A ⊑ B`, `⊑` = injective-hom copy)
  is exactly $\mathrm{ex}(n; K_r)$, and `⊤ : SimpleGraph (Fin r)` is $K_r$. Argument order
  ($n$ first) is used correctly at both call sites — the raw file's local `turanNumber1079 H n`
  had the arguments in the opposite order and the swap was performed correctly.
- `neighborhoodEdgeCount G (G.neighborFinset v)` counts unordered adjacent pairs
  $\{i,j\} \subseteq N(v)$, $i < j$, once each — exactly "edges in the (open) neighbourhood",
  excluding $v$ itself, as intended.
- Not vacuous/trivially false: for each $r$ and large $n$, graphs with
  $\ge \mathrm{ex}(n;K_r)$ edges exist (the Turán graph), and the conclusion is substantive.
  Truth of the RHS (needed for `answer(True)`, see A4): Bollobás–Thomason give it for
  non-Turán $G$; for $G = T(n, r-1)$ itself the conclusion holds **with equality** — every
  vertex has degree $d \ge \frac{r-2}{r-1}n - 1 \ge cn$, and its neighbourhood is the join of
  the other $r-2$ parts, whose sizes differ by at most 1, i.e. exactly $T(d, r-2)$, the
  unique $K_{r-1}$-free maximizer with $\mathrm{ex}(d; K_{r-1})$ edges. (A partition into
  parts differing by ≤ 1 is unique, so "the remaining parts of $T(n,r-1)$" *is* the balanced
  partition of $d$ into $r-2$ parts.) So the site's "unless $G$ is itself the Turán graph"
  caveat concerns a strict-inequality strengthening and does not threaten the non-strict
  formalization. Now documented in the theorem docstring (Addendum fix 2).

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- ℕ subtraction: the only instance is `Fin (r - 1)` under `r ≥ 4` — no truncation. ✓
- Division: none anywhere. `range`: none. ✓
- Degenerate small $n$: at $n = 0$ the hypothesis $0 \ge \mathrm{ex}(0;K_r) = 0$ holds but
  `∃ v : Fin 0` is false; at $n = 1$, $G = K_1$ satisfies the hypothesis but no vertex has
  degree $\ge c \cdot 1 > 0$. So **without** `∃ n₀` the RHS would be false and
  `answer(True) ↔ RHS` would be a *false theorem* — the `n₀` is load-bearing, not decoration
  (see Part E, finding 3). With $n_0 \ge 2$ and $c$ small the statement is the true
  Bollobás–Thomason assertion (for $2 \le n \le r-1$ the hypothesis forces $G = K_n$, whose
  every vertex satisfies the conclusion with equality).
- `≥` vs `>`: main theorem uses `≥ ex(n;K_r)` (source: "at least") and the variant uses
  `> ex(n;K_r)` (source: "$>$") — both match the source exactly. Conclusion `≥ ex(d;K_{r-1})`
  matches "at least". ✓
- No filter encodings, `StrictMono`/`Monotone`, or interval issues arise (the asymptotics are
  encoded by explicit $c, n_0$, which is faithful here).

**A3. Definitions — pass.**

- `neighborhoodEdgeCount` (lines 51–54) matches its docstring and the intended notion,
  including degenerate inputs (`S = ∅` or a singleton gives 0). Each unordered edge inside
  `S` is counted once via the `p.1 < p.2` guard on `Fin n`'s linear order. No `sorry` in any
  definition. Used consistently in both theorems with `S = G.neighborFinset v`.
- The raw file's local `containsSubgraph1079`/`turanNumber1079` (an `sSup` over all vertex
  types of a given cardinality) were replaced during the reuse fix by Mathlib's
  `extremalNumber`; the two are equinumerous notion-for-notion (injective-hom containment ↔
  `IsContained`; `sSup` of the nonempty, $\binom{n}{2}$-bounded edge-count set ↔ `Finset.sup`
  over `SimpleGraph (Fin n)`). No meaning drifted in the swap.

**A4. Question form and answer polarity — pass.**

- No binders before the colon (line 71 is binder-free; the raw file's `(r : ℕ) (hr : r ≥ 4)`

- The Bondy variant is a direct assertion of a solved result (no `answer()`), which is

## Proposed refinements
