# Fable Review: Erdős Problem 1028

**Verdict:** NEEDS REVISION (one Part A defect: the theorem as written is *provably
false* — the lower-bound clause quantifies over all ordered-pair ±1 functions, and any
antisymmetric function makes every discrepancy sum vanish — fixed in place, see Addendum)
**Confidence:** high on the defect itself (falsified by an explicit, machine-checked
counterexample); medium overall — no compiler is available, journal volume numbers are
absent from the recovered `/latex` extraction, and `FormalConjecturesForMathlib/` is not
present in this repository.

## Summary

The problem: let $H(n) = \min_f \max_{X \subseteq \{1,\ldots,n\}}
\left|\sum_{x \neq y \in X} f(x,y)\right|$, where $f$ ranges over ±1-valued functions on
pairs; estimate $H(n)$. The page (recovered from session logs, accessed 2026-02-22)
records the answer $n/4 \le H(n) \ll n^{3/2}$ (Erdős [Er63d]) and $H(n) \gg n^{3/2}$
(Erdős–Spencer [ErSp71]), i.e. $H(n) = \Theta(n^{3/2})$, status **SOLVED (LEAN)**. The
Lean file encodes $\Theta(n^{3/2})$ as a conjunction of an eventual lower bound
(∀ f ∃ X) and an eventual upper bound (∃ f ∀ X) over `discrepancySum`, a sum over
*ordered* pairs $x \neq y$ in $X$. Read back independently, the pre-fix Lean does **not**
assert the problem's proposition: it lets $f$ range over *all* functions
`Fin n → Fin n → ℤ` with ±1 values, and the antisymmetric $f(x,y) = \mathbb{1}[x<y] -
\mathbb{1}[x>y]$ zeroes every subset sum, making the lower-bound clause false for every
$n \ge 1$ and hence the whole theorem false. The intended $f$ is symmetric (an edge
2-colouring of $K_n$ — the page's tags are "graph theory, discrepancy" and [ErSp71] is
"Imbalances in k-colorations"); with symmetry the ordered sum is exactly twice the edge
sum and the encoding is faithful. Fixed by adding symmetry hypotheses to both clauses.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — DEFECT (fixed).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "Let $H(n)=\min_f \max_{X\subseteq
  \{1,\ldots,n\}} \left\lvert \sum_{x\neq y\in X} f(x,y)\right\rvert$, where $f$ ranges
  over all functions $f:X^2\to \{-1,1\}$. Estimate $H(n)$."
- The docstring's normalization of the domain to $\{1,\ldots,n\}^2$ (the page literally
  writes $f : X^2$, which cannot be right since $X$ varies inside the max while $f$ is
  fixed outside it) is correct and necessary.
- The pre-fix theorem (`deepmind/1028.lean:64-74` pre-fix) quantified the lower bound
  over **all** `f : Fin n → Fin n → ℤ` with `∀ i j, f i j = 1 ∨ f i j = -1`. This class
  contains antisymmetric functions, e.g. `f i j = if i < j then 1 else -1`: for every
  subset $X$, the ordered pairs $(x,y)$ and $(y,x)$ cancel, so `discrepancySum n f X = 0`
  for all $X$ (machine-verified exhaustively for $n = 4$). The clause
  `∃ X, C₁ * n^{3/2} ≤ |0|` then fails for every $n \ge 1$ and every $C₁ > 0$, so
  **`erdos_1028` as written was provably false** — a false statement carrying
  `@[category research solved]`. Under the same $f$, the upper-bound clause was
  trivially witnessed (all sums 0), i.e. degenerate in exactly the A1 "degenerate
  witness" sense.
- The mathematically intended class is symmetric $f$ (2-colourings of $E(K_n)$): forced
  by the page's own remark $n/4 \le H(n)$ (arbitrary ordered $f$ would give
  $H(n) \equiv 0$), by the tags ("graph theory"), and by [ErSp71]'s subject
  (imbalances of colourings). For symmetric $f$, the ordered-pair sum equals exactly
  $2\times$ the edge sum (machine-verified), so $\Theta(n^{3/2})$ is convention-robust.
  **Fix applied:** symmetry hypothesis `∀ i j, f i j = f j i` added to both clauses;
  docstrings updated to say "symmetric" and to record the counterexample. Not
  compile-verified.
- Min-max encoding: lower clause (∀f∃X) = $H(n) \ge C_1 n^{3/2}$; upper clause (∃f∀X) =
  $H(n) \le C_2 n^{3/2}$; `∃ C₁ C₂ > 0, ∃ N₀, ∀ n ≥ N₀, …` is the standard rendering of
  $\Theta$. Correct post-fix.
- Indexing: `Fin n` vs $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ is a pure relabeling; no index arithmetic
  appears. Fine.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass (post-fix).**

- Division: only `(3 : ℝ) / 2` (exact) and `(n : ℝ) / 4` in the new variant (real
  division, no zero-divisor issue).
- `Finset.range`: not used; `X.filter (· ≠ x)` correctly excludes only the diagonal

- Strict vs non-strict: `C₁ > 0`, `≤` bounds — matches $\gg/\ll$ conventions.

## E. Prior-review audit (`ai-review/1028.md`)

The prior review's headline conclusion — "**The formalization is mathematically
correct** … No mathematical flaws identified" (§6) — is **wrong**; the theorem it
endorsed is provably false. Specifics:

1. **Fatal miss, §5/§6 (constant-factor hand-wave over a broken quantifier).** §5
   asserts: "The distinction [ordered vs unordered pairs] is immaterial to the
   $\Theta(n^{3/2})$ result since switching between ordered and unordered pairs changes
   the sum by a factor of at most 2, absorbed into the constants." The factor-2
   identity holds **only for symmetric $f$** — and the Lean quantifies over *all* $f$.
   Off the symmetric subspace the "switch" is not a factor of 2: an antisymmetric $f$
   has ordered sum identically 0 while its unordered counterpart is not even defined.
   §6 then "verifies" the lower bound clause (∀f∃X) without testing a single
   non-symmetric $f$. This is the recurring prior-review failure pattern flagged in the
   pipeline (cf. 1000's invented-then-excused off-by-one, 1005's unsound boundedness
   argument): an equivalence argued asymptotically/up-to-constants that silently
   assumes exactly the hypothesis whose absence is the bug. Notably, §6's own diagonal
   analysis ("diagonal entries … are never used") shows the review *did* probe the
   function class — it examined the harmless slack and missed the fatal one.
2. **Correct claims verified:** the def computes the ordered-pair sum (checked); the
   min-max ↔ two-clause decomposition is the right shape (checked); shared $N_0$ and
   outer $C_1, C_2$ scoping are fine (checked); the diagonal-constraint analysis is
   correct; the [Er71, p.107] omission it flagged was real and was subsequently fixed.
3. **§3 "the weaker intermediate result $n/4 \le H(n)$ … is subsumed by the stronger
   $\Theta(n^{3/2})$ statement"** — not literally true: an exact-constant bound for all
   $n$ is not implied by an eventual-constant bound; and as B2 shows, the $n/4$ bound
   has genuine formal content (it is even false at $n = 1$ and convention-sensitive at
   $n = 5$). As an enrichment judgment it was defensible; as a mathematical claim of
   subsumption it is sloppy.
4. **Downstream consequence not caught by any prior stage:** the citation-fix session
   (log ffec6723) *invented* the claim "the website says [Er71, p.107] established the
   upper bound" — the recovered page says no such thing — and edited the docstring
   accordingly; `reviews/1028.md` then passed everything ("No issues found"). Three
   review stages endorsed a false theorem with a misattributed docstring.
