## Summary

The problem (erdosproblems.com/1049, status **OPEN**): "Let $t>1$ be a rational number.
Is $\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{t^n-1} = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\tau(n)}{t^n}$
irrational, where $\tau(n)$ counts the divisors of $n$?" — a conjecture of Chowla;
Erdős [Er48] proved it when $t \ge 2$ is an integer. The Lean theorem
(`conjectures/1049.lean`) asserts: for every `t : ℚ` with `1 < t`, the real number
`∑' (n : ℕ), (1 : ℝ) / ((t : ℝ) ^ (n + 1) - 1)` is irrational. Reindexing $m = n+1$
this is exactly $\sum_{m=1}^\infty 1/(t^m-1)$, the left-hand form of the source's sum
(the two displayed series are equal by the classical Lambert-series identity, valid for
$t > 1$). Read back independently, the Lean and the English agree; the direct-assertion
form for this open yes/no question asserts the conjectured "yes" direction, which is the
established raw-file convention in this repository (cf. `conjectures/249.lean`,
`conjectures/1050.lean`). No semantic defects; only polish-level nits (a dropped `[Er48]`
citation key and absent bibliography), fixed in place per the Addendum.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (all three captures agree): "Let $t>1$ be a
  rational number. Is\[\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{1}{t^n-1}=\sum_{n=1}^\infty
  \frac{\tau(n)}{t^n}\]irrational, where $\tau(n)$ counts the divisors of $n$?"
- Independent back-translation of the theorem: "for all rational $t > 1$,
  $\sum_{n=0}^\infty 1/(t^{n+1}-1)$ is irrational." Substituting $m = n+1$ gives
  $\sum_{m=1}^\infty 1/(t^m-1)$ — **term-by-term exact** against the source's 1-indexed
  sum; the `n + 1` shift correctly bridges `tsum` over ℕ (from 0) and the source (from 1).
- The source displays *two equal series* and asks whether their common value is
  irrational. The file formalizes the left-hand (Lambert) side. This is a faithful and
  complete choice: for rational $t > 1$ we have $0 < 1/t < 1$ and the classical Lambert
  identity $\sum_{n\ge1} x^n/(1-x^n) = \sum_{n\ge1} \tau(n)x^n$ at $x = 1/t$ gives
  $1/(t^n-1) = x^n/(1-x^n)$ termwise, so the two sums are the same real number. (The
  first pipeline session formalized the right-hand $\tau$-form instead; the two are
  equivalent. See Addendum.)
- Quantifier structure: universal over `t : ℚ` with explicit hypothesis `1 < t`, as
  theorem binders — correct for a direct assertion ("Let $t>1$ be a rational number").
- Types: `t : ℚ` matches "rational"; the sum is computed in ℝ (`(t : ℝ)` cast before
  `^` and `-`), where irrationality lives. `Irrational` is Mathlib's
  `x ∉ Set.range ((↑) : ℚ → ℝ)` — the right notion.
- Not vacuous/trivial: for $t > 1$ the terms are positive and dominated by a geometric
  series ($t^m - 1 \ge t^m/2$ for $t^m \ge 2$, so $1/(t^m-1) \le 2t^{-m}$ eventually),
  hence the family is summable and `tsum` is the genuine sum — not the junk value 0
  (which, being rational, would have made the assertion trivially false). A substantive
  open conjecture.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- No ℕ subtraction: `(t : ℝ) ^ (n + 1) - 1` subtracts in ℝ (the exponent `n + 1` is ℕ,
  which is fine for `Monoid.npow` on ℝ).
- Division: `1 / ((t : ℝ) ^ (n + 1) - 1)` — for `1 < t` and `n + 1 ≥ 1`,
  `t ^ (n+1) > 1`, so the denominator is strictly positive; no division by zero at any
  index, including `n = 0` (denominator `t - 1 > 0`).
- No `Finset.range`; the `tsum` index shift is verified term-by-term above (first Lean
  term `n = 0` ↦ source term $m = 1$: $1/(t-1)$ both). No off-by-one.
- No bounded quantifiers, hence no vacuity traps.
- "Infinite sum" encoding: `∑'` (tsum) with summability actually holding (A1), so the
  formal value equals the informal series. Correct.
- No `StrictMono`/`Monotone` or interval-endpoint issues; `1 < t` is the source's strict
  `t > 1`.

**A3. Definitions — pass (trivially).**

- No helper `def`/`abbrev`/`structure` in the file; the only `sorry` is the theorem's
