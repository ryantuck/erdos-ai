## Summary

The problem (verbatim from the recovered page): "A planar graph on $n$ vertices with
$3n-6$ edges (the maximum possible) is called saturated. Does every graph on $n$
vertices with $\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor+\lfloor \frac{n+1}{2}\rfloor$ edges contain a
saturated planar graph with $>3$ vertices?" Status **PROVED** — solved in the
affirmative by Simonovits in his PhD thesis (every such graph contains a $K_4$ or a
$C_l + 2K_1$, $l \geq 3$). The Lean statement (`deepmind/1019.lean:81`) encodes this as
`answer(True) ↔ ∀ n ≥ 4, ∀ G on Fin n with edgeFinset.card ≥ n²/4 + (n+1)/2, ∃ k > 3
and an injective adjacency-preserving embedding of a graph H on Fin k with IsPlanar H ∧
H.edgeFinset.card = 3k − 6`, with planarity axiomatized as an `opaque` predicate
(Mathlib has none). Read back independently, the Lean and the English agree; I found no
semantic defects. The `n ≥ 4` guard is not merely cosmetic — it is load-bearing at
$n = 0$ (see A1) — and the statement is otherwise a faithful, exact translation.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Independent back-translation of lines 81–93: "for every $n \ge 4$ and every graph $G$
  on $n$ vertices with at least $\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor + \lfloor (n+1)/2\rfloor$ edges,
  some graph $H$ on $k > 3$ vertices that is planar with exactly $3k-6$ edges embeds
  into $G$ injectively preserving adjacency." This matches the question as asked, using
  the page's own definition of "saturated" (planar with $3n-6$ edges) verbatim — no
  interpretation of "maximal" is even needed.
- "Contains" = subgraph (not induced): the one-directional
  `H.Adj u v → G.Adj (f u) (f v)` with `Function.Injective f` is the correct encoding;
  Simonovits's $C_l + 2K_1$ witnesses are non-induced subgraphs in general, so an
  induced encoding would have been wrong.
- `≥` vs the source's exact edge count: the containment property is monotone increasing
  in edges (any supergraph of a graph containing a saturated planar subgraph still
  contains it, via a spanning subgraph with exactly the threshold count), so "at least"
  is equivalent to "exactly" here. Faithful.
- **The `n ≥ 4` guard is necessary, not decorative.** For $n \in \{1,2,3\}$ the
  threshold exceeds $\binom{n}{2}$ ($1>0$, $2>1$, $4>3$), so those cases are vacuous
  either way. But at $n = 0$ the threshold is $0 + 0 = 0$, the empty graph *satisfies*
  the edge hypothesis, and no injective `f : Fin k → Fin 0` exists for $k > 3$ — an
  unguarded `∀ n` statement would be **false**, flipping the correct polarity to
  `answer(False)` against the problem's intent. The guard keeps the encoding both
  faithful (to the intended nondegenerate question) and true. Now documented in the
  theorem docstring (Addendum fix 2).
- Not vacuous for $n \ge 4$: $K_4$ has $6 = \lfloor 16/4\rfloor + \lfloor 5/2\rfloor$
  edges, so the hypothesis is satisfiable at the first index; smallest case checks out
  ($6$-edge graph on $4$ vertices is $K_4$, which is itself saturated planar on
  $4 > 3$ vertices).

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- ℕ division: `n ^ 2 / 4` and `(n + 1) / 2` floor exactly as
  $\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor, \lfloor (n+1)/2\rfloor$. Correct by design.
- ℕ subtraction: `3 * k - 6` is safe — `k > 3` forces $k \ge 4$, $3k \ge 12 > 6$; no
  truncation.
- No `Finset.range` indexing anywhere; no filter/limit encodings; no
  `StrictMono`/interval issues.
- Vacuous-quantifier audit: the only near-degeneracy is the $n = 0$ case analyzed in
  A1, correctly excluded by the guard.
- The existential over `dH : DecidableRel H.Adj` (and universal over `dG`) always has a
  classical witness and `edgeFinset.card` is instance-independent — harmless plumbing
  for `edgeFinset`.

**A3. Definitions — pass, with a structural caveat.**

- The only helper is `opaque IsPlanar {V} [Fintype V] (G : SimpleGraph V) : Prop`
  (line 63). No `sorry` in any def/abbrev/structure. The docstring states honestly that
  Mathlib lacks planarity and the predicate is axiomatized.
- Caveat (inherent, not a defect to fix here): with `IsPlanar` opaque, `erdos_1019` is
  a statement *schema* — neither provable nor refutable until the predicate is
  instantiated. This is the best available encoding absent Mathlib planarity, is
  consistent with the sibling files (`deepmind/630.lean:56`, `deepmind/631.lean:68`,
  `deepmind/1018.lean:48` use the identical `opaque` pattern), and crucially — unlike
  the raw `def ... := sorry` (see D/E) — takes `G` as a genuine argument, so
  `IsPlanar G` and `IsPlanar G'` are *not* definitionally equal for distinct graphs.
- Self-enforcing side condition verified: a planar graph on $k \ge 4$ vertices with
  exactly $3k-6$ edges is automatically connected (a disconnected planar graph on
  $k \ge 3$ vertices has $< 3k-6$ edges: components of size $\ge 3$ cost $3k_i - 6$,
  size $2$ at most $1 = 3k_i - 5$, size $1$ zero, so $c \ge 2$ loses at least $3$),
  hence a genuine triangulation; no extra connectivity hypothesis is needed.

**A4. Question form and answer polarity — pass (source-verified).**

- The source is a yes/no question ("Does every graph … contain …?"), so
  `answer(…) ↔ P` is the required shape, and the RHS is the question as asked
  (universal containment statement). RHS true ⟺ "yes" — and Simonovits proved yes, so
  `answer(True)` has the correct polarity. Verified by hand.
- No binders before the colon (line 81, `theorem erdos_1019 :` is binder-free); the
  universal correctly sits inside the iff.
- Raw-vs-styled: the raw first pass (`conjectures/1019.lean:41`) was a bare assertion

  wrapper. Unlike the 1005 restyling (which drifted question → assertion), this drift
  was in the *correct* direction, converting to the mandated question form.

**A5. Solution status — pass (source-verified).**

## E. Prior-review audit (`ai-review/1019.md`)

Accurate on the core arithmetic and encoding, but four substantive findings:

1. **False claim that the source data is unavailable/in another format.** "The website
   does not list references in this format" is wrong: the recovered page cites
   exactly `#1019 : [Er64f] [Er69c] [Er71,p.102]` — the file's keys are the site's
   keys. (Same failure class as the 1001 audit: claiming data unavailable that is in
   fact recoverable. The citation-expansion it requested did subsequently happen in
   the upstream session and matches the `/latex/1019` extraction.)
2. **Unsound small-case justification for the `n ≥ 4` guard.** It asserts "For n ≤ 3,
   the edge count threshold exceeds the maximum possible edges (verified: n=3 …)".
   False at $n = 0$: threshold $0$ does *not* exceed max edges $0$, the hypothesis is
   satisfied by the empty graph, and the conclusion fails — the unguarded statement is
   **false at $n = 0$**, which is precisely what makes the guard load-bearing rather
   than merely convenient. Right conclusion, wrong (invented) reason — the recurring
   pattern from the 1000/1005 audits that would mask a real bug on a problem where
   the degenerate case bites.
3. **Mathematically wrong "stronger result" claim.** "The website describes a
   quantitative strengthening … graphs with ⌊n²/4⌋+k edges contain saturated planar
   subgraphs on ≫ k/n vertices. This is a stronger result (the current formalization
   is the special case k = ⌊(n+1)/2⌋)." Re-derived: at $k = \lfloor (n+1)/2\rfloor$,
   $\gg k/n$ guarantees only $\gg 1/2$ — i.e. *constantly many* vertices with an
   unspecified implicit constant — which does **not** imply a saturated planar
   subgraph on $> 3$ vertices. [Er69c] does not subsume the problem (that is exactly
   why Simonovits's thesis was needed); it is a complementary result, meaningful when
   $k/n \to \infty$. The variant was still worth adding, but not for the stated
   reason.
4. **Imprecise unsoundness diagnosis of the raw `def := sorry`.** It claims the def
   "makes `IsPlanar G` provable for every graph (via sorry), which is unsound".
   `sorry : Prop` elaborates to an *unspecified proposition* (`sorryAx Prop`), not
   `True`; it is no more "provable" than any proposition is by writing another
   `sorry`. The genuine defects are (a) the ignored `_` argument makes all `IsPlanar`
   propositions definitionally equal (`IsPlanar G ↔ IsPlanar G'` by rfl — provable
   *without* further sorries), and (b) the definition drags `sorryAx` into every
   downstream statement's axiom footprint. Right conclusion (opaque is preferable),
   materially imprecise argument.

Minor: its line references (e.g. "line 37" for the opaque, "lines 53–60" for the
theorem) match the upstream `formal-conjectures` copy, not this repo's file (lines
63, 81–92 pre-fix here); its claim that 630/631 "handle planarity differently" is
outdated against the current corpus (both now use the identical opaque pattern). What
it got right (re-derived): the floor-division arithmetic, the $3k-6$ ℕ-subtraction
safety, subgraph-vs-induced interpretation (confirmed by the recovered page's own
definition of "saturated"), the connectivity self-enforcement argument (checked — its
Euler reasoning is sound), the `≥` vs exact-count equivalence, and polarity of
`answer(True)`.
