## Summary

The problem (verbatim from the recovered page): "Let $A$ be an infinite sequence of
integers such that every $n \in A + A$ is squarefree. How fast must $A$ grow?" — status
**OPEN**, [Er81h, p.180]. The page's remarks record Erdős's expectation (exponential
growth is achievable, polynomial growth is not expected) and the partial results of van
Doorn–Tao [vDTa25] ($a_j > 0.24\,j^{4/3}$ for all $j$; a valid sequence with squarefree
terms and $a_j < \exp(5j/\log j)$ for large $j$) and Konyagin [Ko04] (finite case,
problem #1109). The Lean theorem (`conjectures/1103.lean:30–37`) asserts: every strictly
increasing `a : ℕ → ℕ` with `∀ i j, Squarefree (a i + a j)` grows super-polynomially —
`∀ C > 0, ∃ N, ∀ j ≥ N, (j : ℝ)^C < a j`. Read back independently, the Lean matches its
own docstring exactly, and the docstring is a reasonable — and clearly disclosed —
rendering of the open-ended source question as the standard super-polynomial-growth
conjecture. I found no Part A defect; the one substantive nuance (the eventual form is
formally *stronger* than the literal negation of "a sequence of polynomial growth
exists") is an interpretation choice on an open-ended question, now documented in v2 with
the literal form added as a variant.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified), with one documented interpretation
nuance.**

- Independent back-translation of lines 30–37: "for every strictly increasing sequence
  of naturals all of whose pairwise sums (including doubles) are squarefree, and for
  every real $C > 0$, eventually $a_j > j^C$." This matches the theorem docstring
  (lines 23–29) word for word in content.
- The source question "How fast must $A$ grow?" is open-ended; the only precise
  directional content on the page is Erdős's expectation that no such sequence of
  polynomial growth exists. The formalization renders this as the standard
  "super-polynomial growth" conjecture. **Nuance (not graded a defect):** the eventual
  form $\forall C\,\exists N\,\forall j \ge N,\ a_j > j^C$ is *strictly stronger* than
  the literal negation of "a polynomial-growth sequence exists", which only demands that
  each valid sequence escape every fixed bound $K j^C$ (an infinitely-often statement).
  The two genuinely differ for abstract strictly increasing sequences: a sequence with
  very sparse polynomial-sized "dips" ($a_{j_k} = j_k^2$ at $j_k$ growing like a tower)
  and huge values in between escapes every polynomial bound without ever being eventually
  above $j^3$. Whether the gap can be realized *within* the squarefree-sumset class is
  itself unknown, and "must grow super-polynomially" in the analytic-number-theory sense
  standardly means the eventual form, so the chosen encoding is the natural conjecture —
  but the choice deserved documentation. Fixed in v2: docstring note + literal variant
  `no_polynomial_growth` (see Addendum).
- Quantifier structure: sequence universally quantified as a hypothesis-laden binder
  (direct assertion, appropriate here — see A4); `∀ C, ∃ N, ∀ j` is the correct
  eventual-dominance order for the intended reading.
- Types: `ℕ → ℕ` with the comparison in ℝ via `rpow` — correct, since `j ^ C` for real
  `C` needs ℝ. Casts are placed so all arithmetic in the conclusion happens in ℝ. The
  page says "integers" where the file says "positive integers": the page's own remarks
  ($A = \{a_1 < a_2 < \cdots\}$, $a_j > 0.24\,j^{4/3}$) support the positive reading, and
  over ℕ positivity is forced anyway — `Squarefree 0 = False` in Mathlib (take $x = 2$:
  $4 \mid 0$ but $2$ is not a unit), so `Squarefree (a 0 + a 0)` forces $a_0 \ge 1$ (and
  in fact every $a_i$ odd). No defect; documented in v2.
- Indexing: the page's remark is 1-indexed ($a_1 < a_2 < \cdots$), the file 0-indexed.
  For the main statement the conclusion is an eventual property, invariant under the
  shift, and term-by-term: source term $a_j$ ↔ Lean `a (j-1)`, with the Lean claim at
  index $j' = j-1$ reading $a_j > (j-1)^C$ — *weaker* at each fixed index than
  $a_j > j^C$, and over the `∃ N` this makes no difference in either direction (choose
  $N$ one larger; conversely $(j-1)^C < j^C$). Exact, no off-by-one. (The module
  docstring's own restatement "$a_j > 0.24 j^{4/3}$ for all $j$" is also literally true
  under its 0-indexed $A = \{a_0 < a_1 < \cdots\}$ framing, since the shifted bound is
  weaker; the v2 variant states the sharp index-shifted form.)
- Not vacuous, not trivial: the hypothesis is satisfiable — the page records Erdős's
  exponentially growing example and van Doorn–Tao's explicit construction — so the
  universal statement has content. For $C \le 1$ the conclusion follows trivially from
  `StrictMono` + $a_0 \ge 1$ ($a_j \ge a_0 + j > j \ge j^C$ for $j \ge 1$); the content
  is at $C > 1$, as expected. Noted in the v2 docstring.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- No ℕ subtraction anywhere.
- No ℕ division; the only division-adjacent construct is `rpow`. At $j = 0$:
  $(0 : ℝ)^C = 0$ for $C > 0$ (`Real.rpow` convention), and $0 < a_0$ holds anyway;
  in any case $j = 0$ sits below the eventual threshold. Harmless.
- No `Finset.range`; the eventual quantifier is `j ≥ N`, shift-robust (see A1).
- No vacuous bounded quantifiers: `∀ i j : ℕ` over an infinite domain, and the
  hypothesis genuinely constrains (e.g. $i = j$ forces $2a_i$ squarefree, hence every
  $a_i$ odd — the sumset convention $A + A \ni 2a$ is the standard one and matches the
  page's $n \in A + A$).
- "For all sufficiently large $j$" is encoded by hand as `∃ N, ∀ j ≥ N` — a correct and
  self-contained rendering (no filter machinery needed).
- `StrictMono` correctly captures $a_0 < a_1 < \cdots$; strict `<` in the conclusion is
  the right strictness (and over `∀ C` the strict and non-strict eventual forms are
  interderivable by raising $C$ — see Part E item 5 for the prior review's flawed
  justification of this same point).

## E. Prior-review audit (`deepmind/ai-review/1103.md`)

   squarefree numbers* (2025)" with a ✅; the `/latex/1103` extraction instead gives
   "*Growth rates of sequences governed by the squarefree properties of its

5. **Minor — flawed justification, right conclusion.** §6: strict `<` vs `≤` is "fine
