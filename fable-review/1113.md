## Summary

The problem (OPEN, [ErGr80, p.27]): a positive odd integer $m$ such that none of
$2^k m + 1$ are prime for $k \ge 0$ is a Sierpinski number; a set of primes $P$ is a
covering set for $m$ if every $2^k m + 1$ is divisible by some $p \in P$. Are there
Sierpinski numbers with **no finite covering set** of primes? Erdős and Graham thought
yes (otherwise there would be infinitely many Fermat primes). The Lean
(`conjectures/1113.lean:28-30`) directly asserts
`∃ m, IsSierpinskiNumber m ∧ ∀ P : Finset ℕ, ¬ HasFiniteCoveringSet m P`, with the two
helper definitions faithfully rendering the page's definitions (including the $k = 0$
term, exactly as the page states it). Read back independently, the Lean and the English
agree; the direct assertion of the conjectured "yes" direction follows this raw-file
corpus's convention for open yes/no questions. No Part A defect; the nits are
scholarship-level (no bibliography block at all for the two keys used; the Izotov
attribution folded into [FFK08]; the page's rich remarks — Sierpinski's infinitude,
Selfridge's 78557, Guy F13, the FFK revised conjecture and powers theorem, OEIS,
cross-references — all absent).

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "A positive odd integer $m$ such
  that none of $2^km+1$ are prime for $k\geq 0$ is called a Sierpinski number. We say
  that a set of primes $P$ is a covering set for $m$ if every $2^km+1$ is divisible by
  some $p\in P$. Are there Sierpinski numbers with no finite covering set of primes?"
- Independent back-translation of lines 28–30: "there exists a natural number $m$,
  positive, not divisible by 2, with $2^k m + 1$ never prime for any $k \ge 0$, such
  that for every finite set $P$ of naturals, $P$ is not a set of primes all of whose
  members' divisibility covers the family $\{2^k m + 1\}$." This is exactly "there is a
  Sierpinski number with no finite covering set of primes" — the question's affirmative
  direction.
- Quantifiers: `∃ m` inside the statement; finiteness of the covering set is carried by
  the type `Finset ℕ`, which is the right home for "finite covering set" (the page's
  *definition* of covering set does not require finiteness; the *question* is about
  finite ones, and quantifying `∀ P : Finset ℕ, ¬ …` says precisely "no finite set of
  primes covers"). Sets `P` containing a non-prime make `HasFiniteCoveringSet` false by
  its first conjunct, so negating over all of `Finset ℕ` neither weakens nor strengthens
  the intended "no finite covering set *of primes*".
- Types: everything in ℕ; `2 ^ k * m + 1` parses as `((2^k) * m) + 1` (pow > mul > add)
  — correct. `Finset ℕ` vs `Set ℕ`: correct choice, finiteness is the crux of the
  question (every Sierpinski number trivially has the infinite covering family of one
  prime factor per term).
- Indexing: the page's $k \ge 0$ is `∀ k : ℕ` — term-by-term exact, including $k = 0$
  (i.e. $m + 1$ not prime). The page states the $k\geq 0$ convention explicitly, so the
  classical $k \ge 1$ convention question (see Part E) does not arise.
- Not vacuous / no degenerate witness: `IsSierpinskiNumber` rejects $m = 0$ (via both
  conjuncts: `0 < m`, and `2 ∣ 0`) and $m = 1$ ($2^0 \cdot 1 + 1 = 2$ is prime); no
  cheap $m$ satisfies the conjunction, and `∀ P, ¬…` is a substantive condition (the
  known Sierpinski constructions all *have* finite covering sets). The statement is the
  genuinely open existence claim.

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- No ℕ subtraction, no division, no `Finset.range`, no filters/limits anywhere — the
  dominant trap classes are structurally absent.
- "Composite" (docstring/page paraphrase) vs `¬ Nat.Prime` (code): for $m \ge 1$,
  $2^k m + 1 \ge 2$, and on values $\ge 2$ not-prime ⟺ composite; moreover the page's
  own wording is "none of $2^km+1$ are prime", so `¬ Nat.Prime` is in fact the verbatim
  rendering. Exact.
- Vacuous quantifiers: `HasFiniteCoveringSet m ∅` is false (second conjunct fails at
  every `k`), which is the correct degenerate behavior — the empty set covers nothing.
  `∀ p ∈ ∅, Nat.Prime p` being vacuously true is harmless under the conjunction.
- No `StrictMono`/`Monotone`, interval, or strictness issues arise.

**A3. Definitions — pass.**

- `IsSierpinskiNumber` (line 8–9) matches its docstring and the source: positive
  (`0 < m`), odd (`¬ 2 ∣ m`), all terms non-prime (`∀ k`). `0 < m` is formally
  redundant given `¬ 2 ∣ m` (since `2 ∣ 0`) — kept for clarity, fine per checklist.
- `HasFiniteCoveringSet` (lines 13–14) matches its docstring and the page's definition
  of covering set, specialized to finite `P`; degenerate inputs behave correctly (A2).
- No `sorry` in any definition; no drift between the defs and the theorem's use of them.

**A4. Question form and answer polarity — pass under the raw-file convention.**

**A5. Solution status — pass (source-verified).**

## E. Prior-review audit (`deepmind/ai-review/1113.md`)

1. **Arithmetic error in its supporting computation.** To argue the $k = 0$ inclusion

   factorization is wrong: $2 \times 3 \times 13097 = 78582$, while

   The *conclusion* (78558 is composite — it is even) is trivially right, but a wrong
   concrete factorization presented as verification is exactly the
   invent-then-excuse pattern this pipeline's audits keep finding (cf. the 1000/1005
   audits): a fabricated check that happens to land on a true conclusion.
2. **Its $k \ge 0$ vs $k \ge 1$ analysis is correct and source-verified.** The page
   does state $k \ge 0$; the formalization matches the page; the classical definition
   (Sierpiński's $k \cdot 2^n + 1$, $n \ge 1$) is indeed $k \ge 1$. Its observation
   that $k \ge 0$ is strictly stronger than the classical convention is right.
3. **Its degenerate-case claim checks out.** `HasFiniteCoveringSet m ∅` is always false
   (the second conjunct fails at every $k$) — re-derived, correct.
4. **Its polarity check of the styled `answer(sorry) ↔ ∃ m, …` is correct** (RHS true ⟺
   answer "yes"; problem open ⟹ `sorry`), and `research open` matches the recovered
   OPEN banner.
5. **Citations section: endorsements without source access.** "[FFK08] … **Present and
   correct**" endorses bibliographic data (including data like volume numbers that the
   `/latex/1113` extraction does not carry) that it could not have verified against the
   authoritative source; its "Missing [Gu04]" and "naming Izotov explicitly would be
   more accurate" flags were, however, genuine and page-confirmed (both were fixed in
   the archived styled file; the raw file under review here still lacks both — fixed in
   v2).
6. **What it missed:** (a) the two *solved* page results as variants — Sierpinski's
   infinitude [Si60] and the FFK powers theorem ("for every $l\geq 1$ there is an $m$
   such that $2^km^i+1$ is composite for all $1\leq i\leq l$, $k\geq 0$") — it proposed
   only the FFK conjecture, the Fermat-primes implication, and 78557; (b) the $m = 0$
   vacuity trap in the FFK powers statement, which matters the moment anyone formalizes
   the page's literal wording (v2 documents it and encodes via `IsSierpinskiNumber
   (m ^ i)`); (c) its Fermat-primes-implication variant proposal carries no caveat that
   the page never spells out the argument, so a formalizer following it would have to

7. **Reuse survey endorsed:** its `CoveringSystem.lean` no-reuse analysis is sound
