## Summary

The problem: for $x_1, \ldots, x_n \in [-1,1]$ let
$l_k(x) = \prod_{i\neq k}(x-x_i) / \prod_{i\neq k}(x_k-x_i)$ be the Lagrange basis
polynomials. What is the minimal value of
$I(x_1,\ldots,x_n) = \int_{-1}^1 \sum_k |l_k(x)|^2\,dx$ — in particular, is it true that
$\min I = 2 - (1+o(1))\frac{1}{n}$? Status on the page: OPEN. The Lean file
(`conjectures/1131.lean`) defines the basis polynomials pointwise, the $L^2$ functional
as an interval integral, and $\min I$ as an `sInf` over injective node tuples in
$[-1,1]$, and formalizes the "in particular" conjecture as
`Tendsto (fun n => (n : ℝ) * (2 - minLagrangeL2 n)) atTop (nhds 1)` — a direct assertion
of the conjectured affirmative, per this corpus's raw-file convention for open
questions. Read back independently, the Lean and the source agree at every point I
checked, including all degenerate cases; I found no semantic defects, only polish-level
nits and enrichment opportunities (missing page context/citations; the two ESVV94
bounds as variants — with a small-$n$ correction the page's own statement needs).

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "For $x_1,\ldots,x_n\in [-1,1]$
  let \[l_k(x)=\frac{\prod_{i\neq k}(x-x_i)}{\prod_{i\neq k}(x_k-x_i)},\] which are such
  that $l_k(x_k)=1$ and $l_k(x_i)=0$ for $i\neq k$. What is the minimal value of
  \[I(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=\int_{-1}^1 \sum_k \lvert l_k(x)\rvert^2\mathrm{d}x?\] In
  particular, is it true that \[\min I =2-(1+o(1))\frac{1}{n}?\]" The module docstring
  (lines 11–27) reproduces this faithfully.
- Independent back-translation of lines 52–54: "the sequence $n \mapsto n(2 - \min I_n)$
  tends to 1." Since $\min I = 2 - (1+o(1))/n \iff 2 - \min I = (1+o(1))/n \iff
  n(2-\min I) \to 1$, this is exactly the "in particular" conjecture. The docstring
  (lines 49–50) states this equivalence explicitly and correctly.
- `lagrangeBasis` (lines 31–32) is the *factorwise* quotient
  $\prod_{i\neq k} (x-x_i)/(x_k-x_i)$ while the source writes a quotient of products.
  Equal whenever the nodes are pairwise distinct (all denominators nonzero) — and
  `minLagrangeL2` (lines 39–43) only evaluates it under `Function.Injective nodes`, so
  no discrepancy is reachable. (For non-injective nodes Lean's convention $x/0 = 0$
  makes the product 0, but that regime is excluded by the hypothesis.)
- `lagrangeL2` (lines 35–36) uses `^ 2` where the source writes $|\cdot|^2$; equal on
  ℝ. The interval integral over $-1..1$ of a polynomial integrand is well-defined.
- The `sInf` encoding of "minimal value": the defining set is nonempty for every $n$
  (injective tuples exist in $[-1,1]$ for any finite $n$) and bounded below by 0 (the
  integrand is a sum of squares, and the interval is correctly oriented), so `sInf` is a
  genuine infimum. Degenerate cases computed by hand: $n = 0$ gives the set $\{0\}$
  (empty sum, so $I \equiv 0$); $n = 1$ gives $\{2\}$ ($l_1 \equiv 1$ as the empty
  product, $\int_{-1}^1 1 = 2$). Both harmless under `atTop`. Mathematically the inf is
  attained (the functional blows up as nodes collide — for the two closest nodes
  $|x_k - x_j| = \delta \to 0$, $\int l_k^2 \ge c_n^2/(\delta\, 2^{n-2})^2 \to \infty$,
  where $c_n > 0$ lower-bounds the $L^2$ norm of monic degree-$(n{-}1)$ polynomials
  with roots in $[-1,1]$, by compactness), so `sInf` = the source's min.
- Not vacuous: a substantive asymptotic statement, consistent with but not implied by
  the known bounds ($2n/(2n-1) \le n(2 - \min I) \le O((\log n)^2)$, see B2).

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- No `Finset.range`; indexing is by `Fin n`, matching the source's $n$ nodes with no

**B2. Variants and completeness — the formalizable part is captured; enrichment applied.**

- The problem is two-part: the general "What is the minimal value of $I$?" (open-ended
  determine-type request, not formalizable as a precise statement — its omission is
  correct scope, now noted in the v2 module docstring) and the specific asymptotic
  conjecture, which is formalized.
- Page-confirmed enrichment: the ESVV94 upper and lower bounds make worthwhile solved
  variants. **The page's upper bound $\min I \le 2 - 2/(2n-1)$ is literally false at
  $n = 1$**: there $\min I = 2$ (the single basis polynomial is $\equiv 1$) while the
  bound claims $\min I \le 0$. For $n \ge 2$ it is correct, and the witness nodes attain
  it with equality at $n = 2$ ($\min I = 4/3$ at nodes $\pm 1$) and $n = 3$
  ($I(-1,0,1) = 2[1 - \tfrac32 x^2 + \tfrac32 x^4]$-integral $= 8/5 = 2 - 2/5$), both
  verified by direct computation. Formalized in v2 with the guard $n \ge 2$ and the
  counterexample documented (same treatment as 1004's EPS87 bound). The lower bound is
  formalized as $\exists C > 0, \forall n \ge 1$: valid for every $n \ge 1$ since
  $\log 1 = 0$ gives exactly $2 \le \min I = 2$ at $n = 1$ and finitely many $n$ absorb
  into $C$.
- The Fejér [Fe32] max-functional result and the Szabados [Sz66] disproof would need
  Legendre-polynomial machinery not present in the file — noted as skipped, recorded in
  prose instead.

## E. Prior-review audit (`deepmind/ai-review/1131.md`)

Written against the styled copy (`deepmind/deepmind/1131.lean`), so its line numbers
(45–46, 49–50, 53–57, 69–72) do not match the raw file (31–32, 35–36, 39–43, 52–54).
Its mathematics is largely sound — re-derived claim by claim:

1. **Verified correct:** the $n = 0$ and $n = 1$ degenerate values of the `sInf` set
   ($\{0\}$ and $\{2\}$ respectively) — both re-computed and confirmed; the factorwise
   vs quotient-of-products equivalence under distinctness; `x ^ 2` = $|x|^2$ on ℝ; the
   `univ.filter (· ≠ k)` / `univ.erase k` equivalence it cites for 671; the
   consistency check $n(2 - \min I) \ge 2n/(2n-1) \to 1$ (so liminf ≥ 1) and
   $n(2-\min I) \le O((\log n)^2)$ — both re-derived and correct.
2. **Sketchy-but-repairable argument, right conclusion (attainment).** Its claim that
   the infimum is attained "since the L² functional blows up as nodes collide … by a
   compactness argument on the closure" is correct in conclusion but incomplete as
   stated: the blow-up needs a uniform positive lower bound on the $L^2$ norm of the
   numerator polynomial (monic, degree $n-1$, roots in $[-1,1]$ — obtained by
   compactness of the root locus), which the prior review never supplies. Unlike the
   1005 audit's boundedness argument, the gap is fillable and the conclusion true; and
   the review correctly notes the distinction doesn't affect the limit statement.
3. **Missed: the page's upper bound fails at $n = 1$.** It recommends formalizing
   $\min I \le 2 - 2/(2n-1)$ "as a sanity check" with no small-$n$ caveat, but at
   $n = 1$ the bound reads $\min I \le 0$ while $\min I = 2$ — its own $n = 1$
   computation ("$\{2\}$") two sections earlier contradicts the unguarded bound it
   endorses. The v2 variant carries the necessary $n \ge 2$ guard. (Failure class:
   endorsing a false page-stated bound; cf. 1004's EPS87 bound.)
4. **Misattributed key.** Its citation list glosses "**Va99** — Varma (1999)". In this
   corpus and on the site, [Va99] is "Various, *Some of Paul's favorite problems*"
   (booklet for the Budapest 1999 conference), cited by the page with a section pointer
   "[Va99, 2.45]" — section-style pointers ([Va99, 5.74], §7.90 elsewhere) confirm a
   problem booklet, not a paper by Varma. A plausible-looking misattribution (Varma is
   an ESVV94 co-author). Minor, but it would have propagated a wrong bibliography entry.
5. **Context-bound claims:** "The `answer(sorry)` wrapper correctly marks this as a
   True/False open question" is true of the styled copy only; the raw file has no
   wrapper (and needs none, per A4). Its citation list (Er61, Fe32, Sz66, ESVV94,
   Er95e, Va99) matches the recovered page's keys exactly — verified.
