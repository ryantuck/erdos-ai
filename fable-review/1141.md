## Summary

The problem (OPEN on the source page): "Are there infinitely many $n$ such that
$n-k^2$ is prime for all $k$ with $(n,k)=1$ and $k^2<n$?" — [Va99, 1.6], with
the page remarking that [Va99]'s own suggestion that $968$ is the largest such
integer is an error ($968-9 = 959 = 7\cdot 137$), that the qualifying $n$ form
OEIS A214583 with largest known member $1722$, and that ChatGPT and Tang have
shown there are at most $N^{1/2+o(1)}$ such $n$ in $[1,N]$. The Lean
(`conjectures/1141.lean:14-16`) asserts
`Set.Infinite {n : ℕ | ∀ k : ℕ, k ^ 2 < n → Nat.Coprime n k → Nat.Prime (n - k ^ 2)}`.
Read back independently, this is exactly the asked ("yes") direction of the
open question, stated as a direct assertion — the established convention of
this raw corpus for open yes/no questions (cf. sibling 1146; disproved sibling
1140 asserts the true direction). The mathematics is sound, with the edge cases
($k = 0$, $n \in \{0, 1\}$) checked below and confined to finitely many $n$,
hence invisible to `Set.Infinite`. The sole defect is bibliographic: the key
`[Va99,1.6]` is used but never defined.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified + computationally corroborated).**

- Recovered verbatim source statement (see Addendum): "Are there infinitely
  many $n$ such that $n-k^2$ is prime for all $k$ with $(n,k)=1$ and $k^2<n$?"
  Status banner **OPEN**, tooltip "This is open, and cannot be resolved with a
  finite computation."
- Independent back-translation of lines 14–16: "the set of natural numbers $n$
  such that $n - k^2$ is prime for every natural $k$ with $k^2 < n$ and
  $\gcd(n, k) = 1$ is infinite." `Set.Infinite` is the correct encoding of
  "there are infinitely many $n$"; the membership predicate is the source's
  property verbatim ($\gcd(n,k)=1$ ↦ `Nat.Coprime n k`, $k^2<n$ ↦
  `k ^ 2 < n`, "$n-k^2$ is prime" ↦ `Nat.Prime (n - k ^ 2)`).
- Quantifier structure: $n$ is bound by the set-builder inside `Set.Infinite`
  (not a theorem binder); $k$ is universally quantified inside the membership
  predicate with both side conditions as hypotheses. Matches "for all $k$ with
  $(n,k)=1$ and $k^2<n$". Hypothesis order (`k ^ 2 < n` before `Coprime`) is
  immaterial.
- Types: everything in ℕ is appropriate — the source's $n, k$ are positive
  integers, and the ℕ-vs-positivity mismatches are confined to $n \in \{0,1\}$
  and $k = 0$, all checked in A2 and all harmless to infinitude. Negative $k$
  need no representation ($k^2$ is even in $k$).
- Membership verified against the page's own data, not just read off: brute
  force to 5000 under the Lean encoding yields
  $\{0\} \cup \{3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 14, 18, 20, 24, 30, 32, 38, 42, 48, 54, 60,
  62, 68, 72, 80, 84, 90, 98, 108, 110, 132, 138, 140, 150, 180, 182, 198,
  252, 318, 360, 398, 468, 570, 572, 930, 1722\}$ — largest member below 5000
  is exactly the page's "largest known such $n$", $1722$; and $968$ is
  correctly *not* a member (the page's [Va99]-erratum remark), via $k = 3$:
  $\gcd(968,3)=1$, $9 < 968$, $968-9 = 959 = 7 \cdot 137$. The OEIS terms
  themselves are unverifiable offline (DEFERRED), but the two page-stated
  anchors both reproduce.
- Indexing: no sequences, no index shifts to reconcile.
- Not vacuous/trivial: whether the set is infinite is a genuinely open
  question (the page: "cannot be resolved with a finite computation"); the
  known upper bound $N^{1/2+o(1)}$ on its counting function shows the set is
  sparse but does not settle infinitude. No degenerate witness trivializes the
  statement (the only junk member is $n = 0$, one element).

**A2. Lean semantics traps — pass.**

- **ℕ subtraction** `n - k ^ 2` (line 15) is guarded by `k ^ 2 < n`, so it
  never truncates and the argument of `Nat.Prime` is positive. Correct.
- No division anywhere.
- No `Finset.range`, no filters, no `StrictMono`/`Monotone`, no intervals.
- **`k = 0` instance:** `Nat.Coprime n 0 ↔ n = 1` (since `gcd n 0 = n`), so
  for every $n \ge 2$ the $k = 0$ instance is discharged by the coprimality
  hypothesis and the quantifier effectively ranges over $k \ge 1$ — exactly
  the source's reading. At $n = 1$ the instance demands `Nat.Prime (1 - 0)`,
  i.e. `Nat.Prime 1`, false — so $1 \notin$ the set. Under the alternative
  "$k \ge 1$" reading of the source, $1$ would instead be a vacuous member.
  The two readings differ **only** at $n = 1$ (verified computationally: the
  symmetric difference of the two qualifying sets up to 5000 is exactly
  $\{1\}$), a single element with no effect on `Set.Infinite`.
- **Vacuous quantifier at `n = 0`:** no `k` satisfies `k ^ 2 < 0`, so $0$ is
  a spurious member of the set. One junk element; harmless to infinitude.
  (Both blemishes now documented in the v2 docstring.)
- Strict `k ^ 2 < n` matches the source's $k^2 < n$ verbatim; a non-strict
  `≤` would differ only at $n = 1$ ($k = 1$, $\gcd = 1$, demands
  `Nat.Prime 0`) since for $n = k^2 \ge 4$ coprimality already fails
  ($\gcd(k^2, k) = k > 1$) — so the choice is faithful and nothing rides on
  it.
- `Set.Infinite` (as `¬ Set.Finite`) is the standard and correct encoding of
  "infinitely many" for a subset of ℕ; no filter/density subtleties arise.
