## Summary

The problem (recovered verbatim): "If $\delta>0$ and $N$ is sufficiently large
in terms of $\delta$, and $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ is such that
$\sum_{a\in A}\frac{1}{a}>\delta \log N$ then must there exist $S\subseteq A$
such that $\sum_{n\in S}\frac{1}{n}=1$?" The page banner is PROVED (LEAN)
("solved in the affirmative and the proof verified in Lean"), $100 prize:
solved by Bloom [Bl21] via the quantitative threshold
$\sum 1/n \gg \frac{\log\log\log N}{\log\log N}\log N$, improved by
Liu–Sawhney [LiSa24] to $(\log N)^{4/5+o(1)}$. The Lean theorem asserts: for
every real $\delta > 0$ there is $N_0$ such that for all $N \ge N_0$ and every
finite $A$ with $\forall a \in A,\ 1 \le a \le N$ satisfying
$\sum_{a \in A} 1/a > \delta \log N$ (in ℝ), there is a nonempty $S \subseteq A$
with $\sum_{n \in S} 1/n = 1$. Read back independently, this is exactly the
question's affirmative answer — the direction Bloom proved — and it matches the
right-hand side of upstream's `erdos_47 : answer(True) ↔ …` up to a provably
immaterial `S.Nonempty` conjunct. No semantic defects; scholarship and polish
nits only.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity — pass (source-verified).**

- Independent back-translation of `conjectures/47.lean:17-23`: "For every real
  δ > 0 there exists a natural N₀ such that for every natural N ≥ N₀ and every
  finite set A of naturals, all in [1, N], whose real reciprocal sum exceeds
  δ·log N, there is a nonempty finite S ⊆ A whose reciprocal sum equals 1."
  This matches the page's question clause by clause: "N sufficiently large in
  terms of δ" = `∃ N₀, ∀ N ≥ N₀` with δ bound first (correct dependency
  order); "A ⊆ {1,…,N}" = `∀ a ∈ A, 1 ≤ a ∧ a ≤ N` (extensionally
  `A ⊆ Finset.Icc 1 N`, upstream's phrasing); the strict `>` in
  "∑ 1/a > δ log N" is preserved; "∃ S ⊆ A with ∑ 1/n = 1" =
  `∃ S, S.Nonempty ∧ S ⊆ A ∧ ∑ = 1`.
- `S.Nonempty` is a logically redundant strengthening (the empty sum is
  0 ≠ 1, so any witness is automatically nonempty) — equivalent to the source,
  harmless, arguably clarifying.
- Types: sums in ℝ (needed anyway for `Real.log`), naturals coerced inside the
  division `(1 : ℝ) / a` — arithmetic happens in ℝ throughout, as intended.
  (Sibling problems 46/298 compute the reciprocal sum in ℚ; upstream 47 uses
  ℝ. Equality with 1 coincides under the injective cast, so the choice is
  immaterial; here ℝ matches upstream exactly.)
- Indexing: no sequences, no `range` — the interval [1, N] is encoded by
  explicit inequalities, both endpoints inclusive, exactly the source's
  $\{1, \ldots, N\}$.
- Not vacuous, not trivial: for δ < 1 the hypothesis is amply satisfiable
  ($\sum_{a\le N} 1/a = \log N + O(1)$), and the conclusion is Bloom's deep
  theorem. Two degenerate regimes checked: (i) for δ > 1 the hypothesis is
  eventually unsatisfiable, so those δ hold vacuously — but the informal
  statement has the identical property (the content is at small δ), so this
  is faithful, not a defect; (ii) if 1 ∈ A then S = {1} is a trivial witness —
  also present in the informal problem (the page's range starts at 1). Both
  documented in v2.
