## Summary

The page (PROVED, prize \$250) states that the density of the integers with two divisors $d_1<d_2<2d_1$ exists
and equals $1$. Erdős asked in [Er79] for the stronger version with $2$ replaced by any $c>1$, and Maier and
Tenenbaum [MaTe84] proved that too. Erdős had claimed in [Er64h], and retracted in [ErHa79], that the set with
$d_1<d_2<d_1(1+(\log n)^{-\beta})$ has density $1$ for $\beta<\log3-1$. Erdős and Hall [ErHa79] proved density $0$
for $\beta>\log3-1$, and [MaTe84] proves density $1$ for $\beta<\log3-1$.

The Lean states $A(N)/N\to1$ with `Finset.range N` and the shift `n + 1`.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: pass.**

- **Source** (verbatim): "The density of integers which have two divisors $d_1,d_2$ such that $d_1<d_2<2d_1$
  exists and is equal to $1$."
- **Back-translation of the input's theorem:** "With $A(N)=\#\{m\in[1,N]:\exists\, d_1,d_2\mid m,\
  d_1<d_2<2d_1\}$, the ratio $A(N)/N$ tends to $1$."
- **Checks.**
  - *The set.* `HasCloseConsecutiveDivisors n` is `∃ d₁ d₂, d₁ ∣ n ∧ d₂ ∣ n ∧ d₁ < d₂ ∧ d₂ < 2 * d₁`, the page's
    property. For $n\ge1$ a divisor is at least $1$ (`0 ∣ n` only for `n = 0`), and $d_1=0$ is excluded anyway
    because `d₂ < 2 * 0` is false.
  - *The count.* `(Finset.range N).filter (fun n => P (n + 1))` counts the $n\in\{0,\dots,N-1\}$ for which $n+1$
    has the property, that is, the $m\in\{1,\dots,N\}$. This is $A(N)$, with no off-by-one.
    `variants.count_thirty` proves in Lean that this count is $8$ for $N=30$, with the numbers
    $6,12,15,18,20,24,28,30$, and a sieve agrees (Addendum).
  - *"The density exists and equals 1".* For a set of positive integers, natural density $1$ is
    $A(N)/N\to1$, and the limit statement includes the existence. The weaker notions (lower, upper,
    logarithmic density) all equal $1$ when the natural density is $1$, so the choice of notion does not matter.
  - *At small $N$.* For $N=0$ the quotient is $0/0=0$ in Lean. `atTop` ignores finitely many $N$.
  - *The statement is not refuted by numbers, and not visible in them either.* The convergence is extremely
    slow: a sieve gives $A(N)/N=0.392$ at $N=10^3$, $0.476$ at $10^6$ and $0.491$ at $10^7$. The page records the
    density as $1$ [MaTe84], so the data show slow growth and no contradiction. The model did not try to
    reconcile the rate with the literature. A finite computation cannot confirm this statement.
  - *Close versus adjacent.* `variants.consecutive_iff` proves in Lean that `n` has such a pair iff it has such a
    pair with no divisor of `n` strictly between them (take a pair with `d₂ - d₁` minimal). So the word
    `Consecutive` in the name is harmless, although the definition does not require adjacency.
