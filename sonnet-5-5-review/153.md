## Summary

The page (OPEN; tag "sidon sets") asks: for a finite Sidon set $A$ with $A+A=\{s_1<\cdots<s_t\}$, does
$\frac1t\sum_{1\le i<t}(s_{i+1}-s_i)^2\to\infty$ as $\lvert A\rvert\to\infty$? It remarks that a similar problem can be
asked for infinite Sidon sets.

The Lean defines Sidon sets and the sumset on `Finset ℕ`, the sum of squared consecutive gaps of a list, and states
the question as "for every `M` there is `N` such that every Sidon set with at least `N` elements has
`M * t ≤ ∑ gaps²`".

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: pass.**

- **Source** (verbatim): "Let $A$ be a finite Sidon set and $A+A=\{s_1<\cdots<s_t\}$. Is it true that
  $\frac{1}{t}\sum_{1\leq i<t}(s_{i+1}-s_i)^2 \to \infty$ as $\lvert A\rvert\to \infty$?"
- **Back-translation of the input's theorem:** "For every natural number `M` there is `N` such that for every finite
  Sidon set `A` with at least `N` elements, if `S` is the ascending list of `A + A`, then `M` times the length of `S`
  is at most the sum of the squares of the differences of consecutive entries of `S`."
- **Checks.**
  - *Sidon and sumset.* The same definitions as Problem 152: `IsSidonSet` is the standard definition (all sums
    `a + b` with `a ≤ b` distinct), and `sumset A` includes the sums `a + a`.
  - *The sorted list.* `(sumset A).sort (· ≤ ·)` is the ascending list of the distinct elements, of length `t`
    (`Finset.length_sort`), so it is $s_1<\cdots<s_t$.
  - *The sum and its indexing.* `sumSquaredGaps` sums `(b - a) ^ 2` over consecutive pairs `a, b`. The page's sum
    is over $1\le i<t$, that is, $t-1$ pairs, with the divisor $t$ and not $t-1$. `variants.sumSquaredGaps_eq_sum` proves
    in Lean that `sumSquaredGaps l = ∑ i < l.length - 1, (l[i+1] - l[i])²`, so the translation is exact, and the
    input's comparison `M * S.length ≤ …` has the divisor $t=$ `S.length`. The subtraction `b - a` in `ℕ` is exact
    since the list is ascending.
  - *The limit.* "$\to\infty$ as $\lvert A\rvert\to\infty$" is: for every `M` there is `N` such that for all Sidon sets
    of size at least `N` the mean is at least `M`, which is `∀ M ∃ N ∀ A`; with `t ≥ 1` the mean is at least `M` iff
    `M * t ≤ ∑ gaps²`. For `M` natural this is the same as for real `M`.
  - *Non-triviality.* "Sufficiently large" is needed: for `{0, 1}` the mean is $2/3<1$ (`variants.gaps_zero_one`). The
    mean is at least about $4$ for large $\lvert A\rvert$ by an elementary argument: by Cauchy–Schwarz it is at least
    $(2D)^2/(t(t-1))$ with $D=\max A-\min A$, and $D\ge n(n-1)/2$ since the $n(n-1)/2$ positive differences of a Sidon
    set are distinct, so it exceeds $4(n-1)^2/(n+1)^2$ (not formalized). So the statement is true for $M\le3$ (for
    $n\ge14$), and the open content is the growth beyond a constant.
  - *Evidence.* The least mean squared gap over Sidon sets of size $n$ (exact search, Addendum) is
    $0.67,1.33,1.80,2.80,3.52,4.50,5.50$ for $n=2,\dots,8$, increasing at each step.
