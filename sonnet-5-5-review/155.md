## Summary

The page (OPEN; tags "additive combinatorics" and "sidon sets") asks: with $F(N)$ the size of the largest Sidon subset of
$\{1,\dots,N\}$, is it true that for every $k\ge1$ the inequality $F(N+k)\le F(N)+1$ holds for all sufficiently large
$N$? It remarks that this may even hold with $k\approx\epsilon N^{1/2}$.

The Lean defines Sidon sets on `Finset ℕ` and states the question without defining $F$: for every `k ≥ 1`, eventually in
`N`, every Sidon `A ⊆ range (N + k)` has a Sidon `B ⊆ range N` with `A.card ≤ B.card + 1`.

- **The Golomb reformulation.** With $L(n)=$ `golombLength n` (the least $m$ with a Sidon subset of size $n$ in
  $\{0,\dots,m\}$), `le_maxSidon_iff` proves $n\le F(N)\iff L(n)<N$ for $n\ge1$, and `main_iff_gaps` proves that the main
  statement is equivalent to "for every $k$, $L(m+1)+k\le L(m+2)$ for all large $m$". So the problem asks whether the
  differences $L(n+1)-L(n)$ of consecutive optimal Golomb ruler lengths tend to infinity. The inequality
  $F(N+k)\le F(N)+1$ fails exactly at the $N$ with $F(N)=m$ and $L(m+2)<N+k$, so the largest failing $N$ at level $m$ is
  $N=L(m+1)$, and it fails there iff $L(m+2)-L(m+1)<k$.
- **The remark.** `epsilon_sqrt` is one reading. With the classical $F(N)=N^{1/2}+O(N^{1/4})$ (Erdős–Turán, Lindström;
  background knowledge of the model, not from the page, not formalized), $L(n)\sim n^2$ and the average of
  $L(n+1)-L(n)$ is about $2n$. The remark then asks that every gap be at least a constant multiple of $n$. A statement
  for every $\epsilon>0$ would be false: gaps of at least $\epsilon n$ give $L(n)\ge(\epsilon/2-o(1))n^2$, contradicting
  $L(n)\sim n^2$ once $\epsilon>2$ (by hand). **DEFERRED:** the precise meaning of the remark.
