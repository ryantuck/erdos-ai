## Summary

The page (OPEN, no prize) asks: with $s_1<s_2<\cdots$ the squarefree numbers, does
$\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac1x\sum_{s_n\le x}(s_{n+1}-s_n)^\alpha$ exist for every $\alpha\ge0$? Erdős proved it for
$0\le\alpha\le2$, Hooley for $\alpha\le3$, Greaves–Harman–Huxley for $\alpha\le11/3$, and Chan for
$\alpha\le3.75$. Granville proved that it follows from the ABC conjecture for all $\alpha\ge0$.

The Lean defines `nextSquarefree` as the smallest squarefree number above `n`, and states the existence of the
limit over $N\in\mathbb N$ for a real $\alpha\ge0$.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: pass.**

- **Source** (verbatim): "Let $s_1<s_2<\cdots$ be the sequence of squarefree numbers. Is it true that, for any
  $\alpha \geq 0$, $\lim_{x\to \infty}\frac{1}{x}\sum_{s_n\leq x}(s_{n+1}-s_n)^\alpha$ exists?"
- **Back-translation of the input's theorem:** "For every real $\alpha\ge0$ there is a real $L$ such that
  $\frac1N\sum_{s\in[0,N],\ s\text{ squarefree}}(\mathrm{next}(s)-s)^\alpha\to L$ as $N\to\infty$, where
  $\mathrm{next}(s)$ is the least squarefree number above $s$."
- **Checks.**
  - *The sum.* `(Finset.range (N + 1)).filter Squarefree` is the set of squarefree $s\le N$. `Squarefree 0` is
    false (a squarefree number is non-zero), so the range starts effectively at $s=1$, the first term $s_1=1$.
    The gap is the distance to the *next* squarefree number, which may lie beyond $N$, as on the page, where
    $s_{n+1}$ is the successor of $s_n\le x$.
  - *Natural versus real limit.* The page takes $x\to\infty$ over the reals. The sum is constant on
    $[N,N+1)$ and $\frac1x$ lies between $\frac1{N+1}$ and $\frac1N$, so the real limit exists iff the limit over
    $N$ exists, with the same value. This is an informal argument. It is the only step the input takes from
    $x\in\mathbb R$ to $N\in\mathbb N$.
  - *"For any $\alpha\ge0$".* The theorem takes $\alpha$ and `0 ≤ α` as parameters, which is the universal
    statement. The exponent is real, so the power is `Real.rpow`, and the base is a gap, at least $1$, so there
    is no junk value at a negative or zero base. For $\alpha=0$ every term is $1$.
  - *The value at small cases.* `variants.nextSquarefree_seven` proves `nextSquarefree 7 = 10` (`8` and `9` are not
    squarefree), and `variants.gapMoment_one_seven` proves `gapMoment 1 7 = 9 / 7` from the gaps
    $1,1,2,1,1,3$ after $1,2,3,5,6,7$, including the gap that crosses $N=7$. A computation agrees (Addendum).
  - *The successor function.* `variants.nextSquarefree_spec` proves in Lean that `nextSquarefree n` is above `n`,
    squarefree, and below every squarefree number above `n`, so the definition is the intended function once
    the existence is proved.
  - *Numerical sanity.* The averages for $\alpha=0,1,2,3,4$ up to $N=10^9$ converge to $6/\pi^2=0.60793$, to $1$,
    and to about $2.0407$, $5.0429$ and $14.523$. This is consistent with the conjecture, including $\alpha=4$
    beyond the known range. It proves nothing.
