## Summary

The page (OPEN) defines $F(N)$ as the largest size of a subset of $\{1,\dots,N\}$ in which no element
divides the sum of any distinct other elements, and asks to estimate it, in particular whether
$F(N)>N^{1/2-o(1)}$. It records $F(N)<3N^{1/2}+1$ (Erdős–Lev–Rauzy–Sándor–Sárközy), $F(N)\gg N^{1/5}$
(Csaba), and $F(N)\le N^{1/4+o(1)}$ (Pham–Zakharov, because every such set is non-averaging). So the
explicit question has the answer no.

The Lean defines $F(N)$ correctly and then states that $F(N)\ge N^{1/4-\varepsilon}$, a guess that
the page does not make. The statement is open, as far as the sources go, so it is not false. It is
the wrong target.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: defect (fixed).**

- **Source** (verbatim): "Let $F(N)$ be the maximal size of $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ such that no
  $a\in A$ divides the sum of any distinct elements of $A\backslash\{a\}$. Estimate $F(N)$. In
  particular, is it true that $F(N) > N^{1/2-o(1)}$?"
- **Back-translation of the input's theorem:** "For every $\varepsilon>0$ and all large $N$,
  $F(N)\ge N^{1/4-\varepsilon}$."
- **[defect: wrong-target-statement]** This is not the question.
  - The explicit question is about the exponent $1/2$, and the page answers it no.
  - "Estimate $F(N)$" is open-ended. The page says only that the correct growth remains open. The
    known range is $N^{1/5}\ll F(N)\le N^{1/4+o(1)}$, and the page conjectures nothing inside it.
  - The input resolves the open-ended part with its own guess, that the exponent is $1/4$.
- **v2 main theorem:** `¬ (∀ ε > 0, ∀ᶠ N, F N > N ^ (1/2 - ε))`, the negation of the page's question.
- **Reading of "$F(N)>N^{1/2-o(1)}$".** For every $\varepsilon>0$, $F(N)>N^{1/2-\varepsilon}$ for all
  large $N$. That is equivalent to the existence of some $\varepsilon(N)\to0$ with
  $F(N)>N^{1/2-\varepsilon(N)}$: one direction is immediate, and for the other, choose
  thresholds $N_k$ for $\varepsilon=1/k$ and set $\varepsilon(N)=1/k$ on $[N_k,N_{k+1})$.

**A2. Lean semantics traps: pass, with one reading point.**

- **"The sum of any distinct elements".** The input reads it as the sum of any nonempty set of distinct
  elements of $A\setminus\{a\}$, singletons included. So the property includes that no element divides
  another. The other natural reading uses sums of at least two elements. Exhaustive search for
  $N\le45$ (a depth-first search that exploits heredity) gives the two maxima $F_1$ and $F_2$:

  | $N$ | 1 | 2 | 3 | 5 | 10 | 16 | 21 | 30 | 31 | 43 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | $F_1$, one or more summands | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
  | $F_2$, two or more summands | 1 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 6 | 6 | 7 |
  | $3\sqrt N+1$ | 4 | 5.2 | 6.2 | 7.7 | 10.5 | 13.0 | 14.8 | 17.4 | 17.7 | 20.7 |

  - **The readings differ at small $N$** but agree to within a factor $\log N$: $F_1\le F_2$, and
    $F_2\le(\log_2N+1)F_1$. Indeed, in a set valid for the second reading, the elements with a fixed
    number of prime factors (counted with multiplicity) form a divisibility antichain, there are at
    most $\log_2N+1$ such classes, and a subset of a valid set stays valid. A divisibility antichain
    valid for the second reading is valid for the first.
  - **So every statement about exponents is reading-independent**, including the main theorem
    (the upper bound $N^{1/4+o(1)}$ holds for both, since a non-dividing set is non-averaging under
    either reading), `pz_upper` and `pz_tight`. Only a constant-factor statement such as
    `csaba_lower` can depend on it.
  - The prior review asserts that the first reading is "the standard interpretation". The page's
    OEIS sequence A068063 might settle it, but could not be recovered offline.
- **`erdos131F`** is the `sSup` of a set containing $0$ (the empty set is non-dividing) and bounded by
  $N$ (a subset of $\{1,\dots,N\}$ has at most $N$ elements). So it is the true maximum, attained.
  `Finset.erase`, `S.sum id` and divisibility in ℕ are used correctly.
- **Real powers.** `(N : ℝ) ^ (1/2 - ε)` is a real power. For $\varepsilon\ge\tfrac12$ the exponent is
  non-positive and the bound is trivial, which is harmless for a statement over every
  $\varepsilon>0$. In the first pass's statement the case $\varepsilon\ge\tfrac14$ is likewise
  trivial, since $F(N)\ge1$ for $N\ge1$.
- **Agreement with the extremal data.** $F(N)$ stays far below the page's bound $3\sqrt N+1$, as the
  table shows. This is a consistency check on the definition, nothing more.
