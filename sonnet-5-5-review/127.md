## Summary

The page (PROVED at capture) asks whether some infinite sequence $m_i$ has $f(m_i)\to\infty$,
where $f(m)$ is the largest bonus over Edwards' bound $m/2+(\sqrt{8m+1}-1)/8$ guaranteed in every
graph with $m$ edges. It records Edwards' $f(m)\ge0$, Alon's solution ($f(n^2/2)\gg n^{1/2}$),
Alon's $f(m)\ll m^{1/4}$ for all $m$, and that the best constant there is unknown.

The Lean states the same question with a real-valued `f` built from the max cut. The statement and
the definitions agree with the page. The faults are in the surroundings: a docstring remark that is
false for even $n$, and references that are cited but not defined. The status label is right
(PROVED), and it has not changed since capture.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: pass.**

- **Source** (verbatim): "Let $f(m)$ be maximal such that every graph with $m$ edges must contain a
  bipartite graph with $\geq \frac{m}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{8m+1}-1}{8}+f(m)$ edges. Is there an
  infinite sequence of $m_i$ such that $f(m_i)\to \infty$?"
- **Back-translation:** "There is a strictly increasing sequence $(m_i)$ of natural numbers with
  $f(m_i)\to+\infty$, where $f(m)$ is the infimum, over finite graphs $G$ with $m$ edges, of
  $\mathrm{maxBip}(G)-\mathrm{edwardsLB}(m)$."
- **`f` is the page's "maximal" value.** A real $r$ is admissible iff
  $\mathrm{maxBip}(G)\ge\mathrm{edwardsLB}(m)+r$ for every $G$ with $m$ edges, iff $r$ is at most
  the infimum of $\mathrm{maxBip}(G)-\mathrm{edwardsLB}(m)$. So the maximal admissible $r$ is that
  infimum.
- **"Infinite sequence" and "strictly increasing" agree.** Suppose $f(m_i)\to\infty$ along some
  sequence. No value $m$ occurs infinitely often, since $f(m)$ is finite. For each $K$, only finitely
  many $m$ in the set of values have $f(m)\le K$. Listing that set in increasing order gives a
  strictly increasing sequence along which $f\to\infty$. The converse is immediate.
- **Upstream reading.** Upstream takes `f` to be the largest *integer* $k$, which is
  $\lfloor f\rfloor$, and states `Tendsto m atTop atTop ∧ Tendsto (f ∘ m) atTop atTop`. Since
  $\lfloor x\rfloor\to\infty$ iff $x\to\infty$, the two questions agree.

**A2. Lean semantics traps: pass.**

- **`cutSize`** counts ordered pairs $(v,w)\in V\times V$ with $v\in S$, $w\notin S$ and
  `G.Adj v w`. For an undirected edge $\{a,b\}$ with $a\in S$, $b\notin S$ only $(a,b)$
  qualifies, because $(b,a)$ fails $b\in S$. So each cut edge is counted once.
- **`maxBipartiteSubgraphSize`** is `Finset.univ.sup (cutSize G)` over all `S : Finset V`. The
  edges of a bipartite subgraph with parts $A$, $B$ lie in the cut of $A$, and the cut itself is a
  bipartite subgraph. So the maximum cut is the largest bipartite subgraph. For empty `V` the sup
  is over $\{\emptyset\}$ and equals 0.
- **`edwardsLB m`** is $m/2+(\sqrt{8m+1}-1)/8$ in $\mathbb R$, with the cast and `Real.sqrt` as on
  the page.
- **`f` is a true infimum, with no junk value.** The set is nonempty (a path on $m+1$ vertices has
  $m$ edges) and bounded below by $-\mathrm{edwardsLB}(m)$, because cut sizes are nonnegative. The
  cut sizes are integers in $[0,m]$, so the set is finite and the infimum is a minimum.
- **Instance binders inside `∃`.** The binders `(_ : Fintype V) (_ : DecidableEq V)
  (_ : DecidableRel G.Adj)` are the only instances of those classes in scope. `G.edgeFinset` and
  `maxBipartiteSubgraphSize G` therefore use them, and the file compiles.
- **Universe.** `V : Type` loses no graphs, since every finite graph is isomorphic to one on
  `Fin n`.
- **Edge count.** `G.edgeFinset.card` counts undirected edges. Isolated vertices are allowed and
  change nothing.
- **Limit.** `Tendsto (fun i => f (seq i)) atTop atTop` is divergence to $+\infty$ in $\mathbb R$,
  as asked.

## C. Craftsmanship

**C1. Readability: one false docstring claim (fixed).**

- **[defect: misleading-docstring-remark]** The `f` docstring ends "Note that f(C(n,2)) = 0,
  achieved by Kₙ." For the real-valued `f` of this file, that is exact for odd $n$ and false for
  even $n$:
  - **Odd $n=2k+1$.** $m=k(2k+1)$ and $8m+1=(4k+1)^2$, so $\mathrm{edwardsLB}(m)=k(k+1)$, which is
    the max cut of $K_n$. The excess is $0$, and $f(m)=0$ by Edwards.
  - **Even $n=2k$.** $m=k(2k-1)$ and $8m+1=(4k-1)^2$, so $\mathrm{edwardsLB}(m)=k^2-\tfrac14$, which
    is not an integer. Cut sizes are integers, so every graph with $m$ edges has max cut at least
    $k^2$ (Edwards), an excess of at least $\tfrac14$. $K_{2k}$ has max cut exactly $k^2$. So
    $f(\binom{2k}{2})=\tfrac14$.

  | $n$ | $m=\binom n2$ | edwardsLB$(m)$ | max cut of $K_n$ | excess of $K_n$ |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 2 | 1 | 3/4 | 1 | 1/4 |
  | 3 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 0 |
  | 4 | 6 | 15/4 | 4 | 1/4 |
  | 5 | 10 | 6 | 6 | 0 |
  | 6 | 15 | 35/4 | 9 | 1/4 |
  | 7 | 21 | 12 | 12 | 0 |
  | 8 | 28 | 63/4 | 16 | 1/4 |

    lower bound, $f=\tfrac14,0,\tfrac14,0,\tfrac14,0$ exactly at those $m$.
  - **Where the slip comes from.** The page's remark is right for odd $n$, and right for every
    $n$ if $f$ is the largest *integer* admissible (upstream's reading, where $\lfloor\tfrac14\rfloor=0$).
    The input took the real-valued reading, under which the remark fails for even $n$.

    and these bounded values play no role.
