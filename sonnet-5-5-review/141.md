## Summary

The page (OPEN) asks, for $k\ge3$, whether there are $k$ consecutive primes in arithmetic progression.
Green and Tao proved that $k$ primes in arithmetic progression always exist, but these need not be
consecutive, and Erdős called the conjecture "completely hopeless at present". Examples are known for
$k\le10$, and it is open, even for $k=3$, whether there are infinitely many such progressions.

The Lean states the "yes for every $k\ge3$" form and encodes "consecutive" as "no prime strictly between
consecutive terms".

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: pass.**

- **Source** (verbatim): "Let $k\geq 3$. Are there $k$ consecutive primes in arithmetic progression?"
- **Back-translation of the input's theorem:** "For every $k\ge3$ there are natural numbers $a$ and $d>0$
  such that $a+id$ is prime for $0\le i<k$, and for $0\le i<k-1$ no prime $p$ satisfies
  $a+id<p<a+(i+1)d$."
- **Checks.**
  - *Consecutive.* The $k$ terms are primes, increasing because $d>0$, and between two successive terms
    there is no prime. So they are $k$ consecutive primes, and they form an arithmetic progression with
    difference $d$. Conversely every such progression of consecutive primes satisfies the condition. The
    first term is prime by the case $i=0$.
  - *Quantifier structure.* The page poses the question for a given $k$. The input states it for every
    $k\ge3$, which is the conjunction of the questions, and it is also what upstream states.
  - *The range of $i$.* The gap condition covers `i + 1 < k`, that is, the $k-1$ gaps. There is no gap
    after the last term.
  - *Degenerate witnesses.* $a=0$ and $a=1$ fail primality, and $d>0$ excludes a constant progression, so no
    degenerate witness satisfies the statement. For $k\ge3$ the first term is odd (if $a=2$ the next prime is
    $3$ and $4$ is not prime), so $d$ is even. That is compatible with the statement.
- **The encoding accepts real progressions and rejects wrong ones.** `variants.three` to `variants.six` prove
  in Lean that it holds for $k=3,4,5,6$, with the smallest examples:
  - $3,5,7$ ($d=2$);
  - $251,257,263,269$ ($d=6$);
  - $9843019,\dots,9843139$ ($d=30$);
  - $121174811,\dots,121174961$ ($d=30$).

  A search (Addendum) confirms that these are the smallest. The encoding rejects progressions that are not
  consecutive: $3,7,11$ is a progression of primes, but $5$ lies between $3$ and $7$, so the gap condition
  fails.
