## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: pass.**

- **Source** (verbatim): "Let $F_{k}(N)$ be the size of the largest
  $A\subseteq \{1,\ldots,N\}$ such that the product of no $k$ many distinct elements of $A$ is
  a square. Is $F_5(N)=(1-o(1))N$? More generally, is $F_{2k+1}(N)=(1-o(1))N$?"
- **Back-translation of the input:** "for every $k\ge4$ there is $c>0$ such that for every
  $\varepsilon>0$, eventually $F_k(N)\le(1-c+\varepsilon)N$." That is "$F_k(N)\le(1-c_k+o(1))N$
  for $k\ge4$", the page's statement of Tao's theorem.
- **Relation to the questions.**
  - The theorem at $k=5$ gives $\limsup F_5(N)/N\le1-c_5<1$, which contradicts
    $F_5(N)=(1-o(1))N$. For odd $k\ge5$ it likewise contradicts the general question.
  - It is stronger than those negations: it covers every $k\ge4$, even $k$ included, and gives
    a uniform gap.
  - The converse fails: a negation does not give a gap.
- **Is the formal statement true?** For even $k\ge4$ the bound follows from
  $F_k(N)\asymp N/\log N$ [ESS95] (and from $F_4(N)=o(N)$ [Er38]). For odd $k\ge5$ it is
  Tao's theorem. So the statement is true as formalized, granting the page's attributions.

**A2. Lean semantics traps: pass.**

- **`F` is a genuine maximum.** The `sSup` set contains $0$ (the empty $A$, vacuous for
  $k\ge1$) and is bounded by $N$, since $A\subseteq$ `Icc 1 N`.
- **Indexing.** `Finset.Icc 1 N` is $\{1,\dots,N\}$. For $N=0$ the set is empty and `F k 0 = 0`.
  Every product is positive.
- **Distinctness.** The $k$ elements are a sub-finset `B ⊆ A` with `B.card = k`, so they are
  distinct.
- **`k = 0` is degenerate.** `noKSquareProduct 0 A` is false for every `A`, since $B=\emptyset$
  has product $1=1^2$. So `F 0 N = 0`. The theorem requires $k\ge4$.
- **Quantifiers and casts.** `∃ c > 0, ∀ ε > 0, ∀ᶠ N` is the standard one-sided $o(1)$.
  `(1 - c + ε) * N` casts `N : ℕ` to ℝ. Since $c$ is existential, it can be taken small.
- **Brute-force check of the definition.** Two elements have a square product iff they share a
  squarefree part, so $F_2(N)$ is the number of squarefree integers up to $N$. A search over
  squarefree-part vectors over $\mathbb F_2$ gives:

  | $N$ | $F_2$ | squarefree $\le N$ | $F_3$ | $F_4$ | $F_5$ |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 8 | 6 | 6 | 6 | 6 | 7 |
  | 12 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 8 |
  | 16 | 11 | 11 | 10 | 10 | 10 |
  | 20 | 13 | 13 | 12 | 12 | 12 |

  $F_2$ matches the page's $(6/\pi^2+o(1))N$. The other columns say little about asymptotics
  at this size.
