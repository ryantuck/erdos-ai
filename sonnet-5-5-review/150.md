## Summary

The page (PROVED, no prize) defines a minimal cut as a minimal set of vertices whose removal disconnects the graph,
$c(n)$ as the maximum number of minimal cuts on $n$ vertices, and asks whether $c(n)^{1/n}\to\alpha$ for some
$\alpha<2$. It records that Erdős and Nešetřil also ask whether $c(3m+2)=3^m$, Seymour's bound
$c(3m+2)\ge3^m$ (the graph of $m$ independent paths of length $4$ joining two vertices), Bradač's theorem (the limit
exists and is at most $2^{H(1/3)}=1.8899\cdots$), the consequence $\alpha\ge3^{1/3}=1.442\cdots$, and Bradač's
conjecture that this is the value.

The Lean defines vertex separators, minimal vertex cuts, their number, and $c(n)$ on `Fin n`, and states the
question as an existence statement.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: pass for the main theorem; defect in a helper (A3).**

- **Source** (verbatim): "A minimal cut of a graph is a minimal set of vertices whose removal disconnects the graph.
  Let $c(n)$ be the maximum number of minimal cuts a graph on $n$ vertices can have. Does $c(n)^{1/n}\to \alpha$ for
  some $\alpha <2$?"
- **Back-translation of the input's theorem:** "There is a real $\alpha<2$ such that
  $c(n)^{1/n}\to\alpha$, where $c(n)$ is the maximum, over connected graphs on $n$ vertices, of the number of
  inclusion-minimal vertex sets $S$ such that $G-S$ is not connected."
- **Checks.**
  - *The count.* `numMinimalVertexCuts G` is the `Set.ncard` of a set of `Finset (Fin n)`, which is finite, so it is
    the true number. `IsMinimalVertexCut` is inclusion-minimality among separators (`T ⊂ S` is a proper subset).
  - *"Removal disconnects".* $G-S$ must have at least two components. The input's `¬ Connected` also holds when
    $G-S$ is empty (Mathlib's `Connected` is `Preconnected` and `Nonempty`). This is the defect. The correct test is
    `¬ Preconnected`, which fails for the empty graph and for one vertex. `variants.separator_iff_firstPass` proves
    in Lean that the first pass's separators are v2's together with `univ`, and `variants.univ_not_separator` that
    `univ` is not a separator in v2.
  - *Where the defect shows.* A graph with no real vertex cut is complete: if two vertices $u,v$ are not adjacent,
    $V\setminus\{u,v\}$ is a separator, so `univ` is not minimal. The first pass therefore counts $1$ for each complete
    graph and agrees with v2 on every other graph (it adds `univ` exactly when no proper subset is a separator).
    `variants.numMinimalVertexCuts_top` proves in Lean that v2 counts $0$ for the complete graph. The first pass has
    `c 1 = c 2 = 1`, against $0$ for the page's connected graphs on 1 and 2 vertices.
  - *The limit is unaffected.* For $n\ge3$ the maximum number of minimal cuts of a connected graph is at least $1$
    (the path) and is attained at a graph that is not complete, and the complete graph contributes only $1$, so
    `c n` is the same in the two readings from $n=3$ (an argument, not formalized). The exact values
    $c(3),\dots,c(7)=1,2,5,9,14$ (Addendum) are the same in both.
  - *Connected graphs only.* The page says "a graph on $n$ vertices". A disconnected graph has only the empty set as
    a minimal cut (it is already disconnected), so it has $1$ at most, and for $n\ge3$ restricting to connected
    graphs does not change $c(n)$.
  - *Non-triviality.* `variants.c_five` proves in Lean that $c(5)\ge5$, so `c` is not identically small, and the
    values $1,2,5,9,14$ for $n=3,\dots,7$ are of the order of $3^{n/3}$. The statement is not vacuous: `Tendsto` to a real $\alpha<2$ is the content of
    [Br24].
  - *The second question.* "Whether $c(3m+2)=3^m$" is false at $m=1$ under the page's own definition: $C_5$ has 5
    minimal cuts (the five pairs of non-adjacent vertices) and the path on 5 vertices has 3. The exact values give
    $c(5)=5\ne3$, and the graph of $m$ independent paths of length $4$ has $3,20,43,102$ minimal cuts for
    $m=1,\dots,4$ (Addendum), far above $3^m$. Under the notion "minimal sets separating two given vertices" that
    graph has exactly $3^m$, which suggests the question concerns that notion or is recorded loosely. **DEFERRED:**
    [Er88] was not seen.
