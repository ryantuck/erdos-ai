## Summary

The page (PROVED (LEAN)) asks whether a triangle-free graph with maximum degree below $n^{1/2-\epsilon}$
can be made triangle-free of diameter $2$ by adding at most $\delta n^2$ edges. It records that Erdős
and Gyárfás proved this for maximum degree $\ll\log n/\log\log n$, that Simonovits's construction shows
it fails for maximum degree $\le Cn^{1/2}$, and that Alon proved it in a strong form with
$O(n^{2-\epsilon})$ added edges.

The Lean states the question and Alon's strong form directly. The definitions and the statements agree
with the page and with upstream. The faults are in the references.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: pass.**

- **Source** (verbatim): "Let $\epsilon,\delta>0$ and $n$ be sufficiently large in terms of $\epsilon$
  and $\delta$. Let $G$ be a triangle-free graph on $n$ vertices with maximum degree
  $<n^{1/2-\epsilon}$. Can $G$ be made into a triangle-free graph with diameter $2$ by adding at most
  $\delta n^2$ edges?"
- **Back-translation:** "For every $\varepsilon,\delta>0$ there is $N$ such that for all $n\ge N$ and
  every triangle-free $G$ on $n$ vertices with every degree below $n^{1/2-\varepsilon}$, some $H\supseteq G$
  on the same vertices is triangle-free, has diameter at most $2$, and has at most $\delta n^2$ edges
  not in $G$."
- **Item by item.**
  - "$n$ sufficiently large in terms of $\epsilon$ and $\delta$": `∀ ε δ, 0 < ε → 0 < δ → ∀ᶠ n in atTop`;
  - "triangle-free graph on $n$ vertices": `G : SimpleGraph (Fin n)` and `G.CliqueFree 3`;
  - "maximum degree $<n^{1/2-\epsilon}$": `∀ v, (G.degree v : ℝ) < n ^ (1/2 - ε)`;
  - "made into … by adding edges": `G ≤ H` on the same vertex type;
  - "triangle-free with diameter $2$": `H.CliqueFree 3 ∧ HasDiameterAtMostTwo H`;
  - "at most $\delta n^2$ edges": `|E(H)| − |E(G)| ≤ δ n²`, in ℝ.
- **Upstream** states the same with an explicit `∀ x y, x ≠ y → H.Adj x y ∨ ∃ z, …` and
  `(H.edgeFinset \ G.edgeFinset).card`. Since `G ≤ H` the two edge counts agree.
- **The second theorem.** Alon's strong form reads $\forall\varepsilon\,\exists C\,\forall^\infty n$ with
  the bound $Cn^{2-\varepsilon}$, which is the page's "$O(n^{2-\epsilon})$". It implies the first
  theorem, and `variants.main_of_alon` proves that in Lean.

**A2. Lean semantics traps: pass.**

- **No truncated subtraction.** The added edges are counted as a difference in ℝ.
- **Classical instances.** `open Classical` supplies the `Fintype` instances behind `G.degree` and
  `edgeFinset`. The statement does not depend on the choice of instance.
- **Diameter at most $2$ against exactly $2$.** For $n\ge3$ a triangle-free graph of diameter at most
  $2$ has diameter exactly $2$, since the only triangle-free complete graphs are $K_1$ and $K_2$.
  `HasDiameterAtMostTwo` also forces connectedness, since distinct vertices must be adjacent or have a
  common neighbour.
- **Small and large $\epsilon$.** For $\epsilon\ge\tfrac12$ the bound is at most $1$, so $G$ has no
  edges. A star then adds $n-1\le\delta n^2$ edges, so the statement holds trivially. The statement is
  not trivial for small $\epsilon$.
- **Real powers.** `(n : ℝ) ^ ((1 : ℝ) / 2 - ε)` is a real power of a nonnegative base, with the
  exponent forced to ℝ.
