## Summary

The page (OPEN, no prize) asks whether every graph with maximum degree $\Delta$ is the union of at most
$\frac54\Delta^2$ sets of strongly independent edges (induced matchings). It records that this is equivalent to
$\chi(L(G)^2)\le\frac54\Delta^2$, that the bound would be best possible (blow-ups of $C_5$), the weaker conjecture
with $(2-c)\Delta^2$ (Molloy–Reed $1.998\Delta^2$, Bruhn–Joos $1.93$, Bonamy–Perrett–Postle $1.835$, Hurley–de
Joannis de Verclos–Kang $1.772$) and Mahdian's $(2+o(1))\Delta^2/\log\Delta$ for $C_4$-free graphs, the easier
question settled by [CGTT90], and the clique-number results ($\frac32$ [Sl16], $\frac43$ [FaPo19], $\frac54$ for
triangle-free graphs and $1$ for $C_5$-free graphs [CKP20]).

The Lean defines strong independence and the strong chromatic index, and states the conjecture for all finite
graphs.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: pass.**

- **Source** (verbatim): "Let $G$ be a graph with maximum degree $\Delta$. Is $G$ the union of at most
  $\tfrac{5}{4}\Delta^2$ sets of strongly independent edges (sets such that the induced subgraph is the union of
  vertex-disjoint edges)?"
- **Back-translation of the input's theorem:** "For every finite graph $G$, the least $k$ for which the edges of
  $G$ can be coloured with $k$ colours so that two distinct edges of one colour are strongly independent is at most
  $\frac54\Delta(G)^2$."
- **Checks.**
  - *Strongly independent.* The page's "induced subgraph is the union of vertex-disjoint edges" is an induced
    matching: the subgraph induced by the endpoints of the set has exactly the edges of the set, and they are
    pairwise disjoint. `IsStronglyIndepEdgeSet G S` says $S\subseteq E(G)$ and any two distinct edges of $S$ have
    all endpoints distinct and non-adjacent. An edge of $G$ between endpoints of two different edges of $S$ would
    be an extra edge of the induced subgraph, so the two readings agree.
  - *The colouring.* `strongChromaticIndex G` is the `sInf` of the $k$ having `c : G.edgeSet → Fin k` such that
    distinct edges of the same colour satisfy that condition. `variants.strongChromaticIndex_iff` proves in Lean
    that this is the same as every colour class being `IsStronglyIndepEdgeSet`, so the first pass's two
    definitions agree (the second was unused).
  - *Union versus partition.* The page says "union". A cover by strongly independent sets becomes a partition by
    deleting repeats, since a subset of a strongly independent set is strongly independent, so the colouring form
    is equivalent.
  - *The bound.* `(5 / 4 : ℝ) * (G.maxDegree : ℝ) ^ 2` is real and $\chi'_s$ is an integer, so the statement says
    $\chi'_s\le\lfloor\frac54\Delta^2\rfloor$, which is "at most $\frac54\Delta^2$ sets".
  - *No junk value.* For finite $G$ the set of admissible $k$ is nonempty (give each edge its own colour), so `sInf`
    is the least element and not the junk value $0$ of `sInf ∅`.
  - *The page's equivalent form.* `variants.strongChromaticIndex_eq_chromaticNumber` proves in Lean that
    $\chi'_s(G)$ is the chromatic number of the square of Mathlib's `SimpleGraph.lineGraph` (the square is
    `erdos_problem_149.graphSq`: distinct vertices at distance at most $2$), and `variants.chromaticNumber_form`
    states the equivalence for the bound. This is the page's "equivalent to asking whether the chromatic number
    of the square of the line graph $L(G)^2$ is at most $\frac54\Delta^2$".
  - *Sharpness and non-triviality.* The input's docstring says the bound is sharp: "a blowup of $C_5$ with
    $\Delta=2k$ requires exactly $5k^2$ strong colors". `variants.blowup_sharp` proves in Lean that the blow-up of
    $C_5$ by $k$ independent vertices per class has $\Delta=2k$ and $\chi'_s=5k^2=\frac54\Delta^2$ for every
    $k\ge1$, and `variants.c5_sharp` is the case of $C_5$ itself ($\Delta=2$, $\chi'_s=5$). So the constant cannot
    be lowered, and a slip in either definition that made the statement trivially true would have shown here.
  - *Small cases.* The statement is not false at small parameters. An exact search over every labelled graph on at
    most 7 vertices finds no graph above the bound, and equality only at $C_5$ together with isolated vertices or
    disjoint edges (Addendum).
  - *Edge cases.* The edgeless graph, including the empty vertex type, has $\chi'_s=0=\Delta$, so the statement
    is $0\le0$. A graph with $\Delta=1$ is a matching, with $\chi'_s\le1\le1.25$.
  - *Finite graphs.* The page says "a graph", and the Lean quantifies over finite graphs (`Fintype V`), the
    standard reading. An infinite graph of finite maximum degree reduces to the finite case by compactness
    (de Bruijn–Erdős), which is not formalized here.
