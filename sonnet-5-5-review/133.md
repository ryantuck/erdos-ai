## Summary

The page (DISPROVED) defines $f(n)$ as the least possible maximum degree of a triangle-free graph on
$n$ vertices with diameter $2$, and asks for its order of growth and whether $f(n)/\sqrt n\to\infty$.
The answer is no: $f(n)\le(\sqrt2+o(1))\sqrt n$ (Hanson–Seyffarth) and $f(n)\le(2/\sqrt3+o(1))\sqrt n$
(Füredi–Seress), against the Moore bound $f(n)\ge(1-o(1))\sqrt n$. Alon believes $f(n)\sim\sqrt n$.

The Lean sets $f$ up with the wrong extremal operator, so $f\equiv0$ and the statements that should
carry content either fail or hold vacuously. The shape of the file is otherwise right.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: defect (fixed).**

- **Source** (verbatim): "Let $f(n)$ be minimal such that every triangle-free graph $G$ with $n$
  vertices and diameter $2$ contains a vertex with degree $\geq f(n)$. What is the order of growth of
  $f(n)$? Does $f(n)/\sqrt{n}\to \infty$?"
- **The wording of $f$.** "Minimal such that every graph has a vertex of degree $\ge f(n)$" is loose.
  The property "every such graph has a vertex of degree $\ge k$" is true for small $k$ and false for
  large $k$, so the meaningful extremum is the largest $k$, which equals the least possible maximum
  degree over all such graphs.
- **[defect: wrong-extremal-operator]** The input takes `sInf` of `{k | ∀ G …, ∃ v, k ≤ G.degree v}`.
  - For $n\ge1$ the element $0$ belongs to the set: `0 ≤ G.degree v` holds, and a vertex `v` exists.
    So the `sInf` is $0$.
  - For $n=0$ the unique graph on `Fin 0` satisfies the hypotheses and has no vertex, so the set is
    empty and `sInf ∅ = 0`.
  - `variants.first_pass_f_eq_zero` proves `erdos133_f n = 0` for every $n$ with the first pass's
    definition, which v2 keeps as `erdos133_f_first_pass`.
- **Consequences for the first pass's five theorems:**

  | first-pass theorem | with $f\equiv0$ | truth |
  |---|---|---|
  | Moore bound `Nat.sqrt (n-1) ≤ f n` | **false** for $n\ge2$ (proved at $n=2$) | true |
  | Hanson–Seyffarth `f n ≤ (√2+ε)√n` | true, vacuously | true |
  | Füredi–Seress `f n ≤ (2/√3+ε)√n` | true, vacuously | true |
  | ratio bounded `f n ≤ C√n` | true, vacuously | true |
  | Alon `f n/√n → 1` | **false** (proved) | open |

  `variants.first_pass_lower_bound_false` and `variants.first_pass_alon_false` prove the two failures.
- **v2:** `erdos133_f` is the `sSup` of the same set. `variants.f_two` proves $f(2)=1$ with it.

**A2. Lean semantics traps: pass for v2.**

- **The set is bounded for $n\ge1$.** The family of triangle-free graphs on $n$ vertices of diameter at
  most $2$ is nonempty ($K_1$, $K_2$, a star), and any `k` in the set is at most the maximum degree of
  a member. So the `sSup` is the true maximum, which is the least possible maximum degree. For $n=0$
  it is $0$.
- **Exact small values** (exhaustive search over labelled graphs):

  | $n$ | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | $f(n)$ | 1 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 3 |
  | $\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor$ | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 |
  | $\sqrt n$ | 1.41 | 1.73 | 2.00 | 2.24 | 2.45 | 2.65 | 2.83 |

  The Moore bound holds with equality at $n=5$, the 5-cycle, and at $n=10$, the Petersen graph.
- **Diameter at most $2$ against exactly $2$.** `HasDiameterAtMostTwo` says that distinct vertices are
  adjacent or have a common neighbour, so the graph is connected. For $n\ge3$ a triangle-free graph of
  diameter at most $2$ has diameter exactly $2$, since the only triangle-free complete graphs are $K_1$
  and $K_2$. For $n=2$ the family is $\{K_2\}$ and $f(2)=1$, which an exact diameter of $2$ would make
  empty. Nothing changes asymptotically.
- **The `[DecidableRel G.Adj]` binder** inside the set makes `G.degree` available. The set quantifies
  over every graph and every instance, which gives the same condition.
- **`HasTriangle`** is `¬ G.CliqueFree 3` unfolded, with the adjacency order immaterial.
- **Limits.** `Tendsto (fun n => f n / √n) atTop atTop` is divergence to $+\infty$ in ℝ.
  `Nat.sqrt (n - 1)` is $\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor$, and for $n=0$ the truncated subtraction gives
  $0\le f(0)=0$.
