## Summary

The page (OPEN) asks: for $k\ge3$, can the product of $k$ consecutive positive integers ever be powerful,
that is, can every prime that divides it divide it at least twice? Erdős and Selfridge conjectured that
it cannot. They proved that such a product is never a perfect power, and $k=2$ is excluded because
$n(n+1)$ is powerful for infinitely many $n$.

The Lean defines powerful numbers and the product $(m+1)\cdots(m+k)$ and asserts that for every
$k\ge3$ and $m\ge0$ the product is not powerful. That is the conjecture.
