## Summary

The page (PROVED, \$1000) defines $r_k(N)$ as the size of the largest subset of $\{1,\dots,N\}$ with no
non-trivial $k$-term arithmetic progression and asks to prove that $r_k(N)=o(N)$, a conjecture of Erdős
and Turán proved by Szemerédi. The best known bounds are due to Kelley–Meka ($k=3$, with Bloom–Sisask),
Green–Tao ($k=4$) and Leng–Sah–Sawhney ($k\ge5$).

The Lean defines progression-free finite sets of naturals and states the $\varepsilon$–$N_0$ form of
$r_k(N)=o(N)$ for every $k\ge3$.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: pass.**

- **Source** (verbatim): "Let $r_k(N)$ be the size of the largest subset of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ which does not
  contain a non-trivial $k$-term arithmetic progression. Prove that $r_k(N)=o(N)$."
- **Back-translation of the input's theorem:** "For every $k\ge3$ and $\varepsilon>0$ there is $N_0$ such
  that for every $N\ge N_0$ and every $S\subseteq\{0,\dots,N-1\}$ containing no $a,a+d,\dots,a+(k-1)d$ with
  $d>0$, we have $|S|\le\varepsilon N$."
- **Checks.**
  - *The $o(N)$ reading.* $r_k(N)=o(N)$ means $\forall\varepsilon>0$, eventually $r_k(N)\le\varepsilon N$. Since
    $r_k(N)$ is the maximum of $|S|$ over progression-free $S$, this is the input's statement.
    `variants.main_iff_littleO` proves in Lean that, for every $k\ge1$, the statement is equivalent to
    `Tendsto (rk k N / N) atTop (𝓝 0)`, where `rk k N` is the sup of `S.card` over progression-free
    `S ⊆ range N`.
  - *Non-trivial.* A non-trivial progression has $d\ne0$, and a progression with $d<0$ is a reversal of one
    with $d>0$. So "no $d>0$" is the page's condition.
  - *Indexing.* The input uses $\{0,\dots,N-1\}$ (`Finset.range N`) and the page $\{1,\dots,N\}$. A shift by
    one maps progressions to progressions.
  - *Quantifier structure.* `k` and `ε` are theorem parameters, so the statement holds for each, and $N_0$
    depends on both. `S` is quantified after `N`, as it must be.
  - *The start `a`* ranges over ℕ in `APFree`. Only starts that put the last term in `S ⊆ range N` matter.
- **Not vacuous, not trivially false.** `variants.apfree_three_example` proves in Lean that
  `{0, 1, 3, 4, 9, 10, 12, 13}` has no $3$-term progression, so `rk 3 14 ≥ 8` (`variants.rk_three_fourteen`).
  A search gives $r_3(14)=8$ (Addendum).
- **The case $k=3$ is a Mathlib theorem.** `variants.k_eq_three` derives the input's statement for $k=3$ in
  Lean from Mathlib's `roth_3ap_theorem_nat`, through `apfree_three_iff`, which shows that `APFree 3 S`
  is Mathlib's `ThreeAPFree`. If `APFree` or the quantifiers were wrong for $k=3$, this would not go
  through.
