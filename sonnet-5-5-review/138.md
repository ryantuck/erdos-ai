## Summary

The page (OPEN, \$500) defines $W(k)$ as the least $N$ such that every 2-colouring of $\{1,\dots,N\}$
has a monochromatic $k$-term arithmetic progression, and asks to improve the bounds, for example to
prove that $W(k)^{1/k}\to\infty$. Berlekamp, Gowers and Kozik–Shabanov give $p2^p\le W(p+1)$,
$W(k)\le 2^{2^{2^{2^{2^{k+9}}}}}$ and $W(k)\gg2^k$. Erdős also asks whether $W(k)/2^k\to\infty$, whether
$W(k+1)/W(k)\to\infty$ and whether $W(k+1)-W(k)\to\infty$.

The Lean defines monochromatic progressions in $\{0,\dots,N-1\}$, the van der Waerden property and
`W`, with the existence of `W` supplied by an axiom, and asserts `Tendsto (W k ^ (1/k)) atTop atTop`.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: pass.**

- **Source** (verbatim): "Let the van der Waerden number $W(k)$ be such that whenever $N\geq W(k)$ and
  $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ is $2$-coloured there must exist a monochromatic $k$-term arithmetic progression.
  Improve the bounds for $W(k)$ - for example, prove that $W(k)^{1/k}\to \infty$."
- **Back-translation of the input's theorem:** "$W(k)^{1/k}\to\infty$ as $k\to\infty$, where $W(k)$ is
  the least $N$ such that every 2-colouring `c : ℕ → Bool` has $a$ and $d>0$ with
  $a+(k-1)d<N$ and $c(a+id)=c(a)$ for all $i<k$."
- **Checks.**
  - *Indexing.* The input uses $\{0,\dots,N-1\}$ and the page $\{1,\dots,N\}$. A shift by one maps
    progressions to progressions, so the two numbers agree. The theorem docstring says $\{1,\dots,N\}$
    and the definitions say $\{0,\dots,N-1\}$, which is harmless.
  - *The bound `a + (k - 1) * d < N`* says the last term is at most $N-1$. It is exact for $k\ge1$.
  - *Colourings of ℕ.* `VanDerWaerdenProp` quantifies over `c : ℕ → Bool`. Only the values on
    $\{0,\dots,N-1\}$ matter, so this is the same as colourings of the finite set.
  - *Least element and monotonicity.* `W k` is the least `N` with the property, and the property is
    monotone in `N` (`mono`, proved in Lean), so "whenever $N\ge W(k)$" holds.
  - *Positivity.* For $k\ge1$, `W k ≥ 1`, so the base of the real power is positive.
- **Evidence that the definition is right** (see the Addendum). In Lean: `W 1 = 1`, `W 2 = 3`,
  `W 3 = 9`. By search: $W(4)=35$. The case $k=3$ is the one that fixes the off-by-one: with
  `≤ N` in place of `< N`, `W 3` would be 8.
- The main theorem is the page's "for example", in the direction to be proved. The page's "improve the
  bounds" is open-ended, so the bounds and Erdős's questions are covered as variants (B2).

**A2. Lean semantics traps: pass.**

- **ℕ subtraction.** `k - 1` truncates at $k=0$, where the condition reduces to `a < N` and `W 0 = 1`
  instead of the empty-progression value $0$. This is irrelevant to the asymptotic statement. For
  $k\ge1$ the definition is exact.
- **Division.** `(1 : ℝ) / (k : ℝ)` is $0$ at $k=0$, so the term is $1$ there. Irrelevant to the limit.
- **Real power.** `(W k : ℝ) ^ ((1 : ℝ) / (k : ℝ))` is `Real.rpow`. For $k\ge1$ and `W k ≥ 1` it is the
  $k$-th root, and `Tendsto … atTop atTop` is the right notion of "$\to\infty$".
- **`Nat.find`.** It needs a `DecidablePred`, which `open Classical` supplies. `W` is `noncomputable`.
  The axiom is what makes the `Nat.find` call type-check, which is the A3 point.

    `Combinatorics.exists_mono_homothetic_copy` gives, for every finite colouring of ℕ and every finite
