## Summary

The page (SOLVED) asks for the size of $f(n)$, the least number of colours with which the edges of $K_n$
can be coloured so that every $K_4$ contains at least 5 colours. Erdős and Gyárfás proved
$\frac56(n-1)<f(n)<n$ and $f(9)=8$. Bennett–Cushman–Dudek–Pralat proved $f(n)\sim\frac56n$, and
Joos–Mubayi found a shorter proof.

The Lean states the right three theorems, but its colourings live on ordered pairs, so each unordered
pair may carry two colours. With that definition $f$ is $O(\sqrt n)$ and equals $5$ for $4\le n\le16$ (at
least), and the three theorems, which are about the page's $f$, are false.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: defect (fixed).**

- **Source** (verbatim): "Let $f(n)$ be the smallest number of colours required to colour the edges of
  $K_n$ such that every $K_4$ contains at least 5 colours. Determine the size of $f(n)$."
- **Back-translation of the input's `f n`:** "the least $k$ such that the *ordered pairs* of distinct
  vertices of $K_n$ can be given colours in $\{1,\dots,k\}$ so that every 4 vertices see at least 5
  distinct colours among their 12 ordered pairs."
- **[defect: wrong-definition-asymmetric-colouring]** The page's colourings are of edges. The input
  allows `χ x y ≠ χ y x`, and `edgeColors χ S = S.offDiag.image (fun p => χ p.1 p.2)` reads both. A
  4-set then has 12 colour slots instead of 6, and the condition "at least 5 colours" is easier to meet.
  A colouring with 5 colours, for example, can satisfy it.
  - **Contrast with Problem 129.** The same representation is harmless there, because a monochromatic
    clique needs *every* ordered pair to have the colour (`sonnet-5-5-review/129.md`, A1). Here the
    condition is "at least 5 distinct colours", which asymmetry helps. This is why the same type is
    right in one file and wrong in the other.
- **Machine-checked evidence (all in v2, axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound` only).**
  1. `variants.asym9_ok`: the colouring `asym9 : EdgeColoring 9 5` (for example `asym9 0 1 = 1` and
     `asym9 1 0 = 0`) satisfies `IsK4FiveColored`, by `decide +kernel`. So the input's `f 9 ≤ 5`, and
     `variants.first_pass_f9_false : f_first_pass 9 ≠ 8`.
  2. `variants.first_pass_upper`: for every $n\ge4$, `f_first_pass n ≤ 8 * (Nat.sqrt n + 1)`. The proof
     is a first-moment count (`exists_ordered_colouring`, `card_boxed`):
     - There are $k^{n^2}$ maps from the ordered pairs of `Fin n` to `Fin k`.
     - Fix a 4-set $S$ and a 4-set $T$ of colours. Exactly $4^{12}k^{n^2-12}$ maps send the 12 ordered
       pairs of $S$ into $T$.
     - A map is bad iff it does this for some $(S,T)$, since a set of at most 4 colours lies in some
       4-set of colours when $k\ge4$. There are $\binom n4\binom k4$ pairs $(S,T)$.
     - A good map exists if $\binom n4\binom k4 4^{12}<k^{12}$. This follows from
       $n^4k^4 4^{12}<k^{12}$, that is $k^2>64n$, and $k=8(\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor+1)$ satisfies it.
  3. `variants.first_pass_bounds_false`: with `f_first_pass` in place of `f`, "for all large $n$,
     $\frac56(n-1)<f(n)<n$" is false. For every $n\ge107$ the bound $8(\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor+1)$ is
     below $\frac56(n-1)$ (checked numerically up to $2\cdot10^6$; the Lean proof uses $n\ge400$).
  4. `variants.first_pass_asymptotic_false`: with `f_first_pass`, "for every $\varepsilon>0$ and all
     large $n$, $|f(n)/n-\frac56|<\varepsilon$" is false for $\varepsilon=\frac12$, because
     `f_first_pass n / n → 0`.
- **Numerical picture** (computed, not proved in Lean except the $n=9$ entry of the last row):

  | $n$ | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | page's $f(n)$ (symmetric; exhaustive search) | 5 | 5 | 5 | 7 | 7 | 8 |
  | input's `f n` (ordered pairs) | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 |

  The input's `f n` is at least 5 for $n\ge4$ because a single 4-set needs 5 colours. Five colours
  were found by local search for every $n$ from 4 to 16, with all 4-sets checked by the search program,
  and the case $n=9$ is the Lean certificate. The exact first-moment expectation of the number of bad
  4-sets is $0.019$ for $n=50$, $k=40$ (a computed value), so a 40-colour ordered colouring of $K_{50}$
  exists, and $40<\frac56\cdot49$.
- **Why it is easy to miss.** For symmetric $\chi$ the 12 ordered pairs give the 6 edge colours, so the
  two definitions agree on every colouring the page has in mind. The docstrings say "ordered pairs", so
  nothing is hidden, but nothing says that symmetry is needed either.

  other definitions are unchanged. The main theorem is the input's `erdos136_asymptotic` for this `f`.
