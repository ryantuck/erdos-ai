## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: pass.**

- **Source** (verbatim): "Let $f(n)$ be maximal such that if $A\subseteq\mathbb{N}$ has
  $\lvert A\rvert=n$ then $\prod_{a\neq b\in A}(a+b)$ has at least $f(n)$ distinct prime
  factors. Is it true that $f(n)/\log n\to\infty$?"
- **Back-translation:** "for every $C>0$ there is $N$ such that for all $n\ge N$ and every
  $A\subseteq\mathbb N$ with $\lvert A\rvert=n$, the number of distinct primes dividing
  $\prod_{a\ne b}(a+b)$ is at least $C\log n$."
- **Equivalence with $f(n)/\log n\to\infty$.**
  - $f(n)$ is a minimum over a set of natural numbers, so it exists.
  - $f(n)\ge C\log n$ for all large $n$, for every $C$, is $f(n)/\log n\to\infty$.
  - Upstream states it as `Tendsto (fun n => f n / Real.log n) atTop atTop` for a greatest
    lower bound `f`. That is the same thing.

**A2. Lean semantics traps: pass.**

- **Ordered pairs.** `A.offDiag` is the set of ordered pairs $(a,b)$ with $a\ne b$, so every
  unordered pair contributes $a+b$ twice. The set of prime factors is the same as for unordered
  pairs.
- **Never zero.** $a\ne b$ gives $a+b\ge1$, so the product is a positive natural number.
  `Nat.primeFactors` is its set of distinct primes, and `.card` counts them.
- **Small $A$.** For $\lvert A\rvert\le1$ the product is empty, equal to $1$, with $0$ primes.
- **$0\in\mathbb N$.** `Finset ℕ` allows $0\in A$, which changes small values. For $n=2$, $A=\{0,1\}$
  has product $1$ and no primes, while over positive integers the least value is $1$.
  - **It is asymptotically harmless.** Let $A_+=A\setminus\{0\}$. The product over $A$ is divisible
    by the product over $A_+$, and $\lvert A_+\rvert\ge n-1$. So a lower bound $C'\log\lvert A_+\rvert$
    over positive integers gives $(C'-o(1))\log n$ with $0$ allowed. Conversely, positive-integer
    sets are among all sets. So the proposition with $0$ allowed is equivalent to the one
    without, for every $C$.
- **Real log.** `Real.log n` for $n:\mathbb N$ is the real logarithm of the cast, positive for
  $n\ge2$.
- **Brute force** over $A\subseteq\{1,\dots,30\}$ (exhaustive within that range, so these are
  upper bounds on $f$):

  | $n$ | positive integers | with $0$ allowed | example set (positive) | $\log n$ |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 2 | 1 | 0 | $\{1,2\}$ | 0.69 |
  | 3 | 2 | 2 | $\{1,2,7\}$ | 1.10 |
  | 4 | 2 | 2 | $\{1,5,7,11\}$ | 1.39 |
  | 5 | 3 | 3 | $\{1,2,7,11,25\}$ | 1.61 |
  | 6 | 4 | 4 | $\{1,2,3,5,7,13\}$ | 1.79 |

  The definition behaves as intended. These values say nothing about the asymptotics.
