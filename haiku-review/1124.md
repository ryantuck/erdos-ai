## Summary

The problem asks a yes/no question: "Can a square and a circle of the same area be decomposed into a finite number of congruent parts?" Laczkovich proved in 1990 that the answer is yes, using translations only. The Lean file formalizes this by defining the unit square `[0,1]²` and a closed disk of radius `1/√π` (both area 1), then stating that their pieces can be partitioned such that corresponding pieces are related by isometries. The mathematical content is correct, but the Lean statement fails to use the `answer(…) ↔ P` form required by the checklist for yes/no questions: it is a bare existential assertion instead of `answer(True) ↔ ∃ n, …`. This is a violation of A4 and the corpus convention (cf. fable-review/1005, where the same defect was found and fixed).
