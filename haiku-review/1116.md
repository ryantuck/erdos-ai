## Summary

The problem asks: given a meromorphic function $f$, let $n(r, a)$ count the number of roots of $f(z) = a$ in the disc $|z| < r$. Does there exist a meromorphic (or entire) $f$ such that for every $a \ne b$, $\limsup_{r \to \infty} \frac{n(r,a)}{n(r,b)} = \infty$? Gol'dberg and Toppila independently constructed entire functions answering this affirmatively. The Lean file formalizes this as an existential statement over entire functions (differentiable on ℂ), with the limsup condition encoded in its standard ε-δ form: $\forall M, R, \exists r > R$ with $M \cdot n(r,b) < n(r,a)$. The encoding is mathematically sound, but the statement **should be wrapped in `answer(True) ↔`** to match the corpus convention for yes/no questions. Additionally, the **primary source [Ha74] is missing from the citation list**, though [Go78] and [To76] are present.
