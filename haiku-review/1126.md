## Summary

The problem concerns Cauchy's functional equation on ℝ under an almost-everywhere condition. Given a function $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfying $f(x+y) = f(x) + f(y)$ for Lebesgue-almost all $(x,y) \in \mathbb{R}^2$, the theorem asserts the existence of a function $g : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ that is additive everywhere and equals $f$ almost everywhere. This result was independently proved by de Bruijn (1966) and Jurkat (1965). The Lean formalization encodes this statement precisely: the hypothesis uses product Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb{R}^2$, and the conclusion separates the universal quantification over all $(x,y)$ for $g$ from the almost-everywhere condition for the agreement $f = g$.
