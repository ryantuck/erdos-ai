## Summary

The problem: Does there exist a constant c > 0 such that for all large n, every polynomial P of degree n with coefficients ±1 satisfies max_{|z|=1} |P(z)| > (1+c)√n? In other words, do ultraflat Littlewood polynomials exist? The lower bound √n follows trivially from Parseval. The problem is **OPEN** for ±1 coefficients (though resolved affirmatively for general unimodular coefficients).

The Lean statement formalizes the existence claim correctly as a triple-nested quantifier on (c, N₀, z), with well-defined helper functions for Littlewood coefficient validation and polynomial evaluation on the unit circle. The definitions are sound. However, the **critical defect**: the problem is a yes/no question ("Does there exist...?") but is encoded as a bare existential assertion instead of the required `answer(sorry) ↔ (existence statement)` form per the corpus convention for open yes/no questions. This means the theorem statement asserts the answer is yes, contradicting the OPEN status.
