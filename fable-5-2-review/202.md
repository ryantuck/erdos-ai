## Summary

The page asks how many pairwise disjoint congruence classes $a_i \pmod{n_i}$ with distinct moduli $n_1<\dots<n_r\le N$ there
can be; with $f(N)$ the maximum and $L(N)=\exp(\sqrt{\log N\log\log N})$ it records
$N/L(N)^{1+o(1)}<f(N)<N/L(N)^{\sqrt3/2+o(1)}$ [BFV13] and the conjecture that the lower bound is the truth.

The Lean defines `IsPairwiseDisjointSystem S N` for a finite set `S` of pairs (modulus, residue) and asserts: for every
$\varepsilon>0$ and all large $N$, every such system has $|S|\le N/\exp((1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{\log N\log\log N})$.

    pass.

    excludes every other class (`eq_of_modulus_one`, proved); the page presumably has $n_i\ge2$, which changes $f(N)$ by at
    most 1.
