## Summary

The page (PROVED) states: for every $g\ge2$ and all large $n\equiv1,3\pmod6$ there is a Steiner triple system on $n$ vertices in
which any $j$ edges, $2\le j\le g$, span at least $j+3$ vertices. Proved by Kwan, Sah, Sawhney and Simkin.

The Lean defines `IsSteinerTripleSystem` (all edges of size 3; every pair of distinct vertices in exactly one edge) and
asserts the statement with `S ⊆ edges`, `S.card = j`, and `(S.biUnion id).card` for the number of vertices spanned.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: pass.**

- **Source** (verbatim): "For any $g\geq 2$, if $n$ is sufficiently large and $\equiv 1,3\pmod{6}$ then there exists a 3-uniform
  hypergraph on $n$ vertices such that every pair of vertices is contained in exactly one edge (i.e. the graph is a Steiner
  triple system) and for any $2\leq j\leq g$ any collection of $j$ edges contains at least $j+3$ vertices."
- **Back-translation** (lines 37–46): "For every $g\ge2$ there is $N_0$ such that for every $n\ge N_0$ with $n\bmod6\in\{1,3\}$
  there is a family of 3-subsets of $\{0,\dots,n-1\}$ covering every pair exactly once, such that for $2\le j\le g$ every $j$
  members of the family have union of size at least $j+3$."
- **Checks.**
  - *$j=2$.* Two distinct edges share at most one vertex (`inter_card_le_one`, proved), so they span at least 5
    (`two_edges_span`, proved).
  - *$j=3$.* Three edges span at least $9-3=6$ (`three_edges_span`, proved). So a "triangle" (three edges on six vertices) is
    allowed, as it must be: every Steiner triple system with at least two edges through a point contains one.
  - *$j=4$.* Four edges on six vertices (the Pasch configuration) are excluded. A computation (scratchpad) confirms that the
    Fano plane has four lines on six points, so the conclusion fails at $n=7$ for $g\ge4$, and $N_0$ depends on $g$.
  - *$g\le3$.* The theorem is then exactly the existence of Steiner triple systems (`variants.of_kirkman`, proved from
    `variants.kirkman` as a hypothesis, with $N_0=0$).
  - *Degenerate $n$.* $n=1$ (no edges) and $n=3$ (one edge) satisfy the conclusion for every $g$ (`variants.n_eq_one`,
    `variants.n_eq_three`, proved).
