## Summary

The page (OPEN) defines $G_k(N)$, the largest $m$ such that every set of $N$ integers has a $k$-AP-free subset of size $m$,
and $R_k(N)$, the largest size of a $k$-AP-free subset of $\{1,\dots,N\}$, and asks whether $R_3(N)/G_3(N)\to1$.

The Lean defines `HasKTermAP`, `maxAPFreeSize` (a `Finset.sup` over AP-free subsets) and `R_ap`, and asserts: for every
$\varepsilon>0$ and all large $N$, every $S\subseteq\mathbb Z$ with $|S|=N$ has a 3-AP-free $T\subseteq S$ with
$|T|\ge(1-\varepsilon)R_3(N)$.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: pass.**

- **Source** (verbatim): "Let $G_k(N)$ be such that any set of $N$ integers contains a subset of size at least $G_k(N)$ which
  does not contain a $k$-term arithmetic progression. Determine the size of $G_k(N)$. How does it relate to $R_k(N)$, the size of
  the largest subset of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ without a $k$-term arithmetic progression? Is it true that

- **Back-translation** (lines 46–51): "For every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $N_0$ such that for $N\ge N_0$ every $N$-element set of
  integers contains a subset with no 3-term progression of size at least $(1-\varepsilon)R_3(N)$."
- **Checks.**
  - *Equivalence with the limit.* With `G_ap k N` the minimum of `maxAPFreeSize k S` over $|S|=N$ (attained, since it is an
    infimum of naturals over a nonempty set), the input's statement is $(1-\varepsilon)R_3(N)\le G_3(N)$ for large $N$
    (`iff_G_form`, proved). Since $1\le G_3(N)\le R_3(N)$ for $N\ge1$ (`one_le_G_ap`, `G_ap_le_R_ap`, proved), this is
    $R_3(N)/G_3(N)\to1$ (`iff_limit`, proved): one direction uses $\varepsilon'=1-(1+\varepsilon/2)^{-1}$, the other
    $(1-\varepsilon)(1+\varepsilon)\le1$.
  - *The page's values.* `R_ap 3 5 = 4` is proved (`R_ap_three_five`). A brute-force computation (scratchpad) confirms
    $R_3(14)=8$; that $\{0,2,3,4,6\}$ has no 3-AP-free 4-subset, so $G_3(5)\le3$ (and $G_3(5)\ge3$ since no 5 integers have all
    triples in progression); and that $\{0,3,4,5,6,7,8,10,11,12,14,15,16,18\}$ has largest 3-AP-free subset of size 7, so
    $G_3(14)\le7$.
  - *Non-triviality.* For $\varepsilon\ge1$ the statement is trivial; for $\varepsilon<1$ it is the open question.
