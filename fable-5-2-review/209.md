## Summary

The page (DISPROVED) asks whether every collection of $d\ge4$ pairwise non-parallel lines in the plane, with no point on four
of them, has a Gallai triangle: three lines meeting in three points, each on exactly two lines of the collection. Füredi and
Palásti showed this is false for $d$ not divisible by 9, and Escudero for all $d\ge4$.

The Lean defines `IsLine`, `AreParallel`, `pointMultiplicity`, `IsOrdinaryPoint`, `HasGallaiTriangle`, and asserts: for
every $d\ge4$ there is a set `A` of `d` lines, pairwise non-parallel, with every point on at most three of them, and
`¬HasGallaiTriangle A`.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: pass.**

- **Source** (verbatim): "Let $A$ be a finite collection of $d\geq 4$ non-parallel lines in $\mathbb{R}^2$ such that there are
  no points where at least four lines from $A$ meet. Must there exist a 'Gallai triangle' (or 'ordinary triangle'): three
  lines from $A$ which intersect in three points, and each of these intersection points only intersects two lines from $A$?"
  And: "Escudero [Es16] showed this is false for all $d\geq 4$."
- **Back-translation** (lines 49–57): "For every $d\ge4$ there is a set of $d$ lines in $\mathbb R^2$, no two parallel, no
  point on more than three, in which no three lines have three distinct pairwise intersection points that each lie on exactly
  two of the lines."
- **Checks.**
  - *Answers the question.* `question_false_of_main` (proved): the statement implies
    `¬ ∀ d ≥ 4, ∀ A, … → HasGallaiTriangle A`; only $d=4$ is used.
  - *Intersection points.* `eq_of_mem_of_mem` (proved): two distinct lines share at most one point, so `p₁₂`, `p₂₃`, `p₁₃` are
    determined by the lines. The definition does not require the three lines to be non-concurrent separately; the three
    distinctness conditions on the points do that.
  - *Small cases by hand.* $d=4$: three lines through a point $P$ and a fourth line missing $P$; every three lines forming a
    triangle include two lines through $P$, a triple point. $d=5$: lines $L_1,L_2,L_3$ through $P$ and $L_4,L_5$ meeting on
    $L_1$. $d=6$: two pencils of three lines. $d=7$: a line $\ell$, points $A_1,A_2,A_3\in\ell$, and the lines $PA_i$, $QA_i$
    for two further points $P,Q$; any three lines either are concurrent or have a triple point as a vertex. In each case a
    generic choice makes the lines pairwise non-parallel with no point on four of them.
  - *Projective versus affine.* [Es16] is titled for the projective plane. A projective arrangement gives an affine one with
    no parallel lines by sending to infinity a line through none of the intersection points.
