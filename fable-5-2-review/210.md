## Summary

The page (PROVED) defines $f(n)$ as the least number of ordinary lines (lines through exactly two points) determined by $n$
points in the plane, not all on a line, and asks whether $f(n)\to\infty$ and how fast. Motzkin proved $f(n)\to\infty$; Green
and Tao proved $f(n)\ge n/2$ for large $n$, and $f(n)\ge3\lfloor n/4\rfloor$ for large odd $n$.

The Lean defines `LiesOnLine_210`, `NotAllCollinear_210`, `IsOrdinaryPair_210`, and `ordinaryLineCount_210` (ordered ordinary
pairs, halved), and asserts: there is $N_0$ such that every non-collinear $S$ with $|S|\ge N_0$ has
`ordinaryLineCount_210 S ≥ S.card / 2`.

## A. Semantic fidelity

**A1. Statement fidelity: defect.**

- **Source** (verbatim): "Let $f(n)$ be minimal such that the following holds. For any $n$ points in $\mathbb{R}^2$, not all on
  a line, there must be at least $f(n)$ many lines which contain exactly 2 points (called 'ordinary lines'). Does
  $f(n)\to \infty$? How fast?" And: "Green and Tao [GrTa13] proved that $f(n)\geq n/2$ for sufficiently large $n$."
- **Back-translation** (lines 38–43): "There is $N_0$ such that every finite set of at least $N_0$ points, not all collinear,
  determines at least $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ ordinary lines, $n$ being the number of points."
- **Diff.** **[defect: nat-division-floor]** $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ against $\lceil n/2\rceil$. With $n=2m+1$ the input asks for
  $m$ lines, the page for $m+1$. The input's statement is true and follows from the page's
  (`floor_form_of_main`, proved); the converse fails for odd $n$. Upstream writes the Green–Tao bound as `n ≤ 2 * f n`.
- **Checks.**
  - *The floor form is a statement in its own right.* $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ ordinary lines is how the Dirac–Motzkin conjecture
    is often phrased for all $n$; the known exceptions to $\lceil n/2\rceil$ are small ($n=7$ has a configuration with 3
    ordinary lines, by the page's "best possible for $n=7$" for $3n/7$). For large $n$ the page records the stronger
    $\lceil n/2\rceil$, and more for odd $n$.
  - *The first question.* "Does $f(n)\to\infty$?" is Motzkin's theorem; it follows from the theorem, and v2 states it
    (`variants.motzkin`).

**A2. Lean semantics traps: one defect.**
